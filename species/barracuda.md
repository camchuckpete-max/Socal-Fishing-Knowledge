# California Barracuda

Barracuda are the toothy member of the SoCal **"three B's"** — bass,
barracuda, bonito — taken off the same kelp lines, boiler rocks and beach
edges that hold [calico bass](calico-bass.md) and [bonito](bonito.md)
(`6-8KfjEg0x8`, `DnSqw4r7A1s`). Two things make the program its own: teeth
that cut a monofilament leader even in heavy sizes, so the terminal end
changes when you target them (`lm7D9Tlc7Po`), and speed — barracuda are
quicker than yellowtail and reach a chrome jig first, which makes chrome the
barracuda colour and a liability when yellowtail are the target
(`YAKOv9bXKO0`, `PrdPJy26H8c`). They turn up on trips run for bass, bonito and
yellowtail rather than on trips run for them, so the doctrine below works in
both directions — how to catch them, and how to keep them off a bait meant for
something else.

## Where & when

Barracuda hold on the **kelp lines, boiler rocks and beach edges** of the
coast and the islands — the same structure edges the coastal bass relate to
([bass structure](../locations/bass-structure.md), [break walls, jetties and
riprap](../locations/breakwalls-jetties-riprap.md)) — from the Channel
Islands and Santa Monica Bay south through Catalina and San Clemente Island,
and down the Baja Pacific coast at least to La Bocana.
⚠ Fact-check (unverifiable): the beach edge (`DnSqw4r7A1s`) and the kelp
(`M6U_FVdosr4`) are sourced, but no corpus source puts barracuda on boiler
rocks — that structure term is carried over from the bass picture.

Dated SoCal appearances run **late May through October**: Catalina in late
May for anglers fishing surface presentations (`S2L3KLSQ6Is`); the Channel
Islands, Santa Monica Bay and the open beach in July, where "along the beach
you're getting barracuda, calicos and sand bass" (`DnSqw4r7A1s`); Santa Cruz
and Anacapa in early August (`CMQkHQMxbXM`, `-JZpyWLdKlk`); Palos Verdes kelp
and San Clemente Island in October (`0KQ--N5TjqE`, `Klfb433I3Uk`). That spans
the warming transition through fall fragmentation in the
[water regimes](../conditions/water-regimes.md) — [May](../seasonal/may.md),
[June–July](../seasonal/june-july.md), [August](../seasonal/august.md).

**Presence is not the bite.** On the San Clemente Island front side the
yellowtail window is the morning and the day then "becomes a pick-o day" of
bass, barracuda and bonito — barracuda fill the hours after the target
species shuts off, so barracuda hours and yellowtail hours are not the same
hours (`Blh2BA-7Ono`).

**Baja Pacific.** Off Ensenada barracuda are named with bonito and yellowtail
as the standing inshore mix (`0dIwWiOc1NY`), and as an expected species on a
high spot 7–8 mi off the Hotel Coral marina that crests to about 25 ft from
roughly 200 ft — named as a target there, never shown caught (`CKq0Z6ExVs4`).
At La Bocana the inshore rock structure is worked as a distinct
**"Barracuda grounds"** band that a boat leaves behind on the run out to the
tuna grounds (`Cobp85UvHmM`), and jigs fished to roughly 80 ft on that
structure drew a hookup "every single drop" (`qri15R3caYE`).
⚠ Fact-check (unverifiable): "about 80 feet" is verbatim, but the quoted
phrase is not — the auto-captions render it "every single draw" and "every
single saw", never "every single drop".

Zone-by-zone trip detail is in the [zone guides](#zone-guides); trip reports
are in the [evidence file](evidence/barracuda.md).

## Presence & forage

- **Bait size selects for and against them.** A large live mackerel is the
  documented screen: fishing one at San Clemente Island, Catalina or the
  Coronados keeps barracuda, calico and bonito off the bait and leaves
  straight yellowtail — a yellowtail eats a **1½ lb mackerel** without trouble,
  which is the size that screens the smaller fish out
  (`YVHdDbkQrKk`, `w5_x6kkN-xE`).
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the 1½ lb figure answers "how big a
  mackerel" as a yellowtail-capacity aside — "I've seen yellowtail eat one and
  a half pound mackerel, no problem" — and is never stated as the size that
  screens the smaller fish out; the two cites are one re-uploaded episode, not
  two independent sources.
  The same mechanism run the other way — a
  Dana Point kelp session had barracuda "actually biting the smaller
  sardines" (`M6U_FVdosr4`).
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): "they" follows both species on
  camera — "some short barracuda flopping around in the kelp, some bass
  starting to flip around in the kelp. They were actually biting the smaller
  sardines today" — so the smaller-sardine preference is not attributed to
  barracuda alone.
- **They win races for flash.** Barracuda and bonito are quicker than
  yellowtail, so a chrome jig fished where all three are present gets taken by
  the barracuda first (`YAKOv9bXKO0`).
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the source is hedged and scoped to
  chrome yo-yo jigs — "they're a little faster, they're a little quicker than
  the yellowtail, so it's not that the yellowtail won't bite these, it's that
  the barracuda and the bonito may get them first."
- **Squid days put them in the count.** Island variety trips built around live
  squid produce the three B's alongside yellowtail, white seabass and halibut
  (`6-8KfjEg0x8`).
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the three-B's list is that trip's
  stated pre-trip goal, not its production — the recap names whitefish, reds,
  blue bass, bocaccio and lingcod, with none of the three B's landed.
- ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: the natural forage species barracuda
  actually eat in SoCal, and whether kelp-line residency is bait-driven or
  structure-driven.

## Feeding triggers

- **Competition inside the school.** A surface jig dropped by one barracuda is
  eaten by the next fish in the school before it can fall away (`41d5bquXkAc`).
  ⚠ Fact-check (single-source): a single 35-second clip is the corpus's only
  record of intra-school competition on the lure, and no source draws a
  retrieve rule from it.
- **Curiosity is not a strike.** Barracuda, like yellowtail, will follow a jig
  all the way to the boat without eating it; the failure is not triggering the
  instinctive reaction to a bait fish, not absence of fish (`pcwcRdmWmLc`).
- **Daypart.** They are the mid-day pick after the morning yellowtail window
  closes at San Clemente Island (`Blh2BA-7Ono`). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus
  source: mechanism — whether that is a barracuda feeding window or simply
  what is left once the yellowtail stop.
- ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: tide/slack, current, moon-phase or
  temperature gating specific to barracuda.

## Finding them (sign & sonar)

- **Sign:** barracuda run in **loose schools along the kelp line** and hold
  over structure where a boat is anchored — "nice bass, barracuda when they're
  anchored up" (`DnSqw4r7A1s`). They mix into surface activity with bonito, so
  a [boil or breezing fish](../planning/search-and-glassing.md#surface-sign-vocabulary)
  over a kelp edge can be either species
  ([bird reading](../conditions/bird-reading.md)).
- **Sonar signature:** they mark as **scattered, higher-in-the-column fish
  suspended along the kelp/structure edge** rather than pinned to the bottom
  like the bass — a looser, more mobile sign in the same zone. Grade the bait
  picture the same way you would for bass
  ([electronics and sounder](../planning/electronics-and-sounder.md),
  [search and glassing](../planning/search-and-glassing.md)).
  ⚠ Fact-check (unverifiable): no corpus source describes a barracuda sonar
  return; this read is inferred from the bass/kelp-edge picture.
- ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: species-specific sonar depths for
  barracuda.

## Gear summary (class terms)

- **Surface iron — jig-stick class.** A worked barracuda/yellowtail/bonito
  iron outfit is a **10 ft rod rated 30–60 lb** paired with a size-16
  star-drag-class reel (`42A8Owhc8fw`). Jigs: Tady 45, Tady 14A, Salas 7X
  (`pcwcRdmWmLc`); the Murder Weapon Viper is a further swimming iron already
  fished on barracuda (`WE643Fue1_A`) — see [iron jigs](../lures/iron-jigs.md)
  and [surface iron — colour](../techniques/surface-iron-color.md).
- **Small jigs — light casting class**, which
  [gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md) defines as the barracuda/bonito
  small-jig setup.
- **Plastics and jerkbaits — bass class.** A **7'11" medium-heavy rated
  15–35 lb** covers barracuda and bass on plastics and doubles as a
  sliding-sinker, straight-flyline or dropper-loop outfit (`42A8Owhc8fw`).
- **Live bait — flyline class, 25–40 lb**
  ([flyline](../techniques/flyline.md)).
- **Terminal — the one real change from bass gear is the bite tippet:** a
  short single-strand wire tied with a
  [haywire twist](../rigging/haywire-twist.md), or heavier hard mono
  (`lm7D9Tlc7Po`). Hook choice is contested below.

## Doctrine & conflicts

Section order below is not a ranking.

- **Wire or no wire — the choice turns on whether barracuda are the target or
  the tax.** Fishing *for* toothy species, the answer is a short single-strand
  wire bite tippet tied with a haywire twist, because heavy mono still gets
  cut (`lm7D9Tlc7Po`). Fishing *through* them on jigs, the answer is no wire
  anywhere — not on the leader, not as an assist-cord core — accepting the
  loss of an assist hook to the occasional barracuda or shark, and moving the
  boat when toothy fish are thick enough to cost jigs (`-nIhadZwxAA`).
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-internal): [bonito](bonito.md) carries a
  Cameron-adjudicated ruling (2026-08-26) that barracuda and bonito bite-offs
  are both **rare** — "no wire, and no reported case of a fish chewing through
  terminal gear" — which is the opposite premise to the cut-mono doctrine this
  note builds its terminal section on. Adjudicated wording is final and is not
  reconciled here.
  ⚠ Fact-check (unverifiable): the no-wire seminar is Florida/Atlantic — its
  toothy-fish examples are kingfish, wahoo and sharks off Stuart — so applying
  it to SoCal barracuda is this note's extension, not the source's scope, and
  it sits unlabeled beside SoCal doctrine.
- **Treble or single hook — the choice turns on how many barracuda you expect
  to unhook, and on who else is on the deck.** The treble stays on the iron
  for yellowtail, bass, barracuda and bonito, and comes off only when
  barracuda are thick enough that shaking fish free quickly matters
  (`28IttH7P7K0`). On a sport boat the single inline hook is the standing
  choice regardless of numbers: a barracuda thrashing on deck with a treble in
  its snout puts every free hook into the people around it, and half the fish
  can be shaken off at the rail instead (`xgPEt4Zj35Q`, `kt3G72gUldM`).
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the source states a sport-boat
  preference on safety grounds and never mentions fish numbers, so "regardless
  of numbers" is this note's contrast with `28IttH7P7K0`, not the source's
  framing.
- **Retrieve speed — the choice turns on which variable is being set.**
  Choosing among jigs, the slower-swimming jig is the one that gets bit by
  barracuda and calico, where yellowtail want a faster-swimming jig
  (`lV-UcWUvxgc`).
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): doubly hedged in the source — "there
  are times where a slower presentation jig will get you bit, say, on a
  barracuda or a calico. Whereas, with a yellowtail, you might want a faster
  swimming jig" — and immediately qualified by current speed.
  Working a surface iron, the instruction is to wind *faster*
  — fast enough to keep the lure gliding on top where the angler can see it
  (`ejUTAnONf7o`).
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-internal): [surface iron](../techniques/surface-iron.md)
  reads this same cite as "the slowest wind" that keeps the jig gliding, with
  the rod tip as the lever for lifting it *without* winding faster, and
  separately records a flat denial that species-keyed retrieves exist — "a
  barracuda retrieve — no" (`Ec2GaNpiOHI`) — which this whole conflict frame
  is built against without citing it.

## Landing & handling

- **A barracuda left in the sun all day goes to mush** — the meat falls out of
  the fish when it is cut, and a barracuda handled that way is the standing
  SoCal reference point for spoiled flesh (`5to3Q5P7w90`).
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the mush and the meat falling out
  when cut are said of a **dorado** carried in a gunny sack; barracuda enters
  only as the audience simile — "you guys remember the barracuda days, like a
  barracuda's been sitting in the sun all day." No barracuda spoilage is
  described on camera.
  Get them on ice at
  the rail; general method in
  [fish care](../fish-care/README.md).
- **The landed fish is the hook hazard.** A barracuda on the deck thrashes,
  and any hook not buried in its snout — an assist, the free points of a
  treble — ends up in someone's boots (`xgPEt4Zj35Q`).
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the source assigns the roles the
  other way round — the **treble** is the hook buried in the barracuda's snout
  and the **assist** hook is what "get[s] in everybody's boots". Free treble
  points in boots is not in the source.
- ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: bleeding, chilling or ikejime parameters
  for barracuda, and how to handle the teeth when unhooking.
