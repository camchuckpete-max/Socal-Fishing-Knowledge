# Spotted Bay Bass — bottom jig crawl

**One species, one technique.** A craw or creature bait on a jighead, cast and
allowed to sink all the way to the bottom before anything else happens, then
either slow-ground along the bottom or swum straight back, with the trailer
swapped to suit conditions (`lP6cg4eEU6s`). Reach for it in the colder months
when the fish are hard on the bottom and the fast summer crankbait program is
the wrong speed (`46ha7J1Fc6A`).
The general leadhead retrieve, its bottom-contact tell and its
hooksets are in
[leadhead swimbait retrieve](../techniques/leadhead-swimbait-retrieve.md); this
page is what changes because the bait is a crustacean imitation being crawled
for a spotty.

## When this is the call

**Colder months, fish sitting on the bottom.** Spotties hold close to the
bottom, and the colder the water the more that is where the bait has to be
(`lP6cg4eEU6s`) — the row this page serves is
[spotted bay bass — situations → techniques](spotted-bay-bass.md#situations--techniques).
The seasonal split against the fast program is the router's: winter slow and
near the bottom, summer fast as a reaction bite on a swept
[crankbait](../techniques/inshore-crankbaits.md) (`46ha7J1Fc6A`) — see
[spotted bay bass — feeding triggers](spotted-bay-bass.md#feeding-triggers).

**It works from shore as well as from a boat**, and the shore case is where its
main failure mode bites hardest (below).

**Tide and light still pick the water.** Where on the flat or the drop-off to
put the crawl is the router's read — up shallow on a rising or high tide, off
onto the adjacent drop-offs on a falling one, and toward shade on a bright high
tide; both stay in
[spotted bay bass — feeding triggers](spotted-bay-bass.md#feeding-triggers).

## How spotted bay bass changes the execution

**Sink it fully, then pick grind or straight swim.** Cast, let the bait tap out
and sink to the bottom, and only then start the retrieve — either a straight
grind along the bottom or a straight swim, with the trailer on the head
deciding which (`lP6cg4eEU6s`).

**Read the bottom composition off the line and rod tip, and give it several
casts.** With time on a spot you can feel through the line whether the bait is
dragging through soft, mucky bottom, through grass, or coming up over hard
structure — it is a read built across several casts, not one (`lP6cg4eEU6s`).

**Stop periodically to feel back down.** The bait does not stay in contact by
itself: it rides up off the bottom without the angler noticing, especially
fishing from shore, so a deliberate pause to check how far off the bottom the
bait has come is part of the retrieve rather than an interruption to it
(`lP6cg4eEU6s`).

**Vary the rate of fall on purpose.** Rate of fall is the stated variable for
why a fish eats one presentation and not another with a craw or crustacean
bait, and the finesse end of the ladder goes as far as fishing it weightless.
The thing being imitated is a crab or crustacean jetting down to the bottom and
popping back up (`lP6cg4eEU6s`).

## Differs from the general method

Against
[leadhead swimbait retrieve](../techniques/leadhead-swimbait-retrieve.md),
which owns the wind-three-turns / pause / watch-the-line-fall-slack rhythm and
its two hooksets:

- **Bottom contact is read by feel, not off the line going slack.** The general
  note's tell is visual — the line falling slack after a fixed three-turn
  count. Here the angler learns the bottom through the line and rod tip while
  the bait is moving, and the composition (mucky, grass, hard structure) is
  itself the information (`lP6cg4eEU6s`).
- **The bottom check is an inserted pause, not the rhythm.** The general
  retrieve re-finds the bottom every three turns by construction; this one is a
  continuous grind or straight swim in which contact is silently lost, so the
  periodic stop-and-feel is a correction the angler has to remember to make —
  called out specifically for shore fishing (`lP6cg4eEU6s`).
- **Fall rate is a tuned variable rather than a fixed property of the head.**
  The general note treats the leadhead's fast fall as a constraint to work
  around — it is why the class cannot hold a mid-column swim from the beach.
  Here the fall is the trigger, deliberately varied down to weightless to
  imitate a crab jetting to the bottom and popping back up (`lP6cg4eEU6s`).
- **The profile imitated is a crustacean, not a baitfish.** The general note's
  retrieves — glide, crack-slack, smooth swim — are built around squid and
  baitfish behaviour; this program is built around what a crab does off a
  piling (`lP6cg4eEU6s`).
