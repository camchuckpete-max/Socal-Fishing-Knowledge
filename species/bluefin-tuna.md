# Bluefin Tuna

Pacific bluefin run **sub-30 lb schoolies to 300 lb+ cows in the same season**
off SoCal and northern Baja, and grade is unpredictable trip to trip — which is
why bluefin tackle is the one tuna program that never gets scaled down on a
mixed-species day (`PrdPJy26H8c`). They present three ways that share almost no
gear: **sounded deep schools** with no surface sign (a meter-and-jig game),
**surface foamers** (run-and-gun cast-and-wind, and the pickiest of the three),
and **paddy fish** mixed with yellowtail and dorado. This note routes those
situations; the full trolling decision table lives in
[bluefin trolling](bluefin-tuna-trolling.md).

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): `PrdPJy26H8c` says only that "you never
know what size bluefin you're going to run into" — it carries no weight range,
no season framing and no geography; the sub-30 lb-to-300 lb+ span is carried by
other corpus sources (`shZCjX2-fkI`, `5to3Q5P7w90`), not by this cite.

Fish hold on offshore banks and island fathom curves across the
[Bight](../locations/bight-geography.md), aggregating where uphill current
compresses along [island structure](../locations/island-structure.md) and the
named banks in the [zone lexicon](../locations/zone-lexicon.md).

**Presence is not catchability, in both directions.** Bluefin are in the region
outside the slide above — schools were sighted near San Martin Island in
January 2021 in **63–65 °F** water (`shZCjX2-fkI`), and Tanner Bank produced an
anchored bluefin bite in mid-August 2024, months before the fall condensation
window (`ntQXxcH5sjI`). The reverse also holds: a bank **42–43 mi** off
Ensenada held confirmed breezing and kite-raised fish across an August 2024
tournament while ~40 boats landed **4 fish on day one and 7 on day two**
(`Ix0gG0-l3v0`) — a Baja regional pattern, not part of the SoCal slide.

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): `shZCjX2-fkI` gives the 63–65 °F reading
as a separate second-hand aside — "I saw some information the other day that the
water was 63 to 65 degrees where they were catching the fish" — eleven minutes
away from the San Martin sighting and never tied to it; the sighting itself is
hedged ("probably 10 days ago or something like that") and the schools "didn't
bite all that well."

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the anchored mid-August 2024 bite in
`ntQXxcH5sjI` is a yellowtail bite — "the boat did not stop getting bit" on
"slug yellowtail" on day one, with bluefin appearing only on day two as "a few
fish yellowtail and blue fin landed here and there."

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): `Ix0gG0-l3v0` never names the bank and
hedges the run ("I think about 42 43 miles"); the word breezing is never used,
the only kite result is one swirl under the bait, and the counts are hedged
("I'm guessing… like 40 boats", three boats taking the four day-one fish).

**Temperature is soft evidence, not a gate.** Bluefin were seen foaming in
**76 °F**, the warmest water observed since they arrived (`5to3Q5P7w90`) — so
warm SST argues against them without ruling them out. The **break rule** is the
operative read: they sit the **cool side of a break when the water is warm, the
warm side when it's cold** (`OYOda6T3f-8`). An absolute band also stands in the
corpus — fish moving north with cooling water and holding in **70–72 °F**
specifically, stated against the **81 °F** water fished the day before and
**80 °F** in the bay at the dock that morning (`HueC1KHrcVw`) — see
[Doctrine & conflicts](#doctrine--conflicts) for which to weight. Full layer:
[water temperature](../conditions/water-temperature.md).

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-internal): front-matter `sst_band_f: 63-76` builds a
band out of these two outliers, while [water temperature](../conditions/water-temperature.md)
and [September](../seasonal/september.md) both state the 76 °F foaming reading
softens the ceiling **without moving the productive band**, and the 63–65 °F
figure is a January presence reading rather than a band floor (`5to3Q5P7w90`,
`shZCjX2-fkI`).

**Water color: bait beats clarity.** Bluefin swim into the dirtiest, coldest
water available to be on bait, and the **chalky transition water at a color
break** is the water to work because the dirty side holds the bait
(`HWx1jDTGsng`). See [water color](../conditions/water-color.md) and
[current structure](../conditions/current-structure.md).

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): `HWx1jDTGsng` states the dirty/cold case
as a some-days alternative — "they'll go into the dirtiest coldest water some
days… and then other days you find them right where you expect nice blue water"
— and keeps "typically this bluefin is gonna like the cooler side of the water"
as its baseline.

**Advection prior:** open-water bluefin not holding structure move **10–15 nm
overnight**; intraday they move only low single-digit mph. That sets how stale
a report can be before the zone has to be re-searched
([report reading](../planning/report-reading-and-forecasting.md)).

⚠ Fact-check (single-source): the reading that SoCal/northern-Baja bluefin are
a **resident stock** ranging Point Conception to Cedros Island and out to
200–300 mi offshore rests on one seminar's reasoning (`fgTmUq78ofQ`); the
reasoning and its 8–12 lb-juvenile and 2019-sighting basis are in the
[evidence file](evidence/bluefin-tuna.md).

Zone-by-zone trip detail lives in the [zone guides](#zone-guides) below and, until
those exist, in the [evidence file](evidence/bluefin-tuna.md).

## Presence & forage

**Documented natural forage:** saury, anchovy, and unnamed "microbait"
(`mDmbGdQAy-4`); **red crab**, the forage the standard flat-fall colour is chosen
to imitate (`RbqOKkINSCM`) and what the red sparkle on a wing stickbait reads as
from above (`ftEvyfwjZFU`); **flying fish**, the bait the whole kite
program is built on (`vCskOx6N-XM`, `ouBrIdO7d4k`); and **bait squid staged on a
bank**, named as the reason a bite held on one high spot (`Rb5I2ljAqeE`).
Sardine and mackerel are the hook and chum baits, not the documented forage.

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): `RbqOKkINSCM` gives red-crab colour as
one captain's contrarian personal pick against the prevailing glow choice — "a
lot of lures are colored for fishermen… but i like to use natural colors that's
me", and he would switch to glow if glow were getting bit — not as the standard
flat-fall colour.

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the squid-on-the-bank line in
`Rb5I2ljAqeE` says "the reason these fish are here right now is cuz they're
squid up on the bank" with rockfish and yellowtail as the immediate antecedent;
bluefin is inferred from trip context, not stated at that moment.

**Selectivity is the operative fact, and it is what makes foamers hard.** Fish
gorging microbait are "not looking for anything else except what they are
eating," and the bait is often too small to hook (`qLKLlZdiflA`) — foamers
breaking all around a boat produced nothing on a trolled
[spreader bar](../lures/spreader-bar.md) or cast plugs, and only a dead flying
fish under the [kite](../techniques/kite-fishing.md) converted (`mDmbGdQAy-4`).
Fish visibly feeding are not fish that will eat what you have.

**Profile follows the forage by season.** Early-season fish are not yet on
microbait, so **80–100 g** castable jigs match; June through August the same
program slims to **35–60 g** to fit the profile they are eating
(`qLKLlZdiflA`). Microbait feeding is not a daylight-only state — one Cortez-era
trip had fish "gorging on microbait day and night," with a small-profile tungsten
jig the only thing reaching them at weight (`CsKEtrcLgiQ`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): `CsKEtrcLgiQ` names no location — the
trip is aboard the Independence — so "Cortez-era" is not in the source, and the
stated reason is the jig's profile match ("it was just what they were gorging
on"), with the speaker adding "it's definitely not something that you need on
every trip."

**Find the bait before the fish.** The one dependable rule is that bluefin break
every rule, so the search anchors on bait rather than on a temperature or a spot
(`HWx1jDTGsng`). **Bait shape reads feeding state:** broken, harried, moving bait
holds predators under it; a solid unbroken wall usually does not.

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): `HWx1jDTGsng` rejects water COLOUR as the
thing to anchor on ("clean water dirty water whatever"), not temperature — the
same interview calls temperature breaks "always good" and starts every plan from
water temperature and clarity.

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: any stomach-content or diet work for SoCal
bluefin, or the seasonal order in which these forage items dominate — the list
above is what crews named on the water.

## Feeding triggers

- **Dark hours, on the [deep scattering layer](../conditions/deep-scattering-layer.md).**
  Fish rise with the layer after dark and read as depth-called marks — "fish at
  180, fish at 230 ft" — that a jig drops straight down onto (`nRFFM8DT-og`).
  Two mechanisms are on record and they are not the same claim:
  **food-following** (fish feeding on the risen layer), and a **reaction bite**
  triggered by the jig itself (*likely, not measured*) (cameron).
  ⚠ Fact-check (unverifiable): no corpus source ties these night marks to the
  deep scattering layer — `nRFFM8DT-og` describes captain-called depths only
  ("I see fish at 180 I see fish at 230 ft") and never mentions the layer, and
  [deep scattering layer](../conditions/deep-scattering-layer.md) carries no
  night bluefin depths of its own.
- **Clock windows within the night.** One captain's read of the mid-August 2024
  Cortez/Tanner night bite ran two windows — roughly **9:00 pm to
  11:00 pm/midnight**, a lull, then **3:30 am to sunrise** (`LsFMBCa9DOQ`) —
  time-of-night within one cycle, not a moon pattern, and one season's read.
- **Moon.** The **days leading into the full moon** are the strongest documented
  bluefin windows; late-November day windows tie to the **new moon**. On the
  moon, open-water fish travel at night and relocate, so absence of reports is
  coverage bias rather than absence of fish ([moon](../conditions/moon.md)).
  A directly-contradicting read is on file — no reliable moon pattern trip to
  trip (`shZCjX2-fkI`); the decision frame is in
  [Doctrine & conflicts](#doctrine--conflicts).
- **[Slack tide](../conditions/tide-and-slack.md)** is the window the foamer
  program is timed around (cameron).
- **The bite ends faster than it builds.** A bite can shut off "like a light
  switch" with the school unrelocatable afterwards (`zBd1mayUt_I`), and the same
  unpredictability is described from the anchor — a pick that hangs two, three,
  four fish all day, then nothing (`3T4c3Zez_DM`). Plan for a window measured in
  minutes, not for a day-long bite. ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: mechanism
  — what turns a breaking school off. ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source):
  the `3T4c3Zez_DM` pick is described on a morning San Clemente Island bite with
  no anchor job stated, and is hedged both ways — "hanging all day long or at
  least for a while" and "sometimes they shut off."

**Visual sign.** Foamers (surface whitewater), breezers, jumpers, and spots — and
the foamer's shape grades the opportunity: a solid circular patch of whitewater
(a "jacuzzi") is close to a bite every cast, while spread-out directional feeders
are picky, so wait for the circular form. Terns are the key bird, and a **moving
dive-line means bait being pushed by fish underneath**
([bird reading](../conditions/bird-reading.md),
[search & glassing](../planning/search-and-glassing.md)). On radar, run gain near
max at a **4–5 mile** range; bird schools paint as red blotches, sometimes
boat-sized (`HWx1jDTGsng`). Marine mammals carry sign too — a dolphin school and
a blue whale travelling together were both called as holding tuna underneath
(`skRo1z41Dnc`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): `skRo1z41Dnc` calls "a school of porpus
and a blue wh[ale]" as two sightings in the same area, not one school and whale
travelling together, and the fish under them are unspecified "tuna."

**Trolling in one line:** kite when the wind gives you 8–15 kt and the fish are
big; **speed-troll a Mad Mac at 13–15 kt** to cover water and locate when the
wind is **too light to fly the kite, or the fish are too spread out to sit and
kite over** — a coverage need, not a sea state (cameron); spreader bars and
skirts on calmer school-grade days; divers and cedar plugs for mixed grade. The
full table by wind, grade, and water is in
[bluefin trolling](bluefin-tuna-trolling.md).

## Doctrine & conflicts

Section order below is **not** a ranking; each entry states what the choice
turns on.

- **Temperature — relative beats absolute.** The decision turns on whether the
  water gives you a break to read. With a break, use the side rule: cool side
  when the water is warm, warm side when it's cold (`OYOda6T3f-8`). Without one,
  absolute numbers are the fallback and they do not agree — a **70–72 °F**
  preferred band stated against 81 °F water fished the day before
  (`HueC1KHrcVw`),
  fish foaming in **76 °F** (`5to3Q5P7w90`), and **63–65 °F** January presence
  (`shZCjX2-fkI`). Different moments and different sources rather than one
  temperature curve; treat any single number as soft evidence.
- **Moon — a travel prior, not a bite prior.** The choice is what you use the
  phase for. As a *location* input it holds: on the moon, open-water fish travel
  at night and relocate, so plan a re-search rather than a return to yesterday's
  numbers ([moon](../conditions/moon.md)). As a *bite* input it is disputed —
  the days leading into the full moon are the strongest documented windows,
  against a direct on-camera statement that the effect is inconsistent trip to
  trip with no pattern worked out (`shZCjX2-fkI`). The night clock windows under
  [Feeding triggers](#feeding-triggers) are the more actionable timing input.
- **Hook style for big bluefin — circle, with one dissent.** The decision turns
  on grade and leader exposure: a big bluefin's teeth saw through the leader, so
  a circle's corner-of-the-mouth hookset keeps the leader out of the teeth
  (`RbqOKkINSCM`) — the mechanism has physical corroboration, a landed fish
  carrying a hook from an earlier bitten-off leader (`c3NFkQbdDy0`).
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the `c3NFkQbdDy0` caption is ambiguous
  — "the tuna must have ripped its own lip because he still had his hook"
  offers a torn lip as the alternative explanation in the same breath, so the
  corroboration is not as clean as stated. One crew
  states a personal J-over-circle preference for bluefin (`LsFMBCa9DOQ`).
  Sizing and wire-gauge detail in [hooks](../tackle/hooks.md); the mackerel
  flyline rig — San Diego jam knot, no weight or swivel, ~3–4 ft of 80 lb
  straight fluorocarbon — is in [flyline](../techniques/flyline.md).
- **Calendar vs water state.** The **year-anniversary prior** — go back to this
  date last year and the year before and you'll be close, strongest in fall —
  sits against the **water-first stance**: the calendar date doesn't matter,
  they follow water temperature and bait (cameron). The choice turns on what you
  have: the anniversary prior is a route-planning default when conditions data
  is thin, water state overrides it when you have current SST, chlorophyll, and
  bait reports ([year-anniversary prior](../seasonal/year-anniversary-prior.md)).
- **Rig before the bite, not during it** (`RbqOKkINSCM`): one rod pre-rigged with
  a flat-fall ([knife jigs](../lures/knife-jigs.md)) racked for when fish are
  called down deep, one with a popper
  ([poppers & stickbaits](../lures/tuna-poppers-and-stickbaits.md)) for foamers,
  and hooks ready to swap to live-bait rigging when the fleet turns to bait.
  Related standing practice: when a hook pulls, reel in and check the terminal
  end rather than assume the spot went dead — in a thick school another fish can
  swipe and cut the leader, or the bait can pick up unnoticed kelp
  (`elBPRrdkugU`).
- **Find the bait, and expect the rules to break** (`HWx1jDTGsng`): dirty and
  chalky transition water holds bait; in wind with no surface sign fish sound to
  30–50 fathoms and you need power on the meter to reach them; bird radar at
  4–5 mi.
- **Foamer program** (cameron): run-and-gun at working terns, **~2–3 casts per
  school**, with the smaller presentation (Colt-Sniper class) out-fishing the
  bigger one, timed around [slack tide](../conditions/tide-and-slack.md).
  Approach mechanics and common failures are in
  [foamer casting](../techniques/foamer-casting.md).
- **Bluefin gear does not get scaled down on a mixed tuna trip**, unlike
  [skipjack and schoolie yellowfin](skipjack-tuna.md) — grade is unpredictable
  trip to trip, so tackle stays sized for the biggest fish that could show
  (`PrdPJy26H8c`).

## Landing & handling

- **The boat lands the fish, not the angler.** On a 100–300 lb fish, gear-tap the
  boat toward it, walk down-swell for the whole fight, and avoid the death
  circle — full mechanics in
  [fighting a big bluefin](../techniques/fighting-big-bluefin.md), then the gaff
  shot in [gaffing](../fish-care/gaffing.md).
- **A hooked bluefin hunts for abrasion.** It will cut itself off on any edge it
  can find during the fight — another angler's line, the anchor rode, or the
  bottom of the boat — not only its own leader (`c3NFkQbdDy0`). That is the
  argument for the 200 lb bite leader class and for clearing lines on a hookup.
- **Long fights get handed off.** Rods are passed to a mate or another passenger
  when an angler can't keep fighting — standard practice on tough trips
  (`947solNfiPw`) and on a nearly two-hour fight with a 184 lb fish
  (`IxhdiX3oEEs`).
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): `947solNfiPw` frames the handoff the
  other way — anglers hand off because "they just want to catch the fish to have
  the fish", and the captain discourages it ("you paid the money to come enjoy
  the experience so enjoy it"); the "can't do it anymore" line is the crew's own
  fatigue, not the angler's.
- **Care after the gaff:** bleeding, chilling, and handling method is in
  [tuna care](../fish-care/tuna-care.md) and [ikejime](../fish-care/ikejime.md).
