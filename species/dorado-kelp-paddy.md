# Dorado — kelp-paddy flyline

**One species, one technique.** A flylined live sardine pitched at a kelp
paddy is the highest-percentage way to catch a SoCal dorado, and the program
has its own parameters: how the boat comes in, how long the paddy gets before
you leave it, the hook style and size, the leader ladder you walk down when the
fish circle without eating, and the leader maintenance a run of dorado forces.
The general presentation — no weight, cast away from the boat, keep a lively
bait on — is in [flyline](../techniques/flyline.md); this page is what changes
when the fish under the paddy is a dorado.

## When this is the call

**A paddy is found and there are fish on it.** Flylined bait ranks first on the
paddy against iron, poppers and skip jigging, because dorado jump and shake
their heads and a jig hookup often pulls free on the way to the boat where live
bait holds (`P9H-bpzT7eU`); the full ranking, and the alternatives when you
have no bait to spend, are in
[dorado — situations → techniques](dorado.md#situations--techniques).

**The water and the season are the router's job, not this page's.** The
green/blue boundary, the temperature reference band and the July-onward window
are in [dorado — where & when](dorado.md#where--when); the paddy itself as
structure is in [kelp paddies](../conditions/kelp-paddies.md).

**Reach for the step-down half of the program when fish are visible and will
not commit** — circling the boat, refusing the first bait offered. That is the
scenario the leader ladder below exists for (`E4vKwRaRueA`).

## How dorado changes the execution

**Drive right up to the edge — do not sneak up on it.** The stated approach is
to run the boat to the paddy "like it's the dock," drive right up to the edge,
and start fishing, against the common practice of shutting everything down
up-wind and drifting down onto it (`ZFqe49jRgA0`). The evidence offered is the
sportboat fleet: a 65–95 ft steel boat arrives with doors slamming, the PA
running and the deck loud, drives to the kelp, drops in, catches fish and
leaves (`ZFqe49jRgA0`). A drift-down buys nothing on this fishery.

**Qualify in one to two minutes, then leave.** Look in the water for dorado or
yellowtail; failing that, look for the boomerang arcs at 90–120 ft on the
sounder (the sonar read itself is in
[dorado — finding them](dorado.md#finding-them-sign--sonar)). With nothing
visible and nothing on the meter, the paddy gets **about a minute, maybe two**
before you move to the next one (`ZFqe49jRgA0`). A jig cast is the faster
version of the same test: throw a Mad Scad from the tower and watch for a chase
— it is usually a dorado, and no chase means move on (`VgpgJ8IAyJo`). The
reason the clock is this short rather than a patient soak is that paddies
restock at night, so a paddy that is not biting when you find it does not turn
on while you wait (`ZFqe49jRgA0`).

**J-hook, not a circle, because of how the fish eats.** Most SoCal gamefish —
the tunas, yellowtail — come up, grab the bait and immediately turn sideways,
which is what sets a circle hook. Dorado eat a bait and then charge the boat
looking for more, feeding straight ahead and picking off as many baits as they
can, so there is nothing for the circle to set against; the stated preference
is a J-hook for dorado even from an angler who fishes circles for everything
else (`E4vKwRaRueA`). A second, independent reason is a comparatively small
mouth, which a J-hook suits on a flylined sardine (`P9H-bpzT7eU`). Circle hooks
have nonetheless connected on dorado in the corpus, including inside the
offshore paddy program — the counter-examples are kept beside this doctrine in
[dorado — doctrine & conflicts](dorado.md#doctrine--conflicts).

**Count three to five seconds before you put the reel in gear.** On a flylined
sardine, take the bite, give it a good **3–5 second count**, then go into gear
(`P9H-bpzT7eU`). The hook-set that follows is the general one — reel in gear,
rod pointed at the fish, wind tight, then lift, never swing — and it is stated
in [dorado — landing & handling](dorado.md#landing--handling).

**When they will not eat, step the leader and the hook down together.** Dorado
are voracious and can be as picky as any fish; the fix is not a different bait
but a smaller everything. The ladder and the hook band are in Rigs & gear
below. The stated mechanism for the hook half is that the fish is not seeing
the line so much as something out of the ordinary: a sardine dragging an
oversized hook does not swim right, and a bait that does not swim right does
not get bit (`E4vKwRaRueA`).

**Cut the leader back between fish.** Dorado teeth and gill plates chafe the
leader over repeated fights and passes near the boat, and on a paddy that is
biting the same leader section goes through fish after fish. The stated
practice is to cut off **a foot or two**, check for chafe, and re-tie every
time rather than fishing one section through a run of fish (`VWClGAn2WEw`).

## Rigs & gear

- **Rod and reel — [40–60 lb all-day class](../tackle/gear-classes.md)**, star
  or lever drag, the mixed-species setup that covers dorado alongside small
  tuna and yellowtail (`E4vKwRaRueA`); the lighter dorado-dedicated 25–40 lb
  build is in [dorado — gear summary](dorado.md#gear-summary-class-terms).
- **Top shot:** **5–6 ft of fluorocarbon** off the braid, which is where the
  leader ladder is walked (`E4vKwRaRueA`).
- **Leader ladder — start at 30 lb, 40 lb if bigger fish are around, then
  40 → 30 → 20 → 15 lb** as fish refuse (`E4vKwRaRueA`). **~20 lb is the
  working floor**: below it you get bit off a lot, so 15 lb is a last resort
  for a bite that will not come any other way, and if they will eat the heavier
  line you fish the heavier line (`E4vKwRaRueA`).
- **Hooks — matched to the bait, not to the fish.** A **1/0 live-bait J-hook**
  is the all-around size on a typical **~6 in** Southern California bait-receiver
  sardine; step up to **2/0 or 3/0** on big donkey sardines, and step down when
  the bite is finicky (`E4vKwRaRueA`). Style and sizing generally:
  [hooks](../tackle/hooks.md).
- **Jig for qualifying rather than fishing:** a pink-accented Mad Scad-class
  troll/cast lure thrown from the tower (`VgpgJ8IAyJo`) — the brand claim is a
  sponsored one and caps at low confidence; lure detail in
  [tuna poppers & stickbaits](../lures/tuna-poppers-and-stickbaits.md).
- ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: how much fluorocarbon a day of between-fish
  cut-backs actually consumes on a paddy that stays lit, i.e. how much spare
  leader to carry.

## Differs from the general method

Against [flyline](../techniques/flyline.md), which owns the no-weight
presentation, hook position, bait selection and the distance-beats-weight
doctrine:

- **The leader ladder has a floor, and the floor is set by teeth.** The general
  note's finicky-bite downsizing argues from the bait — line too thick and the
  bait cannot swim it convincingly — and caps fly line at 20–25 lb, no more
  than 30, with a short heavy fluoro bite guard as the answer to break-offs.
  The dorado ladder runs the other direction from 40 lb down and stops at
  ~20 lb because below that you get bit off (`E4vKwRaRueA`): a bait-swimming
  ceiling on one side, a bite-off floor on the other.
- **Hook style inverts.** Several builds in the general note run circle hooks
  as the default flyline hook. Dorado is a stated exception, and the reason is
  behavioural — the fish feeds straight ahead into the boat rather than turning
  sideways (`E4vKwRaRueA`).
- **There is a drop-back count.** The general note carries no bite-to-gear
  count; the dorado paddy program states **3–5 seconds** on a flylined sardine
  (`P9H-bpzT7eU`).
- **Leader maintenance is scheduled, not reactive.** The general note re-ties
  after a fish because a light leader picks up nicks in a fight; here the cause
  is dorado teeth and gill plates plus repeated passes at the boat, and the
  cut-back is between every fish on a working paddy (`VWClGAn2WEw`).
- **The approach and the clock are not flyline mechanics at all.** Driving to
  the edge rather than drifting down, and the one-to-two-minute qualify-or-move
  rule (`ZFqe49jRgA0`), belong to fishing a paddy; the general note's own
  advice — get the bait out and away from the boat, keep a lively bait on —
  still applies once the boat is parked.
- **Hook-to-bait matching is NOT a delta.** "Match the hook to the bait, not to
  the fish" is already the general rule in
  [flyline — bait and hook selection](../techniques/flyline.md#bait-and-hook-selection);
  the 1/0 / 2/0–3/0 numbers and the bait-cannot-swim mechanism are stated here
  because this is the source they come from, not because dorado changes the
  rule.
