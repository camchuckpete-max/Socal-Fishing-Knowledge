# Snook (Robalo)

**Single-location, single-source note: Lopez Mateos / Magdalena Bay, Baja
California Sur.** Everything here comes from Capt. Dave Hansen (Your
Saltwater Guide, `dave-hansen` registered) and Larry Hansen (PSA), 2024 Bart
Hall Show seminar, `EcQK5elTPJE`, 2024-01-27 — `medium` confidence (single
mention of these specific claims; Larry Hansen's voice is unregistered and
caps at medium regardless). See
[Lopez Mateos & Magdalena Bay](../locations/bahia-magdalena-lopez-mateos.md)
for the full location profile this species note routes off of.

## Where & when

- Only documented fishery in this corpus: **Lopez Mateos / Magdalena Bay**,
  Baja California Sur — see the
  [location note](../locations/bahia-magdalena-lopez-mateos.md).
- Two distinct zones inside that fishery: a **skinny mangrove interior**
  (down to ~2 ft of water, tight to the roots) and a **nearshore structure
  zone at the boca** (40–50 ft, mixed in with grouper).
- Season: described as biting year-round with no snook-specific month-by-
  month pattern given. The mid-September–to-end-of-December "best variety
  and volume" window logged on the location note is a whole-fishery
  preference (Larry Hansen's), not snook-specific.

## Finding them (sign & sonar)

**⚠ Flagged stub — no sonar signature in the corpus yet.** Neither zone is
described with a meter read in this source: the mangrove program is
precision-cast to *visible* structure (the roots), and the boca program is
bottom structure fished blind over a known mark. Flagged so the gap reads
as "not in the source yet," not "snook can't be found on the meter."
General method: [electronics & sounder](../planning/electronics-and-sounder.md).

- **Mangroves:** fish sit tight to the roots, which are covered in
  oysters. Precision matters — landing the bait even slightly off the root
  still draws a bite, but on unwanted/incidental fish rather than the
  target snook (per Hansen, an off-target cast gets "some kind of weird
  fish," not what he's after).
- **Boca:** no visual or sonar sign given — fished as a known bottom mark
  in 40–50 ft, blind.

## Situations → techniques

| Situation (conditions) | Do this (ranked) | Gear class | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Skinny mangrove water (~2 ft), casting tight to oyster-covered roots | [Mangrove structure livebait](../techniques/mangrove-structure-livebait.md) — precision-cast live bait onto the roots, drag locked near max | Baja Pacific mangrove-snook class — see [gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md) | An inch of slack line among the oyster-covered roots cuts a hooked fish off — leader and drag are both built around giving zero |
| Boca / nearshore structure, 40–50 ft, mixed with grouper | [Sliding sinker — Baja Pacific grouper & snook leader](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md) — Carolina rig, two-speed reel mandatory | Baja Pacific grouper/snook bottom class — see [gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md) | Fight is decided in roughly the first 5 seconds; a hooked snook typically rises rather than diving for the rocks the way grouper do, which is how the crew tells the two apart on the strike |

## Gear summary (class terms)

- **Baja Pacific mangrove-snook class** and **Baja Pacific grouper/snook
  bottom class** — see [gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md) for the
  full spec of each.
- Live bait named in this source: sardine and live shrimp for the boca
  program (bait unspecified for the mangroves). Live shrimp is not sold
  dockside at Lopez Mateos — it has to be arranged with the guide the day
  before so it can be caught overnight; there's no walk-up bait receiver
  for it.

## Doctrine & conflicts

- **Break-off progression, recorded as an attributed anecdote behind the
  current parameter (Dave Hansen):** on his first trips targeting this
  fishery, Hansen lost his first six hooked snook to break-offs, stepping
  leader test up through **60, 80, 100, and finally 130 lb** before landing
  fish. This is the origin story behind, not a separate rule from, the
  100–130 lb leader now specified for the boca program (see
  [sliding sinker](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md)).
- **Species ID caveat:** this source never names the species behind the
  ~100 lb "grouper" caught alongside snook at the boca. It is **not**
  assumed to be the same fish as [cabrilla](cabrilla.md) (Sea of Cortez
  leopard grouper — a different region, a different technique program, and
  a much smaller fish) — kept unidentified here rather than guessed, per
  the KB's naming rule.
