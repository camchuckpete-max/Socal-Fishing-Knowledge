# Snook (Robalo)

The corpus documents snook in exactly one fishery —
[Magdalena Bay / Lopez Mateos](../locations/bahia-magdalena-lopez-mateos.md),
Baja California Sur — and it is two programs, not one. Inside, snook sit tight
against oyster-crusted mangrove roots in water down to about **2 ft** and are
taken on a no-weight [flyline](../techniques/flyline.md) bait placed **on** the
root, not near it (`EcQK5elTPJE`, `dlxA22FVNGc`). Outside at the **boca** — the
bay mouth, about five minutes from the dock in **40–50 ft** — the same fish runs
to at least **42 lb** and is fished blind on a
[sliding sinker](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md) rig alongside grouper to
**100 lb** (`EcQK5elTPJE`). Both programs are built around break-offs: the drag
is locked and the leader heavy, because inside the roots a snook given an inch
of line cuts off on the oysters, and at the boca the leader had to be walked up
to **130 lb** before fish were landed (`EcQK5elTPJE`).

## Where & when

Lopez Mateos / Magdalena Bay is the only snook fishery in the corpus. Three
depth zones hold them: the **mangrove interior** (general depth **12–15 ft**,
with the root program worked down to about **2 ft** and casts landing in
**12–18 in** of water), the **estuary channel** at **25–30 ft**, and the **boca**
at **40–50 ft** (`EcQK5elTPJE`, `dlxA22FVNGc`, `isXJONlpUP4`). Zone detail and
the trip-level story live on the
[location note](../locations/bahia-magdalena-lopez-mateos.md).

Snook bite year-round here and no month-by-month pattern is recorded for them;
the bay as a whole is stated to have no time that it does not bite, with the
inside fishing carrying the winter (`EcQK5elTPJE`).
⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: a seasonal presence, spawning-season or
temperature pattern for snook anywhere on the Baja Pacific coast.

**Presence is not the bite, and the gate is tide, not calendar.** Snook hold in
specific slots inside a cove rather than spreading along it — bigger snook were
seen holding in one productive slot alongside pargo and grouper (`frX09YMQxKE`)
— but with no tidal flow the bite does not happen at all
(`dlxA22FVNGc`). See [Feeding triggers](#feeding-triggers).

Grade splits by zone. The boca produces the big fish: **42 lb** was the best of
one day, and snook **over 20 lb** are named as the reason to fish it
(`EcQK5elTPJE`). The mangrove root program produces small fish — a **5 lb**
snook is called perfect eating size, and "little" and "baby" fish come off the
roots repeatedly (`FxgLol_IHa0`, `dlxA22FVNGc`).

## Presence & forage

- **They hold inside the branches and will not leave them.** Snook sit right on
  the edge of the mangrove roots and do not come out to chase a bait; a cast
  landing short or wide of the branch line does not get bit by a snook
  (`-5kooyIyavs`, `dlxA22FVNGc`). Off-target casts still draw bites, but on
  incidental species rather than snook (`EcQK5elTPJE`).
- **The ambush point is the outflow of a cove, in the dirty water.** As the ebb
  builds, the water leaving the roots turns brown and carries leaves and debris;
  the fish sit in that stained outflow and pick baits out of the drift
  (`dlxA22FVNGc`). The productive geometry named is the narrow slot where dead
  mangroves meet live mangroves, and the pocket at the head of a small cove
  (`dlxA22FVNGc`, `-5kooyIyavs`).
- **Chum puts them on the surface within seconds.** Pieces of sardine thrown at
  the root edge produce visible boils and jumping fish before the first cast,
  and the stated mechanism is that food washing past is what switches them on to
  feed (`dlxA22FVNGc`).
- **The mangrove system's standing forage list** is sardines, mullet, clams,
  oysters and scallops (`EcQK5elTPJE`).
  ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: stomach contents, or any forage species
  named as what snook specifically eat — the list above is what lives in the
  mangroves, not a diet.
- **Hook bait is live sardine inside and live shrimp or sardine at the boca**
  (`EcQK5elTPJE`, `FxgLol_IHa0`). Monterey sardines are throw-netted en route to
  the spot rather than bought (`FxgLol_IHa0`, `-5kooyIyavs`).
- **The same coves hold a wide mixed bag**, which is what an off-target cast
  produces: broomtail grouper, pargo, corvina, triggerfish, small halibut,
  [spotted bay bass](spotted-bay-bass.md) and
  [jack crevalle](pacific-crevalle-jack.md) (`-5kooyIyavs`, `dlxA22FVNGc`,
  `vyX5FGoDH0A`). At the boca the bycatch is grouper to **100 lb**
  (`EcQK5elTPJE`).

## Feeding triggers

- **Tidal movement is the gate, and its direction ranks the windows.** The best
  window is the **ebb running from peak high toward low**; near-peak high is the
  secondary window; the **flood from low toward high** is the poor one, because
  there is not yet enough water over the root zone for fish to stage and feed
  there. Slack — no flow at all — does not produce (`dlxA22FVNGc`). The stated
  mechanism is that the ebb flushes baitfish out of the roots and past the
  ambush points, so the fish get fed on a schedule the tide sets. See
  [tide & slack](../conditions/tide-and-slack.md).
- **Time arrival to tide direction, not to dawn.** "It's not about getting there
  super early, it's not about being the first one, it's about being there when
  the tide is flowing the right direction" (`dlxA22FVNGc`). Direction is readable
  off the surface as leaves and debris drifting past the boat (`dlxA22FVNGc`).
- **A hard ebb shortens the strike window to about a second.** In the channel on
  a fast outgoing tide a well-placed bait is swept out of the zone almost
  immediately, so the program is cast–wind–recast rather than soak
  (`-5kooyIyavs`).
- **Chum is the on-switch, not an attractant that pulls fish in.** The fish are
  already in the cove; the chum tells them food is washing by and starts the
  boils (`dlxA22FVNGc`).
- ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: any light-window, moon-phase or
  water-temperature trigger for the snook bite.

## Finding them (sign & sonar)

- **Inside, the sign is visual and it is on the structure you can see.** Boils on
  chum along the root edge, fish jumping inside a cove, and — in clear water —
  the fish themselves visible in the slot (`dlxA22FVNGc`, `frX09YMQxKE`). The
  meter is not used: the mangrove program is precision-cast to visible roots and
  the boca is fished blind over a known mark.
- **Prospect small spots, then commit.** Fish a series of mangrove pockets, find
  the one where snook are actually biting, and anchor on it rather than keep
  drifting across it (`FxgLol_IHa0`, `dlxA22FVNGc`). Read which way the tide is
  running before the anchor goes down and predict the swing, so the casting side
  of the boat ends up laid along the target root line (`FxgLol_IHa0`).
- **Boca:** no visual or sonar sign is given — it is fished as a known bottom
  mark in **40–50 ft** (`EcQK5elTPJE`).
- ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: a sonar signature or arc depth for snook in
  any of these zones. Silence here is a source gap, not a statement that snook
  cannot be metered. General method:
  [electronics & sounder](../planning/electronics-and-sounder.md).

## Gear summary (class terms)

- **Baja Pacific mangrove-snook class** and **Baja Pacific grouper/snook bottom
  class** — full specs in [gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md); line and
  leader reasoning in [line & leader](../tackle/line-and-leader.md).
- **Casting ability is the gate on the mangrove outfit, not power.** A small
  level-wind conventional is the preferred reel and a light spinning outfit is
  the stated fallback for anyone who cannot place a cast with the conventional
  (`dlxA22FVNGc`).
- **Terminal tackle is consumable inside the mangroves.** Snagged branches and
  fish taken into the roots cost hooks and leader all day, so hooks and spare
  fluorocarbon are packed by the spool (`dlxA22FVNGc`, `vyX5FGoDH0A`).
- **Live shrimp for the boca has to be arranged with the guide the day before**
  so it can be caught overnight; there is no dockside bait receiver stocking it
  at Lopez Mateos (`EcQK5elTPJE`).

## Doctrine & conflicts

Section order below is not a ranking.

- **The mangrove leader build is contested inside a single program, and the
  choice turns on whether the failure mode you fear is the cut-off or the
  refusal.** The heavy position takes the fish's line away: **65 lb** braid to
  an **80 lb** fluorocarbon leader, a **4/0** thin-wire hook, a ~**9 ft** rod
  and a star drag set as tight as the reel allows, because the roots are
  oyster-crusted and a snook that takes an inch of line busts off on them
  (`EcQK5elTPJE`). The stealth position takes the bite first: a size **4**
  thin-wire bronze hook on **25 lb** fluorocarbon so the fish can see neither
  line nor hook in skinny clear water (`dlxA22FVNGc`), fished on a later trip in
  the same water as **60 lb** braid to **35 lb** fluorocarbon (`-5kooyIyavs`).
  Fish size is what separates them — the stealth build is documented on small
  root fish, the heavy build on the grade that runs to 42 lb — but no source
  states that as the rule.
  ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: an explicit rule for when to bring which
  build.
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-internal): the same segment that specifies 25 lb
  fluorocarbon states that anything under **35 lb** has zero chance of coming
  out of the bushes once snagged (`dlxA22FVNGc`); one of the two numbers is
  likely an ASR mis-render of the other.
- **The boca's 100–130 lb leader was arrived at by losing fish, not by
  reasoning.** The first six snook hooked in that fishery all broke off, and the
  leader was stepped up through **60, 80, 100 and finally 130 lb** before fish
  were landed (`EcQK5elTPJE`). This is the origin of the 100–130 lb spec now
  carried on [sliding sinker](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md), not a second
  rule beside it.
- **The ~100 lb "grouper" caught alongside snook at the boca is never
  identified.** It is not assumed to be [cabrilla](cabrilla.md) — a different
  region, a different technique program and a much smaller fish — and stays
  unidentified rather than guessed (`EcQK5elTPJE`).

## Landing & handling

- **The strike read differs by zone.** At the boca a hooked snook comes up
  toward the boat instead of diving for the rock, which is how it is told from a
  grouper on the strike (`EcQK5elTPJE`). Inside the mangroves the opposite is
  reported: a hooked fish runs straight for the roots (`isXJONlpUP4`). Both
  descriptions come from the fish's own structure — there is nothing to rock a
  leader against in open water at the boca, and everything to rock it against in
  the roots.
- **A snook's mouth is abrasive enough to wear a hook out**, so the fish is
  played clean of the structure rather than held against it, and **4X** hooks
  are used — boca fish have straightened them (`EcQK5elTPJE`).
  ⚠ Fact-check (unverifiable): the mouth-abrasion mechanism sits in a heavily
  garbled stretch of the seminar captions; the 4X hook spec and the straightened
  hooks are clear, the stated reason is not.
- **Fish are netted, not gaffed**, in the mangrove and estuary programs
  (`FxgLol_IHa0`, `dlxA22FVNGc`). General net choice and release handling:
  [dehooking & release](../fish-care/dehooking-and-release.md).
- **Snook are a table fish here**, kept and eaten rather than released by
  default; **5 lb** is called perfect eating size (`FxgLol_IHa0`,
  `OVwqUKim9Pc`).
- ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: bleeding, chilling or ikejime parameters for
  snook. General method: [fish care](../fish-care/README.md).
