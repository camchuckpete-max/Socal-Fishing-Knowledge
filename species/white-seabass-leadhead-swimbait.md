# White Seabass — leadhead swimbait

**One species, one technique.** A fluke-style leadhead cast off a drifting boat
over a sonar mark or squid nest is the caster's move when white seabass are on
the meter but nobody has called what part of the column they are riding
(`hjZ3hgq0Bzg`). For this fish the program is a depth search before it is a
retrieve: a countdown cast pair that brackets the column, a midwater swim tried
roughly every third cast, and — for fish holding rather than chasing — a slow
current-worked walk in place of the hard crack. The retrieve family itself
(chop-glide, sweep-and-drop, crack-and-slack, bottom-check-and-suspend) is in
[leadhead swimbait retrieve](../techniques/leadhead-swimbait-retrieve.md); this
page is what changes because the fish is a white seabass.

## When this is the call

**The fish are marked but their depth is not known.** The countdown pair below
exists to answer that question with a rod and reel rather than waiting for the
wheelhouse to call a depth (`hjZ3hgq0Bzg`). Reach for it on a drift over a
sonar mark or a squid nest in open water around the Channel Islands, which is
the water this whole open-water retrieve family is described in
(`hjZ3hgq0Bzg`).

**The bait rail is fishing the bottom.** Where the boat is set up on
[dropper loop](white-seabass-dropper-loop.md) soaks pinned to the bed, a caster
covers the column the soak cannot — see the router's
[situations → techniques](white-seabass.md#situations--techniques) rows for the
flanking-caster case and for when bait is being refused outright.

## How white seabass changes the execution

**Count the first cast to bottom, then bracket the column on the next.** Cast
and count the lure down until it hits bottom — the example given is **30
seconds** — then on the following cast engage the reel at **20 seconds**
instead of waiting for bottom, so the lure works a shallower zone than the one
you just measured (`hjZ3hgq0Bzg`). The first cast is the measurement; the
second is the fishing.

**Fish most casts shallow, but swim one through midwater roughly every third
cast.** From the bracketed depth, keep the casts shallow and deliberately drop
a midwater-column swim in about every third cast (`hjZ3hgq0Bzg`). The stated
reason is a miss the bait rail cannot detect: a school riding mid-column rather
than cruising the bottom is invisible to
[dropper-loop](white-seabass-dropper-loop.md) anglers, and a caster is the one
person aboard who can confirm it without being told where the fish are
(`hjZ3hgq0Bzg`).

**A holding fish gets a slow, current-worked walk, not a crack.** For a white
seabass that "wants to chase something" rather than eat a dead-drifted squid,
cast or drop the lure down and then work it through the current, walking the
rod **slowly down-up-down** — deliberately imitating a live squid holding and
drifting in the current rather than a fleeing baitfish (`qLKLlZdiflA`). Go slow
even though a light, buoyant leadhead already carries a lot of drag in the
water, because seabass are often "extremely lazy" and take their time
(`qLKLlZdiflA`). That slow walk is set against the family's hard-crack call for
this species under [doctrine & conflicts](#doctrine--conflicts).

## Rigs & gear

- **Lure: the fluke-style soft plastic on a leadhead**, the same
  [leadhead + soft-plastic swimbait class](../lures/soft-plastic-swimbaits.md)
  the general retrieve uses. The countdown cadence above is described on a
  **1.5 oz** head, which is what makes the count a depth control rather than a
  guess — that weight sinks to the bottom in 60–100 ft "reasonably quickly"
  and holds a chosen depth on the retrieve (`hjZ3hgq0Bzg`); head spec and sink
  behaviour are in the
  [lure note](../lures/soft-plastic-swimbaits.md). Head weights for a white

  Class terms: [gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md).

## Doctrine & conflicts

Section order below is not a ranking.

**Retrieve speed — the choice turns on how actively the fish are feeding.** The
general note's species split puts white seabass with yellowtail as curious and
mobile fish that respond to the **hard crack** — erratic, darting rod input
(`hjZ3hgq0Bzg`). Against that, the slow current-worked down-up-down walk is
prescribed for the same species as an imitation of a squid holding and drifting
in the current, on the reasoning that seabass are often extremely lazy
(`qLKLlZdiflA`). Both are kept: hard, erratic crack-slack draws a curious,
mobile fish in to look and eat; the slow walk matches fish that are holding and
drifting rather than chasing. Read the fish's state — chasing or holding — and
pick accordingly, rather than treating either as the default.

## Differs from the general method

Against [leadhead swimbait retrieve](../techniques/leadhead-swimbait-retrieve.md),
which owns the retrieve family and its two hooksets:

- **The countdown pair is a depth-search move, not a retrieve move.** The
  general note reads bottom contact by feel — the line going slack on the
  harbor count-and-pause rhythm — and never counts a cast down to bracket a
  column. Timing the sink to bottom and then engaging short of it on the next
  cast is specific to hunting an unknown white seabass depth (`hjZ3hgq0Bzg`).
- **The every-third-cast midwater check has no general equivalent.** Its
  justification is a boat-level one — what the dropper-loop rail cannot see —
  so it only makes sense on a white seabass drift, not as a general cadence.
- **The retrieve choice is contested for this fish only.** The general note
  states the seabass/yellowtail hard crack against the halibut glide as a clean
  species split; the slow current-worked walk conflicts with the seabass half
  of that split and carries its own decision frame above.
- **The gear class is NOT a delta.** A casting rod, a low-profile baitcaster
  and a leadhead + fluke are the general kit; nothing gear-side changes because
  the fish is a white seabass.
