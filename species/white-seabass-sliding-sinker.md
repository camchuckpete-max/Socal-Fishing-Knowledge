# White Seabass — sliding sinker

**One species, one technique.** The slider is the suspend-and-retrieve program:
the **least weight that will still sink a squid** riding free on the line, cast
out, allowed to sink the whole column, then reeled straight back up and thrown
again — the bait is never left lying on the bottom (`ShSxNKAcUB4`). Reach for it
when the fish are called **mid-column or near the surface** rather than pinned to
the bed — a skipper calling fish at **20–30 ft over 60–100 ft** is the textbook
case (`Eo_bA8IWvyU`). The free-sliding Carolina format itself, and the rig's
other species and regions, are in
[sliding sinker](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md); this page is what changes
because the fish is a white seabass and the bait is a squid.

## When this is the call

**Fish called up off the bottom.** Fishing **60 to 100 ft** with the skipper
calling fish at **20 to 30 ft** is exactly when the sliding egg sinker comes out
instead of a bottom rig (`Eo_bA8IWvyU`). This is the row the router ranks the
slider for — see
[white seabass — situations → techniques](white-seabass.md#situations--techniques).

**Any stop where the bait would otherwise end up on the sand.** The suspend rule
is not conditional: a white seabass feeds somewhere between the surface and the
bottom and not in the mud, so a presentation that keeps the squid sinking through
the column is the point of the rig on this fish (`ShSxNKAcUB4`). See
[How white seabass changes the execution](#how-white-seabass-changes-the-execution).

**It is not the call when the fish are on the bed.** Fish called close to the
bottom on a squid nest in **60–90 ft** are a
[dropper-loop](white-seabass-dropper-loop.md) job (`Eo_bA8IWvyU`). The same
choice also runs the other way as a matter of taste rather than depth: an angler
who would rather soak a rod in the holder than cast and retrieve gets the bait
off the sand with a dropper loop tied **12 to 18 in** above the sinker instead
(`ShSxNKAcUB4`).

**It is not the call in smoking current over deep water.** There the slider is
dropped entirely for a heavy squid-pinned jig fished straight down — see
[the heavy-current fallback](#the-heavy-current-fallback--a-squid-pinned-glow-jig)
(`ShSxNKAcUB4`, `Eo_bA8IWvyU`).

## How white seabass changes the execution

**Suspend, never soak — the mouth is the reason.** A white seabass's mouth is set
forward on its head, so it feeds up in the column and does not dig in the sand; a
bait lying on the bottom is close to a **zero-percent** proposition and what eats
it instead is bat rays, shovelnose guitarfish and leopard sharks, whose mouths
are on the underside of the head (`ShSxNKAcUB4`). Every other choice on this page
follows from that. The same mechanism sizes the leader on the
[dropper loop](white-seabass-dropper-loop.md#how-white-seabass-changes-the-execution).

**The cadence is cast, sink to bottom, reel straight up, cast again.** Throw the
bait out and let it sink slowly through the column; the moment it reaches bottom,
reel it back up and throw it straight back out, so the bait is moving constantly
and never rests (`ShSxNKAcUB4`). On close to **nine out of ten** casts something
eats it — white seabass, yellowtail, calico bass, blue perch or sheephead — and
that is the diagnostic as much as the goal: a stretch of casts that draws nothing
at all means the stop is dead and it is time to move (`ShSxNKAcUB4`).

**Weight is sized to slow the sink, not to hold bottom.** Go with the least
weight that will still get the squid down. A squid thrown unweighted is not a
presentation at all — it lands on the surface, lies there, and a seagull takes
it — so the lead exists to sink it as slowly as possible rather than to pin it
(`ShSxNKAcUB4`). Weight is then changed through the day against current and
depth, not set once (`ShSxNKAcUB4`).

**The column is worked in steps, not fished at one depth.** Let line out under
steady tension — never dump it and let it pile in front of you — so the bait
falls cleanly. After it has had a couple of minutes to settle, click into gear
and take a couple of turns to bring the sinker back to the hook and the bait —
roughly 20 ft of separation is the case given — then go back into free spool
and let the two separate and keep sinking. Repeating the cycle
walks the bait progressively deeper through the band the skipper is calling
rather than parking it (`Eo_bA8IWvyU`).

**The bite is taken slowly.** A white seabass chomps and eats its way through a
squid, so the set comes when the rod **loads down** rather than on the first tap
(`Eo_bA8IWvyU`). The soft-mouth chain that follows — stretchy mono, a softer tip,
a lighter drag — is on the router's
[landing & handling](white-seabass.md#landing--handling).

## The heavy-current fallback — a squid-pinned glow jig

When the current is smoking over deep water, the slider is put away and a heavy
jig with the squid pinned on it is dropped straight down instead (`ShSxNKAcUB4`).
The same jig is described independently and in more detail as a **gray-light
technique** in its own right (`Eo_bA8IWvyU`):

- **The jigs.** Two Tady-brand sizes fill the role — a smaller one and the
  **Tady 40** — both large, both carrying an **oversized single hook** built to
  pin a whole fresh-dead squid, and both with a **glow-in-the-dark backside**
  (`Eo_bA8IWvyU`). Load **one to three squid** on the hook, deckhand and captain
  permitting: squid supply is often tight and the crew's call on how many you may
  burn comes first (`Eo_bA8IWvyU`). (asr-uncertain: captions render the smaller
  jig's name as "TLC" in both transcripts that describe it, and the same string
  recurring independently reads as corroboration that it is close to the jig's
  real model designation — but the brand/model is not confirmed against a
  catalogue and is not carried here as one (`ShSxNKAcUB4`, `Eo_bA8IWvyU`).)
- **It is fished as a stationary soak, not a worked jig.** Drop to the bottom,
  take **three to five or six cranks** up — the target is sitting **3 to 6–10 ft**
  off the bottom, clear of snags and bottom bycatch — then leave it hanging in
  the rod holder on the rail. The boat's own bob works the jig, rising and
  falling with it, rather than the angler jigging it (`Eo_bA8IWvyU`).
- **The light window is the point.** Most white seabass are caught in gray
  light — before the sun is up, under a heavy dense fog layer, or tucked behind a
  mountain that keeps the sun off the water — and the glow back is what catches
  the fish's attention first, with the pile of squid as what it then sees
  (`Eo_bA8IWvyU`). It is a bite window, not a location cue; the same window is on
  the router's [feeding triggers](white-seabass.md#feeding-triggers).
- **Bite handling is unchanged:** let it chomp and set when the rod loads down
  (`Eo_bA8IWvyU`).

## Rigs & gear

- **Weight ladder: 1/8 or 1/4 oz where conditions allow, up to 1/2 or 3/4 oz as
  current picks up or the water gets deeper.** A full **1 oz** slider is rarely
  fished on a live or dead squid — it sinks through the column too fast — and
  above that the jig above replaces the rig (`ShSxNKAcUB4`). A second briefing
  from a different channel puts the **slider squid** rig at **1/4 oz or 1/2 oz**
  for the same fish-higher-in-the-column trigger at the Channel Islands
  (`nRIAgz5G_Bc`).
- **The sinker must be free to slide.** Do not pin or stop the weight: it slides
  down the line while the bait trails back behind it, which is the whole
  mechanism (`Eo_bA8IWvyU`).
- **Hook: a thin-wire Mustad, not an octopus hook** — the stated personal
  preference against the octopus pattern the general note defaults to — with the
  squid hooked the same way as the same channel's dedicated hook-a-squid video
  (`ShSxNKAcUB4`). The Channel Islands briefing runs an **Owner Aki Twist**
  instead on its 1/4–1/2 oz slider (`nRIAgz5G_Bc`); that briefing's Aki Twist
  size range, **4/0 to 6/0**, is given for the trip as a whole rather than for
  the slider specifically (`nRIAgz5G_Bc`). Pattern doctrine:
  [hooks](../tackle/hooks.md).
- **Glow-in-the-dark egg sinkers** are a variant of the same free-sliding build,
  fished identically (`Eo_bA8IWvyU`).
- **Bait: whole fresh-dead squid, or fin bait when squid is short** — both fish
  on this rig (`Eo_bA8IWvyU`); live squid also fishes it (`ShSxNKAcUB4`). The
  fin-bait hook sizing is in
  [white seabass — gear summary](white-seabass.md#gear-summary-class-terms).
- **Colour: the orange lead head is a neighbouring rig, not this one.** Orange as
  a **reaction colour** for a seabass watching the squid sink is stated by the
  same source in the same breath as this ladder, and the doctrine lives with the
  lure it belongs to — see
  [white seabass — lead head and squid](white-seabass-leadhead-squid.md#rigs--gear)
  (`ShSxNKAcUB4`). Whether that orange head is the same lure as the heavy
  current-jig above or a separate, lighter, general-purpose pick is not
  established on camera.
- **Rod/reel/line: the 30 lb bait class**, or the 30–40 lb bait/jig conventional
  the rest of the white seabass counter is fished on — a conventional star drag
  fishes the slider fine alongside the dropper loop (`Eo_bA8IWvyU`). Run **a lot
  of monofilament**, not straight braid, because a soft mouth pulls a hook off
  no-stretch line (`Eo_bA8IWvyU`). See
  [gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md) and
  [white seabass — gear summary](white-seabass.md#gear-summary-class-terms).

## Differs from the general method

Against [sliding sinker](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md), which owns the
free-sliding Carolina format, the per-species weight ladders and how to feed a
bite:

- **The rig is never allowed to rest on the bottom.** The general note's default
  is to let the sinker settle, keep it in light contact and feed slack on the
  take; on this fish a bottomed bait is a near-zero proposition and catches rays
  and sharks instead, so the bait is reeled up and thrown again the instant it
  touches down (`ShSxNKAcUB4`).
- **Weight is chosen to slow the sink, not to tick bottom.** The general rule is
  the least lead that *holds* bottom in the drift; here it is the least lead that
  *sinks* a squid at all, with a full ounce already too fast (`ShSxNKAcUB4`).
- **The choice against the dropper loop is a depth call, not a preference.** The
  general note treats the two as rods you might run alongside each other; here
  the skipper's depth call decides which rig is correct — up the column is the
  slider, on the bed is the loop (`Eo_bA8IWvyU`).
- **The build can be simpler than the general Carolina order.** The general
  format is egg sinker → bead → swivel → leader → hook; this build is described
  as the sinker sliding straight down the line to the hook, with no bead, swivel
  or leader named (`ShSxNKAcUB4`) — the same lean form logged for the San Diego
  yellowtail slider on the general note.
- **The hook is thin-wire by preference, against the note's octopus default**
  (`ShSxNKAcUB4`).
- **There is a documented point at which this rig is abandoned.** No other build
  on the general note names the conditions that retire it; here smoking current
  over deep water sends the angler to a heavy jig instead (`ShSxNKAcUB4`).
- **The gear class is NOT a delta on its own.** A 30 lb bait outfit with a mono
  top shot is ordinary SoCal slider tackle; what earns this page is the parameter
  set — the sink-not-hold weight ladder, the never-soak cadence with its mouth
  mechanism, the stepped free-spool column work, the thin-wire hook and the
  heavy-current exit.
