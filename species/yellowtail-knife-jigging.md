# Yellowtail — knife / speed jigging

**One species, one technique.** The speed jig is the yellowtail answer to
*current plus active fish*: a back-weighted jig punched down through moving
water over deep structure and ripped back fast, on the read that an aggressive
fish chases a fleeing bait rather than inspecting a fluttering one. Two things
change against the night-bluefin program that owns
[knife / speed jigging](../techniques/knife-jigging.md) — the weight band is
narrower and topped out by angler workload, and the bite cue runs the other
way: bumps without a hookup mean **speed up**, not slow down.

## When this is the call

Yellowtail metered on deep structure with current running — the axis in the
Sea of Cortez is current and activity level, with light current and inactive
fish going to [slow-pitch](../techniques/slow-pitch-jigging.md) and more
current and more active fish going to the speed jig (`M8hOYQ_6rSg`). The
speed jig's back-weighted profile is what makes it the pick here: it gets
down quick in heavy current for aggressive, fast-moving fish, where the
center-weighted slow-pitch profile is built to hang and flutter
(`M8hOYQ_6rSg`); the profile comparison is in
[knife jigs](../lures/knife-jigs.md). See the router's
[situations → techniques](yellowtail.md#situations--techniques) row for the
ranking against the [yo-yo iron](../techniques/yo-yo-iron.md) over the same
water.

## How yellowtail changes the execution

- **Jig weight: start at about 150 g and work up to about 350 g.** Past
  350 g the jig becomes a lot of work to fish for what it adds — the ceiling
  is the angler, not the sink rate (`M8hOYQ_6rSg`). This is a Sea of Cortez
  yellowtail band, narrower than the captain-called
  [150–400 g bluefin range](../techniques/knife-jigging.md#depth-control--jig-calling).
- **Bumped without a hookup? Speed up.** The stroke is pump up, wind down at
  a pace faster than slow-pitch but not frantic; when fish are hitting the
  jig at that working pace without coming tight, the response is to work it
  *faster*. Yellowtail are aggressive and sometimes the faster the better
  (`M8hOYQ_6rSg`).

## Rigs & gear

[Knife/speed-jig class](../tackle/gear-classes.md) throughout. The Sea of
Cortez quiver's speed-jig outfit — a short 5.5–6 ft rod rated 150–350 g (up
to 400 g), 50–65 lb braid, and a taller/narrower reel chosen because vertical
speed jigging wants as much line gained per pump as possible — is in
[rod & reel selection](../tackle/rod-and-reel-selection.md#baja-sea-of-cortez-gear-quiver)
(`M8hOYQ_6rSg`).

- **A SoCal starter build, relayed secondhand.** A **Phoenix Megalodon
  607** — a jigging rod rated by jig weight rather than line class, this one
  **200–300 g** — paired with a **Shimano Trinidad 20A** and **25–40 yd of
  40–60 lb monofilament** top shot running **straight to the jig, no fluoro
  leader in between**. The top shot is shorter than the same angler's yo-yo
  and jig-stick top shots but still deliberately sizable, to absorb the first
  initial bite and the head shakes that follow. The jig on the rod at filming
  was a **200 g Daiwa SK** (`LTCFjqtSI8g`).
  - **Status: a starting point, not tested doctrine.** The build is relayed
    secondhand — speed/vertical jigging was new to the angler documenting it
    in 2023 and the setup is what he had been told by others already fishing
    it, not his own tested practice (`LTCFjqtSI8g`). Treat it as a documented
    entry point rather than established doctrine.

## Differs from the general method

Against [knife / speed jigging](../techniques/knife-jigging.md), which is
built around night bluefin on deep marks:

- **The weight band is narrower and its ceiling is workload.** 150–350 g for
  yellowtail, capped because a heavier jig is a lot of work — not the
  technique note's sink-rate-versus-current logic that runs the bluefin band
  to 400 g and beyond in wind (`M8hOYQ_6rSg`).
- **The bump-response cue runs opposite to the warm-water advice.** The
  technique note's daytime-warmth guidance is to try slowing down when the
  working pace draws *no* bites; the yellowtail cue answers a different
  signal — fish are already hitting the jig and not getting hooked — and the
  answer there is more speed (`M8hOYQ_6rSg`). Read the symptom before picking:
  no bites at all in warm daytime water points at slowing down; bumps without
  a hookup point at speeding up.
- **The line is far lighter than the night-bluefin floors, and there is no
  bite leader.** 40–60 lb mono tied straight to the jig, against the
  technique note's 65–100 lb braid with 100–200 lb leaders and its crimped
  bite-leader school — the target is yellowtail grade, and stretch is doing
  the shock-absorption job that a heavier system would ask the leader to do
  (`LTCFjqtSI8g`).
- **The gear class alone is not the delta.** A knife/speed-jig outfit is
  ordinary for this method; what earns this page is the parameter set — the
  yellowtail weight band with its stated ceiling, the speed-up bite response,
  and a complete rod/reel/line starter build with its own attributed status.
