# Yellowtail — dropper loop

**One species, one technique.** The dropper loop is the yellowtail program for
fish that are *there and not eating*: a live mackerel pinned a set distance
above a **16 oz torpedo sinker** on the bottom, dropped onto a ball of fish the
meter shows clumped tight to structure. Everything about the SoCal build is
sized to one problem — a fish that will run 30 ft straight back into the rocks
the moment it is hooked — which is why the loop is short, the leader is mono,
the reel stays in gear, and the sinker is tied to break away. The loop knot,
the multi-species sinker ladder and the rig's other targets are in
[dropper loop](../techniques/dropper-loop.md); this page is what changes
because the fish is a yellowtail.

## When this is the call

**Fish clumped tight on the bottom and inactive** — confirmed as yellowtail on
the meter but not feeding. This rig is not the call when fish are hunting: the
logic is to put a bait in front of their faces and **annoy them into eating**,
rather than to attract them. The corresponding sounder read is in
[yellowtail — finding them](yellowtail.md#finding-them-sign--sonar)
(`HTowqnwAMeA`). See the router's
[situations → techniques](yellowtail.md#situations--techniques) row for the
ranking against the [yo-yo iron](../techniques/yo-yo-iron.md), which works the
same water as the reaction presentation.

## How yellowtail changes the execution

Two closely related SoCal yellowtail builds are kept side by side rather than
merged, since their figures differ slightly (12–18 in vs. 14–18 in loop; 3–4 ft
vs. 30–36 in hook-to-sinker distance).

- **Wind up 3–4 cranks** once the sinker hits bottom, so the rig isn't resting
  directly on bottom where it's easier to snag — a different crank count than
  the half-crank figure in
  [dropper loop](../techniques/dropper-loop.md#deploying-it--drop-detect-bottom-read-the-bite),
  kept side by side rather than reconciled (`SAltQjih0ms`).
- **Fish it in gear, never free-spool, once it's down near structure — true
  even on a circle hook:** in free spool, a hooked fish can wrap the line
  around a rock before you come tight to it. This is a line-management/safety
  reason, distinct from the hookset-mechanism reasons behind the general note's
  various sets (`SAltQjih0ms`).
- **A periodic free-spool exception to "always in gear":** the always-in-gear
  rule still holds, but as the boat swings on its drift and the bottom depth
  underneath shifts by roughly a boat length or two — about **five fathoms
  (30 ft)** — the move is to periodically clutch back into free spool just long
  enough to re-drop to bottom, then back into gear. This refines rather than
  contradicts the rule: a deliberate, controlled re-drop, not leaving the reel
  in free spool while fishing (`YntRJAN88fs`).
- **Rod-belt timing:** for anglers fishing a rod belt, seat the rod in the belt
  **before** the bite, while the bait is still soaking, rather than fumbling to
  transfer in after the hookset (`SAltQjih0ms`).
- **Bite feel and hookset:** a yellowtail bite on this rig reads as a **strong
  thump**. Yellowtail take a few seconds to fully consume a dropper-loop bait —
  wait for the **rod to load / tip to bend** before setting the J-hook, then
  **wind down to the fish and reset the hook**, repeating up to a second or
  third time rather than striking repeatedly without first taking up slack; see
  also the flyline sequence in [rod handling for live
  bait](../techniques/rod-handling-live-bait.md#yellowtail-specific-hookset-sequence--count-wait-for-the-bend-then-set)
  (`SAltQjih0ms`).
- **The first 30 ft after hookset is the critical window:** with a snug drag,
  the hardest pull of the fight is the fish's first 30–40 ft of running line off
  the bottom — get that line back in **low gear, under a tight drag**, before it
  can get back into the structure. **Gear-shift timing:** rather than shifting
  to low gear the instant the hook is set, the stated preference is to let the
  fish take its first run and shift down once that run happens, gaining line
  fast from there — a related but distinct scenario from the open-water bluefin
  circling-fish timing in [two-speed reel
  technique](../techniques/two-speed-low-gear-fight.md), which is about a fish
  that has already come up and is circling boatside rather than racing back to
  structure (`SAltQjih0ms`).

### Hooking the bait — two placements, kept side by side

- **Nose hook, sideways through the cartilage** — including with a lighter
  slider (2 oz) instead of the full torpedo sinker. **J-hook, not circle,** is
  the default, though some anglers fish circle hooks on this rig and let the
  drag do the setting instead of swinging (`SAltQjih0ms`).
- **Through the roof of the mouth**, described as working best with
  **mackerel** though it applies to sardine too (`HTowqnwAMeA`):
  - **The move:** open the bait's mouth and pass the hook **from underneath, up
    through the roof of the mouth**. Leave the mouth **open**.
  - **Why not sideways (the common alternative):** a sideways-pinned hook can
    **rotate and re-catch the far side of the bait's head**, which puts the
    point where it cannot hook a fish, or starts the bait **spinning** — which
    kills the presentation on a rig whose whole point is a naturally swimming
    bait. Through-the-roof leaves the hook no axis to flip on.
  - **Why the mouth stays open:** a bait hooked with its mouth clamped shut
    **can't breathe and dies quickly** — the constraint that rules out the
    tidier-looking closed-mouth version.

The two placements are kept side by side, not reconciled; the second is stated
explicitly against the first.

## Rigs & gear

Class terms are in [gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md) — the same heavy
40–60 lb yo-yo/dropper outfit the router lists for this water, with the second
build's mainline heavier still.

- **Loop length: 12–18 in, minimum 12 in.** A loop built out to **24 in** needs
  a **double [San Diego jam knot](../rigging/san-diego-jam-knot.md)** rather
  than a single pass — the longer loop needs the extra security. The loop sits
  **3–4 ft above the sinker** (`SAltQjih0ms`). The corroborating build runs a
  **14–18 in** loop with the hook **30–36 in** above the sinker, tied on with a
  **doubled San Diego jam knot** (`YntRJAN88fs`).
- **Sinker/hook:** a **16 oz torpedo sinker** on the bottom, hook above it a
  **5/0** hook named on camera as an "Owner Gorilla" — asr-uncertain whether
  this is the standard or "Light" version, and this specific brand/model pick
  stays **low confidence** since the same video's closing credits thank Owner
  hooks as a sponsor; the 16 oz sinker weight and general rig mechanism are
  unaffected. Heavier than the 6–10 oz general SoCal reference in
  [dropper loop](../techniques/dropper-loop.md#building-the-rig) — a heavier
  same-fishery data point, not reconciled to one weight (`SAltQjih0ms`). The
  second build runs the same **16 oz torpedo** for yellowtail or grouper
  holding on bottom structure (`YntRJAN88fs`).
- **A breakaway overhand knot above the sinker** — a second, simpler mechanism
  than the weak-link dropper loop in
  [dropper loop](../techniques/dropper-loop.md#weak-link-at-the-bottom-dropper-loop),
  not a substitute for it: tie a plain overhand knot in the leader a few inches
  above the sinker. A solid bottom snag parts that knot first, so only the
  sinker is lost and fishing resumes faster (`SAltQjih0ms`). The second build
  ties the same breakaway overhand knot into **80 lb-test mainline** above the
  sinker (`YntRJAN88fs`).
- **Mono, not fluorocarbon, for the leader** — explicitly **not a line-class or
  line-shy thing**: the leader ties straight onto the mono top shot rather than
  stepping down to fluoro, because this is bottom/structure fishing (yellowtail
  or grouper) rather than a finicky open-water bite where fluoro's invisibility
  matters more (`YntRJAN88fs`).
- **Hook size: upsize from your flyline size.** Flylining a 2/0 means running a
  **3/0 or 4/0** on the dropper loop. Related sizing in
  [hooks](../tackle/hooks.md) (`HTowqnwAMeA`).

## Differs from the general method

Against [dropper loop](../techniques/dropper-loop.md), which owns the loop
knot, the multi-species sinker ladder, the deploy sequence and the rig's
unreconciled hookset spread:

- **The trigger is a feeding state, not a depth.** The general note reaches for
  the rig when fish are on or near the bottom; here the gate is fish metered
  **clumped tight and not feeding** — the bait is there to annoy them into
  eating, not to attract them (`HTowqnwAMeA`).
- **The set waits for the rod to load.** The general note's deploy sequence sets
  on the tip pulling down and staying down, two cranks and a hard lift; the
  yellowtail set waits several seconds for the fish to consume the bait, then
  winds down and resets up to a second or third time (`SAltQjih0ms`).
- **The fight has a named window the general note does not have.** The first
  30–40 ft off the bottom is the whole contest, and the gear-shift decision —
  let the run happen, then drop to low gear — is specific to a fish racing back
  to structure (`SAltQjih0ms`).
- **"Always in gear" is a rule here, with one controlled exception.** The
  general note's periodic re-drop is reasoned from drifting bottom contours;
  the yellowtail version starts from a safety rule (a free-spooled fish wraps
  the rock) and carves out the ~5 fathom drift-swing re-drop as the only
  sanctioned free-spool (`SAltQjih0ms`, `YntRJAN88fs`).
- **The leader material is decided, and the reason is structure.** The general
  note's leader guidance is species- and shyness-driven; this build states mono
  straight off the top shot because the fight is against rock, not against a
  line-shy bite (`YntRJAN88fs`).
- **The crank-up count differs from the general figure.** 3–4 cranks up here vs.
  the general note's half crank — kept side by side, not reconciled
  (`SAltQjih0ms`).
- **The gear class is NOT a delta on its own.** A 40–60 lb bottom outfit is
  ordinary for this rig; what earns this page is the parameter set — the loop
  and hook geometry, the breakaway knot, the two bait placements, the crank
  count, the in-gear safety rule and its exception, the mono reasoning, the
  rod-belt timing, the load-up set and the first-30-ft window.
