# Evidence — California Halibut

[California halibut](../california-halibut.md). One line per observation,
grouped under the parent note's section headings, in its order. Observations
never change the parent's stated doctrine; a contradicting one is also traced
beside that doctrine in the parent.

## Presence & forage

- `OpcKQPA3vAI` (BDOutdoors, Academy Ep. 15): forage named on camera is
  "sardines or anchovy, smelt, whatever, lizard fish"; on a bigger bait,
  "halibut have no problem eating a mackerel or a really big sardine." The
  starve-in-dead-water example is a halibut sitting in **105 ft** of open sand
  off a beach.
- `OpcKQPA3vAI` (BDOutdoors, Academy Ep. 15): the Huntington bubbler zone —
  "dog beach off Huntington," the two inside rigs, shale bottom releasing
  methane whose bubbles trap plankton like a current eddy; three or four of the
  biggest halibut ever seen by the presenter came out of that zone, including
  one "going almost 50 lbs" in about **30 ft** of water taken while fishing the
  bubblers for sand bass and calico.
- `OpcKQPA3vAI` (BDOutdoors, Academy Ep. 15): the 2009 Dana Point tournament
  trip to Trestles — off-colour shallow water just outside the surf line
  produced four or five halibut, a couple legal, the best on a spinnerbait,
  with shale outside the beach named as the reason the conditions set up there.
  Landing a big one without a gaff is noted as the difficulty.

## Spawning

- `ghHQe3fP9U4` (Tackle Express, 2025-11-04): the nest description —
  "a lot of these 19 to 25 in male fish trying to spawn with a 30 to 32 inch
  female fish" — the keep-a-23-to-25-in-male conservation ask, and the
  tell-no-one ask, whose only stated reason is "enjoy it for the rest of your
  life." Spot fidelity is tied to bottom composition "especially when they're
  spawning."

## Finding them (sign & sonar)

- `OpcKQPA3vAI` (BDOutdoors, Academy Ep. 15): the San Diego Bay photo-shoot
  fish — caught on a **strong outgoing tide** on a channel edge with hard
  bottom, after seeing bait marks, by dropping a leadhead and giving it a few
  pops. The 30-years-ago Redondo/Sea Spray halibut trips off the airport beach
  are given as the counter-example: long random sand drifts, some days a bunch
  of legals, some days nothing, with the sand wrongly assumed to be the reason.
- `ghHQe3fP9U4` (Tackle Express, 2025-11-04): the crappie comparison for spot
  fidelity ("you might have three spots and this year spot one was great, next
  year maybe spot three is better"), and the gillnet framing — the **1994**
  inshore gillnet ban mattered because a net on a channel ledge or harbour
  mouth "absolutely mops those things before they get to where they need to
  actually perform that part of their life cycle."
- `EuYOlsnxXko` (Tackle Express, 2025-11-19): the walk-until-you-see-them
  advice is given as a real method, not snark — "halibut are really active
  feeders… you will see in really shallow water halibut blowing up on bait."

## Situations → techniques

- `a5u8BaYzw8c` (BDOutdoors, Nate Winnicki of Pacific Coast Bait and Tackle,
  2023-09-13, "Rigging up for Halibut: Best Ways to Fish a Live Bait"): the
  full live-bait rig walkthrough — leader, hook pairing, sinker shape and
  weight band, rig attitude and the bite read — moved with the program to
  [California halibut — live-bait drift
  evidence](california-halibut-live-bait-drift.md#how-california-halibut-changes-the-execution).
- `9qwsg-e9ECA` (Tackle Express, 2026-02-07, "Unlock the Secret Halibut Rhythm
  for Massive Surf Catches"; unregistered channel — medium confidence): the
  surf drop-shot cadence — cast, put it in gear, drag it "about the length of
  the halibut I want to catch, so **22 to 30 in**," then shake the slack while
  reeling so the whole lure moves forward and the flash tricks dance, and
  repeat; stated radius of attraction is a halibut within **three or four
  feet** of the bait.
- `fHMwcm1xRQk` (Tackle Express, Shannon Gallagher, 2026-01-13, "Halibut Gear &
  Techniques," part 4 of 4; unregistered channel — medium confidence): the
  leadhead retrieve rhythm on a Warbaits head with a 3-in Sudden Impact —
  sink in free spool, three turns of the handle, pause, watch the line fall to
  confirm bottom, repeat — with the explicit caveat that it is "not a terribly
  effective way to fish from the beach" because the head returns to bottom too
  quickly for a mid-column swim. Opens with "never fall for the trap that
  they're lazy."
- `OURS05qEGcA` (Tackle Express, 2026-01-14, "Shannon Gallagher's #1 Halibut
  Technique (Leadhead Swimbaits)"): the same retrieve rhythm, cut as a
  standalone clip; feeds
  [leadhead swimbait retrieve](../../techniques/leadhead-swimbait-retrieve.md).
- `E4CDqBFOaP8` (Tackle Express, 2025-12-23, "Halibut LOVE This Surf Lure |
  Lucky Craft Flash Minnow Explained"; unregistered channel, single mention —
  medium confidence): the Flash Minnow is called effectively synonymous with
  SoCal surf halibut fishing; the shallow-diver reasoning is "I don't want a
  jerkbait that gets down to the fish, I want a jerkbait or minnow lure that
  gets the fish up to the minnow lure," scoped to little wave action and fish
  in **1–4 ft**; past that, at **10 ft**, the call is a leadhead and swimbait,
  "much more effective."
- `46ha7J1Fc6A` (Roman Castro, 2020-03-12, "Fishing Tips And Techniques from
  The Fred Hall Show 2020," Long Beach — speaker Brent of Aquatek Imaging
  Solutions; unregistered speaker, single mention — medium confidence): his
  stated recent go-to is winding a Lucky Craft hard bait back noticeably faster
  than you'd think, because halibut chase it down and commit hard once it is
  moving; **Catalina** named as a place it has worked, and the same fast
  retrieve stated to draw bites from shore.
- `5Oh3IPIC5g4` (Fisher Newb, 2020-03-12, Fred Hall Show seminar — Chad,
  HookUp Baits co-owner; ⚠ promotional seminar source — low confidence):
  size up outside for surf/coastal halibut, run **3/8 or 5/8 oz** inside a bay;
  **mint** and **glow green** named as working, and a white "shad" pattern
  called out for **morning and evening**. Colour detail lives with the lure in
  [tube baits](../../lures/tube-baits.md#color-selection-and-the-oreo-cookie-effect-chad-hookup-baits-co-owner-5oh3ipic5g4-2020).
- `eOLoHxoqk6E` (Tackle Express, 2025-11-28, "Halibut Fishing Lures That WORK,"
  part 3; unregistered channel, single mention — medium confidence): the
  four-colour soft-plastic family — "there's something about those four colors
  that if you just choose that as a solid color, you will get bit" — plus the
  explicitly labelled white-belly theory: a halibut lying on the bottom looking
  up mostly sees a baitfish's white belly, and from below it already has "a
  complete advantage," so solid white reads as an easy, energy-efficient meal.
- `eOLoHxoqk6E` (Tackle Express, 2025-11-28), same session: fishing a Warbaits

  pause**, and the **fourth bite** hooked a fish called big on camera. A single
  session illustrating the aggressive-retrieve instruction, not independent
  confirmation of it. The mini rig is described as the smaller, fanny-pack-
  portable version of a bigger rig he otherwise prefers.
- `ijNOphK4XRA` (Tackle Express, 2026-05-14, "Stop Guessing: The Only 4 Halibut

## Landing & handling

- `a5u8BaYzw8c` (BDOutdoors, Nate Winnicki, 2023-09-13): "they really don't
  fight that hard, but it's all up to the angler to calm that fish down" — one
  long run, high rod tip, no pumping, straight grind on a light drag, and keep
  the head from popping out of the water.
- `1l05hEXDaWw` (Tackle Express, 2026-02-05, "Halibut secret: Keep 'em asleep!
  Light drag is key."; unregistered channel, single mention — medium
  confidence): light drag with steady constant tension so the fish never gets
  the abrupt slack/shock moment that triggers a hard head-shake; called
  especially important on minnow-style hard baits with small trebles. The take
  is framed as a telltale — a heavy, uncertain pull that suddenly does "that
  thing."
- `dWqD8l3jW6w` (Tackle Express, 2026-02-09, "That Halibut THUMP!"; unregistered
  channel, single mention — medium confidence): the surf bite is a distinct
  thump compared to a largemouth eating a skirted jig, called "wonderful" and
  "exciting"; the caution is not to swing immediately — point the rod, turn the
  handle quickly until the line comes tight, then swing, because the slack has
  to come out first. The clip cuts off mid-sentence moving into surf rod/reel
  selection; no usable detail there.
- `akUrZ_OYy4c` (Tackle Express, 2026-02-09, "The exact moment a giant halibut
  sees your boat!"; unregistered channel, single mention — medium confidence):
  a big fish (stated examples **30 in**, **40 in** class) often winds straight
  to the boat with no real fight until it sees the boat, has "a moment of
  panic" and bolts; both slacking the line off and pulling too hard are named
  as the wrong response, and no third correct response is spelled out beyond
  implying steady pressure.
- `Bok0db0G4xE` (Tackle Express, 2026-05-07, "Why you should rethink your
  Halibut setup for bigger bites."; unregistered channel, single mention —
  medium confidence): a typical minnow-style hard bait carries only a **size
  six treble** — "if you hook the biggest halibut of your life" on it "you're
  taking your chances," effectively a trout hook in a 15-lb game fish; the fix
  is to swap the hard bait for a soft-plastic rig with the same swimming action
  on one big single hook, heads and reasoning in
  [inshore crankbaits — rigging](../../techniques/inshore-crankbaits.md#rigging).
- `PJEG_RIkzF0` (Tackle Express, 2026-02-07, "That common fishing net might be
  killing your halibut!"; unregistered channel, single mention — medium
  confidence): large-hole poly rope nets let a halibut's fins pass through and
  get cut, leading to infection and death after release, per studies cited on
  camera; the fine/soft-mesh or rubberised fix is in
  [dehooking & release](../../fish-care/dehooking-and-release.md#landing-net-mesh--protecting-a-released-fishs-fins).
