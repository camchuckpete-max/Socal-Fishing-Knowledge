### Regional range and stock — attributed opinion

- `fgTmUq78ofQ` (SearcherSportfishing, Art Taylor and Mike, via Turner's Outdoorsman, 2021-05-14, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 147 — Live at Turner's Outdoorsman"): as of this seminar the fleet was working three areas — one west of San Diego and two west of Ensenada — the fish having shown up closer to home and further offshore that season, so no boat had needed to run down to Colonet or San Quintín for some time; both captains note a water structure historically productive at Colonet/San Quintín early in the modern SoCal bluefin fishery and expect fish to still hold there if that structure persists, just unfished lately because the closer grounds were producing; **their own stated belief, not corroborated elsewhere in this corpus:** Pacific bluefin off SoCal/northern Baja are a resident stock rather than a purely migratory one, ranging roughly from Point Conception down to Cedros Island and out to 200–300 mi offshore, reasoned from ~15 years (as of 2021, i.e. since roughly 2006) of the fishery holding fish including 100 lb-class bluefin, from routinely catching and releasing 8–12 lb juveniles (read as evidence of local spawning, since a fish that size is only months old), and from a 2019 sighting (per on-camera "two years ago") of a giant school of 100–300 lb tuna jumping just south of San Miguel Island, within 50 mi of Point Conception; kept attributed as a captain's stated opinion and reasoning, not asserted as the parent note's doctrine; `searcher-sportfishing` registered channel.

## Doctrine & conflicts

- `c3NFkQbdDy0` (StokedOnFishing, 2023-09-27 upload, ~90 mi off the California coastline, on anchor, aboard the El Dorado): a landed 40 lb bluefin came aboard still carrying a hook in its mouth from an earlier break-off — the crew's read was that the fish had bitten through another angler's line then torn free of that hook's original set, leaving the hook lodged — corroborating, not new beyond, the "teeth saw through the leader" mechanism; the same stretch carried a general caution that a hooked bluefin will actively seek out and cut itself off on **any** abrasion point it can find during the fight — another angler's line, the anchor rode, or the bottom of the boat — framed on camera as "these things aren't stupid"; unregistered channel.
