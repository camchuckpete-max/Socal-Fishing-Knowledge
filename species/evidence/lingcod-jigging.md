## When this is the call

- `iR8CDCyofDM` (Tackle Express, 2026-04-01, 38 s clip; unregistered channel):
  the big lingcod-specific jigs (a "living squid" and a "ling killer") are
  numbers-versus-size gear — "you're not really looking for a ton of fish,
  you're looking for one big fish to seal the deal and get a picture of a
  lifetime"; if you just want bites, fish a double dropper loop, a "cult
  sniper" (Colt Sniper, asr) or a smaller flat-fall. No weight, colour or rig
  detail is given for either named bait.
- `iAr6sbRC384` (Fisher Newb, 2020-03-16, Fred Hall Show 2020 modern
  rockfishing seminar, presenter Bobby Martinez; unregistered channel):
  "depending on what time of the year they're spawning, if it lands in their
  nest they're gonna eat a big profile bait like this" — stated as the reason
  the presenter carries the bigger profiles for the outer islands. The
  aggression-not-hunger framing this sits inside, and the fact that no months,
  depths or nesting sites are given, stay logged in
  [rockfish & lingcod evidence](rockfish-lingcod.md#spawning).

## How lingcod changes the execution

- `XxN7EzNDnf8` (Tackle Express, 2026-04-07, 40 s clip; unregistered channel):
  larger-profile flat-fall jigs take the same rockfish variety — "vermilions,
  reds, chuckleheads" — and pull the more predatory lingcod, which "just so
  happen to really like these flatfalls"; fished "not much different than how
  I'd fish the Colt Snipers" — sinking all the way to the bottom, taking one or
  two turns up, lifting the rod and letting the jig slowly flutter back down,
  "lift, fall, lift, fall." Duplicate-of the `XxN7EzNDnf8` entry in
  [rockfish & lingcod evidence](rockfish-lingcod.md#situations--techniques),
  which points here.
- `O5aQkex0qGg` (BDOutdoors / SoCal Bight Fishing Academy ep. 12, rockfish
  tackle and techniques, Erik Landesfeind; registered): the jig is the switch
  "if I'm fishing deeper water or I'm fishing in areas where they're not biting
  the slug." His stated favourite "hands down" is an Ahi Assault jig, worn to
  bare metal, which he reads off as "a six ounce, or maybe an eight ounce"
  green mackerel — blue and other colours "all work." He calls the cadence "old
  school jig fishing": drop all the way to the bottom, put the reel in gear,
  take a wind or two so you are up out of the rocks, then big sweeps of the rod
  with a flutter back down between them; if the drift pulls the line off
  vertical, drop back to the bottom, make contact again and take another one or
  two big sweeps. The bite, in his words, is "it'll just be bang, you got one."
- `O5aQkex0qGg` (same source, bottom-hopped presentation): once the bait is down
  and the target is a copper rockfish or a lingcod rather than the suspended
  reds, he fishes it "popping, snapping my rod tip up like that, wind it up,
  snap snap — you want to keep bottom contact."
- `O5aQkex0qGg` (same source, the side bite): "the other problem you have with
  lingcod a lot of times is you will get them biting it here, because they come
  up and bite it from the side, and even a big lingcod with a big mouth can
  still miss that hook" — he sets the hook when it pulls away, and if the fish
  comes off immediately he takes "one or two fast winds," drops straight back
  down to the bottom, lets it sit for a second and starts hopping again,
  because "a lot of times the lings are like, oh that bait got out of my mouth,
  they see it, try racing in… and then they'll come over and eat it again. So
  just be patient with that." Stated for the bottom-hopped slug and carried to
  the jig, which he fishes on the same bottom-contact cadence.

## Rigs & gear

- `O5aQkex0qGg` (BDOutdoors / SoCal Bight Fishing Academy ep. 12, Erik
  Landesfeind; registered): the heavy bass rod is "perfect for this stuff," and
  30 lb braid on a high-capacity level-wind is what lets an Ahi Assault knife
  jig, a diamond jig, a Tady slow-pitch jig or a 3 oz lead head go "straight up
  down on the bottom in 300 feet of water, no problem" without leaving a low
  spool diameter to crank back from depth. Duplicate-of the gear entries in
  [rockfish & lingcod evidence](rockfish-lingcod.md#gear-summary), kept here for
  the jig-specific half.
