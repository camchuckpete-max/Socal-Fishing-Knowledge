## How white seabass changes the execution

- `Eo_bA8IWvyU` (Tackle Express — Harry, tackle-counter walkthrough, "Before You
  Fish for White Seabass"; unregistered channel): dropper-loop white seabass are
  "typically between 60 to about 90 ft and typically close to the bottom"
  because that is where the squid beds are — "the squid are spawning, laying
  their eggs, and seabass are very lazy and very curious creatures, they don't
  like to work too hard for their meal," so they swim the bed and eat the squid
  drifting in the current. Also carried in
  [white seabass evidence](white-seabass.md#presence--forage), where it backs
  the router's forage doctrine.
- `ShSxNKAcUB4` (Your Saltwater Guide — Capt. Dave Hansen, 2020-12-08, "How to
  catch white seabass," filmed 2021-05-25 per the on-camera date; registered
  channel): a white seabass's mouth "is out in front of them," so a bait laying
  on the bottom is "about a zero percent chance" and what you catch instead is
  bat rays, shovelnose and leopard sharks — "their mouths are on the bottom of
  their face." A correctly tied dropper loop is accepted for the same reason
  the suspend-and-retrieve slider is: the hook sits **12–18 in** above the
  weight, off the bottom, where the seabass are swimming back and forth across
  a squid nest. The mouth-geometry half also backs the router's
  [presence & forage](white-seabass.md#presence--forage); the slider program it
  is stated inside is queued separately in
  [`sources/relocation-queue.md`](../../sources/relocation-queue.md).
- `Eo_bA8IWvyU` (same walkthrough, bite handling): "sea bass, you do want to let
  them eat it — they're going to chomp, slowly eat it, and once that rod loads
  down, that's when you're going to want to set the hook." duplicate-of the
  entries above (same session, different claim); the same line also backs the
  router's [landing & handling](white-seabass.md#landing--handling), where it is
  stated as species-general rather than rig-specific.

## Rigs & gear

- `Eo_bA8IWvyU` (same walkthrough, rig roster): the dropper loop as "one of the
  most tested and proven methods" — an Owner Ahi twist long-shank J hook in
  **50–70**, a torpedo sinker **8–12 oz** by depth and current, **4 ft** from
  sinker to hook to keep bat rays and sharks off, and an **8–12 to 16 in**
  dropper tag so the squid frolics in the current. Fresh-dead squid is preferred
  over live ("smack his head on the deck"). Same session, rod/reel/line:
  conventional star-drag reels favour the dropper loop, no two-speed needed, and
  **40 lb is the most common line for the dropper loop** on a 7 ft 8 in
  glass/graphite composite 25–60 lb blank fished with 30–40 lb line; mono
  matters because "seabass have really soft mouths." duplicate-of the execution
  entries above (same session). The rest of this source's rig roster — sliding
  sinkers, the Tady TLC/40 jig soak, flukes and the hookup bait — stays in
  [white seabass evidence](white-seabass.md#situations--techniques).

## Doctrine & conflicts

- `Y2bXn44lfqo` (StokedOnFishing, 2023-08-30, Catalina Island; unregistered
  channel — hook-style tension): the crew described the popular dropper loop
  strung with an Ahi twist (J-style) hook as prone to gut-hooking fish, and used
  that as the reason they prefer actively fishing a squid-baited lead head by
  feel — mouth-hooked, not gut-hooked (see
  [leadhead mods](../../rigging/leadhead-mods.md#rigging-a-leadhead-with-squid-for-white-seabass-xr4nurk-z48)).
  On the same trip's overnight anchor soak they still ran dropper loops, but
  rigged with **circle hooks** instead of the J-style Ahi twist (see
  [dropper loop](../../techniques/dropper-loop.md#building-the-rig)). Gut-hook
  avoidance mattered more on this trip than usual because every fish was
  released alive into the broodstock program. That trip's other material — the
  hookup tell, the MLPA collection permit and the live-transport handling — is
  in [white seabass evidence](white-seabass.md).
