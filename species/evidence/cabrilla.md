# Evidence — Cabrilla (Leopard Grouper)

Trip reports and per-source provenance backing
[cabrilla](../cabrilla.md). One line per observation, grouped under the parent
note's section headings, in its order. Observations never change the parent's
stated doctrine; a contradicting one is also traced beside that doctrine in the
parent.

## Where & when

- `dEPuDrhoClM` (Dirty Hookers, 2023-03-03, Tony Reyes trip, Sea of Cortez):
  cabrilla named on camera as **the leopard grouper**, and as what "everybody
  doing the Tony Reyes trip" hopes for; described as "a calico on steroids" that
  "still will fight like a grouper" and "put a calico's to shame."
- `T3cEQUqiH8E` (BDOutdoors, Bahía de los Ángeles, tournament practice day,
  **May 13**): yellowtail were the highlight of the practice day and only a
  couple of cabrilla came, with cabrilla named as the focus for the two
  tournament days that followed. Sponsor-heavy (Yo-Zuri).
- `6P34jI9q1tE` (BDOutdoors, Bahía de los Ángeles, tournament days): the
  tournament format was **one fish per team per day per species**, the only two
  species being yellowtail and cabrilla, weighed **gilled and gutted** so that
  weights cannot be stuffed into a fish. Day one produced an **18 lb** yellow
  and a **7.5 lb** cabrilla at the scale — the crew's own pre-weigh estimate had
  been "pushing 10–11 pounds" — and the crew judged "the cabrilla fishing was
  better than the yellowtail fishing today. Yellows were just a little bit off."
  Day two the crew ran south to new ground on word that "the cabrilla were a lot
  easier to catch" there, and worked "every single kind of protected cove or lee
  of an island" in weather. Sponsor-heavy (Yo-Zuri).
- `wX7jUycIJ-U` (Bryan Timm, Bahía de los Ángeles): after a yellowtail
  vertical-jigging morning the crew ran inside to an island and "fished cabrilla
  for like half of the morning" — "wasn't full speed by any means but we did get
  our fair share of bites," and "nothing huge."
- `n4nMCLLW3tY` (SD Fish and Sips, Loreto, Isla Monserrat): "Lewis put me on the
  cabrilla, shallow water… five, ten feet of water," on a Shimano jerkbait, and
  a second fish called "a different type of cabrilla" — the corpus's only Loreto
  cabrilla record and its shallowest documented depth.

## Presence & forage

- `6P34jI9q1tE` (BDOutdoors, Bahía de los Ángeles): "cabrilla, we've just been
  targeting vertical walls just like this all over here… they're such a
  structure-oriented fish. They're just like a calico bass… they're going to sit
  behind a boulder or next to the grass or whatever, just ambush whatever comes
  out." The crew cast "on every single vertical wall that we can find."
- `M8hOYQ_6rSg` (Dirty Hookers, 2025-05-20): adds the mechanism behind the
  midday downsize — "sometimes the cabrilla don't want to eat the bigger bait
  in the surface; the smaller baits actually get down and get bit a lot better."
- `M8hOYQ_6rSg` (Dirty Hookers, 2025-05-20): the same episode carries a
  **separate big-grouper bait program** for the Cortez — chunk bait and live
  bait on a 6 ft, 300 lb stiff-crimped leader with a 12 oz Jobu-class hook, a
  350 lb swivel and 8 oz sliding egg sinkers, fished to the bottom then wound up
  three or four turns, with 100 ft mono top shots so a fish running for a rock
  cannot cut braid. On the trip it describes, everyone fishing 100–250 lb landed
  exactly one grouper, a 40–50 lb broomtail (`dEPuDrhoClM`). Both sources treat
  that program as "grouper fishing," a different day from cabrilla fishing, so
  it is recorded here rather than in the parent note's cabrilla routing.

## Spawning

- `EmZO8QiOfik` (Dirty Hookers, 2025-03-04): the whole of the corpus on cabrilla
  spawning is one aside inside the retrieve-action segment — slow-rolling baits
  "do not get bit very well… maybe when they're spawning and they're lethargic
  they might eat the slow-rolling baits, but generally the baits are all fast."
  Ledgered as single-source in
  [fact-check ledger](../../sources/fact-check-ledger.md).

## Feeding triggers

- `T3cEQUqiH8E` (BDOutdoors, Bahía de los Ángeles, practice day): on a day whose
  catch was mostly yellowtail with "a couple cabrilla," the presenter found
  **opaque colours out-bit metallic ones** — sardine and mackerel patterns
  struggled, and yellow-trout, straight white or chartreuse-pink "was getting a
  lot more reaction." Single trip, mixed-species scope, sponsor-heavy episode:
  not carried into the parent's colour doctrine, which is keyed to light level.

## Finding them (sign & sonar)

- `0dNfsle54Wo` (Dirty Hookers, 2023-06-23 upload, Bahía de los Ángeles, Tony
  Reyes mothership): a school appeared ten feet behind the panga and "then it
  was just chaos" — first bite a **7–8 lb** fish that launched clear of the
  water on a jerkbait, then "cabrilla foam, freaking **30 of them** swirling on
  the surface like they were a school of carp… every single one of them above
  five pounds… five to ten, there were some **15s**, I'm pretty sure I saw a
  **20** with its back coming out of the water." The crew relocated schools by
  running to **booby-bird** spots and seagulls, and had **sierra mackerel** —
  "big ones, like six pounders" — in the same foam. A second spot was called "a
  kind of boiler spot, sierra mackerel and **10 to 15 pound** cabrilla on the
  surface break." Later, "two more spots of boiling cabrilla, foaming cabrilla,"
  from which the trip's best fish weighed **17 lb on a spring scale** — taken
  "deep deep water, wide open, away from the rocks."
- `0dNfsle54Wo`: on that same foam stop the presenter "wanted to take the
  opportunity to sling my surface iron into that boil. I done goofed — they
  wanted the jerk bait on that stop." Traced beside the parent's foam router
  row; one stop, not a general rule.

## Situations → techniques

- `dEPuDrhoClM` (Dirty Hookers, 2023-03-03, Tony Reyes trip): the surface-iron
  finding — a **6.5 in OG-class steel bait** in "Katy Perry" colour (sky blue
  one side, white in the middle, pink down the back) fished by another angler on
  the mothership caught "up to **20 cabrilla a day**," going from brand new to
  "hardly any paint left" over the trip, while the presenter's own tried-and-
  true yellowtail irons (a Tady 45, a Tady Starman 112, a stinger-class jig) did
  not produce: "they want a really tight swimming fast jig… I didn't trigger
  those bites because I didn't have something bright coloured that was also a
  fast swimming jig."
- `dEPuDrhoClM`: colour was dialled boat by boat across the mothership — the
  presenter's panga on a pink "wacky mackie" Hydro Minnow with a green-and-blue
  back, another boat on chartreuse and orange, another on purples, another on
  straight orange; his conclusion was "as long as you had some brighter coloured
  baits you had a really good shot at getting bit."
- `dEPuDrhoClM`: trolling is the stated fallback — "if the cabrilla aren't
  biting, a lot of times they do troll for them"; no speed, lure or depth is
  given, which is what the parent's flagged stub row records.
- `EmZO8QiOfik` (Dirty Hookers, 2025-03-04): two micro-lure names in the
  fall/winter downsize list could not be reconstructed from the auto-captions —
  asr-uncertain(micro-lure model names, rendered "cold snipers" and "the
  daicas"). "Jig paras" and the small-profile jerkbaits are carried; the two
  unresolved names are not.
- Jerkbait sizing, hardware attrition and the retrieve cadence moved with the
  program to
  [cabrilla — jerkbait evidence](cabrilla-jerkbait.md).

## Gear summary

- `EmZO8QiOfik` (Dirty Hookers, 2025-03-04): the two cabrilla rods were custom
  builds ("these rods do not exist in this format"), with a named production
  equivalent at 8 ft in medium and heavy — 2 in longer than the 7'10" customs,
  with a slower bend. Named reels: Daiwa Lexa T-Wing 300 preferred over the 400
  on hand fit, with the 400's advantage stated as carrying heavier line while
  holding spool diameter; Daiwa TWS, plain Lexa and Shimano Tranx named as
  interchangeable level-wind options. Product/model picks — `low` confidence per
  the `cesar` sponsored-claim caveat.
- `6P34jI9q1tE` (BDOutdoors, Bahía de los Ángeles): the tournament crew's two
  setups — an 8 ft 50–80 lb stiff Teramar bass rod with a Fathom 400 at 6.2:1
  on 80 lb braid, and a 7'9" 15–50 lb G.Loomis IMX Pro with a Tranx 300 and an
  upgraded power handle, which the presenter had thought under-gunned and which
  landed more yellowtail than the heavy setup. Line was 65 and 80 lb braid to
  16–80 lb fluoro. On day one the rod tip was chipped and frayed the leader all
  day with no fish lost. Sponsor-heavy (Yo-Zuri line and lures) — model praise
  is a promotional claim.
- `6P34jI9q1tE`: the drag position, independent of the `EmZO8QiOfik` studio
  discussion — "you have to get something with a lot of drag power. If you allow
  these fish to pull any type of line, you are going to lose more than you will
  land. That's a guarantee." Traced beside the parent's drag doctrine as shared
  premise, not as a vote for either rod pairing.
- `6P34jI9q1tE`: lure notes — the **Hydro Minnow 170** in a "chicken scratch"
  pattern was the trip's top producer, described as designed for this fishery
  and mimicking the local bait; sinking twitch baits in a smaller size and a
  150S "cast a little bit better in the wind" and, burned, "get smoked by both
  yellowtail and cabrilla," with bigger bites on the larger profile and more
  bites on the smaller. Sponsor-heavy.
- `dEPuDrhoClM` (Dirty Hookers, Tony Reyes trip): 80 lb is the line size to
  bring because "you will be doing cabrilla fishing for the majority of the
  trip," with 6–8 ft of leader — "almost a rod's length" — so a fish that gets
  into the rocks meets leader rather than braid.

## Landing & handling

- `T3cEQUqiH8E` (BDOutdoors, Bahía de los Ángeles): asked how many fish to take,
  the crew answered "we don't keep the cabrilla, maybe one yellowtail"; two
  cabrilla were kept that day and one was **steamed whole**, butterflied and
  unscaled, and eaten alongside fresh yellowtail sashimi "caught like eight
  hours ago."
- `wX7jUycIJ-U` (Bryan Timm, Bahía de los Ángeles): "I think we only kept one
  cabrilla, Mike kept one or two, but other than that we released all the other
  ones."
- `ZYAILGyrkRk` (Dirty Hookers, "Ep. 3", Tony Reyes cabrilla trip): a midday
  update at about 12:00 — "we're bleeding all the fish right now, we come in for
  lunchtime."
- `n4nMCLLW3tY` (SD Fish and Sips, Loreto): on the unhook, "look at him — those
  teeth, crushed it"; the fish was kept for dinner. The same clip compares
  cabrilla scales with a snapper's — asr-uncertain(the comparison species and
  the sentence itself are garbled: "Park all your relatives the cabrilla to me
  don't have as tough as scales"), so the scale claim is not carried into the
  parent note.
- `4Dn7yChmhk0` (Dirty Hookers, 2023-11-22 upload, "Ep. 6: Wide Open Cabrilla
  Fishing," Bahía de los Ángeles): a fish went back "right back in the water for
  you guys to catch… not a graceful release, but we'll take it."
