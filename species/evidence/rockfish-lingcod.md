# Evidence — Rockfish & Lingcod

Trip reports and per-source provenance backing
[rockfish & lingcod](../rockfish-lingcod.md). One line per observation,
grouped under the parent note's section headings, in its order. Observations
never change the parent's stated doctrine; a contradicting one is also traced
beside that doctrine in the parent.

## Where & when

- `Z3rZqy2Pi8E` (BDOutdoors, 2022-11-23, Channel Islands — Santa Rosa and San
  Miguel): a Santa Ana weekend produced limits of reds and lingcod for sport
  boats out of Santa Barbara Landing and for a private boat that fished San
  Miguel; the reporter's own plan was to load up on reds in deeper water, then
  head inside and fish artificial lures in 20–30 ft of clear water where a
  lingcod can be watched rolling on the bottom after it eats a slug — the slug
  is the presentation he has taken lings on at Rosa before, while a crankbait
  or spinner bait is stated only as something to "see if we catch them on" that
  trip, not a reported result. A
  yellowtail taken on an artificial at the same islands was released as
  non-retainable in Southern California waters.
- `0dIwWiOc1NY` (StokedOnFishing, 2026-02-17 upload, Pacific side off Ensenada,
  Baja California, guide Alejandro; unregistered channel): after a yellowtail
  stop went quiet the crew slid out to where "the bottom drops off to about
  **300 ft**" over rocky bottom and split the boat between
  [slow-pitch jigging](../../techniques/slow-pitch-jigging.md) and a "classic"
  dead-bait dropper rig (a couple of hooks above a weight, reeled up off the
  rocks and left to soak), both producing steadily; the bag was
  reds/vermilion (called "red snapper" on camera, then self-corrected to
  rockfish), a bocaccio ("salmon grouper"), and multiple lingcod, with one
  angler remarking that lingcod colour varies fish to fish (blue, brown and
  green all called out). Two bonito came on slow-pitch jigs at the same stop —
  see the [bonito](../bonito.md) Ensenada entry. No depth-limit or season
  detail on camera; Mexican waters, so California regs do not apply.
- `CKq0Z6ExVs4` (StokedOnFishing, 2017-11-25 upload, a high spot roughly
  **7–8 mi** from the Hotel Coral marina, Ensenada, Baja California, "Navico
  Weekend Getaway"; unregistered channel): the spot was described on camera as
  coming up to about **25 ft from roughly 200 ft**, and produced multiple
  lingcod (handled carefully for their teeth) plus a fish the crew called a
  "rock bass" and then nicknamed a "**tree fish**" — a nickname data point not
  otherwise logged in the parent. Whitefish, sheephead and yellowtail from the
  same stop are logged in [ocean whitefish](../ocean-whitefish.md),
  [California sheephead](../sheephead.md) and [yellowtail](../yellowtail.md).
  Same annual Navico supplier-conference trip out of Hotel Coral as the
  `0bcDBGzQnGw` entry, a different year (this video predates it).
- `ilINTeknKB4` (StokedOnFishing, 2015-10-29 upload, San Benito Island, Baja,
  part 3 of the 8-day *Intrepid* charter also logged in
  [yellowtail](../yellowtail.md) and [bluefin tuna](../bluefin-tuna.md);
  unregistered channel, sponsor-heavy episode): a drift over **200 ft** of
  jagged rocky bottom, with the captain warning the rail to get sinkers and
  iron up off the bottom the moment they touched down or lose the terminal
  tackle, produced lingcod and red rockfish ("red snapper" on camera) including
  a double hookup of one of each on the same drift, one of them on a **bonita
  strip** bait. The meter showed a little yellowtail sign at the same spot,
  called "still a little early" for that bite. One red rockfish was released
  for being oversized (garbled on-camera reasoning; not carried as a size
  limit).
- `Y1xeieQI3B4` (StokedOnFishing, 2025-05-10, Cortez Bank aboard the 85 ft
  *El Dorado*; unregistered channel): on day two of a 2.5-day trip, after the
  crew boxed limits of bluefin ("limits of blue fin tuna for 23 passengers")
  and a stack of good-grade yellowtail, Captain TJ switched the whole boat to
  rockfishing to "fill the bags" against a stated **20 fish per angler** boat
  allowance; the stop produced multiple lingcod (one taken "right under the
  gill") and ocean whitefish — see the
  [ocean whitefish](../ocean-whitefish.md) Cortez Bank entry — on jigging gear
  described as mostly braid ("90% braid" and "95% braid" from different
  anglers). A concrete example of the mid-trip pelagic-to-bottomfish switch on
  a multi-day bank charter.
- `fDSd9kqwYW0` (StokedOnFishing, 2023-02-22 upload, Cortez Bank aboard the
  85 ft *El Dorado*, day-and-a-half charter out of Long Beach Sportfishing,
  Captain TJ; unregistered channel): a morning drift **up on a ridge on the
  edge of the bank in roughly 150 ft** — crew instructed to keep the wind in
  their faces, "you want that wind to be in your face so it's pushing the boat
  and your line's going [away]," so lines trailed away from the hull —
  produced reds/vermilion alongside sheephead and ocean whitefish, plus a
  catch called "sandwich groupers" on camera (asr-uncertain: species not
  resolved, not carried as an ID). The crew reported filling a stated
  **10-fish** rockfish allowance ("fulfilled our 10 around on the rockfish")
  before moving shallow and then anchoring on the bank's yellowtail bite — see
  the [yellowtail](../yellowtail.md) Cortez Bank entry.
- `SGbynqaiHdY` (StokedOnFishing, 2017-07-18 upload, Santa Barbara Island,
  "STOKED On Save The Brave," charity charter for 15 military veterans aboard
  the 85 ft *El Dorado*, Long Beach Sport Fishing; unregistered channel):
  pre-trip the captain named rockfishing as the backup if the island's
  yellowtail did not produce, calling for a **1/2 lb sliding sinker**,
  **30 lb test** (25 lb as a lighter option) and **2/0–5/0 hooks**. On the
  water the trip produced a heavy red/vermilion bite on live squid fished on
  light sliding sinkers near kelp and a ridge in roughly **102 ft**, with red
  crab present on the structure; the later repositioning to 20 fathoms on the
  outer edge is logged in [yellowtail](../yellowtail.md).

## Presence & forage

- `iAr6sbRC384` (Fisher Newb, 2020-03-16, Fred Hall Show 2020 modern
  rockfishing seminar, presenter Bobby Martinez; unregistered channel): red
  crab "suspends right over these rockfish spots," and in the several years to
  2020 a lot of rockfish had been suspending off the bottom with the red crab
  around, which is why a red-coloured bait had been a killer; separately,
  anchovies and sardines suspend over the same rocky bottoms and the rockfish
  eat them. The same seminar names a bait-fish-coloured version that "works too
  good" — mackerel and bonito on the way down, plus bocaccio.

## Spawning

- `iAr6sbRC384` (Fisher Newb, 2020-03-16, Fred Hall Show 2020 seminar,
  presenter Bobby Martinez; unregistered channel): the fast-wind slug bite is
  framed as aggression rather than hunger — "it's not like a hungry, it's just
  an aggressive bite" — and, "depending on what time of the year they're
  spawning, if it lands in their nest they're gonna eat a big profile bait
  like this." No months, depths or nesting sites are given. The lure
  consequence he draws from it — carrying the bigger profiles for the outer
  islands — moved to
  [lingcod — jigging evidence](lingcod-jigging.md#when-this-is-the-call).

## Feeding triggers

- `6-8KfjEg0x8` (BDOutdoors, 2026-07-23, Channel Islands out of Ventura): "we
  did not have much current, which at the islands is just as important as the
  bait you're fishing, really — the conditions make or break it out here,"
  said after a shallow stop that nevertheless produced whitefish and lost
  sheephead; the boat then moved to a ~**175 ft** spot and caught blue bass and
  rockfish, with the narrator noting blue bass are a Channel Islands fish you
  "do not get in San Diego." The stated current doctrine and the day's own
  result point opposite ways — traced beside the doctrine in the parent.
- `O5aQkex0qGg` (BDOutdoors / SoCal Bight Fishing Academy ep. 12, rockfish
  tackle and techniques, Erik Landesfeind): reds and lingcod "don't lay on the
  bottom like wet rags" — they chase a bait 30 ft up and eat it, followers are
  common, and a fish that rips off a third of the way up will eat again the
  instant the reel is kicked back into gear.

## Finding them (sign & sonar)

- `11npFUPOJKU` (BDOutdoors, Furuno fish finder / sounder for rockfish): run
  the contour on autopilot over a known hump, waypoint every hard-bottom hit,
  keep the track line running, and read the split-screen low/high frequency
  pair — low paints the bait cloud, high separates the gamefish arches.
- `_ZThckj2TIM` (Roman Castro with Scotty of Brothers Sportfishing, 2020-04-04,
  San Diego rockfishing; capt-scotty-brothers registered): setting the drift is
  a wind-and-current problem solved before the first bait goes down — on a
  south wind he sets up south of the target and lets the wind push north; at
  **35 fathoms (210 ft)** he drifts rather than anchors; he stops the boat cold
  first to read where wind and current take it; and he trims drift speed by
  kicking the outboard over as a rudder, lifting the motor clear to drift
  faster, or streaming a drift chute to slow down. Marks over structure do not
  name the species — the parent's whitefish sibling logs the same line.
- `CKq0Z6ExVs4` (StokedOnFishing, 2017-11-25 upload, Ensenada high spot;
  unregistered channel): the captain repositioned mid-session after the boat's
  drift direction reversed from the first pass, resetting up-drift of the
  structure "to have a good window to... drop down" onto it — a worked example
  of correcting for a drift reversal on a bottom-structure spot rather than
  working it blind. duplicate-of the `CKq0Z6ExVs4` Where & when entry above
  (same stop, different detail).

## Situations → techniques

- `AqW_Z9pFcHU` (BDOutdoors, Ali Hussainy, San Diego rockfishing): the gulp
  program on camera is an **8 oz jig head with an 8 in Gulp grub in white
  glow**, fished in about **270 ft** off San Diego.
- `XxN7EzNDnf8` (Tackle Express, 2026-04-07, 40 s clip; unregistered channel):
  larger-profile flat-fall jigs take the same rockfish variety — "vermilions,
  reds, chuckleheads." The lingcod half of the clip — the more predatory fish
  the larger profile is aimed at, and the sink-to-bottom / one-or-two-turns /
  lift-and-flutter cadence — moved to
  [lingcod — jigging evidence](lingcod-jigging.md#how-lingcod-changes-the-execution).
- `iR8CDCyofDM` (Tackle Express, 2026-04-01, 38 s clip; unregistered channel):
  the big lingcod-specific jigs are numbers-versus-size gear — if you just want
  bites, fish a double dropper loop, a Colt Sniper or a smaller flat-fall. The
  named baits and the one-big-fish framing moved to
  [lingcod — jigging evidence](lingcod-jigging.md#when-this-is-the-call).
- `5Oh3IPIC5g4` (Fisher Newb, Fred Hall HookUp Baits seminar; promotional,
  single product line): the double tube-bait dropper rig is presented for
  rockfish, with rockfish framed as almost a by-catch of the red-crab-coloured
  bait program.

## Gear summary

- `PRNMGpLj7Pw` (BDOutdoors, 2026-01-13, slow-pitch long-range report ep. 12):
  thin braid that touches a pinnacle breaks, so the terminal end is a
  four-turn surgeon's knot joining **50 lb test to about 3 ft of 80 lb** for
  abrasion — "it can be fluorocarbon, it can be mono, it doesn't really matter
  because the fish don't care," and "even a lingcod's teeth are not going to
  bite through the line, but the rocks can be pretty brutal down there." Jigs
  are rated in grams; the presenter's favourite is a **280 g** Williamson
  Kensaki.
- `S9sQ8Vg8-5o` (Tackle Express, 2025-03-28, rockfish tackle gear guide;
  unregistered channel): the full-length source for the Owner Ai/Oy Twist
  size 10 all-purpose double-dropper-loop hook, the sinker-by-depth figures
  (**6–8 oz at 100–150 ft**, **16–20 oz at 300–600+ ft**, carried on
  [rockfish deep-dropping](../../techniques/rockfish-deep-dropping.md#weight--staying-off-the-snag)),
  the ~800–850 ft local-boat depth ceiling, and the mucus-on-the-spines
  handling framing. `D0bQjAL0gGU` (2026-04-13) and `J40VxLNuZhk` (2026-04-05)
  are short re-cuts of this same video — word-for-word repeats, not
  independent sightings.

## Regulations

- `Rf1HKJG-SDg` (2020-12): the 43 bank fell inside a closed rockfish area, so
  effort that used to target it for rockfish shifted to bluefin on the same
  high spot.
- `iAr6sbRC384` (Fisher Newb, 2020-03-16, Fred Hall seminar, Bobby Martinez;
  unregistered channel): stated from recollection a **10 rockfish/day** bag
  with a **separate 10-fish whitefish allowance** stacked on top; the
  surrounding canary-rockfish and species-count detail is too garbled in the
  auto-captions to transcribe (asr-uncertain). He also described the
  depth-based closure line as running at different depths along the same
  stretch of coast — about **240 ft** near the offshore islands vs a
  **75-fathom (450 ft)** line nearer the mainland — such that a boat can be
  legal at one position and illegal a short distance away at a similar depth.
  The chart-app compliance workflow he paired with it moved to
  [electronics and sounder](../../planning/evidence/electronics-and-sounder.md).
- `lF6jQklDCrY` (2019-02-06): the SoCal rockfish season reopening **March 1**,
  and a depth-limit progression read off the then-current regs — **300 ft**,
  held at **350 ft** for "a couple years," then **460 ft** effective
  January 2019 — with the presenter explicitly saying he "could be totally
  wrong" about that reading.
- `AH4Eiya1Hn0` (Capt. Dave Hansen, Your Saltwater Guide, live seminar, 2024
  PCS Show, 2024-03-09; subscription-site pitch — low confidence): the Cow Cod
  Conservation Area's depth restriction loosened in 2023 to allow fishing to
  **600 ft with two hooks** inside the zone, while cow cod itself stays
  no-retain; a ~20 lb cow cod is framed as effectively impossible to return to
  the bottom alive without **40–80 lb of descending weight** or a winch.
- `D0bQjAL0gGU` (Tackle Express, 2026-04-13; unregistered channel): the
  recreational depth allowance changes "depending on what time of the year in
  the season it is," from a fully "unlimited" status down to the deepest he has
  "ever been told" local SoCal boats fish, **about 800–850 ft** (transcribed
  "8 to 850," read as 800–850 ft given his own "still a very, very long way
  down" framing; asr-uncertain). Short re-cut of `S9sQ8Vg8-5o` (2025-03-28) —
  the identical claim word for word, not an independent sighting.

## Landing & handling

- `_ZThckj2TIM` (Scotty, Brothers Sportfishing, via Roman Castro, 2020-04-04;
  registered): rockfish dorsal spines carry a mild venom — minor, not a serious
  hazard, but worth being deliberate about.
- `J40VxLNuZhk` (Tackle Express, 2026-04-05, "Think All Rockfish Are Safe?
  Think Again!"; unregistered channel): all SoCal rockfish carry a stinging
  **mucus** on their spines rather than venom — unpleasant, not the same hazard
  class — with the California scorpionfish (**sculpin**) the one genuinely
  venomous exception whose sting will "ruin your day"; the stated rule is to
  avoid all spines on all rockfish rather than sort them on deck, and deck
  hands catch a sculpin coming over the rail "95% of the time." Short re-cut of
  the closing minute of `S9sQ8Vg8-5o` (2025-03-28) — word for word, not an
  independent sighting.
