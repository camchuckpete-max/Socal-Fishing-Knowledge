## When this is the call

- `3qSY328fFYo` (StokedOnFishing, presenter/angler Capt. Jimmy Decker,
  2014-11-08 upload, SoCal — a half-day marlin trip run to the bottom of the
  **tanker lanes**, about 17 mi out and plotted as **19.2 mi at 193°** from the
  home port, then a further ~35-minute run toward the **277 bank** at the bottom
  of the ridge off Catalina's east end; unregistered channel — medium
  confidence): the corpus's only worked SoCal marlin troll, and the source of
  every parameter on this page. Target water **68°F** was hunted before the jigs
  went in and the water fished read **69.3** then **69.4°F**; mid-morning the
  boat crossed a break of about **two-tenths of a degree** after a friend on
  another boat reported a fish up on the colder side, and worked the cold side
  afterward. Tide as given on camera: a high at **7:30**, called "a pretty
  rolling tide," with the low stated twice (roughly **11:30–12:00**, ASR-garbled,
  later restated as **11:15**) — both readings kept, ledgered as single-source in
  [striped marlin evidence](striped-marlin.md#feeding-triggers).
- `HzE4FHHcvTk` (BDOutdoors, 2022-08-17, launched Newport for the 152 Ridge and
  Avalon Bank): the marlin jigs went out on the bait edge in the dirtier water
  and the bait disappeared as the boat trolled into the clean water — the
  seam-placement rule this page opens with, from a different boat and year than
  the worked stop above.
- `6TBxHnkYXI0` (BDOutdoors, 2025-02-20, SoCal, studio report): the slack-tide
  doctrine — "marlin bite on a slack tide" — with the operational rule attached,
  be in your highest-percentage spot an hour before a tide change.

## How striped marlin changes the execution

- `3qSY328fFYo` (same stop, spread detail): everything was run **short and
  tight** for this boat — on the outriggers roughly **four to five wakes** back,
  with the flatlines run **real close to the boat**. The roster that day: a
  **flying-fish** swimmer in one rigger, an unnamed swimmer captioned "NE"
  (ASR-uncertain, not resolved to a model), a **Hawaiian-style** lure, and a
  small pink lure nicknamed **"the little pinky"** — the lure that had produced
  a fish the week before. Stated sun rule: don't troll into the sun; trolling
  "downhill" with the sun behind the boat makes the jigs much easier to watch,
  and a fish coming up **behind the jigs** close to the boat shows a good
  silhouette against the water, where a fish showing further out ahead toward
  the sun/prop-wash glare is much harder to see. duplicate-of the
  When-this-is-the-call entry above (same session, different claim).
- `3qSY328fFYo` (same stop, spread-watch and sounder detail): a fish "can come
  up and take a whack out of a jig but not pop the outrigger or pop the rubber
  band," so watching the jigs and prop wash is the most important job after
  driving the boat — without a dedicated watcher "you might miss four or five
  fish throughout the course of the day or you think you just got popped off by
  a piece of kelp." The console sounder was set to display to **120 ft** to keep
  bait depth visible under way (100 ft / 30 ft / 20 ft), cross-checked against
  small bait schools skittering at the surface, sauries greyhounding, and a
  large cloud of bait metered directly under the boat before the first drop of
  the day; **mackerel** was called out as identifiable on the meter. The
  captain's stated rule of thumb for jig-raised marlin generally: expect roughly
  **one hookup for every five or six** fish raised or interested. On the jumper
  question the crew had no settled explanation for why marlin jump
  (parasite-shedding and play both offered as guesses); what matters is whether
  a jumper turns into a feeder working bait — those "usually all bite."
  duplicate-of the entries above (same session).

## Rigs & gear

- `3qSY328fFYo` (same stop, result and tackle detail): the stop produced a
  striped marlin estimated on camera at roughly **160 lb**, hooked in the corner
  of the mouth on the **flying-fish** swimmer and released — the radio call is
  "I just released one at 14 and 04 on a flying fish," so the fish ate the jig
  rather than the dropback bait. Exact line class is never stated; mid-fight the
  angler said "now I wish it was 50 lb tackle," which places the outfit lighter
  than 50 lb. Closing tips from the same video: catch a few green-back mackerel
  for dropback bait before you go, pull up-to-date marlin reports rather than
  fishing the calendar, buy the best binoculars you can afford, and bring
  patience — experienced guys go days on end with no bites or sign. duplicate-of
  the entries above (same session).
