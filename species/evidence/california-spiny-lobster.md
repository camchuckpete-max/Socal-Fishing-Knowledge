# Evidence — California Spiny Lobster

Trip reports and per-source provenance backing
[California spiny lobster](../california-spiny-lobster.md). One line per
observation, grouped under the parent note's section headings, in its order.
Observations never change the parent's stated doctrine; a contradicting one is
also traced beside that doctrine in the parent.

## Where & when

- `b19_AJjYCok` (BDOutdoors, Roger Marquez, 2019-11-13, "California Spiny
  Lobster Tips (What You Need to KNOW)"): the parent's primary
  season/licence/gear-count/size doctrine and the stock rig description.
  Filmed on the day of a San Diego Bay trip, dated on camera as November 5 —
  mid-season, and the source of the San Diego Bay location code 18 worked
  example on the report card.
- `YQsbwfQ4wzY` (Your Saltwater Guide, Capt. Dave Hansen, 2019-11-26, "The Best
  Way to Catch Lobsters in California!"; manual captions, registered voice):
  source of the bait-freshness mechanism, the small-bait-jar commercial
  practice, the black PVC bait-tube fix for sea lions, and the Catalina
  20 ft / 30 ft working depths — stated as fished off a 65 ft Hatteras.
- `cEscIy278ew` (Your Saltwater Guide, Capt. Dave Hansen, 2020-11-13, "Advanced
  Lobster Fishing | Tips and Tricks That WORK"): the general 25–30 ft working
  depth, given across a fleet spanning a skiff, a 44 ft Pacifica, a 46 ft
  Bertram and 65 ft Viking/Hatteras sport boats; also the source of the
  shake-into-a-bucket measuring drill and the shorts-over-the-side-immediately
  rule. Most of the video is boat-approach and prop-safety execution, carried
  in [hoop netting](../../techniques/hoop-netting.md).
- `Qs9oEsh3b_w` (Roman Castro / Capt. Scotty, Brothers Sportfishing,
  2020-10-17, "California Spiny Lobster Hoop Net (FULL SETUP)"; `roman-castro`
  registered): source of the GO ID buoy-marking detail, the two-person
  minimum for a 10-net boat limit, and the Mission Bay 25–35 ft / San Diego Bay
  ~55 ft depth figures. The depth numbers are given as the reason for splitting
  a 100 ft factory rope into a ~35 ft working section and a ~60–65 ft
  extension.
- `Ow3an9lSVh4` (StokedOnFishing, 2014-11-03, Catalina Island, "Stoked on
  Simrad, part 3"; sponsor-heavy Simrad/Navico electronics episode,
  unregistered channel — medium confidence): the crew's first lobster trip of a
  season, run on the way home from two days of SoCal tuna fishing. The
  presenter states lobster sit shallow in the summer months in some years and
  have been found out in deep water in other seasons, so the crew spread nets
  across different depths. The stop produced four lobster in one net, one
  short released, and a fish called a **3½ pounder**, ending in limits; the
  carapace measurement is demonstrated on camera from between the eyes to
  where the tail starts.

## Presence & forage

- `YQsbwfQ4wzY` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2019-11-26): the bait-detection
  mechanism is attributed on camera to a commercial lobster fisherman friend
  who "studied the lobsters" — the antennae are said to pick up the electrical
  field coming off bait as it breaks down, so lobster "start marching right to
  your bait." Second-hand and uncorroborated; ledgered as single-source.
- `EiItVWqFMYc` (Your Saltwater Guide, Capt. Dave Hansen, 2021-01-05, "Lobster
  Fishing Tips | How Long do we Keep the Hoop Nets Soaked?"): the
  aggregation rule — "if there's one there's way more there" — and the
  split-the-spread search method. Also the source of the shorts-back-in-the-net
  practice ("nothing attracts a crowd like a crowd"), carried in
  [hoop netting](../../techniques/hoop-netting.md), with the stated legal
  reading that a lobster inside a net that can crawl out is not in possession.
  The last two minutes are a website-membership promo.

## Spawning

- `JiJYamo0QHk` (Utah Spahghettzi Björker, 2019-03-12, "Fred Hall 2019 Dave
  Hansen Part 2"; live seminar, dave-hansen registered voice): the
  egg-bearing-in-January claim is delivered as a rhetorical question — "if they
  cared about the lobsters they'd end the season in January because what do all
  the lobsters have on them right now" — and the auto-captions render the
  answer as "hey lots of them," read here as "eggs, lots of them."
  asr-uncertain(the word "eggs" at both points). The same passage's
  "four to five pound females into a pot of boiling water with legs" is read as
  "with eggs" on the same grounds.

## Feeding triggers

- `EiItVWqFMYc` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2021-01-05): the light window is given
  as nets in an hour before dark, first pull about half an hour after the sun
  goes under the water — "it's dark on the bottom, it's still gray light out."
  The bounded-crawl claim is stated as shared knowledge with the audience
  ("all of you that have hoop netted know what I'm talking about").
- `fri_BWI-VA0` (Your Saltwater Guide, Capt. Dave Hansen, 2022-02-12, "Moon
  Phases and how It affects your fishing"): the lobster moon doctrine sits
  inside a general moon-phase video whose main subject is pelagic and bass
  fishing; the lobster section is the contrast case. Full writeup in
  [moon](../../conditions/moon.md#lobster-darkest-no-moon-nights-are-best).
- `YQsbwfQ4wzY` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2019-11-26): the rain trigger's first
  dated appearance — "when the rain starts... you're gonna catch lobsters of a
  lifetime up in Long Beach in LA Harbor." Framed as a lean with no mechanism
  attached at this point, and paired with a paid-forum offer of specific
  Catalina and harbour spots as the season progresses.
- `sIoNELGlxmk` (Your Saltwater Guide, Capt. Dave Hansen, 2023-03-04, live
  seminar at the PCS Show): the same voice repeating the rain trigger four
  years later, this time with the mechanism — "they hate fresh water... hate
  hate hate it" — and with the origin story below.
- **Observed** `sIoNELGlxmk` (dave-hansen, Long Beach Harbor, dated on camera
  to 2006–2007): running his own early hoop-net charters out of Rainbow Harbor
  — chosen over the rest of Long Beach Harbor because he could not afford the
  fuel to run further — at **$100/person for up to 6 anglers over 5 hours**,
  with no established program yet built, the crew caught lobster at **every
  spot fished** in the harbor on the trip after it rained. This is the
  observation the rain doctrine was learned from, not independent confirmation
  of it.

## Situations → techniques

- `b19_AJjYCok` (BDOutdoors, 2019-11-13): the sea-lion problem is stated as the
  reason for a clip-in closed bait cage on a rigid "pro" net; bait named is
  mackerel, anchovies or cut-up salmon heads.
- `YQsbwfQ4wzY` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2019-11-26): salmon heads are called a
  hassle to get and use *and* the bait sea lions love most, which is why the
  black tube replaced them — sea lions are described as visual hunters that
  will crush a bait cage and blow bubbles into it once they have located it.

## Landing & handling

- `Q196GVwVqro` (Your Saltwater Guide, Capt. Dave Hansen with Justin Botrell of
  B-Line Sportfishing, 2024-03-07, live seminar at the PCS Show): the
  never-stop-pulling rule comes from a live-streamed pull where a single
  lobster covered the whole ring of a **36 in** hoop net, was too big to pass
  through the opening, and was nearly lost when a crewman stopped hauling to
  reach for it; it was released and called possibly a United States record.
  Same seminar: the California record of **18¾ lb** in a hoop net, weighed
  because the fish was donated to the Long Beach aquarium; a **March 20**
  season close for 2024; and Botrell's stated **2,178 lobster** for the season
  on his Long Beach operation, with **50–60 lobster** a night cited as
  routine. The last third of the video is a guide-service contest promo.
- `JiJYamo0QHk` (Utah Spahghettzi Björker, 2019-03-12): the big-fish retention
  argument — a **15 lb** fish taken the night after the record, the "you can
  only keep seven, period" framing, and the claim that the commercial fleet had
  its best-ever start to a season in the same period. Delivered as seminar
  polemic against catch-and-release posts on the message boards; ledgered as
  single-source.
- `sIoNELGlxmk` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2023-03-04): the don't-eat-the-digestive-
  tract point is made by a young guest brought on stage who had made a lobster-
  cleaning video for the channel; no further cleaning procedure is given.
