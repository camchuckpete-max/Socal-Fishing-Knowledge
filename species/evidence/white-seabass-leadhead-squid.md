## When this is the call

- `Xr4nURK-Z48` (BDOutdoors — Ricky, 2022-06-16, Catalina Island): the two-squid
  lead head "can mimic squid mating... that's going to generally be going on
  during the nighttime hours and the gray light hours," which is when it "can be
  super effective"; daytime fish are catchable, "but generally you're going to
  have the better sea bass fishing during the gray light and night hours." Also
  carried in [white seabass evidence](white-seabass.md#presence--forage), where
  the same session backs the router's squid-as-staple forage doctrine.

## How white seabass changes the execution

- `Xr4nURK-Z48` (same session, rigging and count): standard market wild-caught
  squid, "a little bit on the small side, but they'll work just fine," hooked
  "kind of similar to a swimbait" — threaded through the top of the mantle and
  pushed up onto the lead head, then a second squid pinned on through the top of
  the mantle, "two squids, maybe three if they're a little small."
- `Eo_bA8IWvyU` (Tackle Express — Harry, tackle-counter walkthrough, "Before You
  Fish for White Seabass"; unregistered channel): lead heads and squid are "a
  similar concept to our sliding egg sinker, but I get to fish these a little
  differently" — a full squid on the head, "flick it out behind the boat, let it
  sink back. I do like there to be some current when I fish these lead heads
  just that way I could get it back behind the boat and away from us." The
  column is fished "from pretty high to the surface" down to wherever the
  skipper is calling the fish. On the sink, "always have some good tension on the
  free spool. That way it's not just sinking like a rock. I want that to kind of
  flow, dance, and go with the current by itself." Once at a sufficient depth or
  distance, "I'll click it into gear and I might wind and pop the rod... you're
  getting the tail of the squid to pop, flick, pop, flick... trying to get that
  fish to become from curious to becoming a biter."
- `Eo_bA8IWvyU` (same walkthrough, bite handling): "sea bass, you do want to let
  them eat it — they're going to chomp, slowly eat it, and once that rod loads
  down, that's when you're going to want to set the hook." Stated species-general
  rather than lead-head-specific; also carried in
  [white seabass evidence](white-seabass.md#feeding-triggers) and
  [white seabass — dropper loop evidence](white-seabass-dropper-loop.md).

## Rigs & gear

- `ShSxNKAcUB4` (Your Saltwater Guide — Capt. Dave Hansen, 2020-12-08, "How to
  catch white seabass," filmed 2021-05-25 per the on-camera date; registered
  channel): the lead heads he fishes are orange, poured by a friend and "not for
  sale anywhere," with the shop-counter instruction to "look for an orange
  headed lead head — it's gonna have to have a big hook to support your squid on
  it." They are "not very heavy, they sink down the water column good," and "I
  believe that that orange on the head is a reaction color to that sea bass —
  when they see that squid sinking down in the water column with this tail all
  bright orange they have a tendency to want to eat it." Stated in the same
  breath as the heavy white TLC jig for smoldering current and the
  don't-let-it-lay-on-the-bottom rule, whose suspend-and-retrieve program stays
  in [white seabass evidence](white-seabass.md#situations--techniques).
- `Eo_bA8IWvyU` (same walkthrough, reels): the low-profile big casting series
  (a Shimano Tranx 400 named) is called "extremely ideal for all of our
  artificial stuff, our flukes, hookup baits, or even a sliding egg sinker, even
  a leadhead and squid" because of the spool tensioner and internal brakes;
  limited spool capacity is the trade, so straight mono or braid compensated with
  a soft rod. duplicate-of the entries above (same session, different claim);
  also carried in
  [white seabass evidence](white-seabass.md#gear-summary-class-terms).
