## Where & when

- `4uNPLknRAQg` (SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Talk Live Recap (Wahoo
  5.5.20)," 2020-05-06, San Diego long-range): asked the best time of year for
  wahoo, the answer is warmer water first and fall second — "late September,
  October, early November" — with Captain Mike Tahr's second-week-of-November
  2019 trip, the boat's last of that season, cited on camera as producing
  "tremendous catches." Some 7- and 8-day trips leave as early as July to be
  the first boat at Alijos Rocks or the Ridge when there is nothing to catch

## Presence & forage

- `4uNPLknRAQg` (SearcherSportfishing, 2020-05-06): "those fish move into the
  structure or find a kelp paddy to be on" as the water warms; Captain Mike's
  2019 wahoo trip fish were on kelp paddies; the bite is "a lot of times
  associated with structure and current."
- `J3FGJj5zYPE` (SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol 227 (Wahoo
  Lures)," 2023-09-14, filmed at Fisherman's Landing before the annual 8-day to
  Alijos Rocks): "Wahoo likes to target a variety of different fish as they're
  swimming through the rocks," so the colour that gets bit varies — pink one
  day, blue or green another; carry all three.

## Feeding triggers

- `4uNPLknRAQg` (SearcherSportfishing, 2020-05-06): "there's always a chance
  whenever you're in the right condition water, you know, 72° or warmer than
  that"; the stated seasonal mechanism is that the water warms incrementally
  through summer and hurricane season can push warm water up.

## Finding them (sign & sonar)

- `4uNPLknRAQg` (SearcherSportfishing, 2020-05-06): kelp paddies given as "another
  way of locating wahoo" — "they get gathered up on kelp patties," so troll
  around the paddy or in its vicinity. On the bomb cast, the crew calls the
  depth so anglers do not sink to the bottom and catch triggerfish or
  whitefish: in 90–100 ft, stay in the 60 ft-to-surface band.

## Situations → techniques

- `it_YYh_8Z-w` (SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol.197 (Trolling
  Lures for Wahoo)," 2022-09-22, Logan, Fisherman's Landing): two unspecified
  DTX Minnow sizes recommended for any long-range trip, called out at Alijos
  Rocks and "the Ridge," on the stated mechanism that the DTX runs deeper than
  most trolling lures.
- `9IhmYstB8sA` (SearcherSportfishing, 2019-11-14): names the Nomad DTX 200 for
  high-speed trolling plus a second plug whose brand is not resolvable from the
  captions — asr-uncertain(second plug brand), so it is not carried as a named
  product. Same tip gives the live-bait leader at 200 or 300 lb wire with small
  connector rings, a sardine, and "don't swing — wind through the bite."
- `4t_Z75shK_E` (StokedOnFishing, 2020-09-25, Magdalena Bay, dockside
  pre-trip): a lure-company owner/guide describes his own transit lure — a
  magnet holds the hook against the body so it "runs true" up to 20 kt, letting
  the boat pick up a wahoo "in the middle of nowhere" at transit speed. Model
  name not resolvable from the captions. Promotional claim: the owner
  describing his own product, capped at low confidence.
- `J3FGJj5zYPE` (SearcherSportfishing, 2023-09-14): the "on the slide" cast —
  when a trolling rod hooks up, cast off the opposite side of the boat, let it
  sink, then reel fast; "nine times out of ten you'll get picked up."
- `4uNPLknRAQg` (SearcherSportfishing, 2020-05-06): trolling speeds 7–8 kt, up
  to 10.5 kt once in water above 72 °F; Yo-Zuri Marauder and a large Rapala
  named alongside the Nomad; hooks sharpened out of the package because the jaw
  is bony; on the boat's slide after a hookup, free-spool the trolling lure for
  a 10–15 count to drop it back another 50–60 ft, then wind it in for a second
  chance.

## Gear summary

- `4uNPLknRAQg` (SearcherSportfishing, 2020-05-06): braid by application —
  trolling 100–135 lb, casting 80 lb, live bait 60–65 lb; mono top shots —
  40–60 lb for a casting lure, 40 lb for live bait (25 lb "if you're feeling
  sporty"), 80 lb for the trolling outfit; the top shot should be at least as
  long as your longest cast so no connection runs through the guides. Deck
  safety: closed-toed shoes, because loose wahoo end up lying on the deck.
- `uWGGeDOprsE` (SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Tips for Alijos Rocks and The
  Ridge," 2009-08-25 — same upload date as `_r08B4bItAY`): the rod/reel quiver
  and terminal tackle for the same 7-day Alijos Rocks/"the Ridge" trip type;
  carried in full in
  [Searcher five-outfit quiver](../../tackle/searcher-alijos-rocks-ridge-7-day-quiver.md).

## Doctrine & conflicts

- `jqyu3wZdNF4` (SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 21,"
  2018-11-08): the earliest-dated wahoo tackle tip from this channel in the KB.
  Wahoo often take only half a live bait; engaging the reel right after that
  first hit is said to end the bite outright — "you don't have a bite anymore"
  — with no mechanism stated for why. The fix is free spool immediately on the
  bite, leaving the half-eaten bait as a chunk; framed on camera as the secret
  to wahoo live-bait fishing.
- `vNIazq1aVwc` (SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 126 (Live Bait
  Wahoo Fishing)," 2020-11-19): the crew has "seen on multiple occasions" that
  you put the reel in gear to set on the bite and come up with nothing there,
  because the wahoo ate half the bait, leaving a chunk with the hook still in
  it; back to free spool and count to about 15, and "you'd be amazed how many
  times" the chunk is still on the hook when you wind in. Reactive trigger,
  where `jqyu3wZdNF4` is proactive.
- `4uNPLknRAQg` (SearcherSportfishing, 2020-05-06): the same eat-half mechanism
  framed as learned "through decades of fishing," with the fix at free spool
  and 10–15 seconds; the trigger is stated as the bait having gone dead after
  the bite rather than a failed hookset. Chronologically between the other two.
- `97clKtVsEOs` (StokedOnFishing, 2015-10-29, Alijos Rocks): one angler
  preferred trolling a bomb-style lure over a jig-and-reel presentation
  specifically for hookup ratio on the wahoo's back-half strike.
- `-62xDo4UQzo` (SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 85 (Wahoo
  lures)," 2020-01-30): the wahoo bomb and Raider jig are the two most-used
  cast lures for wahoo, fished on wire leader with a brief sink and a
  full-speed burn retrieve.
- `0zgmoOH3Qag` (SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 122 (Haywire
  twist)," 2020-10-22): names a "Catch"-brand jig as a cast alternative when
  not trolling, on a haywire-twisted wire leader.
- `JHMCguO7sXE` (SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 97 (Popper/
  Spinning reel)," 2020-04-30, Fred Hall Show, Penn booth): names poppers as
  good for wahoo alongside bluefin, yellowfin and yellowtail, with a generic
  popper leader of 2–2.5 ft of 100 lb fluorocarbon or mono, no wire and no
  wahoo-specific caveat. Reads as a generic multi-species popper tip that did
  not consider the teeth.
- `4uNPLknRAQg` (SearcherSportfishing, 2020-05-06): a customer named on camera
  as **Howard Fulmer** prefers heavy fluorocarbon over wire on wahoo lures,
  reasoning that with a full box he can risk losing one or two to a bite-off;
  the host's own stated preference is wire on everything — "wow, this lure is
  25 bucks... I'm putting a piece of wire on that" — but he frames Howard's
  approach as valid for someone willing to take the trade.
- `S80GRyuAbLY` (SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol 160 (Wahoo Wire
  Leaders)," 2021-11-04): a sleeve-crimped (not haywire-twisted) wahoo
  live-bait wire leader at 60 lb on a 1/0 or 2/0 hook.
- `_Z4yMtrYgeA` (SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 123 (Wahoo
  Leader)," 2020-10-29): duplicate-of `S80GRyuAbLY` — the same demonstration,
  aired a year earlier; re-cut footage, not an independent confirmation of the
  60 lb figure.
- `G81HN0dIDg0` (SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol.193 (Wahoo
  Lures 101)," 2022-08-11, Taylor): 44 lb wire to a 3/0 hook for flylining
  wahoo — roughly a fourth to a seventh of the 200–300 lb figure from
  `9IhmYstB8sA`, with no stated reason for the gap.

## Landing & handling

- `4uNPLknRAQg` (SearcherSportfishing, 2020-05-06): let a wound-in bomb,
  casting lure or trolling plug settle and count four or five before lifting it
  out of the water — a following wahoo can come aboard with it, and "it's
  happened many many times." Gaffed fish are announced, the deck cleared, and a
  separate crew member neutralizes the fish with a bat (sometimes two or three
  hits) before hooks are removed; anglers are told not to pull their own hooks
  or reach into the fish box, because a flopping wahoo causes "pretty bad
  laceration." Full procedure carried in
  [wahoo handling & deck safety](../../fish-care/wahoo-handling.md).
