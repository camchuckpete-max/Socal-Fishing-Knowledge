## Where & when

- `OYOda6T3f-8` (BDOutdoors — Erik Landesfeind, Bight report, 2022-10-20,
  SoCal): with fall fragmentation under way, the warmest water on the coast ran
  from the West End of Clemente through Mackerel Bank down to the 181/182
  Ridge, "and that's where all that yellow fin is"; the bigger fish, 50 lb
  class, were coming off the front side of Clemente. Method stated as find the
  warmest water you can, then cross it against the chlorophyll chart for the
  cleanest water.
- `5to3Q5P7w90` (BDOutdoors, Bight report, 2022-09-14, SoCal): in mid-September
  the bulk of the yellowfin was still down toward the border out of San Diego,
  with sportboats farther north just starting to pick some up — attributed on
  camera to the warmer water sitting south and working up the coast.
- `CMQkHQMxbXM` (BDOutdoors, Bight report, 2022-08-03, SoCal): a private-boat
  day out of Mackerel Bank → the 289 → the 14 that found yellowfin and 60–80 lb
  bluefin in two areas; small spots of yellowfin boiling at the 14 refused
  surface lures before wind chop shut down spotting.

## Presence & forage

- `VgpgJ8IAyJo` (BDOutdoors — Ali Hussainy/Local Knowledge, 2020-06-08):
  a Riptide 105 Longcast is thrown at bluefin busting on "little tiny
  anchovies" and "also works great on yellowfin that are feeding on the same
  bait" — the corpus's one explicit yellowfin forage species, named from what
  the lure matches rather than from a stomach.
- `ouBrIdO7d4k` (SD Fish and Sips, Pacific Coast Sportfishing Show, 2025-03-08,
  booth interview): a travelling dolphin pod scatters the bait balls it drives,
  and the yellowfin school is hunting those scattered balls rather than riding
  the pod — "they're not always right on them, they're out on the flanks, out
  in front of them," with terns working 50 yd ahead of the pod. Approach detail
  (7–9 mph, stay outside the pod, never drive through it, small double-hook
  tungsten-head trolling lure on 60–80 lb) is carried in
  [trolling](../../techniques/trolling.md#socal-backside-of-catalina--porpoise-associated-yellowfin-finesse-trolling).
- `Fq4aRI3YrKE` (StokedOnFishing, "Stoked on Long-Range Fishing, part 2,"
  2015-01-18, Baja "lower banks"): a large bait ball sitting directly under the
  boat, with fish blowing up all around on the surface, was the crew's stated
  reason for holding position rather than moving grounds. How the ball was
  detected — sonar or surface sign — not specified on camera.

## Feeding triggers

- `lxFNVdDhMy4` (BDOutdoors, "How to Fish on a Charter Boat," 2019-11-11,
  sportboat): the captain called the yellowfin in 40 ft and the deckhand
  chummed heavily to draw the school up into hook range; "like two minutes of
  chumming" and the side scan filled with fish. Same clip: anglers fish the side
  the drift is carrying toward, wind in the face, because the other rail is the
  long side of the boat.
- `qv0QbLgp72o` (Your Saltwater Guide — Dave Hansen, 2019-12-23): brailing a
  scoop of small bait at the corner had yellowfin and skipjack "going absolutely
  bananas," with the stated mechanism that small chum bait "changes the prey
  density level in the water" and makes the fish react. Stated preference is big
  bait on the hook, small bait for chum. `dave-hansen` registered.
- `6zYRI1ZQU3c` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2019-12-26, San Diego): a chum line
  pulled a school into a full-on bite at the boat, with free-swimming yellowfin
  charging right up alongside the hull in the frenzy — an on-the-water example
  of the chum-pulls-fish-to-the-surface effect.
- `fri_BWI-VA0` (Your Saltwater Guide — Dave Hansen, "Moon Phases and how It
  affects your fishing," 2022-02-12): albacore, bluefin and yellowfin have been
  seen to move 20 mi after a full-moon night, with the stated mechanism that a
  bright moon gives schooling fish the confidence to travel and feed at night;
  the day after, they bite poorly first thing and better late. Advice given is
  not to schedule a once-or-twice-a-year trip on a full moon unless you can fish
  late. `dave-hansen` registered.

## Finding them (sign & sonar)

- `SgF5hRlEGqU` (Your Saltwater Guide — Dave Hansen, 2022-02-27): with several
  dolphin pods around, pick the one carrying its own bird marker — even a single
  bird sitting on a pod is the tell that that group is holding fish. Set the
  sounder range manually rather than auto for these shallow marks in deep open
  water. `dave-hansen` registered, single mention.
- `fK2AT460xW4` (Your Saltwater Guide — Dave Hansen, "How to Catch Tuna | Live
  Bait Rig FOR Sub Surface to DEEP," 2021-04-27): there is no "porpoise"
  carrying tuna anywhere in the ocean — the animals are dolphin, and the two
  SoCal-relevant types are common dolphin and spotted spinner dolphin. Yellowfin
  suspend underneath these travelling schools and mostly do not surface-swim
  with the pod, which is why the school can look empty from above while holding
  fish. `dave-hansen` registered, single mention of this claim.
- `H-vIGWPIPVc` (StokedOnFishing — presenter Shay, "Yellowfin Tuna vs Bonita,"
  2019-10-30, San Clemente Island): spotted a large dolphin school offshore of
  the island and ran up to troll around it with a deep-diving hardbait
  (Nomad-class, sardine colour) and a
  [cedar plug](../../lures/cedar-plug.md); the deep diver got bit and the boat
  landed a yellowfin of about 60 lb — the troll-to-locate pattern triggered by a
  dolphin school rather than a paddy or bird sign. Unregistered channel.
- `3T4c3Zez_DM` (StokedOnFishing, "Top Gun 80 Epic 5 Day, part 2," 2017-05-09,
  offshore San Clemente Island, 5-day charter): after the morning bluefin bite
  eased the boat ran offshore, and working birds plus surface boils marked a
  yellowfin school — "they're boiling... they're chowing... birds are here" —
  graded around 30 lb. Bait type not stated for this catch; the video's earlier
  squid mention belongs to the separate morning bluefin session. This is the
  primary footage that `tU4jhAkdzNw` recaps secondhand. Unregistered channel.
- `tU4jhAkdzNw` (StokedOnFishing, "Top Gun 80 Epic 5 Day, part 3," 2017-05-11,
  SoCal islands, exact zone not named on camera; crew credited as Stan
  Vanderburgh): the filmed afternoon picked about 50 yellowfin with the bite

## Situations → techniques

- `8M4QhL-Qb7E` (BDOutdoors — Jordan Jennings, "Yellowfin Tuna Tricks & Tips,"
  2021-08-16, San Diego, private boat): the whole private-boat live-bait
  programme — a two-scoop bait maximum against the sportboat's bottomless chum;
  a Gamakatsu Nautilus 2/0 circle as the default, matched to healthy sardines;
  scaling the 30 lb setup down to a 20 or 15 lb leader with a size 1 thin-wire
  Nautilus circle when a chummed school rolls on freebies but will not eat the
  hook bait, then working back up to 25 and 30; the bait-net trick, learned from
  a friend on a 20–25 lb school that would not eat — dip three to five baits,
  nose-hook one, and throw the netful with the hook bait mixed in so it reads as
  "a little mini bait ball"; and the slow troll — nose-hooked bait, one engine
  just in gear, bait well back off the props, clicker set just heavy enough that
  the bait cannot pull line by itself, then free spool a 10-count on the bite
  before going to strike and winding tight on the circle.
- `5Oh3IPIC5g4` (Chad, HookUp Baits co-owner, Fred Hall 2020 seminar): with
  skipjack blanketing the surface over yellowfin holding below, a fast-sinking
  1 oz swimbait worked quickly through the skipjack layer beats live bait,
  because live bait gets intercepted by the skipjack before it reaches the
  yellowfin. Promotional seminar source, low confidence.
- `TU5quAG4atM` (SearcherSportfishing — Steve Carson, 2024-06-13): the daytime
  dart jig for fish around the boat that will not bite well, keyed on small
  bait — sink a light castable jig and crank hard the instant it stops, on a
  Penn Fathom 30-class with 65 lb braid, 50 lb mono top shot and an 80 lb fluoro
  leader. Named on camera for both bluefin and yellowfin. Single mention.
- `BQ2U1PqxWi8` (StokedOnFishing, 2020-04-01, East Cape): the boat opened the
  day slow-trolling large live scad mackerel (called "cab macs" on camera before
  being corrected to "scad mackerel"), with strikes on these big baits described
  as "vicious." A flat-fall jig worked with an active "ripping" retrieve
  vertically off the side while the boat continued its slow troll also produced
  a hookup on a 15–20 lb-class fish — a second presentation run alongside the
  trolled live bait in the same drift. Grade ran from an estimated 15–20 lb
  early to fish called "forties, fifties" later. An on-camera line about a
  circle-hook requirement was too garbled to extract and is not carried.
- `YUdbrIm9vrE` (StokedOnFishing, "Cedros Island Goes Off," 2024-03-20 upload,
  October trip with the year not stated on camera, offshore Cedros Island, Baja,
  Cedros Outdoor Adventures charter): trolling roughly 20–30 mi offshore, the
  bite came fast and multiple once it turned on — a double hookup struck just
  behind the stern, and a separate stretch put three fish in the well within
  about 10 seconds ("I didn't even have 10 seconds to even get the camera on").
  Spread and lure detail (Rapala-class trolled hardbaits, colour-matched
  doubles, a 60 lb line class) is in
  [trolling — Cedros Island offshore troll](../../techniques/trolling.md).
  Unregistered channel.

- `CMQkHQMxbXM` (BDOutdoors — Erik Landesfeind, Bight report, 2022-08-03): "it's

  situation. Unregistered channel.
