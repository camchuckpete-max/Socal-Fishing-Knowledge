## How yellowtail changes the execution

- `0HwYl0HO2tc` (HookUp Baits designer seminar, 2018): the surface program —
  cast beyond a visible push or boil, rod tip low, crank-pop-crank-pop — with
  the stated gear mechanism that a spinning reel pops the tip and takes up slack
  in one motion where baitcast anglers fall into a choppier pop-pop-pop-crank.
  Sponsor/promotional context: the speaker designs the bait.
- `0HwYl0HO2tc` (HookUp Baits designer seminar, 2018; Cedros Island, Baja, on a
  group trip): the half-yo-yo cadence — bait to bottom, retrieve about halfway,
  bail open and fall back to bottom, then retrieve the rest of the way, at
  roughly half the speed of a bare-iron yo-yo — demonstrated into a yellowtail
  push moving through. A single self-reported illustration from the product's
  own designer, not independently corroborated in this corpus. The trip's other
  Cedros observations are in
  [Cedros Island evidence](../../locations/evidence/cedros-island.md).
- `5Oh3IPIC5g4` (HookUp Baits co-owner seminar, 2020): the corroborating
  pop-retrieve, called "absolutely deadly" on yellowtail after 30 years fishing
  them — cast, roughly a 10 count of sink, then a medium-speed crank rod-tip-down
  with the rod tucked under the arm, popping the tip through the retrieve ("bop
  bop") so the bait kicks left-right at about the speed of a swimming baitfish
  rather than a fast burn. Independent of `0HwYl0HO2tc` as a speaker but not as
  a commercial interest — both principals of the same bait company.
- `5Oh3IPIC5g4` (HookUp Baits co-owner seminar, 2020; Coronado Islands):
  repeated follows with no takes on the standard 1–1.5 oz "Big Game"/"Bullet"
  bait, converted to immediate committed eats as soon as the crew switched to a
  smaller-profile 5/8 oz bait on lighter gear. The same profile-versus-distance
  trade is documented independently for
  [surface iron](../../techniques/surface-iron.md), which is what lifts this
  above a single-source product claim.
- `5Oh3IPIC5g4` (HookUp Baits co-owner seminar, 2020): the 1–1.5 oz bullet
  trolled from a private boat, described as tracking straight and true with a
  tight little wiggle at roughly 3–4 mph, called "the magic speed" for locating
  yellowtail before switching to casting; stated alongside a roughly 1 mph
  slow-trolled live sardine and a roughly 5 mph faster trolled lure —
  asr-uncertain (the faster lure is not confidently identified in the captions).
  The single-hookup workflow (skip the double dropper rig, keep the other rod
  rigged and in hand so it can be fired straight back out) comes from the same
  passage.
