# Evidence — California Barracuda

Trip reports and per-source provenance backing
[California barracuda](../barracuda.md). One line per observation, grouped
under the parent note's section headings, in its order. Observations never
change the parent's stated doctrine; a contradicting one is also traced beside
that doctrine in the parent.

## Where & when

### SoCal coast and islands

- `S2L3KLSQ6Is` (BDOutdoors, 2022-05-26, Catalina Island): weekly Bight report
  — a few barracuda alongside yellowtail, bonito and bass for anglers fishing
  surface presentations; the earliest-dated SoCal barracuda mention in this
  note's corpus.
- `DnSqw4r7A1s` (BDOutdoors, 2022-07-14, Channel Islands / Santa Monica Bay /
  open beach): island anglers getting calicos and "nice barracuda"; along the
  beach barracuda, calicos and sand bass together; still barracuda up in Santa
  Monica Bay; and "nice bass, barracuda when they're anchored up" at a
  separate zone in the same report.
- `Blh2BA-7Ono` (BDOutdoors, 2022-07-21, San Clemente Island front side, per a
  Thunderbird captain's report): the front-side yellowtail bite is "a morning
  thing" that "kind of slows down, becomes a pick-o day, bass, barracuda stuff
  like that in the mix of bonita" — get there early if yellowtail is the
  target; the same report frames a Channel Islands trip as a plan to catch
  "bass, barracuda, maybe rockfish" rather than expecting white seabass, and
  notes a 50 lb yellowtail on a sport boat getting yellowtail, bass, bonito
  and barracuda.
- `CMQkHQMxbXM` (BDOutdoors, 2022-08-03, Santa Cruz and Anacapa islands):
  "still some barracuda caught" at the north Channel Islands, with the report
  cautioning the zone was not doable that week except aboard a boat.
- `-JZpyWLdKlk` (BDOutdoors, 2022-08-11, Channel Islands): slow fishing early
  in the week produced barracuda and occasional yellowtail; heading into the
  full moon the report listed rockfish, "a few barracudas," and white seabass
  that "might bite a couple more days on this moon" — the moon claim is
  attached to the seabass, not to the barracuda.
- `0KQ--N5TjqE` (BDOutdoors / SoCal Bight Fishing Academy Ep. 14, 2023-10-06,
  Palos Verdes kelp — spots named on camera as the outside kelp "below Rocky,"
  "shark fin," and "Flat Rock," fished out of Long Beach in warm Santa Ana
  conditions on Thursday 2023-10-05): while patterning calico bass on the
  leading outside edge of laid-down kelp, one angler caught a barracuda and
  the boat then left the area — the stated reason for moving was generic
  conditions and no remaining bait, not the barracuda.
- `Klfb433I3Uk` (StokedOnFishing, 2019-10-04, San Clemente Island): barracuda
  caught on a yellowtail charter, called on camera before the fish was
  identified; unregistered channel.
- `M6U_FVdosr4` (StokedOnFishing, 2013-07-04, Dana Wharf kids-club trip, Dana
  Point kelp): "barracuda are coming in" mid-session, and the day's wrap named
  "some short barracuda flopping around in the kelp" plus bass — the fish were
  "actually biting the smaller sardines today, which was a surprise";
  unregistered channel. The word "short" is the note's only trace of a legal
  minimum size; no release and no gauge is stated on camera.
- `6-8KfjEg0x8` (BDOutdoors, 2026-07-23, Channel Islands): a live-squid
  variety day framed around yellowtail, white seabass and halibut as the main
  goals with "a lot of bass, barracuda, bonito, the three B's around," closing
  on shallow rockfish and lingcod.
- `HMdrP4-i9MM` (StokedOnFishing, 2022-10-11, SoCal coast): barracuda named
  among fish "zipping around" a down-coast stretch alongside small fish and
  yellowtail; no barracuda catch shown; unregistered channel.
- `oadK6zIYyCo` (StokedOnFishing, 2016-12-23 upload, Catalina Island, a family
  day at anchor; sponsor-heavy dealership-ad episode): a barracuda came on a
  **surface iron** — an old Tady 45 ("chatty 45" in captions) let sink to
  roughly **30 ft** before the retrieve; the full sink-it-out technique
  detail, including the on-camera generalization of the tip and its
  cross-reference to Cedros, is logged in
  [surface iron](../../techniques/surface-iron.md#retrieve--cadence);
  unregistered channel. The corpus's only dated SoCal (non-Baja) barracuda
  catch on a named lure.

### Baja Pacific

- `qri15R3caYE` (StokedOnFishing, 2018-02-01 upload, La Bocana, Baja
  California Sur, Pacific side; "Stoked On La Bocana," part 1): fishing rock
  structure a few miles from a beachfront co-op fishing camp, barracuda hit
  repeatedly on a dropped jig/plastic fished to roughly **80 ft**, described
  on camera as producing a hookup "every single drop"; the lure name is
  garbled beyond confident recovery in the source captions and is not carried
  — asr-uncertain(lure name). Barracuda was also named on camera as one of the
  trip's mixed-bag species alongside calico bass, yellowtail, sand bass and
  bonito (see the [calico bass](../calico-bass.md) La Bocana entry);
  unregistered channel.
- `Cobp85UvHmM` (StokedOnFishing, 2018-02-07 upload, La Bocana, Baja
  California Sur; part 2 of the same series): running offshore, the crew
  narrated leaving "the Barracuda grounds" for "some tuna grounds" — the
  inshore band is named as barracuda water distinct from the offshore tuna
  water; later, working inshore, barracuda is listed with bonito and sierra as
  the fish you get "past" on the way to a nice yellowtail; unregistered
  channel.
- `CKq0Z6ExVs4` (StokedOnFishing, 2017-11-25 upload, a high spot roughly
  **7–8 mi** from the Hotel Coral marina, Ensenada, Baja California; "Navico
  Weekend Getaway"): barracuda was named on camera, alongside bottomfish and
  yellowtail, as one of the species the crew expected from this high spot
  (described as coming up to about **25 ft from roughly 200 ft** — see the
  [rockfish & lingcod](../rockfish-lingcod.md) Ensenada entry for the
  bottomfish actually landed there); no barracuda was shown caught, so this is
  a named-target data point, not a confirmed catch; unregistered channel.
- `0dIwWiOc1NY` (StokedOnFishing, 2026-02-17 upload, Ensenada, Baja
  California): a local crew named "barracudas, bonitas, yellowtails" as the
  day's catch expectation alongside tuna; unregistered channel.

### Out of region — not carried as doctrine

- `HC64Tzc_aTo` (Benjie Kim Fishing, 2026-02-11 upload, Bahía de los Ángeles,
  Sea of Cortez, shore/beach session): repeated shore-caught barracuda — one
  angler counted a seventh barracuda that day — with fish given to a local;
  Sea-of-Cortez barracuda are not confirmed as the same species as California
  barracuda, and the parent note's region gate excludes `cortez-north`, so
  this is logged, not carried.
- `ikiXx9sUsoA` (SD Fish and Sips, 2022-11-30 upload, Loreto, Baja California
  Sur, Sea of Cortez, November 2022): a trolled fish was called as a barracuda
  from the fight — "not feeling the yellowtail head shakes" and "it looks kind
  of long" — a fight-feel identification data point, out of region for this
  note.
- `M8hOYQ_6rSg` (Dirty Hookers, 2025-05-20 upload, Sea of Cortez): barracuda
  used as a **live bait** for grouper, rigged with trap hooks toward the
  middle or tail of the body because a grouper of 100 lb or less grabs the
  tail of a long bait rather than swallowing it whole — barracuda as forage
  rather than target, out of region.

## Presence & forage

- `YVHdDbkQrKk` (Your Saltwater Guide / Capt. Dave Hansen, 2022-02-21) and
  `w5_x6kkN-xE` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2021-04-06, same coaching restated):
  at San Clemente Island, Catalina or the Coronados, fish a large live
  mackerel when you want straight yellowtail and do not want calico bass,
  barracuda or bonito — the stated mechanism is that the big bait keeps the
  smaller fish off it, with "I've seen yellowtail eat one and a half pound
  mackerel, no problem" as the size reference; `w5_x6kkN-xE` is duplicate-of
  the same coaching in
  `YVHdDbkQrKk`, kept as a separate line.
- `gKrYKvqHUjk` (Your Saltwater Guide / Capt. Dave Hansen, 2020-12-22):
  fly-lining named as the presentation for "tuna, bass, barracuda, bonita" —
  a species-list mention only, no barracuda-specific parameter.
- `AH4Eiya1Hn0` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2024-03-09, PCS Show seminar, SoCal):
  after lobster season closes, the stated season plan is white seabass and
  yellowtail plus "the fun fish to catch — calico bass, barracuda, bonito, the
  actual icon fish that we have here" — barracuda as a named seasonal target,
  not a technique claim.

## Feeding triggers

- `41d5bquXkAc` (Tackle Express, 2022-06-01, location not stated): short clip
  titled for the behaviour — a surface jig dropped by one barracuda is eaten
  by another immediately, with a fish landed on camera; the corpus's only
  direct observation of intra-school competition on the lure.
- `pcwcRdmWmLc` (BDOutdoors, 2025-02-28 upload, Bight report of 2025-02-27):
  in a surface-iron segment, yellowtail are "the kings of having their nose on
  your jig all the way back to the boat and not biting it," and "barracuda as
  well — they're just not interested, they're curious, but you haven't
  triggered that instinctual reaction to eat that bait fish."

## Situations → techniques

- `PrdPJy26H8c` (Dirty Hookers, 2019-03-05, SoCal): "the only time I'll throw
  chrome is either for barracuda or tuna"; the same segment recounts an 18 lb
  yellowtail caught while fishing for barracuda and initially mistaken for a
  barracuda dogged by a sea lion.
- `YAKOv9bXKO0` (BDOutdoors, 2026-02-05, long-range report, yo-yo segment):
  the problem with chrome lures anywhere bonito or barracuda are present is
  that "they're a little faster, they're a little quicker than the yellowtail,
  so it's not that the yellowtail won't bite these, it's that the barracuda
  and the bonito may get them first" — the mechanism behind chrome being a
  barracuda colour; the same segment separately covers a Colonet bloom.
- `ejUTAnONf7o` (Strictly Irons, 2026-04-21, SoCal): on-the-water surface-iron
  coaching aimed at a barracuda — stop the cast, start reeling, keep the rod
  tip up, "you want that surface lure to be gliding on the surface... keep
  going a little faster, get it up there where you can see it"; the episode is
  otherwise a yellowtail surface-iron test.
- `lV-UcWUvxgc` (Benjie Kim Fishing, 2025-08-12, SoCal sport-boat tutorial):
  "there are times where a slower presentation jig will get you bit, say, on a
  barracuda or a calico, whereas with a yellowtail you might want a faster
  swimming jig" — jig swim speed as a species-selection variable; the same
  passage notes a slower jig still swims when the current is ripping.
- `hjZ3hgq0Bzg` (Tackle Express, 2025-04-28) and `Oi5n-uVpY9g` (Tackle
  Express, 2025-05-02, same white-fluke material restated): white flukes in an
  ElaZtech-type compound ("elasc" in the captions — asr-uncertain(compound
  name)) are tougher and still "get torn up by barracuda," recommended when
  you expect to tear baits up; `Oi5n-uVpY9g` is duplicate-of the
  `hjZ3hgq0Bzg` passage, kept as a separate line.
- `28IttH7P7K0` (Fish the Legend San Diego Sportfishing, 2026-07-13): asked
  single vs treble on irons — "for yellowtail fishing, bass, barracuda and
  bonito, all that kind of stuff, leave it the way it is, treble hook," with
  the exception stated in the same breath: "the time I really switch to the
  single hook is like when there's a whole lot of barracuda and I'm just
  shaking them off real quick."
- `kt3G72gUldM` (Tackle Express, 2026-07-16, stick baits): run one single
  assist hook in the back rather than the factory treble — "you're not really
  worried about losing the fish you want, you're worried about when you're
  going to catch 40 barracuda on this thing," and being able to shake the rod
  and "have half of them come off" is easier on the crew.
- `-nIhadZwxAA` (CustomRodandReel seminar with Benny Ortiz, 2025-05-20,
  slow-pitch jigging): "I don't use wire on a leader, I don't use wire as a
  core for my assist cord — I would much rather lose an assist hook for the
  shark and barracuda that has come at me"; and "if there's a whole bunch of
  toothy stuff out there, move your boat, get away... I'd rather do that than
  lose a bunch of jigs to barracudas and kingfish."
- `lm7D9Tlc7Po` (BDOutdoors, Capt. Scott Goodwin, 2020-02-03): rigging for
  "toothy critters like wahoo and kingfish and barracuda — anything that has
  teeth that can cut a regular monofilament leader even if it's heavy" using a
  haywire twist on single-strand wire.

## Gear summary

- `42A8Owhc8fw` (Dirty Hookers, 2023-08-02, SoCal sport boat, tackle-bag
  walkthrough): the surface-iron outfit described as the "yellowtail rod,
  barracuda rod, bonito rod" is a Phenix Axis 1009 HJ, **10 ft, rated
  30–60 lb**, paired with a Trinidad 16A, brought because counts showed good
  numbers of calico bass and barracuda plus bonito and hit-or-miss yellowtail;
  the plastics/jerkbait rod is a Phenix M1 7'11" medium-heavy rated
  **15–35 lb**, chosen as one universal rod — "light enough to be fun on
  smaller fish and just heavy enough to still handle some of the bigger fish
  if there's bigger barracuda or bigger bass" — doubling as a sliding-sinker,
  straight-flyline or dropper-loop outfit; rod count was deliberately limited
  out of courtesy for rod-holder space on a crowded boat.
- `WE643Fue1_A` (Dirty Hookers, 2022-09-14 upload, Cedros Island tackle
  breakdown): the Murder Weapon Viper is a newer swimming iron the angler had
  "used for barracuda already — swims pretty good," being taken to Cedros as a
  first try on yellowtail; the barracuda use is the corroborated part.
- `xgPEt4Zj35Q` (Tackle Express, 2026-07-14, sport-boat lure rigging): replace
  the factory treble with a single inline hook (an Owner inline single named on
  camera) because "you get this lure and it's got the treble hook right in the
  barracuda's snout, they put that barracuda on the deck and now that thing's
  thrashing around and your assist hook's getting in everybody's boots."

## Landing & handling

- `5to3Q5P7w90` (BDOutdoors, 2022-09-15 upload, Bight report of 2022-09-14,
  fish-care segment): a sport-boat dorado brought to work in a gunny sack came
  out "a floppy mushy mess" with the meat falling out when cut, described by
  reference to "the barracuda days — like a barracuda's been sitting in the
  sun all day"; barracuda-in-the-sun is used as the audience's shared
  reference point for spoiled flesh.
