## How calico bass changes the execution

- `lP6cg4eEU6s` (Fisher Newb, 2020-03-06, Sav-On Tackle / IROD seminar): the
  boiler-bite program this page is built from, given in a seminar Q&A — "I
  really like the Lucky Craft 119" for "a big boiler calico situation, less
  kelp, more on the open side of the water," followed by "like a 5 inch, 9 inch
  trailer"; the clarity read, "with the boiler calicos I just basically look
  for water clarity … where there's less movement you're gonna have
  crystal-clear water" against "normal seafoam" green colour "clouded up a
  little bit" where "the bait would get lost," with the clarity line named as
  the water he wants to work; the profile order, baitfish/anchovy first, then
  "crab" and crustacean-style, then "something shiny"; and the target-selection
  rule, "I don't want to sit there … and convince a bass to eat … I'm looking
  for the fish that is locked, loaded and ready to go and I swim my lure past
  it." The July–August size-up and speed-up ("their metabolism also speeds up …
  they're going to want to eat more, the bait is bigger … don't be scared to
  fish fast") is stated for warm-water bass in the same session and is carried
  here as the seasonal rule for this program. Unregistered channel;
  single-camera seminar with speakers not diarized, the speaker addressed only
  as "Jeremy" in the Q&A — medium confidence. The "Lucky Craft 119" model
  number is as heard on camera and is not independently confirmed. The same
  seminar's scent doctrine, its crankbait depth-rating reasoning and its
  night-fishing colour material stay on their own notes — see
  [swimbaits — scent](../../techniques/swimbaits.md#scent),
  [inshore crankbaits](../../techniques/inshore-crankbaits.md) and
  [night bass fishing](../../techniques/night-bass-fishing.md).
