# Evidence — Snook (Robalo)

Per-source provenance backing [snook](../snook.md). One line per observation,
grouped under the parent note's section headings, in its order. Observations
never change the parent's stated doctrine; a contradicting one is also traced
beside that doctrine in the parent.

**Confidence note (demoted from the parent's prose, 2026-08-27):** the note is
`confidence: medium` and the whole species rests on one fishery and one channel
family. The doctrine spine is a single 2024 Bart Hall Show seminar
(`EcQK5elTPJE`) delivered by Capt. Dave Hansen (Your Saltwater Guide,
`dave-hansen` registered) and Larry Hansen (PSA, unregistered — an unregistered
voice caps at medium regardless); the mangrove execution detail comes from four
short Your Saltwater Guide clips off one 2022 Lopez Mateos trip; the two
StokedOnFishing episodes are an unregistered channel and sponsor-heavy. No
source in the corpus is about snook as a fishery in its own right.

## Where & when

- `EcQK5elTPJE` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2024-01-27, Bart Hall Show stage,
  Lopez Mateos / Magdalena Bay): Lopez Mateos pitched as one of two places in
  the world where a trailer boat can land a 40 lb snook and a big offshore fish
  the same day; the boca called a world-class grouper and snook fishery about
  five minutes from the dock in 40–50 ft. asr-uncertain: the second species in
  the "40 lb snook and an 80 lb ___" pairing is unrecoverable from the captions.
  Promotional context — the seminar is selling a lodge operation at Lopez
  Mateos.
- `EcQK5elTPJE`: the best snook of one day weighed 42 lb, offered to the room as
  a figure nobody present could match; snook "over 20 lb" named as the draw.
- `EcQK5elTPJE`: the bay stated to have no time of year that it does not bite —
  inside grouper fishing "phenomenal" in winter, the offshore species showing in
  summer, and the mangroves fishable when it blows outside.
- `frX09YMQxKE` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2022-01-28, Lopez Mateos mangroves):
  presenter free-dove the slot he had been casting to and reported little
  pargos, little groupers and "some bigger snook" holding in it; the slot then
  produced repeat bites on flylined bait. Three-minute clip, no gear spec.
- `FxgLol_IHa0` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2022-02-22, Lopez Mateos mangroves): a
  5 lb snook netted immediately after the anchor came tight, called perfect
  eating size.

## Presence & forage

- `dlxA22FVNGc` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2022-01-23, Lopez Mateos mangroves): live
  sardines chummed at the root edge put snook and groupers boiling behind a dead
  tree before the first cast; casts landing in 12–18 in of water, sometimes
  less.
- `-5kooyIyavs` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2022-02-24, Lopez Mateos mangroves): one
  small cove named as having produced snook, corvina, pargo, triggerfish and
  baby halibut; "those fish will not come out of those branches" given as the
  reason placement decides the day.
- `vyX5FGoDH0A` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2022-01-26, Lopez Mateos mangroves):
  wide-open snook session; a fish first called a grouper corrected on camera to
  a spotted bay bass, with the crew noting they had come to Lopez Mateos to
  catch one. Also: repeated hook losses to fish taken into the bushes, down to
  the last box of hooks with a day still to fish.
- `FxgLol_IHa0`: bait was Monterey sardines throw-netted by the guide (Ruben, a
  Lopez Mateos boat owner) on the run up to the spot; a Monterey sardine put in
  by the mangroves described as a near-instant bite.
- `EcQK5elTPJE`: mangrove system forage and shellfish named as sardines, mullet,
  clams, oysters and scallops; boca bycatch grouper to 100 lb, species never
  named.

## Feeding triggers

- `dlxA22FVNGc`: the trip arrived at peak high and fished the ebb; tidal
  direction called the make-or-break variable, with the flood from low toward
  high named as the poor window because the root zone has no water in it yet.
  Drifting leaves and debris used on camera as the flow indicator.
- `-5kooyIyavs`: on a hard outgoing tide in the channel a well-placed bait was
  in the strike zone for about a second before the current swept it out, so the
  crew wound in and recast continuously rather than soaking.
- `dlxA22FVNGc`: as the ebb built, the outflow water turned brown and loaded
  with leaves and branches; the presenter's read was that the fish sit in that
  stained water and ambush bait coming through the cove.

## Finding them (sign & sonar)

- `FxgLol_IHa0`: the crew hit a series of small mangrove spots before settling
  on the one where snook were biting, then anchored on it; the anchor was laid
  against a known outgoing tide so the boat swung with its starboard side along
  the mangrove edge, and the first cast after the anchor came tight was bit.
- `dlxA22FVNGc`: the repeatedly productive target described as the slot where
  the dead mangroves meet the live mangroves, and the pocket at the head of a
  small cove — a spot "most people would just pass right by".

## Situations → techniques

- `OVwqUKim9Pc` (StokedOnFishing, 2020-10-14, Magdalena Bay estuary): a snook
  taken on a white bucktail jig — the corpus's only record of a snook on a lure.
  The presentation, outfit and per-source provenance moved to
  [bucktail jigs evidence](../../lures/evidence/bucktail-jigs.md) with the lure
  spec (relocation, 2026-08-29).
- `isXJONlpUP4` (StokedOnFishing, 2020-10-07, Magdalena Bay estuary with the
  local guide Enrique): trolling small lures along the mangrove edge in 25–30 ft
  of channel; a heavy fish lost into the mangroves was guessed as "probably
  snook or more bigger than the palometa" and never seen. The day's landed fish
  were palometa and pargo, not snook. Unregistered channel; sponsor-heavy.
- `dlxA22FVNGc`: a small level-wind conventional (an Okuma Komodo 300-series
  named) given as the presenter's reel, with a light spinning outfit offered as
  the fallback for anyone who cannot cast the conventional accurately. Product
  naming is incidental, not a sponsored placement.
- `-5kooyIyavs`: the mangrove terminal build stated as a thin-wire Mustad hook
  tied to 35 lb fluorocarbon tied to 60 lb braid, nothing else in line, and "if
  you can put the bait in the spot you can get a bite almost every single cast".

## Doctrine & conflicts

- `EcQK5elTPJE`: the heavy mangrove build given from the stage as 65 lb braid,
  80 lb fluorocarbon, a 4/0 thin-wire hook, a 9 ft rod and a star drag as tight
  as it goes, with the reasoning that the roots are solid oysters and a fish
  that takes an inch of line busts off on them; an off-root cast gets "some kind
  of weird fish", not the target.
- `dlxA22FVNGc`: the stealth mangrove build given on the water as a size 4
  thin-wire bronze Mustad hook on 25 lb fluorocarbon "to make sure the fish
  can't see the line, can't see the hook", in the same paragraph as the claim
  that anything under 35 lb has zero chance of coming out of the bushes once
  snagged. asr-uncertain(25 vs 35 lb fluorocarbon) — backs the
  `⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-internal)` flag in the parent.
- `EcQK5elTPJE`: the break-off progression told as a personal correction — the
  first six snook hooked in that fishery all snapped off, and the leader was
  stepped 60 → 80 → 100 → 130 lb before fish were landed.

## Landing & handling

- `EcQK5elTPJE`: the boca strike read given as the species tell — a grouper
  dives for the rocks and snaps you off, a snook comes up toward the boat and
  takes drag against the locked-down setting, and the fight is decided in about
  five seconds. Pictures of 4X hooks straightened by these fish were referenced
  on stage but not shown in the captions. asr-uncertain(the snook mouth-abrasion
  mechanism) — the surrounding captions are badly garbled.
- `isXJONlpUP4`: the local guide's read inside the estuary was the opposite —
  a hooked fish "normally is going to [run] straight to the mangroves" — given
  while explaining a fish lost to the roots.
- `dlxA22FVNGc`, `FxgLol_IHa0`: snook netted rather than gaffed on both clips,
  with an on-camera aside that the net is lifted straight up rather than swung
  through the fish "like you're scooping ice cream" — general netting technique,
  not snook doctrine, so it is not carried in the parent.
- `OVwqUKim9Pc`: the estuary snook kept and taken home, "very good to eat".
