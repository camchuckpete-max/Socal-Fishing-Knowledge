# Evidence — Skipjack Tuna

Per-source provenance backing [skipjack tuna](../skipjack-tuna.md). One line per
observation, grouped under the parent note's section headings, in its order.
Observations never change the parent's stated doctrine; a contradicting one is
also traced beside that doctrine in the parent.

**Confidence note (demoted from the parent's prose, 2026-08-27):** the note is
`confidence: medium` because no corpus video is about skipjack as a fishery.
Every record is an aside inside a video about something else — a charter-boat
etiquette clip, a terminal-tackle walkthrough, a sponsored electronics episode,
a Fred Hall seminar, or on-the-water footage of a tuna trip. The core "aggressive
everyday bycatch of the warm-water tuna grounds" framing rests on BD charter
footage (`lxFNVdDhMy4`).

## Where & when

- `i3qIAHW-SJc` (StokedOnFishing, 2016-08-08, Cortez Bank, aboard the *Top Gun
  80*): day one of a five-day trip put "yellowtail and yellowfin and some
  skippies in the mix" on the bank. Unregistered channel; no skipjack-specific
  gear, depth or count given. Same segment: 25 lb clear line with no
  fluorocarbon, hot bait, and casting the bait away from the boat.
- `0bcDBGzQnGw` (StokedOnFishing, 2018-12-17, about 35 mi from Ensenada, Baja
  Pacific north, with Navico/Tito Ortiz): "skipjack, skipjack" called on a
  hooked fish, immediately followed by "oh that's yellowfin right there" and
  then yellowtail — the three species coming across the same troll. Sponsor-heavy
  episode (Simrad); unregistered channel.
- `Vphyeoxd7R4` (SearcherSportfishing, 2009-09-05, offshore, dock interviews for
  an August 11–15 trip): two separate anglers report skipjack in the day's
  count — one with "three bluefin and one skipjack" at 35 lb per bluefin, one
  with "yellowfin, yellowtail, skipjack."
- `v8tPYYKM2JE` (SearcherSportfishing, 2010-02-11, dock interview): a joking "I'm
  going to catch a 7 lb skipjack with this and tear his head off" about a Penn
  International 50W with 80 lb top shot on 130 lb Spectra — the only weight
  figure attached to a skipjack anywhere in the corpus. Ledger row:
  unverifiable, on the parent's retired "a few pounds to low teens" size range.

## Feeding triggers

- `lxFNVdDhMy4` (BDOutdoors, 2019-11-11, offshore charter boat, presenter not
  named on camera): the whole chum-to-cast sequence. The captain says the
  yellowfin are in 40 ft of water; the deckhand ("Scottie") chums hard to draw
  the school up; "there was a well check, it was like two minutes of chumming —
  just look at that little side scan here, that indicates that there's fish";
  marks are called at "about 40 to 50 feet down"; the hookups come "on a
  burning" cast, with a skipjack landed on a Colt Sniper (captions render it
  "cold safer"). The same segment gives the drift-side rule: "the drift is
  coming this way and so all the anglers should be fishing on the right side of
  the boat… you want wind in your face when you're fishing that side." ASR is
  heavy throughout — names and the phrase "school of the elephan" (read as
  yellowfin) are unreliable; the numbers and the sequence are clear.

## Situations → techniques

- `5Oh3IPIC5g4` (Fisher Newb, 2020-03-12, Fred Hall Show seminar — Chad, HookUp
  Baits co-owner, with an interviewer): the punch-through exchange — "sometimes
  you get in and you got Skippy's all over the surface and the yellowfin are
  below it… the bullet goes really fast and you just let it go right through the
  Skippy… and get the yellowfin," corroborated by the interviewer as something
  many people have told him: "if you're throwing a live bait out there you catch
  skipjack every time; the guy throws on a one ounce bullet, he gets through
  those skipjack and he's catching that yellowfin every time." Promotional
  seminar for the presenter's own lure brand — **low confidence** on the
  product-specific claims; the surface-layer/underneath geometry is corroborated
  independently by `KajL5TsgBzU`. Parent's deep link:
  [tube-bait fishing — tuna patties, boils, and punching through a skipjack layer](../../techniques/tube-bait-fishing.md#tuna-patties-boils-and-punching-through-a-skipjack-layer-chad-hookup-baits-co-owner-5oh3ipic5g4).
- `KajL5TsgBzU` (BDOutdoors, 2025-07-17, long-range report): the independent
  corroboration, from the yellowtail sliding-sinker walkthrough — "you may get
  into a bite, especially on the longer trips, where you have bonita or skipjack
  or something like that biting your baits if they're all near the surface, so
  that sliding sinker rig is key," with sinker weight given as 1 oz shown, 2 oz
  in hand, and a working range of half an ounce to 3 oz depending on current and
  wind. Scoped to long-range trips.
- `HpPFogLwKOw` (StokedOnFishing, 2015-06-08, *Independence* long-range tuna
  trip, part 3): the kite segment — the kite "allows you to fish a variety of
  baits, anywhere from two sardines at once to a squid to a flying fish to a live
  skipjack if you got a lot of wind that can hold it up, and you can fish a heavy
  line," and it also gets a bait away from the boat when there is no current.
  Unregistered channel; the live-skipjack bait is one item in a list, not a
  worked program.
- `n4nMCLLW3tY` (SD Fish and Sips, 2023-08-04, Loreto / Puerto Escondido, Sea of
  Cortez): a fish coming up on a slow-troll is called "or skip Jack — keep it for
  some chunk bait," the second corpus record of the keep-for-chunk rule.
- `tcso7Lpm_Xs` (StokedOnFishing, 2018-03-09 upload, Buena Vista Beach Resort,
  East Cape, Baja California Sur): roughly **15–20 skipjack** came aboard as
  bycatch on a single morning's offshore troll targeting
  tuna/dorado/wahoo/marlin, alongside 15–16 dorado mostly released and one
  outsized snapper. No lure or leader detail attaches to the skipjack. See the
  [trolling East Cape entry](../../techniques/trolling.md#east-cape-baja--buena-vista-beach-resort-offshore-troll-for-tunadoradowahoomarlin).
  Unregistered channel — medium confidence.

## Gear summary (class terms)

- `PrdPJy26H8c` (Dirty Hookers, 2019-03-05 — Ray Sharifi; `ray-sharifi` is a
  registered voice, capped to SoCal topics, so this is medium confidence): the
  downsizing rule, stated over a jig box — "when I go tuna fishing I pretty much
  downsize everything unless we're going bluefin fishing, cuz you never know what
  size bluefin you're going to run into, but skipjack and schoolie yellowfin I
  just treat them as bonito and I downsize everything." The box runs 80 g to
  500 g flat-falls; the 80 g is named for "bonito, also for yellowfin and
  skipjack," alongside small Colt Sniper stickbaits and a mini yo-yo jig.
- `jQW2HLkMsmY` (Dirty Hookers, 2022-05-02, bluefin terminal-tackle breakdown):
  "all of my tuna — yellowfin, bluefin, skipjack — they were all caught on Owner
  Mutu circle hooks," with size 4 for lighter line and smaller grade and size 2,
  1/0 or 2/0 for heavier line and bigger fish. Stated inside a bluefin video, so
  the hook sizes are scoped to a mixed tuna trip, not measured on skipjack.
- `uyjTdgIw-1k` context (StokedOnFishing, 2014-11-03, "Stoked on Simrad, part
  2"): the companion episode to `Ow3an9lSVh4`, where chunked bonito, mackerel,
  skipjack and/or squid rather than pristine sardines is what kept the school at
  the boat. Carried in full on
  [chunking](../../techniques/chunking.md); noted here because it is why the
  parent's chunk-bait row exists.
