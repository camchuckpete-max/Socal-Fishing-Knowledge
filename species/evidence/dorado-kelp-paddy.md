## When this is the call

- `P9H-bpzT7eU` (SearcherSportfishing, Carl Schmidt, Fisherman's Landing,
  2022-09-29, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 198 — Dorado Fishing 101," SoCal):
  the live-bait-over-jig ranking is stated as crew consensus — "ask any crew
  member or any captain on the sport boats, they're going to say stick to live
  bait" — because a jig hookup pulls free on a head-shaking dorado where live
  bait holds. Registered channel, single mention on each claim — medium
  confidence. Duplicate-of the `P9H-bpzT7eU` entry in
  [dorado evidence](dorado.md), kept here for the paddy-program detail.

## How dorado changes the execution

- `ZFqe49jRgA0` (Your Saltwater Guide / Capt. Dave Hansen, 2019-05-26, "Top 5
  Offshore Fishing Tips You Must Know!"): source of the
  drive-right-up-to-the-edge rule and the one-to-two-minute paddy test. The
  approach is stated against a named alternative — getting up-wind, killing the
  stereo, running the bait pump and drifting down — with the sportboat fleet
  offered as the counter-evidence: a 65–95 ft steel boat with doors slamming
  and the PA running drives right to the kelp, everyone drops in, they catch
  fish and leave. The dwell rule is stated as a personal clock ("I can give it
  about a minute maybe two") once nothing is visible in the water and nothing
  is on the meter, with the restock-at-night mechanism as the reason waiting
  does not pay — "not very often do you wait 20 minutes and then start to
  bite… they're either biting or they're not biting." The same entry's sonar
  and time-of-day material is logged in [dorado evidence](dorado.md).
  Registered voice — high confidence.
- `VgpgJ8IAyJo` (BDOutdoors, Ali Hussainy, 2020-06-08, "Best Offshore Lures for
  Dorado, Yellowfin, Bluefin Tuna | NOMAD"): the tower-cast qualify test is
  described against the previous season's kelp-paddy episode — "if we weren't
  sure if anything was on that particular kelp… I'd throw this from the tower.
  If we didn't see something come out and chase it — and it was usually a
  dorado — we would just move on to the next one." A Nomad brand feature, so
  the lure's own claims cap at low confidence; the test procedure is logged
  here separately from the product claim, which stays in
  [dorado evidence](dorado.md).
- `E4vKwRaRueA` (BDOutdoors, Ali Hussainy, 2021-09-21, "Dorado Fishing
  Tactics," ~15 mi off San Diego): source of the J-hook rationale, the leader
  step-down ladder and the hook-to-bait matching. The hook-style call is stated
  as a deliberate exception by an angler who fishes circle hooks for
  everything — dorado "eat a bait and they charge the boat looking for more
  bait… happy to feed going straight forward in a line, picking off as many
  baits as they can," where the tunas and yellowtail "grab that bait and
  immediately turn sideways." The ladder is walked on camera from a 30 lb
  general leader (40 lb with bigger fish around) down through 20 lb to 15 lb,
  with the floor stated as a preference rather than a rule — "I don't like to
  fish below 20 pounds, I feel like you get bit off a lot." The 1/0 hook is
  matched on camera against a bait called "your typical Southern California
  sardine that you're going to get from the bait receiver… about six inches,"
  nose-hooked. Registered source — high confidence. Duplicate-of the
  `E4vKwRaRueA` entry in [dorado evidence](dorado.md), kept here for the
  execution parameters.
- `P9H-bpzT7eU` (same video as the entry above): the drop-back is stated as a
  count on the flylined sardine — "cast it out there, you get bit, give it a
  good three to five second count and put it in gear." The small-mouth reason
  for a J-hook comes from the same source;
  asr-uncertain(the auto-captions render it as "they have a pretty small
  amount," read in context as "small mouth"). Registered channel, single
  mention — medium confidence.
- `VWClGAn2WEw` (StokedOnFishing, 2022-10-13, offshore SoCal, "Offshore Kelp
  Paddy Fishing On the ElDorado," a 2.5-day El Dorado charter out of Long Beach
  Sportfishing): after a run of consecutive dorado hookups on the same leader,
  the crew described dorado's teeth and gill plates as prone to chafing the
  leader over repeated fights and passes near the boat — "cut off a foot or
  two, make sure you don't have any chafing and re-tie every time" — rather
  than fishing the same leader section through many fish in a row. The same
  trip's plan-B pivot from a dead bluefin bite into a loaded paddy bite is
  logged in [dorado evidence](dorado.md). Unregistered channel — medium
  confidence.
