## How spotted bay bass changes the execution

- `46ha7J1Fc6A` (Roman Castro, 2020 Fred Hall Show, Long Beach, 2020-03-12):
  Rick Fuentes (Fishing Syndicate) on the San Diego Bay / Mission Bay dock,
  piling and mooring program this page is built from — an all-graphite, super
  light and sensitive casting rod rated 12–20 lb (the 7 ft 6 in medium-heavy
  size is named later in the same video by the rod builder, not by Fuentes),
  sensitive enough that "you can literally feel the tail kicking on the swim
  bait" and fished with 3 in and 4 in swimbaits and Band of Anglers dart-spins
  on a 3/8–1/2 oz jighead; 200-size low-profile reels, stepped to a 300 in the
  heavier structure work, at a preferred 6.6:1 ratio for the speed to "turn that
  head around real quick and get them out of the structure" where docks are "only
  2 3 feet in between"; 20–30 lb braid chosen for strength and zero stretch but
  backed by a leader because braid is poor in abrasion resistance, with about
  15 lb fluorocarbon recommended in the shallower structure water expressly
  because "I've caught many many really big halibut in the same area that you
  fish the spotted bay bass"; and the fight rule — once the hook is set "you got
  to be able to just whine on that fish and get them out of there," while "if you
  try to pump them in and drop your rod tip down that's usually you're done."
  Unregistered speaker, single mention; a booth interview at a consumer tackle
  show, so the rod and bait models named are the interviewee's own product line.
  The same interview's Newport Harbor deep-eelgrass crankbait extension and its
  structure/camouflage material stay on the router — see
  [spotted bay bass evidence](spotted-bay-bass.md#situations--techniques).
