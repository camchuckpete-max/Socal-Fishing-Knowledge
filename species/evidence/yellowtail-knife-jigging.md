## When this is the call

- `M8hOYQ_6rSg` (Cesar, via Dirty Hookers, 2025-05-20; Cesar is a registered
  voice, single mention — medium confidence): a Sea of Cortez trip-prep tackle
  talk covering the whole quiver. Sources the current-and-activity decision
  axis (light current / less active fish → slow-pitch; more current / more
  active fish → speed jig) and the back-weighted-profile reasoning for why the
  speed jig is the pick on aggressive, fast-moving fish in heavy current. The
  same axis is logged from this source in
  [slow-pitch jigging](../../techniques/slow-pitch-jigging.md), from the
  slow-pitch side.

## How yellowtail changes the execution

- `M8hOYQ_6rSg`: the yellowtail speed-jig weight band — start about 150 g,
  work up to about 350 g, with anything past 350 g called "a lot of work" —
  and the speed-up-not-slow-down bite response (fish bumping the jig at the
  working pace without a solid hookup means work it faster; "for yellowtail,
  sometimes the faster the better"). The general pump-up/wind-down stroke and
  its faster-than-slow-pitch pace, from the same source, stay on
  [knife / speed jigging](../../techniques/knife-jigging.md#mechanics).

## Rigs & gear

- `M8hOYQ_6rSg`: the Sea of Cortez speed-jig outfit (5.5–6 ft rod rated
  150–350 g up to 400 g, 50–65 lb braid with a stated preference for 50, a
  taller/narrower "yellowtail-special"-size reel for line gained per pump),
  carried in full in
  [rod & reel selection](../../tackle/rod-and-reel-selection.md#baja-sea-of-cortez-gear-quiver)
  and linked rather than duplicated here.
- `LTCFjqtSI8g` (Ray Sharifi, Dirty Hookers, "Yellowtail Arsenal Updated,"
  2023-03-29; `ray-sharifi` registered, SoCal-capped — medium confidence; the
  source states plainly that speed/vertical jigging is new to him in 2023 and
  that he is relaying what he has been told by others rather than his own
  tested doctrine, declining to go further on the setup for that reason —
  kept as a tentative starting point, not established practice): Phoenix
  Megalodon 607 (rated by jig weight, 200–300 g) paired with a Shimano
  Trinidad 20A, 25–40 yd of 40–60 lb monofilament top shot straight to the
  jig, no fluoro leader, with the shock-absorption reasoning for keeping the
  top shot sizable; jig on the rod at filming a Daiwa SK, 200 g. Demonstrated
  for yellowtail; the source frames speed/vertical/slow-pitch jigging
  generally as effective for "tuna or yellowtail" but does not tie this
  specific build to tuna.
