## When this is the call

- `Eo_bA8IWvyU` (Tackle Express, 2025-04-16, dockside tackle-counter walkthrough

  as the trigger — "if we're fishing 60 to 100 ft of water and the skipper says,
  'Hey, we got fish in 20 to 30 feet' … that's when I'd fish something like
  this," against the dropper loop for fish "all the way on the bottom."
- `ShSxNKAcUB4` (Your Saltwater Guide, upload 2020-12-08, on-camera recording

  fishing a known spot already anchored on, "looking for [current] or tidal
  movement or slack tide"; the dropper loop is offered in the same breath as the
  alternative for anglers who prefer a rod in the holder, at 12–18 in above the
  weight.

## How white seabass changes the execution

- `ShSxNKAcUB4`: the mouth mechanism is the spine of the whole segment — "a
  white sea bass's mouth is out in front of them … this is the reason why most of
  you only catch bat rays … if your bait is laying on the bottom you got about a
  zero percent chance of catching a sea bass," with shovelnose and leopard sharks
  named alongside the rays because "their mouths are on the bottom of their
  face."
- `ShSxNKAcUB4`: the cadence is stated as a loop — "throw it out into the water
  and let it slowly sink down the water column, when it gets all the way down to
  the bottom reel it back up and throw it back out … I never let my bait set on
  the bottom." The hit rate and the move rule come together: "nine out of ten
  times … it's gonna get a bite on something — white sea bass, yellowtail, calico
  bass, blue perch, sheephead … if nothing's biting it, it's time to move."
- `ShSxNKAcUB4`: the reason for weighting a squid at all is given as a seagull
  problem — "if you've never thrown a squid out, the minute it hits the surface it
  just lays there and then a seagull eats it" — so the lead is "just enough to get
  me down to the bottom as slow as possible."
- `Eo_bA8IWvyU`: the stepped free-spool cycle is demonstrated at the counter —
  "you don't want to just dump it and let it pile in front of you … keep
  consistent tension … if I've let it sink for say a couple minutes, I might click
  it into gear, take a couple turns … get that sinker back to the hook with my
  bait, back into free spool, and I'll let it start to separate again," with the
  stated aim of "covering a good amount of the water column."

## The heavy-current fallback — a squid-pinned glow jig

- `ShSxNKAcUB4`: the exit condition is stated as "unless the current is
  smoldering [smoking] — then if it's smoldering and I'm in deeper water I'll
  probably use a jig … a TLC with the squid hooked on it and drop it straight
  down, because it's very heavy." asr-uncertain(jig name): "TLC" is the caption
  rendering; no catalogue confirmation.
- `Eo_bA8IWvyU`: the same two jigs are shown on the counter — "these are both
  made by Tatty [Tady]; this is a smaller TLC and this is the 40" — with the
  large single hook explained as what lets you "pin a full fresh dead squid," one
  to three of them, and the deckhand check stated twice because "the squid can be
  very tough to find … running on very limited supply."
- `Eo_bA8IWvyU`: the soak is described as deliberately passive — "hit the bottom
  and maybe take three to five, six cranks up off the bottom … probably 3 to about
  6 to 10 feet up … I just simply let it set there on the rail. I'll be sitting,
  hanging out, talking with my buddies … as we bob up and bob down, this jig is
  slowly going to rise, slowly going to fall."
- `Eo_bA8IWvyU`: the gray-light framing is stated as a general white seabass fact
  and then applied to this jig — "most sea bass are caught in gray light …
  before the sun comes up … a heavy fog layer … tucked and blocked behind a
  mountain" — with the glow back as the attention-getter and "the pile of squid on
  the bottom" as what the fish then sees.

## Rigs & gear

- `ShSxNKAcUB4`: the ladder is given as a personal decision procedure — "if I can
  get away with an eighth of an ounce or a quarter of an ounce of weight I'm going
  to use it, but if there's more current and if I'm in deeper water then I'm gonna
  go to a heavier weight, I may go up to a half, maybe three-quarters of an ounce.
  Very rarely will I fish a 1 ounce slider on a live squid or a dead squid because
  … it's sinking through the water column too fast."
- `ShSxNKAcUB4`: the rig is described without a bead, swivel or leader — "a
  sliding sinker attached right to your line, sliding right down to your hook" —
  and the hook as a personal preference against the common pattern: "some people
  like to use octopus hook, I like to use a thin wire … Mustad hook," with squid
  rigging deferred to the same channel's hook-a-squid video.
- `ShSxNKAcUB4`: the orange lead head is introduced in the same run of speech —
  poured by a friend and "not for sale anywhere," with the shop-counter substitute
  being any orange head with "a big hook to support your squid," and the
  reaction-colour reasoning attached to the sink. That doctrine is carried on
  [white seabass — lead head and squid](../white-seabass-leadhead-squid.md); it is
  logged here only because the source states it inside the slider segment, and the
  video never settles whether the orange head and the heavy current jig are the
  same lure.
- `Eo_bA8IWvyU`: the sliding egg sinker is shown in plain and glow-in-the-dark
  versions, with the free-slide requirement stated explicitly — "you don't want to
  make the weight not be able to move" — and the bait options given as "full fresh
  dead squid or you can even use fin bait as well."
- `nRIAgz5G_Bc` (Tackle Express, 2026-06-30, 2:39 dockside gear list for Ventura
  and Oxnard trips to the Channel Islands; presenter "James"; unregistered
  retailer channel, closes on a tackleexpress.com pitch — medium, and the model
  picks are sponsored claims): "some sliders, the slider squid with that Aki Twist
  hook paired up with a 1/4 oz or a 1/2 oz slider … another great way to
  capitalize a bite when you're fishing the Channel Islands and the fish are
  higher up in the water column." The Aki Twist size range given at the top of the
  same list — "between the 4/0 and the 6/0 size" — is stated for the trip as a
  whole, not for the slider.
- `Eo_bA8IWvyU`: the reel discussion puts the conventional star drag as best for
  the dropper loop but explicitly able to fish "these larger jigs, the sliders,
  this sliding egg sinker, our lead heads, and all the artificials as well"; the
  line rule is stated as "you want a lot of monofilament … if you're running
  straight braid, there's a very high probability that you might just simply yank
  the hook out of his face."
