# Evidence — Swordfish (Broadbill)

Per-source provenance backing [swordfish](../swordfish.md). One line per
observation, grouped under the parent note's section headings, in its order.
Observations never change the parent's stated doctrine; a contradicting one is
also traced beside that doctrine in the parent.

**Provenance note (demoted from the parent's prose, 2026-08-27):** the parent's
SoCal doctrine comes almost entirely from one source, the **Costa-sponsored
BDOutdoors deep-drop webinar of 2021-02-04** (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`) with Duane Diego
Mellor (San Diego), Rush Maltz (Key West) and Ali Hussainy — a **sponsor-tied**
broadcast that closes with a sunglasses giveaway, on auto-generated captions, and
describing the **2019 and 2020** seasons. All three speakers are registered
(`sources/source-registry.md`), which is why the parent sits at `confidence:
high`; the Florida material in it is region-labeled, never merged into SoCal
doctrine. `KuVwmfF6RAo` (Duane Diego Mellor, BDOutdoors, 2020-07-01) carries
manual captions. `1iV1GnF4K-c` (Bad Company Fishing Adventures, 2024-03-16) is an
**unregistered channel** and caps at medium.

## Where & when

- `yMiBtZ7k8-w` (BDOutdoors, 2021-02-04, San Diego offshore): the 2019 season is
  described as good for the buoy-gear crews in middle-to-late September and "red
  hot" in October, carrying into December with the last fish about a week before
  Christmas; the 2020 season is placed later, with the hot stretch about 10 days
  before Thanksgiving.
- `yMiBtZ7k8-w` (BDOutdoors, 2021-02-04, San Diego offshore): the
  presence-vs-catchability line the parent carries — the same program in August
  can go five trips without a bite, September gets a couple, October gets a bite
  a trip or more.
- `yMiBtZ7k8-w` (BDOutdoors, 2021-02-04, San Diego offshore): a June trip run to
  "work the kinks out ahead of the season" produced what the speaker calls the
  biggest bite he has ever had, hook pulled — the counter-example behind the
  parent's holdover-fish-but-not-the-fuel framing.
- `KuVwmfF6RAo` (BDOutdoors, 2020-07-01, the 178 bank): a working drift in about
  1,090 ft, inside the 900–1,400 ft band the operation fishes; the same clip
  opens on a reef stop with a bait ball and trailing yellowtail, unrelated to the
  sword segment.
- `XLVUhV8DW64` (BDOutdoors, 2022-10-13, SoCal offshore): the weekly report puts
  swordfish in the mid-October offshore mix — "fish spread all over the place
  right now, we've got marlin, we've got bluefin, yellowfin, swordfish, deep-drop
  breads are starting to catch those too" (ASR garble on "deep drop") — with a
  named charter finally getting a nice one that week.
- **Florida contrast, not SoCal:** `yMiBtZ7k8-w` (BDOutdoors, 2021-02-04, Florida
  Keys): the Keys guide reports fish on 12 of his last 13 trips, sometimes three
  in a day with none over 100 lb, and declines to name a season at all — any
  month with a weather window produces. Carried as a region label in the parent's
  Doctrine & conflicts.

## Presence & forage

- `yMiBtZ7k8-w` (BDOutdoors, 2021-02-04, San Diego offshore): stomach contents
  reported on cleaning — mostly hake and rockfish, some squid on some fish, and
  one fish that dumped roughly seven pounds of small suspending rockfish on the
  scale.
- `yMiBtZ7k8-w` (BDOutdoors, 2021-02-04, Catalina): a fish caught at Catalina by
  an angler the speaker cannot name held 30–40 regulation-sized mullet — relayed
  second-hand, and the parent's single-source flag sits on it; the reasoning
  given on camera is that mullet do not live offshore, so the fish had been up in
  the kelp on the beach.
- `yMiBtZ7k8-w` (BDOutdoors, 2021-02-04, SoCal): the mako is named as the only
  predator on a big swordfish, from stories and photos of swords with the tail
  chewed off and a mako circling; the SoCal captain says he has never seen it
  himself — hearsay, kept out of the parent.
- **Florida parallel, not SoCal:** `yMiBtZ7k8-w` (BDOutdoors, 2021-02-04, Florida
  Keys): Keys fish come up with bills broken and beaten from rooting in mud and
  rocks, and mangrove snapper turn up in stomachs — the east-coast version of the
  parent's bottom-feeding evidence, which in SoCal rests on the rockfish instead.

## Feeding triggers

- `yMiBtZ7k8-w` (BDOutdoors, 2021-02-04, San Diego offshore): the moon connection
  is stated as observed and explicitly not understood — "we definitely know
  there's a connection to the moon out here, I don't know if we fully understand
  it."
- **Florida contrast, not SoCal:** `yMiBtZ7k8-w` (BDOutdoors, 2021-02-04, Florida
  Keys): the Keys guide's favourite window is the week leading up to and the week
  after the full moon, while also reporting fish caught mid-phase; his pre-trip
  reads are moon, weather, SST and how much water the tide is moving, because his
  program has to hit specific spots against a Gulf Stream drift.

## Finding them (sign & sonar)

- `KuVwmfF6RAo` (BDOutdoors, 2020-07-01, the 178 bank): the sonar signature the
  parent carries — a feed layer at about 50 fathoms (300–350 ft), fish marking
  200–300 ft under the bait as a long return rather than a boomerang, with TVG
  boosted to 9 on the boat's unit to get a return back from 1,000-plus feet.
- `yMiBtZ7k8-w` (BDOutdoors, 2021-02-04, San Diego offshore): the DSL screenshot
  discussed on camera was painted with a Furuno/Airmar D26 HID 1 kW transducer
  with TVG run at roughly 55–57; the general translation offered is to set TVG
  mid-range (2 of 3 on a Simrad) and back the ping speed off (about 15 of 20),
  which is the settings material that lives in
  [electronics & sounder](../../planning/electronics-and-sounder.md).
- `yMiBtZ7k8-w` (BDOutdoors, 2021-02-04, San Diego offshore): the five-year error
  the fishery corrected — dropping to the bottom and working up 100 ft at a time,
  which in 2,000 ft of water never put the bait in the bite zone at all. The DSL
  relationship, from PIER sat-tag work (Scott Aalbers, Chugey Sepúlveda) off
  harpoon boats, is named as what changed everything.
- `OYOda6T3f-8` (BDOutdoors, 2022-10-20, SoCal island zone): a spotter plane is
  up at the islands looking for swordfish and watching the bluefin that were
  still foaming up there, with the report adding that nobody is fishing that much
  at the time and the weekend weather would not have allowed it.
- `1iV1GnF4K-c` (Bad Company Fishing Adventures, 2024-03-16, SoCal): unregistered
  channel — surface swordfish is named the favourite fishery of the two speakers,
  with the claim that multi-day offshore trips see far more daytime swords than
  people expect if you are looking at the water correctly, and that daytime
  swordfishing is low-percentage and "an art."

## Situations → techniques

- `yMiBtZ7k8-w` (BDOutdoors, 2021-02-04, San Diego offshore): across the 2019 and
  2020 seasons the San Diego program put the hook in and pulled on 30-plus fish;
  exactly two of those absolutely piled on the bait, with the bait swallowed into
  the belly, and the rest were subtler bites — the conversion problem the parent's
  lead states.
- `yMiBtZ7k8-w` (BDOutdoors, 2021-02-04, SoCal): the first two squid ever
  deployed by the San Diego program got bit, given on camera as one reason squid
  stayed the standard bait; a big mackerel tried once during a peak-season window
  swam the leader up and was abandoned.
- **Florida contrast, not SoCal:** `yMiBtZ7k8-w` (BDOutdoors, 2021-02-04, Florida
  Keys): the Keys program moved off squid to belly baits (dolphin, bonito) and
  eels because other squid shred a squid bait on a long drift, and reports three
  fish on one eel bait in a single trip. Bait-choice reasoning, region-labeled.

## Doctrine & conflicts

- `cameron`: daytime swordfish is a possible future target, manual
  (non-electric) reels only, needing different line configurations and a library
  of banked bulk braid spools swapped between reel setups per program.
  A self-imposed gear constraint rather than fishery doctrine — the fishery
  itself runs both manual and electric-assist — so it belongs in
  [Cameron's rods](../../profiles/cameron/rods.md), which already carries it as
  an open item beside the Fathom 80 respool; the parent keeps only the
  both-ways statement in its gear summary.

## Landing & handling

- `yMiBtZ7k8-w` (BDOutdoors, 2021-02-04, SoCal): the hook-hold ranking is stated
  from experience — a circle in the corner of the mouth is described as
  unremovable ("you might as well hook a leather Home Depot glove"), against a
  roof-of-mouth hold soft enough to poke a finger through toward the brain.
- **Florida contrast, not SoCal:** `yMiBtZ7k8-w` (BDOutdoors, 2021-02-04, Florida
  Keys): fight durations of one, two and five hours are quoted for the Keys
  fishery, and a big fish taken by the Keys guide and the host together ran six
  hours in a harness; after the lead came off that fish sounded to 600–700 ft and
  sat motionless until it was lifted about 100 ft past the thermocline. Kept out
  of the parent because no SoCal fight duration is stated.
  ⚠ asr-uncertain: the speaker attribution on the six-hour fish is ambiguous in
  the auto-captions; the fishery it happened in is not named on camera, only
  implied by the surrounding Keys discussion.
- `yMiBtZ7k8-w` (BDOutdoors, 2021-02-04, SoCal): the harpoon dart used at the
  boat in the Keys is ruled out for SoCal in one line — "we can't do the dart
  here" — with no reason given; the parent carries it under Regulations with an
  unverifiable flag.

## Zone material (pending zone guides)

- **San Diego offshore banks** — `yMiBtZ7k8-w` (BDOutdoors, 2021-02-04): the
  rotation named on camera is the Ridge, the 9 (9 Mile Bank), the 14 (14 Mile
  Bank), the 43, the 178 and the 302, all fished on a preferred down-and-in drift
  along the contour edges; on a break day the 138 and the 182 are picked off the
  SST/chlorophyll plot on screen for the mixing water and heavy current flow
  sitting on them.
- **San Diego offshore banks** — `KuVwmfF6RAo` (BDOutdoors, 2020-07-01, the 178):
  the corpus's only filmed sword drift — about 1,090 ft of water, feed layer at
  roughly 50 fathoms, fish marking 200–300 ft under it.
- **Catalina / island zone** — `yMiBtZ7k8-w` (BDOutdoors, 2021-02-04): the only
  Catalina swordfish in the corpus is the second-hand mullet fish; no Catalina
  drift, depth, run or seasonal window is described.
- **Catalina / island zone** — `OYOda6T3f-8` (BDOutdoors, 2022-10-20): a spotter
  plane up at the islands hunting swordfish in October, with nobody fishing it
  much at the time and the weekend weather against it.
