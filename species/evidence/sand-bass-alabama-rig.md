## How barred sand bass changes the execution

- `P2OzCf2CwXI` (BDOutdoors, "Sand Bass 101," 2026-07-06, studio): the A-rig
  walk-through this page is built from — named as the always-option for a
  dedicated bass fisherman whether the fish are really fired up or really not,
  prefaced with the warning that there is no more expensive way to fish for sand
  bass because you pay for the rig, the plastics and the heads; a lob cast
  rather than a hard cast because the rigs break off; bites coming a lot on the
  sink, as with the single plastics; at or close-ish to the bottom, into gear and
  low and slow back to the boat on the handle with maybe a couple of little
  pumps, cruising the baits up through the column while explicitly not fishing
  too close to the structure; and the chain reaction off a schooled, spawning
  aggregation — one bite fires the next until four or five fish, sometimes a
  full limit, are on a single cast, loading the rod like a 20 lb yellowtail.
  Registered voice; a studio tackle walk-through, not a trip report. The same
  source's parent-note entry, covering its live-bait slider, plastics, weedless
  and dropper-loop material, is in
  [sand bass evidence](sand-bass.md#situations--techniques).

## Rigs & gear

- `P2OzCf2CwXI` (BDOutdoors, "Sand Bass 101," 2026-07-06, studio): the outfit is
  given once for all the artificials — the A-rig, the heavier baits, the larger
  tube baits and two plastics on a three-way swivel — as a low-profile ~300-size
  reel (a size either way is fine) on a slightly stiffer bass rod rated 15–50 lb,
  chosen for feel and for controlling the sink or fall. No A-rig-specific
  hardware, line class or head weight is given for sand bass.
