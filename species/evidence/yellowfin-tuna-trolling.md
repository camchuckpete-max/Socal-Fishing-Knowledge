## When this is the call

- `ouBrIdO7d4k` (SD Fish and Sips, "Pacific Coast Sportfishing Show 2025,"
  2025-03-08 upload, filmed 2025-03-06, booth interview at the OC Fairgrounds
  trade show; backside of Catalina Island, socal-bight): recapping a season run
  mostly as half-day Catalina charters with "not a lot of time offshore with
  clients," the speaker described a standing personal method for finding
  dolphin-associated yellowfin on the backside of the island during the
  **lobster opener**, dated on camera as "first week of October, end of
  September," and credited it with **60–70 lb** fish taken on the dolphin. The
  transcript is not speaker-diarized and the two first names exchanged in the
  scene (Jerry, Dennis) cannot be confidently matched to who is talking, so he
  is logged unnamed rather than guessed. Unregistered channel/speaker, single
  mention.
- `fK2AT460xW4` (Your Saltwater Guide — Dave Hansen, "How to Catch Tuna | Live
  Bait Rig FOR Sub Surface to DEEP," 2021-04-27): yellowfin suspend underneath
  travelling dolphin schools and mostly do not surface-swim with the pod, which
  is why the school can look empty from above while holding fish; the two
  SoCal-relevant pod types are common dolphin and spotted spinner dolphin.
  `dave-hansen` registered, single mention of this claim. The parent cites this
  for the travelling-pod half of the positional read only; the forage
  relationship itself stays in
  [yellowfin tuna](../yellowfin-tuna.md#presence--forage).

## How yellowfin changes the execution

- `ouBrIdO7d4k` (SD Fish and Sips, 2025-03-08, booth interview): the pod
  programme's parameters as stated — trolled on **60 or 80 lb** straight to the
  lure at **7–9 mph**, "not very fast"; get **way out in front** of a moving
  pod and never cut through it, because under bow pressure the animals "hear the
  boat, see what's going on," then "split or they'll just take off" and "they
  Dodge it"; the same never-go-through rule applied to a trolled Mad Mac for
  bluefin ("even blue fin to the Mad Mac, I never go through them"). On where
  the fish sit: "they're on the edges, they're up in front, on the flanks" of a
  working pod — once the dolphin scatter a bait ball "the yellow will start
  foaming out in front of them, out to the side of them," with terns visible
  "50 yard out in front" — while "there's always yellowfin underneath them,"
  so the two positions are the same fish read at two pod states rather than
  competing claims. Volunteered in the same passage and carried on the parent as
  a labelled out-of-region contrast: the same small trolled feather is a Hawaii
  skipjack staple.
- `H-vIGWPIPVc` (StokedOnFishing — presenter Shay, "Yellowfin Tuna vs Bonita,"
  2019-10-30, San Clemente Island): from inside, the crew spotted "a big big big
  porpoise school," ran out and trolled around it with a **deep diver and a
  cedar plug**; the deep diver — believed on camera to be a Nomad in sardine
  colour — got bit and the boat landed a yellowfin "about sixty pound." The
  troll-to-locate pattern triggered by a dolphin school rather than by a paddy
  or bird sign. Unregistered channel.
- `0bcDBGzQnGw` (StokedOnFishing, "Fishing In Baja with Navico & Tito Ortiz,
  Part 1," 2018-12-17 upload, offshore Ensenada, Baja California; footage from
  the annual September Navico supplier conference trip out of Hotel Coral, exact
  year not stated on camera): after "a couple hours of desert" with no action,
  the crew slow-trolled cedar plugs around a large dolphin/porpoise school —
  "the name of the game is finding life." The school broke into a **full
  foamer**; the crew stopped trolling and switched to flylined sardine plus
  chunk cut bait worked into it. Several fish broke off on the flyline gear
  ("lost about three or four") before one was gaffed and boated; the species of
  the boated fish is not stated on camera, tuna was the stop's stated target, and
  a striped marlin was seen jumping at the boat during the same stop.
  Unregistered channel.
- `YUdbrIm9vrE` (StokedOnFishing, "Cedros Island Goes Off," 2024-03-20 upload,
  October trip with the year not stated on camera, roughly 20–30 mi offshore of
  Cedros Island, Baja; Cedros Outdoor Adventures charter with Jose): day one of a
  5-day trip ran straight offshore for yellowfin and dorado. Two trolled
  Rapala-class hardbaits (captions render "ralas"/"ralos" — ASR-uncertain, read
  against the established Rapala-class product family) were run **one short, one
  long** on a **60 lb** line class, one line later swapped to a surface popper.
  Running the same colour on both — **purple/purple** named on camera — was
  called out as the reason simultaneous doubles came aboard, including on a
  hardbait-to-feather swap; the captain separately reported that switching to a
  different-colour Rapala two days earlier, on a different day's trip out of the
  same operation, had NOT produced the same doubles — same-source corroboration,
  not independent confirmation. Later the same day a flylined live sardine drew
  hookups until the bait stopped drawing bites, at which point the crew was told
  to "forget the bait" and put the feathers back on — the same feather/hardbait
  combination that had produced the morning's tuna — which restarted the bite;
  the catches from that stretch were dorado rather than more tuna. The bite's own
  pace when it turned on: a double struck just behind the stern, and a separate
  stretch put three fish in the well inside about 10 seconds. Unregistered
  channel.
