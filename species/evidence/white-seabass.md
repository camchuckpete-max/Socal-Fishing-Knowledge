# Evidence — White Seabass

observation, grouped under the parent note's section headings, in its order.

**Registration note (demoted from the parent's prose, 2026-08-27):** the parent
is `confidence: high` because the moon / slack-tide / off-color-triad /

## Presence & forage

- `Xr4nURK-Z48` (BDOutdoors — Ricky, 2022-06-16, Catalina Island): filmed in
  "early springtime," which "for a lot of people" means white seabass fishing;
  squid is the staple bait and "that's what most of the bites are coming on";
  standard market wild-caught squid, "a little bit on the small side, but they'll
  work just fine." The same session's rigging half — the two-squid (three if
  small) threading and the mating mimicry that puts the bite in the night and
  gray-light hours — moved to
  [white seabass — lead head and squid evidence](white-seabass-leadhead-squid.md#how-white-seabass-changes-the-execution).
- `Eo_bA8IWvyU` (Tackle Express — Harry, tackle-counter walkthrough,
  "Before You Fish for White Seabass"; unregistered channel): dropper-loop white
  seabass are "typically between 60 to about 90 ft and typically close to the
  bottom" because that is where the squid beds are — "the squid are spawning,
  laying their eggs, and seabass are very lazy and very curious creatures, they
  don't like to work too hard for their meal," so they swim the bed and eat the
  squid drifting in the current. Fin bait is the stated alternative forage and
  hook bait: "not only do sea bass eat squid, they'll eat fin bait as well,
  sardines as well as mackerel."

## Feeding triggers

- `Eo_bA8IWvyU` (same walkthrough, light window): "Most sea bass are caught in
  gray light" — before the sun comes up, or any low-light state, including a
  heavy fog layer or being "tucked and blocked behind a mountain that is
  preventing the sun from hitting the water." duplicate-of the Presence & forage
  entry above (same session, different claim).
- `Eo_bA8IWvyU` (same walkthrough, curiosity mechanism): the wind-and-pop cadence
  on a lead head or fluke is aimed at "trying to get that fish to become from
  curious to becoming a biter" — the lead-head version of that cadence, with its
  flick-behind-the-boat cast and thumb-tensioned sink, moved to
  [white seabass — lead head and squid evidence](white-seabass-leadhead-squid.md#how-white-seabass-changes-the-execution);
  on the bite, "sea bass, you do want to let them
  eat it — they're going to chomp, slowly eat it, and once that rod loads down,
  that's when you're going to want to set the hook." duplicate-of the entries
  above (same session).
- `6TBxHnkYXI0` (BDOutdoors, 2025-02-20, SoCal, studio report): the slack-tide
  doctrine as stated — white seabass "can be a very tidal bite," and like marlin
  and bluefin foamers they pop on the slack; the mechanism is credited on camera
  to Sean Morgan — at the slack the water column goes quiet, bait rises, and
  predators hunt better. Operational rule attached: be on your highest-percentage
  spot an hour before the tide change. The same segment gives the chum-bucket
  plankton→bait→seabass chain, the drift line as the true-current read, and the
  anchor-up-current-of-the-spot geometry with the standoff scaled to current and
  to break-off risk from a big yellowtail sharing the spot.

## Finding them (sign & sonar)

- `OpcKQPA3vAI` (BDOutdoors / SoCal Bight Fishing Academy — Erik Landesfeind,
  finding coastal and island halibut): the off-color "seabass/halibut color"
  water triad — off-color water plus current plus bait, all three, with the local
  variation (a dirty patch with a hard rip or foam edge inside cleaner water)
  rather than a whole island being that color. Registered voice; the parent's
  triad doctrine and the shared halibut framing come from here.
- `82gEHYel-4U` (StokedOnFishing, 2023-09-06, Catalina Island; unregistered
  channel): the pull-up check-in — is the water milky/off-color, is the current
  running uphill or downhill, and does that direction go together with the reef
  or kelp line being fished (swell and wind direction factor in). On one stop the
  current read "downhill" with swell and wind pushing milky water off the beach;
  they fished it and flagged the coming tide change as the cue to "bolt" once the
  current turned. On a separate beach-adjacent stretch the current ran straight
  off the beach carrying off-color water past the rocks with a **color break on
  both sides**, called "perfect conditions" — there current *direction* mattered
  less than on the reef stops, and as long as a trackable off-color plume ("that
  river") ran visibly from the beach all the way in, the fish held right in it.
- `Y2bXn44lfqo` (StokedOnFishing, 2023-08-30, Catalina Island; unregistered
  channel): on the hookup, head shakes followed by a hard, changing-direction run
  is the crew's tell for white seabass over halibut — halibut also head-shake but
  do not take off running that way. Same trip: anchored in **74 ft** (the
  captain's usual preferred depth on this spot is **~70 ft**) but held off
  committing to the exact spot because the current was a "slight uphill" — not
  the direction wanted — waiting to slide in once the current read right.

## Situations → techniques

- `ShSxNKAcUB4` (Your Saltwater Guide — Capt. Dave Hansen, 2020-12-08, "How to
  catch white seabass," filmed 2021-05-25 per the on-camera date): the
  suspend-and-retrieve program — a white seabass's mouth "is out in front of
  them," so a bait laying on the bottom is "about a zero percent chance" and what
  you catch instead is bat rays, shovelnose and leopard sharks, "their mouths are
  on the bottom of their face." Rig a sliding sinker to a thin-wire hook (a
  Mustad, not an octopus hook), use the least weight that will still sink —
  1/8 or 1/4 oz preferred, up to 1/2–3/4 oz in more current or deeper water,
  rarely 1 oz — cast, let it sink the whole column, then reel up and recast
  immediately, never letting it sit. A squid is not flylined because it "just
  lays there and then a seagull eats it." Constant recasting also raises
  yellowtail, calico bass, blue perch and sheephead — "if something's biting it
  that's good, if nothing's biting it it's time to move." A heavy white TLC jig
  with a squid dropped straight down is the smoldering-current alternative; the
  same source's orange-lead-head reaction-colour doctrine moved to
  [white seabass — lead head and squid evidence](white-seabass-leadhead-squid.md#rigs--gear).
  The same
  source's dropper-loop acceptance and its 12–18 in hook-above-the-weight figure
  moved to
  [white seabass — dropper loop evidence](white-seabass-dropper-loop.md#how-white-seabass-changes-the-execution).
- `Eo_bA8IWvyU` (same walkthrough, rig roster): fresh-dead squid is preferred
  over live ("smack his head on the deck"). The dropper-loop half of this roster
  — the rig called "one of the most tested and proven methods," its Owner Ahi
  twist hook, torpedo sinker, sinker-to-hook leader and dropper tag — moved to
  [white seabass — dropper loop evidence](white-seabass-dropper-loop.md#rigs--gear).
  Sliding glow or plain egg sinkers are
  the pick when the skipper calls fish at 20–30 ft in 60–100 ft of water, fished
  with tension on the free spool and periodic click-into-gear-and-wind to re-set
  the sinker to the bait. The Tady TLC / Tady 40 glow-backed single-hook jig,
  baited with 1–3 whole squid (check with the deckhands before using more than
  one — squid supply is often limited), dropped to bottom and cranked up 3–5 or
  6 to 10 ft, then left in the rod holder to rise and fall with the boat's roll.
  Sudden Impact 8 in flukes on a Gitsum or Warbaits Neck Breaker head, **1/2–3/4 oz
  on a beautiful day, 1–2 oz in more current or wind**, worked sink-pop-flutter.
  The garlic-scented WSB Special hookup bait in **5/8 oz, 1 oz and 1.5 oz**,
  fished with thumb tension on the sink and one pop every 5–10 seconds, "do not
  blink on the sink" because the bite comes on the fall — and on a missed tap,
  wind into it rather than swinging, then free-spool and pop to draw a second
  bite.
- `X-itrm5QkcM` (Tackle Express; unregistered channel): the flanking-fluke
  argument — the bait rail's effective swath is narrow (roughly 30–40 ft by the
  boat's length), so casters working the flanks cover more total water, and since
  white seabass swim through rather than hold still the two presentations are
  complementary rather than competing.
- `hjZ3hgq0Bzg` (Tackle Express; unregistered channel): the erratic-crack
  retrieve mechanics for a fluke on a lead head — chop-glide, sweep-drop,
  crack-slack — logged in
  [leadhead swimbait retrieve](../../techniques/leadhead-swimbait-retrieve.md).

## Gear summary (class terms)

- `Eo_bA8IWvyU` (same walkthrough, rod/reel/line): conventional star-drag reels
  favour the dropper loop and low-profile baitcasters favour casting artificials;
  no two-speed is needed. Line is called the most important variable — "seabass
  have really soft mouths, to where if you don't have any mono, if you're running
  straight braid, there's a very high probability that you might just simply yank
  the hook out of his face" — so **no less than 50–100 yd of mono**, up to half a
  spool or a full spool, a lighter drag than usual except against nasty kelp or
  rock, and a soft/parabolic rod to compensate when spool capacity forces braid.
  Rods named: a 7 ft 11 in medium-heavy 15–40 lb parabolic swimbait rod, a 7 ft
  8 in glass/graphite composite 25–60 lb fished with 30–40 lb line (40 lb the
  most common for the dropper loop), and an 8 ft 25–40 lb moderate composite
  rated 3/4–3 oz.
- `9MzNNwHiBXs` (Tackle Express, charter bait-tank hook kit; unregistered
  channel): Owner Flyliner **1/0 and 2** for **5–7 in** sardines and mackerel as
  the fin-bait backup when squid is unavailable — the same size pair and the same
  bait range given independently in `Eo_bA8IWvyU`, which is what resolved the
  earlier asr-uncertain flag on those sizes.
- `QEmxUIGmKbo` (Tackle Express, 2026-07-08 upload, "Ditch the heavy tuna gear";
  unregistered channel): Daiwa Lexa, Penn Fathom and Shimano Tranx named as the
  casting-reel step down from the heavy tuna-class reels anglers over-bring for
  this species, with spinning rods called a good option for anglers new to
  conventional gear.

## Doctrine & conflicts

- `Z3rZqy2Pi8E` (BDOutdoors, 2022-11-23, San Clemente Island): late-fall seabass
  and bass wanted off-color water with the current running **into** the island —
  the seasonal exception to the same author's parallel-current prior in
  `6TBxHnkYXI0`. Both statements stand; the parent carries the decision frame.
- `Eo_bA8IWvyU` (garlic mechanism): the WSB Special hookup bait's factory garlic
  scent is framed as a primary attractant — "it has 17 out of the 19 amino acids,
  which is going to grab the attention of those fish, it's triggering all their
  predatory instincts." The HookUp Baits co-owner describes the same brand's
  garlic differently, as a cover scent masking the plastic's manufacturing smell,
  in [tube baits](../../lures/tube-baits.md#scent). Two speakers, one product
  line, two stated mechanisms.
- `Y2bXn44lfqo` (hook-style tension): the Ahi-twist-J vs circle-hook conflict on
  the dropper loop, and the release-only reasoning behind it, moved with the

## Hatchery & broodstock program (Catalina collection trips)

Program and trip context, not fishing doctrine any angler can replicate. Flagged
`⚠ misplaced-content` in the parent and queued in
[`sources/relocation-queue.md`](../../sources/relocation-queue.md).

- `xFS3MW4GpDU` (StokedOnFishing, 2023-08-23, Catalina Island; unregistered
  channel): the crew — volunteers Jock and Charlie Albright, fishing the
  Hatchery's collection permit — hold this program's fishing to **shallower than
  60 ft, most of it in 40 ft or less**, because past roughly **70 ft** white
  seabass are "very sensitive," their air bladders inflate on the way up, and
  reviving them for the net pen becomes very difficult. A live-transport
  constraint for the broodstock program, not a general depth or bite rule; the
  general island squid-nest depths are 60–100+ ft. Trip timed to May/June (see
  Where & when above).
- `Y2bXn44lfqo` (StokedOnFishing, 2023-08-30, Catalina Island; unregistered
  channel): the crew fished a Catalina MLPA (Marine Protected Area) under a
  **dedicated collection permit** to gather live broodstock for the Carlsbad
  hatchery program, not as recreational fishing — fishing inside an MLPA is
  otherwise closed to the public (California / CDFW MLPA network, as of
  2026-08-14; verify current regs and boundaries before assuming any access). All
  non-target catch (halibut, yellowtail in the 20–30 lb range, calico bass) was
  released. Landed white seabass were held in a covered/pressurized holding tank
  (a "fish donut"), scanned for an implanted PIT tag near the cheek to log
  recapture history, and moved to a net pen within about **45 minutes** of
  landing; large fish are prone to flipping upside down in the transport tank,
  and the crew described a fish found that way, still breathing and calm, as
  normal so long as it reaches the pen inside that window.
- `82gEHYel-4U` (StokedOnFishing, 2023-09-06; unregistered channel): the effort
  runs through the **Hubbs-SeaWorld Research Institute** (HSWRI, hswri.org),
  described on camera as started in **1992**; a volunteer grow-out net-pen
  facility in Newport Harbor — one volunteer's tenure dates to 1993 — was the
  first of its kind in Orange County and was still operating as of this
  2023-09-06 upload, having released roughly **3,000 fish in early August 2023**
  (resolved from "three or four weeks ago" relative to the upload date). This
  trip delivered the program's **40th fish** to the net pens across the last
  three trips (**22 from this trip alone**), described by the crew as more fish
  than the same three-trip stretch in 2022. Program leadership credited new
  DNA/genetic studies with confirming — their framing, no longer just
  "experimental" — that the hatchery measurably benefits the wild white seabass
  fishery; a program-status claim from the operators, not a fishing-technique
  claim. A name given on camera for a program founder/leader was garbled by
  auto-captions in two different ways and is omitted rather than guessed (see the
  extraction log for this video). asr-uncertain(founder name).
- `zn4n7k3iaZo` (StokedOnFishing, 2023-08-09, Bill Shedd — HSWRI chairman, CCA
  California chairman; unregistered channel): the hatchery effort traces to the
  **late 1970s**, when Milt Shedd (Bill Shedd's father) funded HSWRI and tasked
  a then-graduate student, Don Kent, with figuring out how to raise white seabass
  after the species had become scarce in the sport-fishing catch — earlier than
  the "started in 1992" figure above, which may instead mark when the Carlsbad
  hatchery facility itself (as against the research effort) came online; not
  reconciled. The formal **OREHP — Ocean Resources Enhancement (and Hatchery)
  Program** — followed via California legislation **in the 1980s**; it is a
  California Department of Fish and Wildlife (formerly Fish and Game) program run
  in partnership with HSWRI as the research/hatchery arm and with the
  sport-fishing community — United Anglers of Southern California early on, now
  **CCA California** — organizing broodstock collection and the volunteer
  grow-out network. California Coastal Commission and SONGS (San Onofre Nuclear
  Generating Station) mitigation funding also factor into the funding history. As
  of this 2023-08-09 upload, Shedd put cumulative releases at **over 2 million,
  "close to two and a half million"** hatchery white seabass, reared through
  **eight or nine** volunteer grow-out facilities up and down the California
  coast, representing an estimated **30,000–40,000+ cumulative volunteer hours**.
- `zn4n7k3iaZo` (same source, tag-return discrepancy): program critics — some
  within CDFW, some outside the program — point to **less than 1% of tested adult
  wild-caught white seabass carrying a tag** as evidence the hatchery has not
  meaningfully supplemented the wild population. Shedd and program supporters
  counter that the state's **gillnet survey** of juvenile (sub-legal-size) white
  seabass repeatedly finds **double-digit percentages** of hatchery-origin fish,
  which is hard to square with adult returns under 1%. The standing theory
  offered for the gap is that the tag, described as "as big as a flea," works
  loose or loses its magnetization with age. New genetic-marker studies, results
  pending at the time of the video (expected "within the next few months" of
  2023-08-09), aim to resolve it; Shedd's stated expectation on camera is that
  they will show a materially higher hatchery-fish percentage than the sub-1%
  figure. duplicate-of the program-origin entry above (same session, different
  claim). Not a settled fact — attributed program claims, not fishing doctrine.
