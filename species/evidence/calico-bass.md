# Evidence — Calico Bass (Kelp Bass)

Trip reports and per-source provenance backing
[calico bass](../calico-bass.md). One line per observation, grouped under the
parent note's section headings, in its order. Observations never change the
parent's stated doctrine; one that materially supports or contradicts it also
leaves a one-line cited trace beside that doctrine in the parent.

Most of this file is StokedOnFishing footage — an unregistered channel, so
every entry from it caps at medium confidence and several are sponsor-heavy
or reaction-only episodes. Provenance tails record that per entry.

## Where & when — SoCal coast and islands

- `zUFbCIWZZMw` (StokedOnFishing, 2014-11-03 upload, Bird Rock, Catalina
  Island): an anchored stop produced 3–4 lb calico alongside yellowtail
  (confirmed on live squid) and barracuda from the same anchor — see the
  parallel entry in [yellowtail](../yellowtail.md). Sponsor-heavy Simrad
  electronics/autopilot demo episode; unregistered channel.
- `_KldpqPPT1c` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2019-05-23, outside Cat Harbor,
  Catalina): anchored just outside the harbor, not inside it, for a steady,
  not-wide-open kelp bite — consistent bites with hooked fish repeatedly
  running into the kelp. No current, water-colour or tide detail given.
- `oadK6zIYyCo` (StokedOnFishing, 2016-12-23 upload, Catalina Island): a
  family day at anchor with live squid and sardine both soaking and plastics
  also offered; a small calico came up on the **sardine**, not the squid.
  Sponsor-heavy dealership-ad episode; unregistered channel.
- `EU_Dod4wfYw` (StokedOnFishing w/ Capt. Benny Florentino, 2012-03-22, Santa
  Barbara Island): a wide-open kelp bite, multiple quality calico in quick
  succession including a fish estimated at 4½–5 lb; kelp present at the spot
  and the crew remarked there had been noticeably more kelp on a prior visit.
  No current/water-colour/tide detail; audio quality poor, no further
  technique detail reliably extractable.
- `9xNhdu2aBqE` (StokedOnFishing, 2015-10-28, San Clemente Island West End): a
  green-stained, near-opaque water colour with roughly 5–10 ft visibility
  called out as the preferred read for kelp-edge bass — the stain gives fish
  "a place to hide" and makes them more comfortable feeding up in the open;
  paired with moving current ("fish eat when the current's good," read off
  whether the kelp is laid down under the surface vs. floating slack on top).
  Unregistered channel, single mention.
- `HMdrP4-i9MM` (StokedOnFishing, 2022-10-11, off North County San Diego near
  Camp Pendleton, boat departed Dana Point and worked south): an annual
  Okuma-employee kids/family trip aboard a Dana Wharf Sportfishing charter
  (captain named on camera as Brian, boat name not stated) found calico over
  hard bottom with light kelp growth that did not reach the surface — kelp
  riding roughly 20 ft off the bottom in ~40–42 ft of water. Pre-trip
  game-plan announcement also flagged barracuda and 25–30 lb "green fish"
  (yellowtail) recently seen in the same stretch, though the day stayed on
  calico and [sand bass](../sand-bass.md). Techniques worked:
  [dropper loop](../../techniques/dropper-loop.md) for the kids,
  [flylined](../../techniques/flyline.md) sardine, a strip of squid or sardine
  fished dropper-style, and an unspecified soft plastic ("rubber lure"). Legal
  size called out on
  camera as 14 in for both kelp bass and sand bass. Unregistered channel.
- `M6U_FVdosr4` (StokedOnFishing, 2013-07-04 upload, Dana Point kelp bed, Sun
  Fun out of Dana Wharf Sportfishing, "Dana Wharf Kids Club Sunday's," a
  recurring Sunday second-half-day trip): the boat anchored rather than
  drifted on a light current running through the kelp bed. Speaker roles are
  not diarized in the source; attribution to specific crew is inferred from
  context. Unregistered channel. See the squid/sardine reversal under Doctrine
  & conflicts below.

## Doctrine & conflicts

- `M6U_FVdosr4` (StokedOnFishing, 2013-07-04 upload, Dana Point kelp bed): a
  same-day bait-choice reversal. Before departure, boat crew called a strip of
  squid over a slab-sardine strip for the trip, backing it with that morning's
  first-trip tally — 5 sand bass and 3 calico, every one on a strip of squid.
  By the day's end, crew (Shane, role unspecified in the transcript) reported
  the opposite: bass were "actually biting the smaller sardines today," which
  he called a surprise. The two results sit side by side. Unregistered channel.
- `oadK6zIYyCo` (StokedOnFishing, 2016-12-23 upload, Catalina Island):
  duplicate-of the Catalina entry above, kept here as the squid-vs-sardine
  data point — with both live squid and sardine soaking, the calico took the
  sardine. Unregistered channel.
- `iQLyBzhOSi8` (StokedOnFishing, "California Offshore Challenge, part 1,"
  day-one setup, Two Harbors, Catalina Island): the dockside briefing named a
  stack of live variables without resolving them into a plan ("we don't have a
  game plan just yet") — a full moon, "pretty big tides," "pretty extreme
  temperatures," and heavy squid around the island. Swell was tracked day by
  day across the event window: 5–7 ft the day before day one, 4–6 ft on day
  one, 4–6 ft forecast for day two, 3–5 ft forecast for the following Sunday —
  a generally easing trend; a small craft advisory was mentioned in passing for
  part of the field. The intro framed the event's typical January timing as a
  hazard combination at "the local Islands" of thick kelp, razor-sharp boiler
  rocks and big waves that "can ruin the day," and organizers warned anglers to
  be careful of the big swells that day. One team ran for San Clemente Island
  ("Clemente"/"the big island") rather than stay at Catalina, citing "some
  really big swells" for the crossing; that team landed at Northwest Harbor —
  a spot this KB documents (see [chunking](../../techniques/chunking.md) and
  [the spot list](../../sources/spot-lists.md)) — where the water was dirty and
  washed out, with conditions described as running from "a different direction
  than we normally get," and a little current noted along the front side. The
  team worked back north from the harbor, then down the front side. Dating: the
  `IATPg9110CE`/`PKf7G3uL4io` entries date the fourth-annual event to roughly
  early November 2014 off the shared 2014-11-11 upload, but this video's
  on-camera slate reads "at Cat[alina] Twin Harbor's 2012, fourth annual COOC"
  (~00:03:29), so the footage may document the 2012 running with 2014-11-11
  only a later upload/re-upload date. No visibility or current-strength given,
  and nothing confirms the "different direction" remark meant current rather
  than swell or wind. Unregistered channel.
- `IATPg9110CE` (StokedOnFishing, "California Offshore Challenge, part 3,"
  event dated to roughly early November 2014 per the 2014-11-11 upload, exact
  on-camera date not given): day two of the fourth annual tournament (weigh-ins
  at Two Harbors, Catalina) saw team Killer Whale fish Santa Barbara Island's
  kelp/rock structure while team Salty stayed at Catalina. Killer Whale's
  day-one run had gone to San Nicholas Island then Santa Barbara Island, where
  they found only a small beach bite — Santa Barbara Island itself was "blown
  out" on day one; by day two "the swell coming down" had improved it enough
  that the crew skipped a longer run and worked the island in place. There they
  worked fish tight against the rocks ("all the way up against that rock") and
  through a narrow gap between two rocks, landing a five-fish limit that added
  24 lb to their day-one weight for a 47 lb two-day total — enough to win.
  Team Salty weighed a comparatively poor 16 lb on day two after leading day
  one with 26 lb, no reason given for Catalina's drop-off. A data point for
  Santa Barbara Island kelp/rock structure producing once swell drops, not
  evidence about Catalina's conditions; no current or water-colour detail.
  Unregistered channel.
- `PKf7G3uL4io` (StokedOnFishing, "California Offshore Challenge, part 2," same
  fourth-annual tournament and 2014-11-11 upload as `IATPg9110CE`): in a
  day-one wrap interview the Catalina-based team Salty (nicknamed "Salty Balls"
  here) described day one at Catalina as "real tough, small fish in the
  morning," worked through roughly 30 to 40 spots, and finished with five nice
  fish including an angler's personal-best calico at 8.96 lb. They called out a
  deliberate change from past tournaments — "you got to hold onto the kicker"

  they hadn't done "the past couple years" despite otherwise good bags. Their
  day-one target was 25 lb; they weighed 26-plus, matching the 26 lb day-one
  total in `IATPg9110CE`. Framed as the team's fourth year chasing a Catalina
  win without closing it out (prior second/third-place finishes, no absolute
  years given). Unregistered channel.

## Regulations

  species. Unregistered channel.
