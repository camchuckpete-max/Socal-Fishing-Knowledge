# Evidence — Striped Marlin

Per-source provenance backing [striped marlin](../striped-marlin.md). One line
per observation, grouped under the parent note's section headings, in its order.
Observations never change the parent's stated doctrine; a contradicting one is
also traced beside that doctrine in the parent.

**Confidence note (demoted from the parent's prose, 2026-08-27):** the note is
`confidence: medium` because the corpus holds no dedicated SoCal marlin seminar.
The SoCal doctrine — fall timing, scattered fish, slack-tide biter, clean water
off the bait edge, the outrigger-and-circling fleet tell — comes from
single-to-repeated mentions across the 2022–2025 BDOutdoors Bight reports plus
one on-the-water marlin trip on an unregistered channel. Every Baja record is a
charter trip on that same unregistered channel, region-labeled rather than
merged into the SoCal doctrine.

## Presence & forage

- `h3PTupup17I` (BDOutdoors / SoCal Bight Fishing Academy — Erik Landesfeind,
  oceanography episode): the summer uphill current pulling water up from Mexico
  hits the lower end of the offshore banks, creates upwelling, makes the
  phytoplankton, brings in the zooplankton, attracts the bait fish, "and then
  the tuna Marlin and everything else show up along with that." Registered
  voice; the KB's mechanism source for why the banks hold pelagics.
- `9xHgdtNek1U` (Your Saltwater Guide — Capt. Dave Hansen, flyline rigs
  explained): the presentation-by-species list — "I like the fly line mackerel
  for marlin," against sardines for tuna/bass/barracuda/bonito/yellowtail and
  anchovies for calico bass. Registered voice; one line inside a general
  flylining tutorial, not a marlin program.

## Feeding triggers

- `6TBxHnkYXI0` (BDOutdoors, 2025-02-20, SoCal, studio report): the slack-tide
  doctrine as stated — "a lot of times fish will bite on the slack tide, bluefin
  come up foaming on a slack tide, marlin bite on a slack tide" — with the
  mechanism credited on camera to Sean Morgan ("he's got a lot of marlin"): at
  the slack of the high or low the ocean goes quiet, the water stops moving up
  and down, and the fish can hunt better. The operational rule attached: be in
  your highest-percentage spot an hour before a tide change. Same segment gives
  a downhill (north-to-south) current as the general coastal-spot rule of thumb.
- `3qSY328fFYo` (StokedOnFishing, presenter/angler Capt. Jimmy Decker,
  2014-11-08 upload, SoCal — the tanker lanes about 17 mi out, plotted as
  19.2 mi at 193°, then on toward the **277 bank** at the bottom of the ridge off
  Catalina's east end; unregistered channel — medium confidence): the corpus's
  only worked SoCal marlin trip, on a center console. Tide timing as given on
  camera — a high at **7:30**, called "a pretty rolling tide," and a low stated
  twice, first as roughly **11:30–12:00** (ASR-garbled at that point, not
  confidently resolved) and later restated as **11:15**; the two readings are
  kept side by side rather than reconciled, and are ledgered as single-source.
  The stated slack mechanism: "when the tide goes slack the bait fish pop up,
  come closer to the surface and the fish come up," with jig strikes "an hour or
  so before or after the tides." Result: a striped marlin estimated on camera at
  roughly **160 lb**, hooked in the corner of the mouth on a flying-fish-pattern
  swimming lure and released — the radio call is "I just released one at 14 and
  04 on a flying fish," so the fish ate the jig, not the dropback bait (the
  pre-v2 note read it as taken on a live mackerel dropback once the fish showed
  in the spread; that reading is retired, not conserved as fact). Mid-fight the
  angler said "now I wish it was 50 lb tackle," which places the outfit lighter
  than 50 lb. Full spread setup, the troll-downhill sun rule and sounder use
  from this same stop are logged in
  [striped marlin — trolling evidence](striped-marlin-trolling.md), and the
  dropback-bait mechanics in
  [bait-and-switch](../../techniques/bait-and-switch.md).
- `3qSY328fFYo` (same stop, temperature detail): target water was **68°F**, the
  fished water read **69.3** then **69.4°F**, and mid-morning the boat crossed a
  break of about **two-tenths of a degree** after a friend on another boat
  reported a fish up on the colder side; they worked the cold side afterward and
  the strike followed a troll past a kelp paddy with the graph "all lit up with
  bait." duplicate-of the entry above (same session, different claim).

## Finding them (sign & sonar)

- `3qSY328fFYo` (same stop, search detail): the console sounder was set to
  display to **120 ft** to keep bait depth visible under way (100 ft / 30 ft /
  20 ft), cross-checked against sauries greyhounding at the surface; mackerel was
  identified on the meter. The jig-watch rule — a fish "can come up and take a
  whack out of a jig but not pop the outrigger or pop the rubber band," so
  without a dedicated watcher "you might miss four or five fish throughout the
  course of the day or you think you just got popped off by a piece of kelp."
  Closing tips: catch a few green-back mackerel for dropback bait before you go,
  do the homework and pull up-to-date marlin reports, buy the best binoculars you
  can afford, and bring patience — experienced guys go days on end with no bites
  or sign. duplicate-of the Feeding-triggers entries above (same session).

## Situations → techniques

- `e73wPONTOJU` (StokedOnFishing, 2022-10-06 upload, SoCal island zone;
  unregistered channel — medium confidence): after the crew gave up on the
  morning's bluefin bite and turned to run down toward kelp paddies for
  dorado/yellowtail, they hooked and landed a striped marlin — called on camera
  "first Marlin I've seen yet" / "first one we've got a chance to actually put on
  the boat," the boat's first marlin of the trip, and put in the deep freezer.
  The parent's pre-v2 reading was that what was in the water at the moment of the
  bite is not clearly identifiable on camera (audio too garbled to confirm lure
  or bait), so it is not logged as a targeted technique; the transcript also
  carries "she saw him hit the Jigs," which points at the trolled spread. Both
  readings kept; distinct from the dedicated bait-and-switch program in the
  parent's table.
- `m2q22sPPkEM` (BDOutdoors — Capt. Dave Hansen, 2019-09-10, hook-to-bait
  matching, filmed just after four months guiding out of Cabo San Lucas):
  "there was a lot of fish down there, a lot of my clients don't understand when
  they're bit, so we use these [circle] hooks for hooking the mackerel — we nose
  hook them… the tension of the water alone will set the hook in the fish's
  mouth… this will actually hook the fish in the corner of the mouth," with
  "because my guys can't feel the bite 90% of the time"; for anglers who can feel
  it, a long-shank J hook, chosen because a longer shank "won't have a tendency
  to get sawed off so much." Registered voice, but a Cabo (`baja-pacific-south`)
  charter fishery — no SoCal source states a dropback hook style. Hook size for
  the marlin mackerel is not given cleanly (the same segment gives 4/0 thin wire
  for sardines, No. 4 for anchovies, and only "the way bigger hook for
  mackerel"); asr-uncertain on the circle-hook size.

## Landing & handling

- `Dq1x__MI8Wk` (Your Saltwater Guide — Capt. Dave Hansen, 2022-01-08, dockside
  fillet demonstration): striped marlin called "one of my very most favorite fish
  to eat" and "the very best marlin to eat" against blue, black and white marlin;
  **pink meat** is the stated quality tell ("if you can get them pink meat like
  this, that means it's really going to be good to eat"); the bloodline and
  darker meat are trimmed out of each fillet, the skin is filleted off rather
  than torn, and the rib racks go to the smokehouse. Registered voice; no
  bleeding, chilling or at-the-boat handling is shown or described.
