# Swordfish (Broadbill)

SoCal broadbill are a **daytime deep-drop** fishery built on one fact: the fish
ride the [deep scattering layer](../conditions/deep-scattering-layer.md) by day,
so you hunt the layer and its bait over deep contour structure in **150–350
fathoms** and hang a dead bait in or just below it (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`). It is
weather-gated and seasonal — **late September through December**, and off the
table above **15 kt** of wind (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`). Hooking is not landing: one San
Diego program put the hook in and pulled on **30-plus** fish across the 2019 and
2020 seasons and rates getting them into the boat the hard part (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
This note routes; the method is
[deep-drop swordfishing](../techniques/deep-drop-swordfishing.md).

## Where & when

**Season — late September into December, peaking October to mid-November.** The
bite runs with the [fall fragmentation regime](../conditions/water-regimes.md)
entered from late warm-stable. It is interannual: 2019 ran hot from middle-to-late
September through a red-hot October, while 2020 peaked around **10 days before
Thanksgiving**; the last fish of 2019 came about **a week before Christmas**
(`yMiBtZ7k8-w`). See [October](../seasonal/october.md),
[November–December](../seasonal/november-december.md).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the seminar names the window as "a
September, late September, in the December thing" and gives two divergent annual
peaks — 2019 late September into a red-hot October, 2020 about 10 days before
Thanksgiving. A continuous "October to mid-November" peak is never stated; it is
the envelope of two years that did not overlap.

**Presence vs catchability.** Holdover fish are around year-round, but running
the same program in August can go **five trips without a bite**; September
produces a couple; by October it is **a bite a trip if not more**
(`yMiBtZ7k8-w`). January through August the fish are there and the fuel is not
justified (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`) — that spans the winter/upwelling, warming-transition
and early-to-peak warm-stable regimes. By October the swordfish sit in the
general offshore mix alongside marlin, bluefin and yellowfin (`XLVUhV8DW64`).

**Where — deep contour structure, 150–350 fathoms.** The hot bites come on
**contour lines, thumbs, notches and humps** in that band (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`);
worked ground includes the Ridge, the 9 Mile Bank, the 14 Mile Bank, the 43,
the [178](../locations/north-9-mile-bank-178.md), the
[302](../locations/coronados-230-302-226.md), and the
[138](../locations/51-181-138.md) and [182](../locations/corner-140-182.md) on
break days (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`). A worked
drift at the 178 sat in about **1,090 ft**, inside the **900–1,400 ft** band the
fishery covers (`KuVwmfF6RAo`). The west-coast model is to **set up and let the
fish come along the fathom-curve highways**, where east-coast crews go to their
fish (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`). See [island structure](../locations/island-structure.md),
[zone lexicon](../locations/zone-lexicon.md).

**Fishable day.** Above **15 kt** nobody fishes — you drift too fast to present a
bait; **10–13 kt** is the working maximum in the traditional northwest wind, and
above roughly **2–2.5 kt** of drift the presentation dies (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`). A
**drift sock** extends marginal days and keeps the bow into the current
(`yMiBtZ7k8-w`). See [sea-state](../conditions/sea-state.md); drift mechanics in
the [technique note](../techniques/deep-drop-swordfishing.md).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): both gates are hedged on camera. The wind
line is "anything over 15 knots guys aren't really wanting to fish, you're moving
really fast" — a preference, not "nobody fishes" — and the drift figure is given
as the range actually fished, "anywhere from a one to two, two and a half knot
drift… I'm not getting the presentation I like," i.e. presentation degrading
inside the range rather than a hard 2.5 kt cutoff.
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the drift sock is an untried purchase in
the source — "bought a drift sock this year… to be honest I just haven't put it
out yet" — and both benefits are stated as expectation ("I think it will extend
the number of days"), not as practice.

**Water.** Pick the day off SST and chlorophyll breaks — converging water masses
that concentrate bait — and prefer **one big powerful long break, a "highway,"**
over a field of finger rips (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`). See
[water color](../conditions/water-color.md),
[water temperature](../conditions/water-temperature.md).

## Presence & forage

**Why they are on the ground: the layer.** The DSL is a band of life **50 ft to
300–400 ft thick** that rises at night to feed and sinks back to 600–900 ft by
day; whether it is plankton, hake or squid, it is the reason a predator is there
(`yMiBtZ7k8-w`). Satellite tagging by the PIER institute — fish tagged from
harpoon boats rather than harpooned — put the fish **in the layer during the
day**, and that finding is what opened the recreational fishery
(`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).

**Stomach contents (SoCal fish).** Hake and small suspending rockfish dominate,
with squid on some fish; one fish dumped roughly **seven pounds** of small
suspending rockfish on the scale (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`). Rockfish only live within
**50–100 ft of the bottom**, so a sword carrying them has been rooting on the
bottom, not only cruising the layer (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).

**They range off the deep ground.** A Catalina-caught fish held **30–40
regulation-sized mullet**, a bait that does not live offshore — that fish had
been up in the kelp on the beach (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
⚠ Fact-check (single-source): the Catalina mullet fish is relayed second-hand in
the seminar, with the angler unnamed and no date; it is the corpus's only
evidence that SoCal swords feed inshore.

**Squid.** Big market squid are seen in the deck lights at the same banks where
the fishery drops baits, which is why they are the standard hook bait
(`yMiBtZ7k8-w`). Giant squid hold in the same areas swordfish hold in, and a
year with them around shreds any bait put down there (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the deck-light sighting is given on camera
as one captain's personal reason for liking the bait — "I like them because
they're big, I like them because I see them in our deck lights all the time at
our local banks" — not as the fishery's reason for a standard. The hook baits
themselves are big squid bought "at our local foreign markets" and through
wholesale seafood distributors, and are never identified as the same squid seen
in the deck lights.

**How they eat.** Swordfish are clumsy, unselective feeders — anything with a
wiggle to it gets eaten (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`). They are also bad at it: fish routinely
get balled up in the bait instead of eating it cleanly, and one came to the boat
snagged in the tail (`1iV1GnF4K-c`).

## Feeding triggers

- **Moon.** Bites go up on the **new and full moon and the weeks in front of and
  behind them** (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`); fishing around the moon is the stock advice for
  anyone starting in this fishery. See [moon](../conditions/moon.md).
  ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: mechanism — the connection is stated as
  observed and explicitly not understood.
- **Light, through the layer.** The DSL rises at night and sinks by day, and the
  fish go with it — which is why night swordfish are caught **shallow**, and why
  the daytime bait depth tracks wherever the layer settles (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
- **A falling bait.** Bites cluster in the **first five to ten minutes** of a
  drift, on the rod deployed first, while the bait is still going down: sinking
  against the lead the bait swims fast, as though fleeing, where a bait already
  at depth just sits there (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
- **Structure crossed by a current edge.** Bites come in bunches when the break
  lies on the structure — the "banger days" where the whole fleet gets whacked at
  once, on ground that could not buy a bite a week earlier (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`). See
  [current structure](../conditions/current-structure.md).
- ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: any tide or slack window for SoCal
  swordfish. The only tide-and-current planning in the corpus is Florida Keys
  practice, where the drift itself is the constraint.

## Finding them (sign & sonar)

**Surface sign comes first.** Hunt **rips, scum lines and converging water
masses**; once in the zone, find the **biggest "highway rip"** in it and work
that rather than a field of finger rips (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`). Whales, dolphins and
birds over a spot are worth stopping for even though the bait goes down a
thousand feet below them — the mammals are on the same food (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`). See
[search & glassing](../planning/search-and-glassing.md),
[bird reading](../conditions/bird-reading.md).

**What the meter has to show before you stop:**

- **DSL present at ≥600 ft**, preferred **~950 ft / 150 fathoms or deeper**
  (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-internal): the corpus's only filmed SoCal sword
  drift works a feed layer "down about 50 or so fathoms, 300, 350 feet" in about
  1,090 ft of water at the 178 (`KuVwmfF6RAo`) — half the depth this stop
  criterion sets as the minimum, on the same ground, and the two numbers are
  never reconciled.
- **DSL *plus* chunky bait balls** — anchovy, hake or squid balled and squeezed —
  at **100–150+ fathoms**, which is when bites come close to assured
  (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`). A bare layer with no bait in it is not a stop.
- **The fish themselves paint as a long return**, not the boomerang shape bait
  and schooling fish give, and sit **200–300 ft under the feed layer**
  (`KuVwmfF6RAo`). Reading a return from 1,000+ ft takes gain and time-variable
  gain pushed hard and the ping speed backed off; the unit-by-unit settings live
  in [electronics & sounder](../planning/electronics-and-sounder.md)
  (`KuVwmfF6RAo`, `yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
- **Do not blind-drop.** Find the feed layer and the structure first; the meter
  is the tool that decides the stop (`KuVwmfF6RAo`).

**Surface fish.** Finning swordfish are sighted in SoCal — the claim from crews
who run offshore for days at a time is that there are far more of them than
people think, and that seeing them is a function of looking at the water
correctly (`1iV1GnF4K-c`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the cited exchange says there are "a lot
more daytime swords" than people know "when you're looking at the water
correctly" — it never says finning, and never names SoCal. The only finning
description in that source is the Brazil aggregation, which the same crew says
"we don't see here, no, never see that."
⚠ Flagged stub — no corpus source yet: the sight-fishing sequence on a finner
(approach, bait, cast) is absent from the corpus. A spotter plane worked the
island zone in October hunting swordfish while it watched a
[bluefin](bluefin-tuna.md) foamer that was still up there (`OYOda6T3f-8`).

## Gear summary (class terms)

- **Rod/reel — deep-drop / swordfish class:** heavy stand-up or bent-butt,
  2-speed lever-drag, run **manual or electric-assist**. The SoCal fight is
  usually made from the rod holder on the electric, because most fish swim up
  against the lead rather than pulling against the rod (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): "2-speed lever-drag" is never said in
  the cited seminar; the only drag hardware named for the SoCal program is a star
  drag ("we start a little heavier, but we're using star drag"). The stand-up /
  bent-butt build and the manual-or-electric choice are stated.
- **Line:** **80 lb braid** main line, chosen to cut the water on a drifty day
  (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`). See [line & leader](../tackle/line-and-leader.md).
- **Terminal:** heavy **bite-leader class** (200 lb+) on a short leader, with a
  breakaway **7–10.5 lb** lead matched to the drift (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`). Parameters
  in the [technique note](../techniques/deep-drop-swordfishing.md) and
  [bite leaders](../rigging/bite-leaders.md); class terms in
  [gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md).
- **Bait:** dead baits fished deep — squid is the SoCal standard; a big mackerel
  was tried once and swam the leader up, and was dropped (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).

## Doctrine & conflicts

Section order below is not a ranking.

**Structure or the break — an open question, not settled doctrine.** The same
break runs across the structure and then out into the escarpment or canyon; the
structure fishes for a couple of weeks or a moon phase, goes quiet, and boats on
buoy gear keep scoring out in the deep water. When to stay on the structure and
when to follow the break off it is stated on camera as the unsolved part of the
SoCal fishery (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: the signal that says to leave the structure.

**Region label, not a conflict: SoCal has a season, the Florida Keys do not.**
The Keys daytime fishery is described as producing in any month with a weather
window, while SoCal timing is treated as decisive (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`). Two
fisheries, two answers. East-coast deep-drop numbers — bottom-oriented at about
1,800 ft, heavier leads, long leaders — sit in the labeled contrast block in
[deep-drop swordfishing](../techniques/deep-drop-swordfishing.md) and do not
transfer to the Bight.

## Landing & handling

- **The mouth is soft.** The roof of a swordfish's mouth is soft enough to push a
  finger through toward the brain, and fish pull off when heat is put on them;
  the hookups that hold are the **corner of the mouth** or deep in the gullet
  (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`). That anatomy is what drives the low-drag doctrine in the
  [technique note](../techniques/deep-drop-swordfishing.md).
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-internal): the technique note holds no low-drag
  doctrine to point at. Its "Drag & the endgame (SoCal)" section states the
  opposite frame — the setting "turns on how much resistance the rig already
  carries, not on a single correct number," both captains start *heavier* at
  strike (18–20 lb on one build) so the hook buries, and "the setting is more a
  preference than a critical one" because a buried circle hook holds regardless.
- **The fight changes at 30 ft**, when the breakaway lead comes off and the load
  transfers from the weight to the rod tip; from there a fish at a standstill
  darts under the boat and into the motors, so the boat stays in gear
  (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-internal):
  [deep-drop swordfishing](../techniques/deep-drop-swordfishing.md) states under
  Bite handling that "the breakaway lead drops on the hookset so you fight the
  fish clean" — the weight gone at the strike, not at 30 ft — which would mean
  the whole fight is fought clean rather than against the lead, and would remove
  the transfer this bullet describes. The same technique note's endgame section
  agrees with this router (lead taken off by hand at the boat), so the two notes
  disagree about when the weight leaves the line.
- **Expect an early look.** Swordfish will come up to the boat inside the first
  ten minutes of a fight to check the boat out, long before they are done
  (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
- **Kill shot to the gill plate** — the flying gaff goes in the gill plate,
  preferably under the vertebra and back through, then gaffs and a tail rope;
  the sequence is in the
  [technique note](../techniques/deep-drop-swordfishing.md) (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
- ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: bleeding, chilling, ikejime or butchering a
  swordfish kept to eat. General care: [fish care](../fish-care/README.md).
