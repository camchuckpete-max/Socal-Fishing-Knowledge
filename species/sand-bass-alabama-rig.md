# Barred Sand Bass — Alabama rig

**One species, one technique.** The A-rig is the multi-bait swing for a
dedicated sand bass fisherman: a lob cast rather than a hard one because the
arms break off, bites taken on the sink, then into gear at or near the bottom
for a low-and-slow cruise back up through the column, deliberately standing off
the structure (`P2OzCf2CwXI`). Reach for it when a spawning school is balled up
— one bite fires the next until four or five fish, sometimes a limit, come in on
a single cast — and equally on the days the fish are *not* fired up
(`P2OzCf2CwXI`). The wire-harness hardware, hook-count rule and knot are in
[swimbaits — umbrella rig / A-rig](../techniques/swimbaits.md#multi-bait-rigs);
this page is what changes because the fish is a barred sand bass.

## When this is the call

**Both ends of the mood range.** It is the one sand bass program that applies to
fish that are really fired up *and* to fish that are really not
(`P2OzCf2CwXI`) — see
[barred sand bass — feeding triggers](sand-bass.md#feeding-triggers). It has its
own row in
[barred sand bass — situations → techniques](sand-bass.md#situations--techniques),
sitting beside the two-bait double rig, which chases the same followers one bait
at a time.

**The spawning meatball is the picture.** A tight ball of fish mid-screen over a
sand flat in summer is what turns the chain reaction on; the sonar read and the
spawn timing are in
[barred sand bass — finding them](sand-bass.md#finding-them-sign--sonar) and
[barred sand bass — spawning](sand-bass.md#spawning).

**It is the most expensive way to fish for sand bass.** There is no costlier
rig in this fishery — you pay for the harness, then again for the plastics and
the heads that go on it — so it is an investment for a dedicated bass fisherman
rather than a default, and what it buys back is the most fun way to catch them
(`P2OzCf2CwXI`).

## How barred sand bass changes the execution

**Lob it, don't bomb it.** The cast is a deliberate lob: cast an A-rig too hard
and the arms break off (`P2OzCf2CwXI`). That failure mode sets the whole
delivery — distance is traded for keeping the rig intact.

**It gets bit a lot on the sink.** Like the single plastics, the A-rig takes a
large share of its bites on the fall, so the sink is fished, not waited out
(`P2OzCf2CwXI`). The free-spool tells for the single-plastics program — the sink
rate speeds up, or you feel a tap on the line — are in
[barred sand bass — situations → techniques](sand-bass.md#situations--techniques).

**Into gear at or near the bottom, then low and slow up the column.** Once the
rig hits bottom or gets close-ish to it, put it in gear and bring it low and
slow back to the boat — turning the handle, maybe a couple of little pumps —
cruising the baits up through the column (`P2OzCf2CwXI`). That upward cruise
past a school is the presentation, not the retrieve of a bottom lure.

**Stand off the structure.** You do not want to be fishing this close to the
structure (`P2OzCf2CwXI`) — the arms and multiple heads are what make contact
expensive, so the A-rig is worked over and past structure rather than into it.

**One bite fires the next.** When the fish are schooled up and spawning and see
the rig cruise past, one bite fires up the next, which fires up the one after
that, until four or five fish are on — sometimes a full limit on a single cast —
and the load feels like pulling up a 20 lb yellowtail (`P2OzCf2CwXI`).
⚠ Fact-check (single-source): the four-or-five-fish chain reaction and the
20 lb yellowtail comparison rest on this one video alone, uncorroborated
elsewhere in the corpus. The
double rig gets at the same followers one bait at a time; the A-rig gets them as
a chain reaction.

## Rigs & gear

- **Rod: a slightly stiffer bass rod rated 15–50 lb**, for feel and for
  controlling the sink or fall — the same outfit used for the heavier baits, the
  larger tube baits and the two-plastic three-way rig (`P2OzCf2CwXI`).
- **Reel: a low-profile ~300 size**, with a size either way acceptable
  (`P2OzCf2CwXI`).
  ⚠ Fact-check (single-source): the 15–50 lb rod rating and ~300-size reel
  rest on this one video alone; no other cited source in the note or
  elsewhere in the KB corroborates the figures.
- **Class term:** the
  [low-profile bass class](../tackle/gear-classes.md) — this is the cast-and-wind
  artificials outfit, not a rig built specifically for the A-rig
  (`P2OzCf2CwXI`).
- **Baits:** several small swimbaits on leadheads on a wire harness; the
  paddle-tail and colour list is in
  [soft-plastic swimbaits](../lures/soft-plastic-swimbaits.md).

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: for barred sand bass specifically, no source
gives the A-rig's arm count, head weights, line and leader classes, or how many
hooks are run — the only hardware build in the corpus is the Mission Bay
spotted-bay-bass build in
[swimbaits](../techniques/swimbaits.md#multi-bait-rigs).

## Differs from the general method

Against
[swimbaits — umbrella rig / A-rig](../techniques/swimbaits.md#multi-bait-rigs),
whose A-rig material is a Mission Bay spotted-bay-bass hardware build:

- **The general note builds the rig; this page fishes it.** Arm bending, the
  five-hook saltwater / three-hook freshwater rule and the Palomar-over-uni knot
  are hardware decisions made before the cast; nothing in them describes a
  presentation for sand bass (`P2OzCf2CwXI`).
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the sand bass studio video never
  mentions a hook count, arm count, or knot — those figures are mis-scoped
  here; per [swimbaits](../techniques/swimbaits.md#multi-bait-rigs) they
  belong to the Mission Bay spotted-bay-bass build and are cited there to
  `G6YRT4HNxr8`.
- **The retrieve path is vertical, not horizontal.** The bay build is a straight
  grind held just above the eelgrass canopy — one depth, all the way back. The
  sand bass program sinks to at or near the bottom first and then cruises the
  baits *up* through the column (`P2OzCf2CwXI`).
- **The bite window is the fall.** The bay build's triggers are the grind and a
  varied straight-vs-angled tug that changes the fall angle mid-retrieve; here a
  large share of bites come on the initial sink, before the rig is ever in gear
  (`P2OzCf2CwXI`).
- **Cast style is a stated constraint here.** The general note pitches the bay
  rig to docks and pilings on a long cast; the sand bass instruction is the
  opposite — lob it, because a hard cast breaks the arms off (`P2OzCf2CwXI`).
- **Standoff replaces structure-hugging.** The bay build works the canopy and
  the pilings; this program deliberately keeps off the structure
  (`P2OzCf2CwXI`).
- **The payoff is a chain reaction off a spawning school**, a scenario the bay
  build never describes (`P2OzCf2CwXI`).
