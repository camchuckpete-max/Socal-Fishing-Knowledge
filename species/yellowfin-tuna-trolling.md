# Yellowfin Tuna — trolling

**One species, one technique.** Yellowfin trolling is two programmes sharing one
shape — lures in the water while the boat searches. The open-zone version drags
[cedar plugs](../lures/cedar-plug.md) and
[feathers](../lures/tuna-feathers-and-skirts.md) at **~6–6.5 kt** and is judged
by the strike that tells you where a school is (cameron). The pod version is a
specific approach on a sighted dolphin/porpoise school at **7–9 mph**, where the
geometry of the pass matters more than the lure does (`ouBrIdO7d4k`). Spread
size, setback staggering, rod-tip elevation and the sweeping-arc search stay in
[trolling](../techniques/trolling.md); this page is what changes because the fish
is a yellowfin.

## When this is the call

**Open zone or a spread of paddies, no visual fish.** This is the
troll-to-locate row on
[yellowfin — situations → techniques](yellowfin-tuna.md#situations--techniques).
The troll is a *search* tool as much as a catch method — the point is the strike
that locates a school, and the answer to it is to stop and convert with
[flylined bait](../techniques/flyline.md) or cast lures rather than to keep
dragging (`0bcDBGzQnGw`).

**A dolphin or porpoise pod is in sight and nothing is showing on the surface.**
Yellowfin suspend **underneath** a merely travelling pod and mostly do not
surface-swim with it, which is why a pod that looks empty from above is still
worth a pass (`fK2AT460xW4`). Picking between several pods — take the one
carrying its own bird — is search doctrine and stays on the router's
[finding them](yellowfin-tuna.md#finding-them-sign--sonar) section.

**The named SoCal window for the pod programme is the lobster opener — "first
week of October, end of September" — on the backside of
[Catalina](../locations/catalina-island-backside.md)**, where the method is
credited with **60–70 lb** fish taken on the dolphin (`ouBrIdO7d4k`).

**Stop trolling when the fish come up.** A pod that breaks into a full foamer
ends the troll: off [Ensenada](../locations/ensenada.md) the crew pulled the
cedar plugs and switched to flylined sardine plus
[chunk bait](../techniques/chunking.md) worked into the school (`0bcDBGzQnGw`).

## How yellowfin changes the execution

**Two speed bands for two targets.** Cedar plugs and feathers run at
**~6–6.5 kt** in an open-zone search (cameron) — against **5–5.5 kt** for the
same plug behind [bluefin](bluefin-tuna-trolling.md), so the plug's speed is set
by which tuna you are looking for. The small finesse lure worked around a
dolphin pod runs faster, at **7–9 mph**, described on camera as "not very fast"
(`ouBrIdO7d4k`).

**Approach geometry is the technique on a pod — never drive through it.** Stay
well off a moving pod and get **way out in front** of it, then troll past.
Busting through puts bow pressure on the animals: they hear and see the boat,
split or bolt, and the school **dodges the lure** (`ouBrIdO7d4k`). The same
never-go-through rule is applied by the same captain to a trolled
[Mad Mac](../lures/mad-mac.md) for bluefin, so it is a pod rule rather than a
yellowfin one (`ouBrIdO7d4k`).

**Where the fish sit is what decides where the lure goes.** Under a pod that is
merely **travelling**, the yellowfin are underneath it and mostly out of sight
(`fK2AT460xW4`). Once the pod is actively **feeding** and the bait ball starts
breaking up, the school is hunting the scattered balls instead: the fish foam
out **on the flanks and out in front**, sometimes as far as **50 yd** ahead of
the pod with terns picking there (`ouBrIdO7d4k`). Read which of the two you are
looking at before committing the pass — the flanks-and-front water is the target
on a feeding pod, and there are always some fish underneath either way
(`ouBrIdO7d4k`). The two positions sit side by side rather than in conflict: one
describes the travelling pattern, the other the feeding one.

**Pair a deep diver with a surface plug on a pod.** Off
[San Clemente Island](../locations/san-clemente-island-front-side.md) a
**deep-diving hardbait** (Nomad-class, sardine colour) and a
[cedar plug](../lures/cedar-plug.md) were trolled together around a big porpoise
school; the deep diver got bit and the boat landed a yellowfin of about
**60 lb** (`H-vIGWPIPVc`).

**Colour-match the pair to get doubles.** On an October offshore troll
**20–30 mi** out from [Cedros Island](../locations/cedros-island.md), two lines
were run **one short, one long**, and running the *same* colour on both —
**purple/purple** named on camera — was credited with the simultaneous doubles
that came aboard, including on a hardbait-to-feather swap. The captain reported
that a different-colour Rapala two days earlier had not produced the same
doubles (`YUdbrIm9vrE`) — same-source corroboration, not independent
confirmation.

**Feathers can restart a bite that live bait has killed.** In the same Cedros
session a flylined sardine drew hookups until it stopped drawing them; the crew
was told to "forget the bait" and put the feathers back on — the same
feather/hardbait combination that had produced the morning's tuna — and the bite
restarted (`YUdbrIm9vrE`). This runs the opposite direction to the
stop-and-convert rule above and is kept beside it, not reconciled: convert on the
strike, but the troll is still available when the converted bite dies.

## Rigs & gear

- **Open-zone search: light troll class.** [Cedar plugs](../lures/cedar-plug.md)
  and [tuna feathers & skirts](../lures/tuna-feathers-and-skirts.md) at
  ~6–6.5 kt (cameron). Class terms in
  [gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md).
- **The pod finesse lure: a re-headed King Buster-class bait.** A small
  **King Buster**-style finesse trolling lure, re-headed with a **tungsten
  1/2 oz head** because the stock plastic head lets the lure pop out of the
  water at speed, rigged with a small **Yamashita stainless double hook** and
  trolled on **60 or 80 lb straight to the lure** — no leader step named
  (`ouBrIdO7d4k`). The lure's own spec entry is in
  [tuna feathers & skirts](../lures/tuna-feathers-and-skirts.md). The idea came
  from a circa-mid-2010s Dana Point programme trolling cheap, fragile stock King
  Busters around porpoise pods for yellowfin, rebuilt with the tungsten head once
  the small profile was confirmed to work (`ouBrIdO7d4k`).
- **Pod pair: a deep diver plus a plug.** Nomad-class deep-diving hardbait in
  sardine colour alongside a cedar plug (`H-vIGWPIPVc`).
- **Cedros offshore: 60 lb line class** on the trolling outfits, running
  Rapala-class hardbaits and feathers; the reel and rod named on camera are
  ASR-uncertain and are not carried (`YUdbrIm9vrE`).
- ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: rod, reel, main-line or leader build for the
  cedar-plug-and-feather open-zone programme, and **setback distances** for any
  yellowfin spread — the corpus gives speeds, a 60 lb line class and a
  one-short-one-long position pair, never a distance behind the boat.

## Differs from the general method

Against [trolling](../techniques/trolling.md), which owns spread size, setback
staggering, the rod-tip-elevation depth rule, rod-to-lure pairing and the
sweeping-arc search:

- **On a pod, the sweep is replaced by an approach line.** The general note's
  search pattern is big zigzag sweeps back and forth through a bird/bait area;
  the pod programme is a single deliberate geometry — stay outside the school,
  get well out in front, never cut through — with a stated failure mode if you
  get it wrong (`ouBrIdO7d4k`).
- **Speed is keyed to the target, not to the lure.** The general note pushes
  per-lure speed down to the lure notes; here the same cedar plug runs 6–6.5 kt
  for yellowfin and 5–5.5 kt for bluefin (cameron), and the pod lure runs
  7–9 mph (`ouBrIdO7d4k`).
- **The strike ends the troll.** The general note advises trolling less once
  schools are visible on the surface; the yellowfin version makes that a hard
  switch executed on the strike itself — stop, and convert the stop with bait or
  cast lures (`0bcDBGzQnGw`).
- **Colour-matching the pair is a stated producer.** The general note staggers
  the spread to keep lures in clean water and out of each other; the yellowfin
  Cedros programme adds a colour rule on top of the one-short-one-long positions,
  credited with the doubles (`YUdbrIm9vrE`).
- **NOT a delta: the never-go-through-the-pod rule.** The captain who states it
  applies it to bluefin on a Mad Mac as readily as to yellowfin — it is pod
  doctrine that this page repeats for completeness, not something yellowfin
  change (`ouBrIdO7d4k`).

**Labelled contrast, out of region:** the same small trolled-feather approach is
named on camera as a Hawaii **skipjack** staple — "in Hawaii, man, that's a
staple" — volunteered alongside the SoCal method and carried here as context
only, never as SoCal/Baja doctrine (`ouBrIdO7d4k`).
