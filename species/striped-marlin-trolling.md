# Striped Marlin — trolling

**One species, one technique.** The SoCal marlin troll is a **short, tight**
lure spread — riggers **four to five wakes** back and flatlines **real close to
the boat** on the corpus's one worked stop — dragged along the clean side of a
bait/colour seam with a live mackerel rigged the whole time to drop back to
whatever shows (`3qSY328fFYo`).
It is a searching-and-raising program rather than a catching one: roughly
**one hookup for every five or six** fish raised, so the spread is built to be
*seen* by the crew as much as by the fish (`3qSY328fFYo`). The general spread
mechanics — stagger, holder geometry, sweeping arcs — are in
[trolling](../techniques/trolling.md); this page is what changes because the
fish is a striped marlin.

## When this is the call

**Mid-August through October, on the clean side of a bait/colour edge, in the
tide window.** This is the top row of
[striped marlin — situations → techniques](striped-marlin.md#situations--techniques);
the seasonal and geographic pattern behind it stays on the router.

**Put the jigs in on the edge, then troll into the clean water.** Bait sits in
the dirtier water on the colour line and the marlin hold in the blue beside it,
so the spread goes out where there is still bait and is trolled toward "the
clean water where the marlin would likely live" (`HzE4FHHcvTk`).

**Hunt the temperature before the jigs go in.** The target water on the corpus's
worked SoCal stop was **68°F**, and the water actually fished read
**69.3–69.4°F** (`3qSY328fFYo`).

**Be set up before the tide change, not after it.** Jig strikes come about
**an hour before or after** the change, so the spread should already be working
the highest-percentage ground an hour ahead of it (`3qSY328fFYo`,
`6TBxHnkYXI0`). See [tide & slack](../conditions/tide-and-slack.md).

**The ground worked in the corpus.** From the home port, **19.2 miles at 193°**
to the bottom of the **tanker lanes** (roughly 17 miles out), then a further
**~35-minute** run on toward the **277 bank** at the bottom of the ridge off
Catalina's east end (`3qSY328fFYo`). See
[the Slide / 152 / 277](../locations/slide-152-277.md).

**It is not the call once a fish is up and looking.** A raised or sighted fish
is handed to the pitch rod — the dropback itself is
[bait-and-switch](../techniques/bait-and-switch.md), not this page.

## How striped marlin changes the execution

**Everything runs short and tight.** On the corpus's worked SoCal stop the whole
spread ran short and tight for that boat: on the outriggers roughly **four to
five wakes** back, the flatlines **real close to the boat** (`3qSY328fFYo`).
The stated reason the spread is compressed is the watching problem below: a
marlin's approach has to be visible from the helm.
⚠ Fact-check (single-source): the four-to-five-wakes/flatlines-close setback
figures are one captain's setup on his own boat, with no second source.

**The roster is swimmers plus one small colour.** The spread pulled on the
corpus's worked stop was a **flying-fish-pattern swimmer** in one rigger, a
second swimmer (captioned "NE" — asr-uncertain, not resolved to a model), a
**Hawaiian-style lure**, and a small pink lure nicknamed **"the little pinky"**
in the corner, which had produced a fish the week before (`3qSY328fFYo`). The
fish landed that day ate the flying-fish swimmer (`3qSY328fFYo`). Lure specs
live in [tuna/marlin feathers and skirts](../lures/tuna-feathers-and-skirts.md).

**Troll downhill — never into the sun.** Running with the sun behind the boat
makes the jigs clearly visible astern. The stated mechanism is silhouette: a
fish that comes up **behind the jigs**, close to the boat, shows against the
water and is easy to pick out, while a fish showing further out ahead of the
boat toward the sun and prop-wash glare is much harder to see (`3qSY328fFYo`).

**Watching the jigs and prop wash is the top job after driving the boat.** A
marlin can take a whack at a jig without popping the outrigger release clip or
the rubber-band flatline release — it can hit and trip nothing — so somebody
has to be on the spread continuously. The stated cost of not doing it is
**four or five fish in a day**, each one read at the time as a piece of kelp
popping a line (`3qSY328fFYo`).

**The sounder is run for bait, not for the fish.** Marlin are not a meter
target; set the console display to **120 ft** so bait depth is readable under
way — whether it is at 100, 30 or 20 ft — and lay the spread accordingly
(`3qSY328fFYo`). Mackerel was identified on the meter on that stop, and the
meter read was cross-checked against surface sign (small schools skittering,
sauries greyhounding, a bait cloud metered under the boat before the first
drop) (`3qSY328fFYo`). See
[electronics & sounder](../planning/electronics-and-sounder.md).

**Expect to raise far more fish than you hook.** The stated rule of thumb for
jig-raised marlin is roughly **one hookup for every five or six** fish
raised or interested (`3qSY328fFYo`) — which is why the pitch bait stays rigged
and why the spread-watch discipline above is worth a dedicated crew member.

**A jumper behind the spread is not a biter; a feeder is** — the read is on the
router, at
[striped marlin — feeding triggers](striped-marlin.md#feeding-triggers)
(`3qSY328fFYo`).

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: a troll **speed** for the SoCal marlin
spread, a setback in feet or boat-lengths behind the four-to-five-wakes figure,
any skirt-colour logic beyond the one pink lure that had produced, and how the
spread is arranged on a boat with no outriggers.

## Rigs & gear

- **Trolling class 60–100 lb** pulling
  [marlin skirts and feathers](../lures/tuna-feathers-and-skirts.md); class
  terms in [gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md).
- **What was actually fished is lighter than the class default.** Mid-fight on
  the roughly **160 lb** fish the angler said he wished it were **50 lb**
  tackle, which places that outfit below 50 lb; the exact class is never named
  on camera (`3qSY328fFYo`).
- **Releases:** outrigger release clips on the rigger lines and **rubber-band
  releases** on the flatlines — both of which a marlin can hit without tripping
  (`3qSY328fFYo`).
- **Pitch rod:** a flyline / live-bait class outfit carrying a live green-back
  mackerel, rigged and ready for the whole troll; catch the dropback baits
  before you leave (`3qSY328fFYo`). Delivery is
  [bait-and-switch](../techniques/bait-and-switch.md).
- **Optics count as tackle here.** Buy the best **binoculars** you can afford —
  on a sight fishery they are in use all day (`3qSY328fFYo`). See
  [search & glassing](../planning/search-and-glassing.md).

## Differs from the general method

Against [trolling](../techniques/trolling.md), which owns staggered setbacks,
shotgun/gunnel holder geometry, the rod-tip elevation rule and the
sweeping-arc search pattern:

- **The setback stagger collapses.** The general note runs the furthest-back
  lure long out of the elevated shotgun and staggers the rest into clean water;
  the marlin spread on the corpus's worked stop was deliberately short and
  tight — riggers at four to five wakes, flatlines close aboard, on that boat
  (`3qSY328fFYo`).
- **Heading is chosen by sun angle, not only by sign.** The general note's
  steering rules are sweeping arcs through bait and birds and staying out of
  other boats' prop wash (`HzE4FHHcvTk`); the marlin troll adds a hard rule
  against trolling into the sun, for visibility rather than for the fish
  (`3qSY328fFYo`).
- **The spread is manned, not just set.** The general note has no watcher role;
  here a dedicated pair of eyes on the jigs and prop wash is second only to
  driving, because the release hardware does not reliably report a marlin's
  strike (`3qSY328fFYo`).
- **The sounder is part of the troll.** General trolling treats the meter as
  optional; this program runs it at a fixed **120 ft** to place the spread on
  bait while under way (`3qSY328fFYo`).
- **A strike is not the objective.** Most raised fish never load a rod — about
  one in five or six converts — so a live bait rides the whole troll to finish
  what the jigs start, which no general trolling spread requires
  (`3qSY328fFYo`).
