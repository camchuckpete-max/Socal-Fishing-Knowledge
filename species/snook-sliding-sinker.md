# Snook (Robalo) — sliding sinker

**One species, one technique.** The boca program at
[Lopez Mateos / Magdalena Bay](../locations/bahia-magdalena-lopez-mateos.md) is
a Carolina rig dropped blind into **40–50 ft** on a **4/0-size two-speed** reel,
with the drag locked as tight as the reel will go and a **6 ft** leader of
**100–130 lb** behind the swivel (`EcQK5elTPJE`). Reach for it when the target
is a **20 lb-plus** snook rather than the small fish the mangrove roots give up,
and accept that grouper to **100 lb** eat the same bait on the same drop. The
free-sliding Carolina format itself — weight riding above the swivel, line
running through the lead — is in
[sliding sinker](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md); this page is what changes
because the fish is a snook at this mark.

## When this is the call

**Outside the bay mouth, on a known bottom mark, for grade.** The boca sits
about **five minutes from the dock** in **40–50 ft**, and is fished blind — no
visual or sonar sign is given for it (`EcQK5elTPJE`). This is the row the router
ranks for the outside program; see
[snook — situations → techniques](snook.md#situations--techniques). It is the
opposite trip from the inside program: the boca is where the grade is, with a
**42 lb** snook as the best of one day and snook **over 20 lb** named as the
reason to run outside at all (`EcQK5elTPJE`). The zone-by-zone grade split is on
the [router](snook.md#where--when).

**Bring it expecting a mixed bag you cannot choose between.** Grouper to
**100 lb** come off the same drop, and the tackle is sized for them rather than
for the snook (`EcQK5elTPJE`). That grouper is never given a species name in the
source and is **not** assumed to be [cabrilla](cabrilla.md) — a different
region, a different technique program and a much smaller fish — so the build
below is specified against an unidentified 100 lb-class fish, not against a
named one.

**It is not the call inside.** The inside program fishes down to about **2 ft**
of water over oyster-crusted roots on a no-weight
[flyline](../techniques/flyline.md) bait (`EcQK5elTPJE`); why a sinker cannot be
run in there is on the router's
[mangrove rows](snook.md#situations--techniques). See also
[mangrove structure livebait](../techniques/mangrove-structure-livebait.md).

## How snook changes the execution

**The drag is locked and the fight is decided in about five seconds.** Fish the
drag "super tight," as tight as the reel allows, so the fish cannot pull against
it: "that fight is won in the first 5 seconds … you literally got 5 seconds to
turn the fish" (`EcQK5elTPJE`). Either the fish is turned before it reaches
structure or it is gone. This is the inverse of the general note's default — see
[Differs from the general method](#differs-from-the-general-method).

**Which species you have is readable off the strike, and it decides the fight.**
A hooked grouper heads straight back for the rock and snaps you off there; a
hooked snook does not necessarily rock you — it comes **up** toward the boat
instead of diving, and starts taking drag against the locked setting once it is
off the bottom (`EcQK5elTPJE`). That rise-versus-dive read is how the crew tells
the two apart before the fish is up.

**Play a hooked snook clean of the structure rather than holding it against
it** — its mouth is abrasive enough to wear the hook out over a long fight
(`EcQK5elTPJE`).
⚠ Fact-check (unverifiable): the play-it-clean instruction is legible in the
captions; the mouth-abrasion mechanism behind it sits in a badly garbled stretch
and is a reconstruction — the same flag this claim carries on the
[router](snook.md#landing--handling).

**The hook is the documented failure point at the top of the grade.** 4X hooks
have been straightened out on fish up to the 100 lb grouper class this mark
produces (`EcQK5elTPJE`), which is the argument against showing up with lighter
terminal tackle rather than a reason to fish the drag softer.

## Rigs & gear

- **Reel: a 4/0-size two-speed is mandatory.** "Do not show up there with a
  single-speed reel — you'll be breaking off all day" against the structure at
  this mark (`EcQK5elTPJE`). This is the
  [Baja Pacific grouper/snook bottom class](../tackle/gear-classes.md).
- **Rig order:** 80 lb braid → **80 lb fluorocarbon** → sinker → swivel → about
  **6 ft** of **100–130 lb** leader → hook — the standard
  [sliding sinker](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md) order, with the weight
  riding free above the swivel (`EcQK5elTPJE`). No sinker weight is stated on
  camera.
  ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: the sinker weight or style for this build,
  and whether a bead is run above the swivel.
- **The 100–130 lb leader was arrived at by losing fish, not by reasoning.** The
  first six snook hooked in this fishery all broke off, and the leader was
  stepped up through **60, 80, 100 and finally 130 lb** before fish were landed
  (`EcQK5elTPJE`). The progression is the origin of the spec above, not a second
  rule beside it. Leader material reasoning generally:
  [line & leader](../tackle/line-and-leader.md).
- **Hook: 4X strong, sized to the bait** — sardine or live shrimp
  (`EcQK5elTPJE`). Specified by wire strength rather than by pattern or aught
  size; see [hooks](../tackle/hooks.md).
- **Bait: live shrimp or sardine, and the shrimp has to be arranged with the
  guide the day before** so it can be caught overnight — there is no dockside
  bait receiver stocking live shrimp at Lopez Mateos (`EcQK5elTPJE`). Live
  shrimp is named as the bait for snook specifically.

## Differs from the general method

Against [sliding sinker](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md), which owns the
free-sliding Carolina format, the per-species weight ladders and how to feed a
bite:

- **The free take is given up, not exploited.** The general note's whole
  argument is that line running through the lead lets you give slack and let the
  fish turn before you come tight, and its listed failure is coming tight too
  soon. Here the drag is locked at maximum and the fish must be turned inside
  five seconds — the rig's stealth is bought for the leader's sake, not the
  bite's (`EcQK5elTPJE`).
- **The build is specified by reel and leader, not by a weight ladder.** Every
  other build on the general note is organized around the sinker — 1/4–1/2 oz on
  sheephead, 1/8–3/4 oz on white seabass, 2–4 oz on San Diego yellowtail, 8–24 oz
  in the Sea of Cortez. This one never names a weight and instead makes the
  **reel's gearing** a hard requirement, which no other build on the note does
  (`EcQK5elTPJE`).
- **Same leader length as the Sea of Cortez build, a third of the test.** Both
  regional bottom builds settle on about **6 ft**, but the Cortez cabrilla/
  grouper rig runs **300–400 lb** to a 12/0-class hook where this one runs
  **100–130 lb** to a 4X hook — different region, different species mix,
  different specs, and the two are not reconciled to one number. See
  [the Sea of Cortez build](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md#baja-sea-of-cortez-grouper-leader).
- **The rock-cutoff fix is the leader itself, not a long mono top shot.** The
  Cortez build answers rock contact with a 30 yd-plus mono top shot above a
  heavy bite leader; this build answers it with a locked drag and a five-second
  turn, and stepped the bite leader up until fish stopped breaking off
  (`EcQK5elTPJE`). Neither source addresses the other.
- **Bait supply is a precondition.** No other build on the general note requires
  arranging bait with a guide a day ahead (`EcQK5elTPJE`).
