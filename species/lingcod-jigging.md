# Lingcod — jigging

**One species, one technique.** A metal jig worked in contact with the rock is
the lingcod half of the [rockfish & lingcod](rockfish-lingcod.md) program, and
it has parameters the rest of the complex does not: profile size doubles as a
size filter, the cadence is keyed to the bottom rather than to a counted depth,
and a lingcod's side-on bite makes the miss — not the eat — the thing you have
to fish through. The general fall-triggered presentation is in
[flat-fall jigging](../techniques/flat-fall-jigging.md); this page is what
changes when the fish on the rock is a lingcod.

## When this is the call

**The slug is not getting bit, or the water is deep.** The jig is the stated
switch from the [lead-head slug](../techniques/leadhead-swimbait-retrieve.md)
when you move into deeper water or onto a spot where the fish are not eating
plastic (`O5aQkex0qGg`). Where the slug still works, the router ranks it first —
see [rockfish & lingcod — situations → techniques](rockfish-lingcod.md#situations--techniques).

**You want one fish, not a sack.** Big-profile lingcod jigs are explicitly not
the numbers play: "you're not really looking for a ton of fish, you're looking
for one big fish." If you simply want to catch fish and do not care how, the
answer is a [double dropper loop](../techniques/dropper-loop.md), a Colt Sniper
or a smaller flat-fall (`iR8CDCyofDM`). That trade is the whole reason to pick
up the big jig.

**Spawn season, at the outer islands.** Depending on the time of year lingcod
are spawning, a big-profile bait that lands in a nest is eaten as a territorial
response rather than a hunger response — which is the stated reason to carry
bigger profiles when you fish the outer islands (`iAr6sbRC384`). The nesting
behaviour itself, and the flagged gap on when the SoCal spawn actually runs,
stay in [rockfish & lingcod — spawning](rockfish-lingcod.md#spawning).

**Season and weather are the router's job.** The Nov–Dec Santa Ana window that
opens the shallow Rosa/Miguel high spots, and the crossing it is gated behind,
are in [rockfish & lingcod — where & when](rockfish-lingcod.md#where--when).

## How lingcod changes the execution

**Profile is the size filter.** The larger-profile jigs still take the rockfish
variety — vermilions, reds, chuckleheads — but going bigger is what puts you on
the more predatory lingcod, and the flat-fall profile is one they particularly
like (`XxN7EzNDnf8`). At the top of the range sit the dedicated large-profile
lingcod baits, the living-squid and ling-killer class, fished to incentivise one
big fish onto the line rather than to keep the rail bent (`iR8CDCyofDM`).

**Flat-fall cadence: bottom, one or two turns, lift, flutter.** Sink the jig
all the way to the bottom, take maybe one or two turns up off it, lift the rod,
and let it slowly flutter back down — lift, fall, lift, fall. It is fished not
much differently from a Colt Sniper (`XxN7EzNDnf8`).

**Iron cadence: big sweeps, and re-set on the bottom every time the drift pulls
you off it.** On a **6 oz Ahi-Assault-class iron** the same shape is worked
harder — drop all the way to the bottom, put the reel in gear, take a wind or
two so the jig is up out of the rocks, then big sweeps of the rod with a flutter
back down between each one. As the boat drifts and the line scopes out, drop
back to the bottom, re-establish contact, and take another one or two sweeps.
The eat arrives with no warning inside that cadence — "it'll just be bang, you
got one" (`O5aQkex0qGg`).

**On the bottom-hopped presentation, snap the tip and keep contact.** Once the
bait is down and lingcod (or a copper rockfish) are the target rather than the
suspended reds, the retrieve is popping and snapping the rod tip up, winding up,
snap-snap — keeping bottom contact throughout (`O5aQkex0qGg`).

**They bite from the side and miss the hook.** A lingcod comes up and takes the
jig from the side, and even a big fish with a big mouth misses the hook — you
feel the bite and pull the jig away from it (`O5aQkex0qGg`).

**The recovery is a drop-back, then hop again.** Set the hook on the first bite;
if the fish comes off immediately, take one or two fast winds, drop straight
back down to the bottom, let it sit there for a second, and start hopping again.
The stated read is that the lingcod watched the bait get out of its mouth, saw
it go, and comes back to eat it again — so be patient rather than reeling up to
re-drop from the surface (`O5aQkex0qGg`).

## Rigs & gear

- **Rod/reel — [heavy swimbait/rockfish class](../tackle/gear-classes.md) on a
  level-wind with 30 lb braid.** The heavy bass rod is the stated jig rod, and
  the thin braid is what gets a jig straight down in 300 ft or more and leaves
  enough spool diameter to crank it back (`O5aQkex0qGg`); the full outfit is in
  [rockfish & lingcod — gear summary](rockfish-lingcod.md#gear-summary-class-terms).
- **Iron — 6 oz Ahi-Assault class**, the middle size in that line ("six ounce or
  maybe an eight ounce" — self-hedged on camera), green mackerel and blue among
  the colours, all of which are said to work (`O5aQkex0qGg`). Class detail:
  [iron jigs](../lures/iron-jigs.md).
- **Flat-fall — the larger-profile end of the class** rather than the small
  numbers sizes (`XxN7EzNDnf8`, `iR8CDCyofDM`); specs in
  [knife jigs](../lures/knife-jigs.md).
- **Dedicated lingcod baits — living-squid and ling-killer class**
  (`iR8CDCyofDM`). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: weight, colour or rigging
  for either bait; they are named on camera and nothing more.
- **Leader is the rockfish-wide abrasion build, not a lingcod build** — the
  rocks, not the teeth, are what cut you off; see
  [rockfish & lingcod — gear summary](rockfish-lingcod.md#gear-summary-class-terms)
  and [line and leader](../tackle/line-and-leader.md).
- ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: whether the big-profile lingcod program
  calls for a heavier rod, leader or hook than the 6 oz iron build above.

## Differs from the general method

Against [flat-fall jigging](../techniques/flat-fall-jigging.md), which owns the
free-spool drop, the count-down to a captain-called depth and the partial
wind-up/re-drop cycle for deep bluefin:

- **The bottom is the datum, not a counted depth.** The general note counts the
  sink to 30 or 40 and warns that line-out is not true depth; the lingcod
  program sinks all the way to the rock and works one or two turns up from it
  (`XxN7EzNDnf8`, `O5aQkex0qGg`). The general note's warning against holding one
  static guessed depth becomes, here, the rule to drop back and re-make bottom
  contact every time the drift scopes the line out (`O5aQkex0qGg`).
- **There is an active rod stroke.** The general note works a flat-fall
  passively, without a jigging stroke. The lingcod version lifts the rod on
  every cycle (`XxN7EzNDnf8`), and the iron version drives big sweeps of the rod
  with a flutter between them (`O5aQkex0qGg`).
- **Profile is chosen to select fish, not to beat the drift.** The general note
  picks jig weight by wind and drift so the lure reaches the zone. Here the big
  profile is chosen to filter for one large fish (`iR8CDCyofDM`) and, in the
  spawn window, to draw a nest defence (`iAr6sbRC384`).
- **The missed bite has a defined recovery.** The general note has no drop-back
  protocol; the lingcod side-bite miss gets one or two fast winds, a drop
  straight back to the bottom, a pause, and the hop restarted (`O5aQkex0qGg`).
- **Against [yo-yo iron](../techniques/yo-yo-iron.md)**, which winds the instant
  the jig touches and never lets it sit, this program deliberately returns to
  the bottom between sweeps (`O5aQkex0qGg`) — the yo-yo rule exists to keep a
  yellowtail jig away from bottom fish, which is exactly the fish being targeted
  here.
- **Hook configuration and leader material are NOT deltas.** No source states a
  lingcod-specific change to either; the general note's hook-upsize logic and
  the rockfish-wide abrasion leader both stand as written.
