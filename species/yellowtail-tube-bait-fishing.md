# Yellowtail — tube-bait fishing

**One species, one technique.** The [tube bait](../lures/tube-baits.md) is
fished at yellowtail on two planes and searched with on a third: a
**crank-pop-crank-pop** retrieve on top when fish are showing, a **half-yo-yo**
sink-retrieve-refall-retrieve down deep when they are not, and a **3–4 mph
troll** of the same bait purely to find them. What changes against
[tube-bait fishing](../techniques/tube-bait-fishing.md) in general is the
retrieve itself — the general note's other applications are a drift-bounce and
a sink-and-wait, this one is a worked bait — plus a size call that runs
opposite to the bass program's big-bait filter. Both sources are HookUp Baits'
own principals presenting their own product at trade shows, so every
performance claim on this page stays **low confidence**.

## When this is the call

**Fish showing on top.** A visible push or boil within casting range is the
surface cadence's cue: cast past it and work the bait back through it
(`0HwYl0HO2tc`). See the router's
[situations → techniques](yellowtail.md#situations--techniques) row for how
this ranks against [surface iron](../techniques/surface-iron.md) and the
stickbait over the same water.

**Fish on the structure but not showing.** When nothing is on top, the same
bait is fished as a half-yo-yo down the water column rather than switched away
from (`0HwYl0HO2tc`) — the tube-bait alternative to going to
[yo-yo iron](../techniques/yo-yo-iron.md).

**Fish not located at all.** A trolled bullet bait at **3–4 mph** is a search
mode, not a presentation: it finds the school, and the program switches to
casting once it has (`5Oh3IPIC5g4`). See
[trolling](../techniques/trolling.md#socal-local-coast--trolling-hard-baits-to-prospect-a-zone-for-yellowtail)
for the general prospect-a-zone method this sits inside.

**Fish following without eating.** Repeated follows and no takes is the cue to
drop bait profile, not to change technique (`5Oh3IPIC5g4`) — the fix is below.

## How yellowtail changes the execution

**On top: crank-pop-crank-pop, rod tip low.** Cast beyond a visible push or
boil, keep the rod tip low, and retrieve on a **crank-pop-crank-pop** cadence.
It is easier on a spinning reel than a baitcaster, because a spinning reel lets
you pop the tip and take up slack in the same motion, where baitcast anglers
tend to fall into a choppier pop-pop-pop-crank pattern (`0HwYl0HO2tc`).

**A second, corroborating pop-retrieve, called "absolutely deadly" on
yellowtail after 30 years fishing them** (`5Oh3IPIC5g4`): cast, let the bait
sink on roughly a **10 count**, then retrieve rod-tip-down — rod tucked under
the arm — on a **medium-speed crank**, popping the tip through the retrieve
("bop bop") so the bait kicks left-right at about the speed of a swimming
baitfish, not a fast burn. Two independent sources arriving at the same
crank-and-pop shape is the strongest thing on this page; the speed reference
(a swimming baitfish) is what keeps it from being fished as a burn.

**Down, half-yo-yo variant.** When fish aren't showing on top: sink the bait to
bottom, retrieve **about halfway** back up, open the bail and let it fall back
to bottom a second time, then retrieve **the rest of the way** to the boat —
worked at roughly **half the speed** of a bare-iron yo-yo (`0HwYl0HO2tc`).
This is a distinct cadence from the crank-then-freespool and
continuous-wind-to-the-top cadences in
[yo-yo iron](../techniques/yo-yo-iron.md#mechanics), kept as a tube-bait
variant rather than merged into that note. Illustrated on a group trip to
Cedros Island, Baja, fished into a yellowtail push moving through
(`0HwYl0HO2tc`) — see [Cedros Island](../locations/cedros-island.md).

**Follows without commitment: downsize the profile.** At the
[Coronado Islands](../locations/coronado-islands.md), repeated follows with no
takes on the standard **1–1.5 oz "Big Game"/"Bullet"** bait stopped as soon as
the crew switched to a smaller-profile **5/8 oz** bait on lighter gear, drawing
immediate, committed eats (`5Oh3IPIC5g4`). This is the same
profile-versus-distance trade documented for
[surface iron](../techniques/surface-iron.md): go **1–1.5 oz** for distance
when fish are boat-shy or water needs covering, drop to **5/8 oz** for a
smaller profile once fish are showing but refusing. The corroboration across
two unrelated bait classes is what makes the follows-fix a yellowtail read
rather than a tube-bait quirk.

**Troll speed to locate: 3–4 mph, "the magic speed."** On a private boat the
**1–1.5 oz "bullet"** bait tracks straight and true with a **tight little
wiggle** at roughly **3–4 mph**, which is the speed for **locating**
yellowtail — not the presentation once fish are found, which switches to
casting (`5Oh3IPIC5g4`). It sits between two other trolled-search speeds
documented on camera: slow-trolling live sardines at roughly **1 mph**
(thorough but covers little ground) and a faster trolled lure at roughly
**5 mph** (a different, unnamed lure — asr-uncertain, not confidently
identified).

**Single-hookup workflow on the troll.** Because a hookup is one fish at a time
on a single-bait troll, the [double dropper
rig](../techniques/tube-bait-fishing.md#double-bait-dropper-rig-bass-yellowtail-and-rockfish-to-200-ft)
is skipped for yellowtail trolling — the other rod stays **rigged and in hand**
rather than stowed while the first fish is fought, so it can be fired right
back out to keep working the strike zone instead of losing search time
re-rigging (`5Oh3IPIC5g4`).

## Rigs & gear

- **Bass casting / swimbait class** throughout — class terms in
  [gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md#lure-classes-cross-reference). The
  parabolic-bend rod requirement and the line-class-by-bait-weight ladder are
  general method, keyed to bait weight rather than to the species:
  [tube-bait fishing](../techniques/tube-bait-fishing.md#rod-parabolic-bend-for-big-baits).
- **Bait weight: 1–1.5 oz "Big Game"/"Bullet" as the standard, dropping to
  5/8 oz on lighter gear when fish follow without committing** (`5Oh3IPIC5g4`).
  The same 1–1.5 oz bullet is what gets trolled at 3–4 mph.
- **One bait, not the double dropper rig.** The double rig's own scope
  statement rules yellowtail out — hookups on it "usually don't work out well"
  and a single bait is fished for yellowtail and tuna instead (`5Oh3IPIC5g4`);
  the build and that scope note live on
  [tube-bait fishing](../techniques/tube-bait-fishing.md#double-bait-dropper-rig-bass-yellowtail-and-rockfish-to-200-ft).
- ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: rod length or rating, line class, leader
  class or hook change stated specifically for yellowtail on this bait, and no
  stated depth or duration for the half-yo-yo's bottom cycles.

## Differs from the general method

Against [tube-bait fishing](../techniques/tube-bait-fishing.md), which owns the
rod and line ladder, the slack-line sink read, the double-bait dropper rig, the
party-boat drift bounce and the tuna casts:

- **The bait is worked, not carried.** The general note's other applications
  present the bait passively — the drifting party-boat cast bounces down-current
  and the tuna casts are sink counts (a 20–30 ft paddy sink, a 50 count into a
  boil). The yellowtail program is an active retrieve on both planes: a
  crank-and-pop on top and a half-speed half-yo-yo down (`0HwYl0HO2tc`,
  `5Oh3IPIC5g4`).
- **Downsizing is the fix, where the general note sizes up.** The general note
  runs 3–4 oz deliberately as a size filter — "big bait, big fish," which
  screens out sub-3 lb bass and roughly doubles average grade. Here a 1–1.5 oz
  standard is dropped to 5/8 oz to convert follows into eats: the size lever is
  being pulled to *get bit at all*, not to upgrade the grade (`5Oh3IPIC5g4`).
- **Trolling is a yellowtail-only mode for this bait.** No other application on
  the general note trolls it; the 3–4 mph locating speed and its
  keep-the-other-rod-in-hand workflow exist because the fish must be found
  before it can be cast to (`5Oh3IPIC5g4`).
- **The rig is deliberately single.** Every other structure application on the
  general note can run the two-bait dropper; yellowtail is the one it is
  explicitly withheld from (`5Oh3IPIC5g4`).
- **NOT a delta: the sink read and the rod/line ladder.** "Don't blink on the
  sink," the yellow-braid slack read and the parabolic-rod/line-class pairing
  apply here unchanged — they are keyed to the bait's weight and to how a tube
  bait falls, not to what is eating it.
