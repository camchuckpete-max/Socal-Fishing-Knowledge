# Calico Bass (Kelp Bass)

Calico bass sit on a **defined structure edge** — kelp, reef, boiler rock,
breakwall — and eat what the **current** sweeps past them, so a coastal bass
day is planned off the current read, not the tide clock (`0KQ--N5TjqE`). That
is the load-bearing contrast with the harbor
[spotted bay bass](spotted-bay-bass.md), which are fully tide-driven: for
calico you read the current *across the kelp*. The fishery is year-round and
peaks in the warm months, and it grades geographically — the Baja Pacific
islands from Cedros down to La Bocana give up 4–9 lb fish at volume
(`NGxyOlPx3ug`, `qri15R3caYE`), where a SoCal 7–10 lb fish is a deliberate
trophy hunt on 8–10 in baits rather than a by-product of a numbers day
(`ZoIzpyyS9xw`).

## Where & when

Calico hold on kelp beds, reef, boiler rocks, breakwalls and jetties, and
island structure from the coast out to Catalina, San Clemente and the Channel
Islands, and down the Baja Pacific coast at least to La Bocana. Fish the
**edges and pinch-points** — leading edge, outside edge, isolated stringers
off the main bed — not the dense middle
([bass structure](../locations/bass-structure.md),
[break walls, jetties and riprap](../locations/breakwalls-jetties-riprap.md),
[island structure](../locations/island-structure.md)).

**They do not migrate.** Calico are pelagic spawners whose recruits settle at
the islands or coastal grounds where they hatched and largely stay put; the
only movement is structure pile to structure pile (`LqW32NSkObY`).

**Season.** A year-round coastal fishery that peaks in the warm months. The
[warming transition regime](../conditions/water-regimes.md) (typically spring,
April–May) fires the shallow bite; the warm-stable regime (summer) is the core
kelp season; the fall fragmentation regime's transitional months (typically
October into December) can pull another degree of warmth out of stable water.
Spring and summer are the most active months, on both the spawn and the warmer
water (`LqW32NSkObY`). See [June–July](../seasonal/june-july.md),
[August](../seasonal/august.md), [October](../seasonal/october.md), and the
winter/upwelling-regime bass logic in
[February–March](../seasonal/february-march.md).

**Temperature.** The preference band is roughly **65–68 °F**; past about 70 °F
fish become more lethargic and drop to cooler water, though they will still
hit a top-water bait (`LqW32NSkObY`). See
[water temperature](../conditions/water-temperature.md) and
[water color](../conditions/water-color.md); track temperature and colour spot
to spot and take the warmer, better patch (`0KQ--N5TjqE`) — unless a bait event
is running, which overrides the water read entirely (see
[Feeding triggers](#feeding-triggers)).

**Depth.** The commonly worked bands off Dana Point are **0–8 ft** and **20–100
ft**, occasionally a little past 100 ft (`LqW32NSkObY`).
⚠ Fact-check (single-source): the same source is skeptical of a cited FishBase
figure of fish to ~275 ft, saying he has not personally seen them that deep;
the corpus carries no second depth ceiling.

**Presence is not the bite.** The fish stay on the structure year-round, but on
a clear full moon they gorge overnight and shut down by mid-morning — good
early, then dead until late in the day, with San Clemente Island named as the
exception to the pattern (`Blh2BA-7Ono`). The winter version of the same day is
reaction-bite-only (`5D1vx29LVpI`). Plan coastal bass segments for first light
or late afternoon on the full ([moon](../conditions/moon.md)).

Zone-by-zone trip detail is in the [zone guides](#zone-guides); trip reports
are in the [evidence file](evidence/calico-bass.md).

## Presence & forage

**Why they are where they are — the current builds the pantry.** Current
running uphill into an island stacks against the shelves on the ridge, and
swell pushes the collected nutrients up against the beach; that concentration
draws bait fish, and calico use the resulting dirty water as cover to feed in
(`HnqiE05vdXs`, Catalina Ridge, August 2022). The same read is why an off-colour
patch is not automatically a bad patch: a green-stained, near-opaque water
colour with roughly **5–10 ft visibility** was called the preferred kelp-edge
read at San Clemente Island because the stain gives fish a place to hide and
makes them comfortable feeding up in the open (`9xNhdu2aBqE`).

Documented forage — what calico are recorded eating, whether taken naturally or
off a hook:

- **Grunion.** A kelp-bed blitz had bass puking up grunion, and the fish
  refused bigger baits for anything with a grunion profile (`OYOda6T3f-8`).
- **Squid.** Heavy squid around Catalina Island is what a tournament field
  planned around (`iQLyBzhOSi8`), and anchored stops on live squid produce
  calico alongside yellowtail and barracuda (`zUFbCIWZZMw`).
- **Mackerel.** Big calico eat a live mackerel fished off a kite — the bait
  size normally used to screen the smaller inshore species out (`YVHdDbkQrKk`).
- **Anchovy.** A small-anchovy chum line has calico jumping out of the water
  before the bait lands (`qv0QbLgp72o`).

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: crustacean or benthic forage, and any
seasonal shift in what calico are eating.

## Spawning

Calico are **pelagic spawners** — eggs and larvae drift, and the recruits
settle out at the islands or coastal grounds where they hatched rather than
returning to a natal site (`LqW32NSkObY`).

**Warmer water is spawning water,** and spawning-season fish are more
aggressive and hungrier, which is part of why spring and summer are the most
active months (`LqW32NSkObY`). Summer evening trips connect with calico
**staging to spawn in 40–60 ft over hard bottom** as the light fades
(`RUuPNpK0x-s`); the source scopes it only as "coastal," so treat it as a
seasonal depth pattern rather than a spot.

**Calico aggregate to spawn in summer**, alongside black seabass and grouper —
the aggregation is what made them catchable "by the hundred" and, once fished
through, is what the Cedros Island operators identified as the cause of the
collapse there (`ZBRSB4iwtbU`, `ldVj0BoB-kE`, `Zo92MG459gQ`). See
[Regulations](#regulations) for the release agreement that followed.

Fish take roughly **5–7 years to reach legal size** (`LqW32NSkObY`), which is
the arithmetic behind the voluntary-release ethic in
[Landing & handling](#landing--handling).

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: the calendar window of the SoCal spawn, and
whether the bite shuts down post-spawn.

## Feeding triggers

- **Current is the gate.** The parallel-current rule governs: current running
  **parallel** to the shoreline (uphill or downhill) makes the edge fishable;
  **into** the beach is marginal; **off** the beach is garbage and gets skipped
  (`0KQ--N5TjqE`, [current structure](../conditions/current-structure.md)).
  Mechanism: calico face into the current and ambush from the front of the
  structure, so parallel flow is what delivers food past the edge they are
  sitting on (`Rwy4MqeXCIU`).
- **Light angle sets where they hold, independent of moon phase.** On bright,
  sunny, stable-light days calico "levitate" up into the kelp canopy to get out
  of direct light, concentrating along the **outer canopy edge** worked with the
  current — precise enough to "pinpoint the money shots." Overcast or cloudy
  conditions **spread the fish out** through the water column and structure, so
  a wider area can produce (`RUuPNpK0x-s`). Bright sunny days also push bait
  higher in a warmer surface layer and produce more top-water action
  (`LqW32NSkObY`).
- **Gray light, both ends of the day.** Fish an hour before sunrise into the
  early morning, and again the hour before sunset — the window credited with
  deciding a large share of SoCal bass-tournament results, including a 30-minute
  giant-bass frenzy right at first light. It applies to wall fishing as well as
  open kelp and reef (`RUuPNpK0x-s`).
- **Full moon inverts the day.** Clear full-moon nights have the fish feeding
  overnight, so they bite early and then stop until late in the day; the
  instruction is not to launch until noon or one o'clock if first light is not
  reachable (`Blh2BA-7Ono`). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: mechanism — the
  source explicitly rejects moon visibility as the reason and offers no
  alternative. In winter the same day gives up only a **reaction bite**: a fish
  that gorged overnight snaps at a small bait and takes the whole thing, where
  a long bait leaves it swiping behind the tail (`5D1vx29LVpI`).
- **A bait event overrides the water read.** A grunion blitz in ugly, stagnant,
  no-current kelp produced every-cast bass for two hours (`OYOda6T3f-8`) — when
  the bait is there, fish it regardless of temperature or colour.

## Finding them (sign & sonar)

- **The edge is the spot.** On any kelp head the biting fish sit on the
  **up-current / leading edge** — "hit three or four sides of it and you know in
  two or three casts" (`0KQ--N5TjqE`). Calico face into the current and ambush
  from the front of the kelp, so a lure swept past the leading edge draws them
  out (`Rwy4MqeXCIU`). A 2016 tournament day at San Clemente Island had most
  fish on that front edge with bites falling off past it (`tJFSV3AcIdE`); the
  same island in December 2013 also held fish **up underneath the floating
  canopy itself** — a nuance alongside the leading-edge rule, not a replacement
  for it (`P6Slg6RQiXw`, see the flipping row in
  [Situations → techniques](#situations--techniques)).
- **Read the current off the kelp:** kelp **pulled 20 ft under** = raging
  current (surface presentations dead); **laid down** = strong; **resurfacing**
  = slacking, switch presentation back
  ([current diagnostics](../conditions/current-diagnostics.md)).
- **Birds:** **cormorants diving *in* the kelp** = actively feeding = a calico
  tell, better than gulls inshore; a mixed pile *on* kelp or structure can be a
  bait event worth a look. Gulls locked to a kelp edge with nothing better
  around are worth checking ([bird reading](../conditions/bird-reading.md)).
- **Surface sign:** watch for calico **flipping at the surface** as they chase
  bait up, often under a knot of hovering birds, and cast onto that activity.
  Cast the jig as far as you can, but on a long-range beach stop the fish are
  usually **within 30–40 yards of the boat**, not far out (`r9bF3VtzDUg`). See
  [surface sign vocabulary](../planning/search-and-glassing.md#surface-sign-vocabulary).
- **Sonar signature:** calico mark **tight to the kelp and structure edges and
  on hard bottom** — sitting on the reef-to-sand transition and the outside kelp
  line rather than roaming open water. Typical working depths run the **outside
  kelp edge ~50 ft in to ~25 ft**, and calico will rise **10–15 ft off 80–100 ft
  of reef** to eat a crankbait or sunk iron. Light kelp growth that never
  reaches the surface still holds them — kelp riding roughly 20 ft off the
  bottom in 40–42 ft of water off North County San Diego (`HMdrP4-i9MM`). A
  solid unbroken wall of bait usually has no predators on it; broken or harried
  bait does ([electronics and sounder](../planning/electronics-and-sounder.md),
  [search and glassing](../planning/search-and-glassing.md)).

## Gear summary (class terms)

- **Rod/reel:** 7–9 ft **graphite-composite bass class** (200–300 size reel,
  30–65 lb braid) for slugs, weedless rigs and hard baits; a **jig-stick class**
  for surface iron (the lighter star-drag build and the jig sizes are in
  [calico bass — surface iron](calico-bass-surface-iron.md#rigs--gear)); a
  **crank class** (8–10 ft, 200 reel) for cranking. See
  [gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md).
- **Terminal/lures:** [soft-plastic swimbaits](../lures/soft-plastic-swimbaits.md)
  and slugs on keel-weighted weedless or leadhead hooks; surface
  [iron jigs](../lures/iron-jigs.md); crankbaits and small hard baits with
  beefed hooks and split rings. Heavy fluoro leader (40–65 lb) for kelp.
  Colt Sniper-class
  [stickbaits and jerkbaits](../lures/tuna-poppers-and-stickbaits.md) are also
  fished on top for calico, sharing gear with the yellowtail application.

## Regulations

- **Minimum size — California / CDFW, as of 2026-08; verify current rules
  before fishing.** Calico (kelp) bass minimum legal size is **14 in**
  (`r6j5w40fVHI`), the same figure called on camera for both kelp bass and sand
  bass (`HMdrP4-i9MM`).
- **Bag limit — California / CDFW, as of 2019-03-13; the oldest regulatory
  figure in this note, verify before relying on it.** A bag limit of **5** is
  stated for calico bass (`LqW32NSkObY`).
- **Cedros Island, Baja California — operator agreement, as reported on or
  before 2023-03-26; verify current status before fishing.** The local fishing
  companies collectively adopted **100% catch-and-release** for calico bass,
  grouper (cabrilla) and black seabass. This is an **operator-driven agreement
  among the local companies, not a Mexican federal or state regulation**, so
  compliance depends on operator adherence rather than government enforcement.
  Stated rationale: catches of these species had dropped from routinely "by the
  hundred" to "none or very few," attributed to pressure on the summer spawning
  aggregations (`ZBRSB4iwtbU`, `ldVj0BoB-kE`, `Zo92MG459gQ`). One interview
  re-cut across three uploads, not three independent confirmations; the earliest
  upload is what dates the recording. A Baja operator policy differing from a
  CDFW limit is a **region label, not a doctrine conflict**.

Registered in [regulatory claims](../sources/regulatory-claims.md).

## Doctrine & conflicts

Section order below is not a ranking.

- **The core program.** Calico are current-driven and tide-indifferent: fish the
  parallel-current leading edge, cover water and let a handful of casts grade
  each bed, and let the kelp's posture pick the presentation
  (`0KQ--N5TjqE`, `YIABTTYXeqc`, `kwMIgkCtFUE`).
- **Parallel current is the prior, not the only state.** Late fall at San
  Clemente Island the bass wanted **off-colour water with current running *into*
  the island** — the fish used the dirt to ambush against the structure
  (`Z3rZqy2Pi8E`), and the same mechanism is what stacks nutrients and bait
  against a Catalina beach on an uphill current (`HnqiE05vdXs`). Encode parallel
  as the prior, into-the-beach as a viable off-colour state, off-the-beach as
  dead ([current structure](../conditions/current-structure.md)).
- **Numbers or grade — the choice turns on bait size, and you cannot have
  both.** A 1–4 lb fish comes easily on 4–5.5 in baits; reaching the 7 lb+
  class — the band defined on camera as "trophy," 7–10 lb — requires stepping
  up to **8–10 in** baits, and the trade is stated directly: *"the bigger the
  lure, the bigger the fish… you're probably going to get fewer bites but
  they're going to be bigger"* (`ZoIzpyyS9xw`). Four panelists converged on it
  independently. Decide before you launch which day you are fishing; the
  general bait-sizing guidance
  is in [swimbaits](../techniques/swimbaits.md) and
  [soft-plastic swimbaits](../lures/soft-plastic-swimbaits.md).
- **Effort or timing — these are answers to different questions.** Asked how to
  catch a big calico, Jimmy Decker (Decker's Guide Service) answers with volume
  of effort: *"throw a big bait, spend a lot of time on the water… forget what
  they tell you about the tide, the moon, the wind, just get on a boat and get
  your bait in the water, because you can't catch them sitting on the couch"*
  (`ZoIzpyyS9xw`). That sets how many days you fish; the gray-light, full-moon
  and current rules above set which hours of a given day you fish.
- **Squid or sardine — the corpus does not settle it.** At a Dana Point kelp bed
  the morning's tally was 5 sand bass and 3 calico, every one on a strip of
  squid, and by the same day's end the bass were "actually biting the smaller
  sardines," which the crew called a surprise (`M6U_FVdosr4`); at Catalina, with
  live squid and sardine both soaking, the calico took the sardine
  (`oadK6zIYyCo`). Carry both and let the fish pick.
  ⚠ Fact-check (single-source): each result is one trip, and no source gives a
  condition that predicts which bait wins.
- **A bait event trumps the water read** — a grunion blitz in ugly, stagnant,
  no-current kelp produced every-cast bass (`OYOda6T3f-8`).

## Landing & handling

Procedure for getting the hook out of a deep-hooked fish and measuring it
without wrecking it is in
[dehooking & release handling](../fish-care/dehooking-and-release.md).

- **Grab them by the lower lip** to control the fish for hook removal.
- **Rub your hands together to build a light lather or foam, then rinse the fish
  off** before it goes back — a slime-coat step ahead of release
  (`KTsXdQXAnkU`).
- **Support a tournament-weighed fish by the middle of the body, not the tail**,
  before releasing it (`SdwwpQMJEOI`).
- **The release ethic is voluntary and regionally consistent.** With calico
  taking 5–7 years to reach legal size (`LqW32NSkObY`), the SoCal slogan is "our
  bass are slow to grow, so let them go to fight another day," with
  photo-and-release the encouraged local practice for calico and sand bass
  (`usHl-4SfqDA`); the Cedros operators' 100% release agreement is the Baja form
  of the same reasoning (see [Regulations](#regulations)). Guided days run at
  99% release (`P6Slg6RQiXw`), and a big fish is often not weighed at all —
  "we're not going to weigh him… slow to grow so let him go" (`_r_qKX_7080`).
- ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: whether calico from the deeper 80–100 ft
  band suffer barotrauma and need venting. Dock staff at a bass tournament asked
  whether any fish "need[ed] to be fizzed" without demonstrating it
  (`SdwwpQMJEOI`).
