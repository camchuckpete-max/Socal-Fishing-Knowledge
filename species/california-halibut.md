# California Halibut

California halibut lie in the sand within **5–20 ft of a hard edge**, flip sand
over their backs and ambush bait the current pins against that edge, so the
search is never for the fish — it is for the structure, the current and the
bait (`OpcKQPA3vAI`). Two size classes split the fishery: bay and harbor fish
run **17–33 in**, while a deeper population in **80–200 ft** holds the big ones
(`ghHQe3fP9U4`). Inshore they push under **15 ft** twice a year, in spring
behind the grunion runs and again in fall on structure
(`ghHQe3fP9U4`, `EuYOlsnxXko`). Nothing about them marks on a sounder, which is
why short, controlled drifts on read structure beat long random sand drifts.

## Where & when

Halibut are inshore fish that move around looking for feed rather than holding
one spot and waiting for bait to arrive (`OpcKQPA3vAI`). The fishery splits by
depth: larger fish spend most of their time in **80–200 ft** and only
occasionally give up a small one, while bay and harbor fish run **17–33 in**
with an occasional 30 lb outlier — a fish that outgrows what the harbor can
feed relocates to more productive deeper water (`ghHQe3fP9U4`). Across bays,
harbors and beaches together, halibut come into **less than 15 ft** in spring
and again in fall (`ghHQe3fP9U4`). Within those windows, timing is set by
current and tide, not by the calendar — see
[Feeding triggers](#feeding-triggers).

- **Bays and harbors** — channel edges on a strong tide. The San Diego Bay
  shipping-lane walls and the break-wall mouth are a major "thumb over the
  hose," with sandy bottom right beside the hard edge (`OpcKQPA3vAI`). See
  [bays and harbors](../locations/bays-and-harbors.md).
- **Open-coast beaches** — off-color water just outside the surf line, where
  shale or other hard bottom sits outside the beach and bait gets pushed in.
  A fish of almost **50 lb** came off a Huntington bubbler zone in **30 ft**
  (`OpcKQPA3vAI`).
- **Islands and squid beds** — backside Catalina, the Santa Rosa flats, San
  Clemente Island and Coronado Island in **60–100+ ft**: halibut sit on the
  structure *beside* the squid nests, and stay on that structure after the
  squid leaves (`OpcKQPA3vAI`). The
  [warming transition regime](../conditions/water-regimes.md) and its squid-bed
  months ([April priors](../seasonal/april.md) carry the squid cycle) stack big
  island fish next to the nests. See
  [island structure](../locations/island-structure.md).
- **Spring beaches follow the grunion cycle; fall beaches do not**
  (`EuYOlsnxXko`). In fall the bait inshore is smaller — most baits hatch
  through spring and summer — and fish sit farther off the beach, more often in
  a **9–11 ft** zone on beaches and in bays, keyed to structure: protected
  beaches with eelgrass, point breaks, and "finger rock" areas where rock has
  eroded into strips with sand filled between them. Use a spring grunion run to
  locate the productive zones, then fish those same zones in fall once the
  grunion cue is gone.

Presence is not a bite: milky "seabass/halibut color" water covering a whole
beach or island holds fish only where a local variation sets up
(`OpcKQPA3vAI`, `6TBxHnkYXI0`) — see [Finding them](#finding-them-sign--sonar).
Trip reports by zone are in the [evidence file](evidence/california-halibut.md).

## Presence & forage

- **Current across structure is what puts bait — and therefore halibut — on a
  spot.** Current crossing a rock, a channel edge, a shale patch or a dredged
  lip speeds up as the depth changes and spins off an eddy; the eddy traps
  plankton; the plankton feeds bait fish, which line up pointing into the
  current around the structure (`OpcKQPA3vAI`). That is what a halibut lying in
  the sand alongside shoots up into.
- **Relief does not have to be much.** A rock **15 ft across** with relief low
  enough that it barely paints on the sounder changes the current, traps
  plankton, holds bait, and gives a halibut a place to lie and pick off bait
  without competing with a calico, which gets to food far faster
  (`OpcKQPA3vAI`).
- **Dead sand starves them.** A halibut sitting in **105 ft** of open sand off
  a beach could starve before a bait fish swam into feeding range
  (`OpcKQPA3vAI`) — the mechanism behind "sand next to structure."
- **Bubbler zones work like current eddies.** Shale off Huntington vents
  methane, and those bubble areas trap plankton and aggregate bait the same way
  an eddy does (`OpcKQPA3vAI`).
- **Natural forage named in the corpus:** sardine, anchovy, smelt and lizard
  fish, with no problem eating a mackerel or a really big sardine
  (`OpcKQPA3vAI`); market squid on the island nests (`OpcKQPA3vAI`); grunion
  staging off the beach ahead of a spring run (`EuYOlsnxXko`).
- **Depth follows the bait, not a number.** Bait sometimes sits in 20 ft and
  sometimes in 80 ft, and a halibut does not starve in 20 ft while the bait is
  in 80 (`OpcKQPA3vAI`).
- **They are active feeders, not ambush-only.** They chase bait, move to get
  it, and blow up on it in knee-deep water (`EuYOlsnxXko`, `fHMwcm1xRQk`).

## Spawning

- **Nests are aggregations, not solitary pairs:** a cluster of **19–25 in**
  males around one larger **30–32 in** female (`ghHQe3fP9U4`).
- **Certain spots hold the aggregations year after year**, tied more to bottom
  composition than to bait alone — spot fidelity is strongest when they are
  spawning (`ghHQe3fP9U4`).
- **Fish reach the nests along fixed travel paths.** Halibut living on sandy
  structure outside a bay, harbor or beach move into the **9–15 ft** spawning
  zone along well-established corridors (`ghHQe3fP9U4`) — which is what makes
  the travel line fishable, see [Finding them](#finding-them-sign--sonar).
- **A voluntary keep rule on a found nest, not a legal one:** keep one of the
  abundant **23–25 in** males rather than the single female, since removing her
  costs the whole nest its chance to spawn, and keep the nest's location to
  yourself (`ghHQe3fP9U4`). Requirements are in
  [Regulations](#regulations); this is a recommendation.

  long a nest holds on a spot.

## Feeding triggers

- **Current is the gate.** Off-color water with no current and no bait produces
  nothing; the three components — off-color water, current, bait — have to be
  present together (`OpcKQPA3vAI`).
- **Tide change makes or kills a bite.** Be on the highest-percentage spot an
  hour before the change (`6TBxHnkYXI0`). A **strong outgoing tide** over a
  hard channel edge in San Diego Bay is the worked example (`OpcKQPA3vAI`).
  See [tide and slack](../conditions/tide-and-slack.md).
- **Spring beach windows are set by the grunion cycle** (`EuYOlsnxXko`).
  Grunion spawn on a predictable set of tides and come up the beach on **high
  tides after dark**; staging fish show **100–500 ft off the beach** from
  around midday before a run, marked by gulls, pelicans and terns working that
  zone. Two windows bracket the run:
  - **The incoming tide that afternoon**, when an onshore wind in your face
    forces a castable lure and the rising water covers structure you can wade a
    few feet to reach.
  - **The following morning's low tide**, when the fish that came up on the
    high tide are pushed back gradually rather than leaving at once, and
    concentrate in **knee- to hip-deep water** at the sand-water edge — often
    shallower than a waders angler is standing.
- **Overcast extends the window.** Those mornings run overcast about **90%** of
  the time, lengthening the low-light period and with it the bite window on
  reaction baits (`EuYOlsnxXko`).

## Finding them (sign & sonar)

**You cannot mark a halibut** — they lie flat and flick sand over their backs
(`OpcKQPA3vAI`). You mark the conditions that put one there. General method:
[search and glassing](../planning/search-and-glassing.md).

- **The water triad — off-color plus current plus bait, all three.** Milky
  "seabass/halibut color" water alone only says current or surge is churning
  silt (`OpcKQPA3vAI`). If a whole island or beach is that color, it is not a
  spot: what fishes is a **local variation** — a dirty patch with a hard rip
  and a dirty foam edge inside otherwise cleaner water, where halibut, white
  seabass and calico all hide in the dirt to ambush bait drifting out of the
  clean water (`OpcKQPA3vAI`, `6TBxHnkYXI0`). See
  [water color](../conditions/water-color.md).
- **Give a spot 20–30 minutes, no longer.** If you are not marking bait or
  catching *anything* — leopard shark, sargo, a small calico all count as
  feeding sign — leave (`OpcKQPA3vAI`).
- **Bait volume is the best single search indicator**; halibut are very good at
  finding wherever the most bait is (`ghHQe3fP9U4`).
- **On the meter, hunt the soft/hard transition and structure edges, and grade
  the bait by height.** Bait 20 ft down over 80 ft of water will not fire a
  halibut; bait pinned near the bottom next to a rock will (`OpcKQPA3vAI`).
  Charting with hard-bottom shading is what turns a long random sand drift into
  a short drift on located shale and small rock (`OpcKQPA3vAI`). Method:
  [electronics and sounder](../planning/electronics-and-sounder.md).
- **Spot fidelity, with rotation.** Halibut return to the same physical spots
  year after year, but which of several known spots produces shifts between
  years — recheck them rather than writing one off after a slow season
  (`ghHQe3fP9U4`). The same holds on a beach that looks uniform end to end: a
  few sub-spots outfish the rest, tied to bait, eelgrass, depth changes, and
  how the deeper water leads up to the flat.
- **Think like a gillnet — fish the travel line, not just the endpoint.** Work
  where a fish would move between deep water and the shallow zone
  (`ghHQe3fP9U4`).
- **They are depth-specific.** A fish caught in **14 ft** is a reason to look
  for similar habitat in **13–15 ft** rather than at a different depth, and the
  same holds in deeper water (`ghHQe3fP9U4`).
- **Visual sign in the shallows:** halibut blowing up on bait in knee-deep
  water (`EuYOlsnxXko`).
- **Drift discipline:** drift *along* the structure, not across it — start
  **20 ft up-current** of the spot and end **20 ft past** it, and if it looked
  good and produced nothing, drift the other side (`OpcKQPA3vAI`). You may have
  to wait for conditions that let you drift the right way.

## Gear summary (class terms)

- **Boat bait — [30 lb bait class](../tackle/gear-classes.md)** (soft-tipped
  star-drag) at the islands and offshore; a **15 lb inshore rod**, the same one
  fished for spotties, in the bay. Halibut do not pull hard, so line class is
  about bait presentation and bottom contact rather than strength
  (`a5u8BaYzw8c`).
- **Surf rods, by lure class:** a light, moderate-action spinning rod (~9½ ft,
  2500–3000 reel, PE1/~10 lb 8-carrier braid) covers both minnow hardbaits and
  leadhead swimbaits from the beach; a heavier "striper rod" class (~9'6")
  steps up for weedless swimbaits and bigger plugs in kelp (`fHMwcm1xRQk`) —
  see [surf halibut rod lineup](../tackle/tackle-express-surf-halibut-rod-lineup.md).
- **Terminal:** a [trap rig](../rigging/trap-rig.md) off a three-way with a
  breakaway clip so sinkers swap by depth and current, on a short fluorocarbon
  leader (`a5u8BaYzw8c`). Hook pairing by size, leader length and class, and
  the sinker shape and weight band are in
  [live-bait drift](california-halibut-live-bait-drift.md).
- **Hooks on hard baits:** a stock **size six treble** is effectively a trout
  hook in a 15-lb game fish, so a big fish on one is a gamble; the swap is a
  soft-plastic rig with the same swimming action on one big single hook
  (`Bok0db0G4xE`) — heads and reasoning in
  [inshore crankbaits — rigging](../techniques/inshore-crankbaits.md#rigging).
- **Colors:** hardbaits start at white, or white with a pink belly, with pink
  called a classic halibut color and especially a surf-halibut one
  (`Qqdt6f8Mjd4`); soft-plastic reaction
  baits run a four-color family — white, chartreuse, pink, mint
  (`eOLoHxoqk6E`). The stated reason solid white draws bites: a halibut lying
  on the bottom looking up mostly sees a baitfish's white belly, and from that
  position it already holds the advantage, so a solid white lure reads as an
  easy, energy-efficient meal (`eOLoHxoqk6E`). ⚠ Fact-check (single-source):
  one source, offered on camera as a theory.
- **Tube baits sized by water:** size up outside in the surf and coastal zone,
  run **3/8 or 5/8 oz** inside a bay; **mint** and **glow green** named as
  working, and a white "shad" pattern for **morning and evening**
  (`5Oh3IPIC5g4`) — see
  [tube baits](../lures/tube-baits.md#color-selection-and-the-oreo-cookie-effect-chad-hookup-baits-co-owner-5oh3ipic5g4-2020).
  ⚠ misplaced-content: the color list is lure implementation and belongs with
  the lure note, not in the router.
- **Depth control from the beach is a rod-handling lever, not only a lure
  choice:** tip low runs the lure deepest, mid-height moderates it, tip up
  keeps it off the bottom, the same across leadhead plastics, metals and minnow
  hardbaits (`QJd7A6oiV4U`) — see
  [beach depth control](../techniques/beach-lure-depth-control.md).

## Doctrine & conflicts

Section order below is not a ranking.

- **Isolate structure or cover water — the choice turns on whether the spot is
  a defined edge you can drift correctly, or open flat.** On a located edge,
  sand next to structure plus short drifts along it beats long random sand
  drifts (`OpcKQPA3vAI`). On open water, winding a hard bait back noticeably
  faster than instinct suggests draws halibut into chasing and committing hard,
  from a boat and from shore alike (`46ha7J1Fc6A`), and in **1–4 ft** of surf a
  shallow-running minnow works because the fish comes up to the lure rather
  than the lure digging into sand (`E4CDqBFOaP8`).
- **Sportboat and private-boat tactics differ by line count.** Thirty lines
  dragging a long drift is a numbers game that works on a sportboat; a
  two-angler private boat should isolate the structure and pick it apart — that
  is the whole advantage (`OpcKQPA3vAI`).
- **Hook-pull: leadhead against fin bait — a hookup-geometry claim.** A
  large-hook lead head sits past the fish's lips so the hook lands in the back
  of the skull and a hooked fish stays hooked; against that, **90%** of the
  lost-halibut heartbreak stories are attributed to a poorly hooked fin bait on
  a [trap rig](../rigging/trap-rig.md) that panics at the boat and pulls the
  hook (`hjZ3hgq0Bzg`). ⚠ Fact-check (single-source): one source's account, not
  a KB-wide ruling on fluke versus bait hookup rates.
- **The water triad is shared with [white seabass](white-seabass.md)** — same
  milky "seabass/halibut color" water, same all-three requirement. The two
  species diverge on depth and presentation, so the shared read selects the
  zone, not the program.

## Landing & handling

- **One long bolting run, then a straight light-drag grind.** Keep a high rod
  tip and do not pump; if you stay calm the fish stays calm and lays out just
  under the surface (`a5u8BaYzw8c`). Keep the head **underwater** to the gaff —
  the head breaking the surface is when they go zero-to-100 and throw the hook.
- **Why light drag works:** steady constant tension keeps the rod bent and the
  whole system under elastic load, so the fish never gets the abrupt
  slack/shock moment that triggers a hard head-shake (`1l05hEXDaWw`).
  Especially important on minnow-style hard baits with small trebles.
- **The boat itself triggers a second panic run.** A big fish (**30 in**,
  **40 in** class) often winds straight in with no real fight until it sees the
  boat, then bolts; both slacking the line off and pulling too hard against
  that run pull the hook (`akUrZ_OYy4c`).
- **Hook-set depends on what it ate.** On a lure the bite is a distinct thump:
  point the rod, wind fast until the line comes tight, and only then swing —
  the slack has to come out first (`dWqD8l3jW6w`). On bait the bite is subtle,
  so lift and wind rather than swinging (`a5u8BaYzw8c`) — how the take reads on
  the drift is in [live-bait drift](california-halibut-live-bait-drift.md).
- **Gaffing:** a **3-in hook on a ~6-ft gaff** covers halibut up to 40–50 lb —
  orientation and safety in [gaffing](../fish-care/gaffing.md).
- **Releasing:** large-hole poly rope nets let a halibut's fins pass through
  and get cut, leading to infection and death after release (`PJEG_RIkzF0`) —
  the fine-mesh or rubberized fix is in
  [dehooking and release](../fish-care/dehooking-and-release.md#landing-net-mesh--protecting-a-released-fishs-fins).

  for California halibut. General method:
