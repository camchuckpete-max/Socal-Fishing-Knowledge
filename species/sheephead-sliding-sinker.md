# California Sheephead — sliding sinker

**One species, one technique.** The dedicated SoCal sheephead program is a chum
line plus a thin strip of cut bait **suspended off the bottom** on a slider
capped at **1/4–1/2 oz**, pinned on a **No. 2 or No. 4** thin-wire bronze or
black hook — never chrome (`OSbAHdB4uPs`). Reach for it anchored on an
artificial reef or inshore rock, especially in the cold-water months when the
summer pelagic season has shut off and the fish that lives here every day is the
one worth catching (`OSbAHdB4uPs`). The free-sliding Carolina format and how a
fish takes line through the lead are in
[sliding sinker](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md); this page is what changes
because the fish is a sheephead.

## When this is the call

**Anchored on a chummed artificial reef or inshore rock.** This is the row the
router ranks first for the dedicated fishery — see
[California sheephead — situations → techniques](sheephead.md#situations--techniques).
The gate is the chum, not the season: the reef holds fish year-round and gives
them up once the chum line is running — "you get that chum in the water … you'll
start to catch sheephead" (`OSbAHdB4uPs`). Getting the chum to hold beside the
boat is [chunking](../techniques/chunking.md).

**Cold water is the argument for it, not against it.** The program is pitched
explicitly at the season after the dorado, yellowtail and bluefin run ends —
"just because the water got cold, it's not time to put away your stuff"
(`OSbAHdB4uPs`) — and the small hooks, small weights and willing fish also make
it the call when the job is teaching a new angler or a kid, with dinner going
home at the end (`OSbAHdB4uPs`).

**It is not the call on a deep bank or island bottom stop.** Out on the banks
and deep island rock the same species comes over the rail on squid dropped to
the bottom for rockfish; that is a rockfish drift that sheephead join, not this
program, and the two rules do not contradict — see
[California sheephead — doctrine & conflicts](sheephead.md#doctrine--conflicts).

## How California sheephead changes the execution

**Suspend the bait; a bottomed bait is the failure mode.** A sheephead's mouth
is at the **front of the face, not underneath**, so it feeds up into the column
rather than down into the sand. Lay the bait on the bottom and what eats it is
shovelnose sharks, bat rays, smoothhound and leopard sharks instead
(`OSbAHdB4uPs`). Everything else in the rig — the weight ceiling, the light
line, the small hook — exists to keep the strip hanging above the deck. The
general note reaches the same conclusion for white seabass from the same
forward-mouth mechanism, but works it as a cast-and-retrieve; here the bait is
suspended over a chum line at anchor.

**The weight is a strike-zone control, not a bottom-finding tool.** A quarter to
a half ounce, and **never heavier than a half — "you don't want to go flying
by"** (`OSbAHdB4uPs`). The variable being managed is how long the bait spends in
front of a fish that is holding above the structure; a heavier slider carries
the strip past it and down into the ray zone.

**The bait is cut thin so a small mouth can suck it straight in.** Cut clam,
mussel and squid into little strips rather than chunks (`OSbAHdB4uPs`). The
squid version is hooked at the very tip so the rest threads on "just like a
piece of spaghetti" and **hangs loose below the hook**, never bunched into "a
big ball … a big wad … a square meal" (`yKaHcxX46l4`); the loose tail also puts
"a tiny bit of action" on the strip in the water (`yKaHcxX46l4`).

**Small hook is the whole point, not a compromise.** On the strip presentation
"sheephead are going to eat this smaller hook all day every day"
(`yKaHcxX46l4`), which is why the size call sits at No. 2/No. 4 rather than
being scaled up to the fish's weight.

## Rigs & gear

- **Weight: a small slider, 1/4 oz or 1/2 oz, never heavier than 1/2 oz**
  (`OSbAHdB4uPs`). This is the lightest tier on
  [sliding sinker](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md) — the same range as the
  white seabass ladder's light end, and far below the 2–4 oz yellowtail and
  8–24 oz Baja grouper builds.
- **Hook: No. 2 or No. 4, thin-wire, bronze or black finish — never chrome**
  ("give the chrome hooks to your buddies") (`OSbAHdB4uPs`). A third species
  corroborating the bronze/black-over-chrome stealth doctrine in
  [hooks](../tackle/hooks.md). ⚠ Fact-check (single-source): the weight
  ceiling and this hook size/finish call both rest on this one tips video and
  are numerically precise and load-bearing for the whole program.
- **Bait, ranked: pismo clam → mussel → squid.** Pismo clam is the go-to for
  **big** sheephead when the budget allows; mussel and squid are the everyday
  options, and all three are cut into strips rather than chunks
  (`OSbAHdB4uPs`). **Mussel suits a treble hook** because it is a big wad of
  slime with nothing for a single point to grip (`OSbAHdB4uPs`). ⚠ Fact-check
  (contradicted-by-source): the source lists bait in varying order ("that
  strip of squid or that piece of muscle or pismo clam," then "get some
  clams get some mussels get some squid") and calls pismo clam the go-to for
  big fish, but never ranks mussel above squid — the strict
  pismo-clam→mussel→squid hierarchy stated here is not in the source.
- **Squid prep:** heads, tentacles and guts go into the chum bag, the body is
  split and cut into thin strips (`yKaHcxX46l4`) — full strip prep in
  [hooks](../tackle/hooks.md), chum delivery in
  [chunking](../techniques/chunking.md).
- **Rod/reel:** light spinning or a small level-wind, on the light-line finesse
  build in [chunking](../techniques/chunking.md). Class terms in
  [gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md).
- **Terminal chain:** the chummed-reef program ties the hook straight to the
  mono — no swivel, no beads — because heavy line, big hooks, "swivels and
  beads and crap" are what put reef fish off (`f4qYtHACGyk`). That rule is
  stated for the split-shot version of the reef build, not for the slider; no
  source describes the slider's own chain.
- A jumbo sheephead is credited to a sliding-egg-sinker rig in a clip **title
  only**, with no rigging shown or described in the footage (`QEpa3WYbEa8`).

## Differs from the general method

Against [sliding sinker](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md), which owns the
free-sliding Carolina format, the per-species weight ladders and how to feed a
bite:

- **Bottom contact is the error, not the goal.** The general note's default is
  the sinker settling to the bottom and held in light contact; here the bait
  must never reach it, because the fish feeds forward and the bottom belongs to
  rays and sharks (`OSbAHdB4uPs`).
- **The weight rule is a hard ceiling, not a drift-sizing rule.** The general
  rule is the least lead that will still tick bottom, sized to depth and
  current. This program caps at 1/2 oz outright, on the strike-zone reasoning
  rather than on what holds bottom (`OSbAHdB4uPs`).
- **The hook is the smallest on the note.** No. 2/No. 4 thin wire, against the
  octopus-offset default, the 2/0–4/0 J on the San Diego yellowtail build and
  the 12/0-class Baja grouper hook — and the finish is specified, which no other
  build on the general note does (`OSbAHdB4uPs`).
- **The bait is cut, not live or whole.** The general note's builds run live
  bait or whole fresh dead squid; this one runs thin strips of clam, mussel or
  squid, cut small for a small mouth (`OSbAHdB4uPs`, `yKaHcxX46l4`).
- **The rig is simplified, not just downsized.** The chummed shallow-reef
  program ties the hook straight to the mono with no swivel or beads
  (`f4qYtHACGyk`), where the general format runs sinker → bead → swivel →
  leader → hook — though that straight-tie rule is on record for the reef
  build's split-shot version rather than for the slider itself.
- **Chum is a precondition, not an option.** No other build on the general note
  depends on a chum line to produce the bite (`OSbAHdB4uPs`).
