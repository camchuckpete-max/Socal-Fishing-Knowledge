# Yellowfin Tuna — flyline

**One species, one technique.** A flylined live sardine on a circle hook is the
bread-and-butter yellowfin programme once a school is located, and almost
everything specific to it is bait economy and bite handling: chum goes in before
any hook bait, the leader and hook step down a ladder when the school gets
picky, the bait net doubles as a presentation tool, and the bite is answered
with a counted wait rather than a swing (`8M4QhL-Qb7E`). The unweighted
presentation, hook placement and bait keeping stay in
[flyline](../techniques/flyline.md); this page is what changes because the fish
is a yellowfin.

## When this is the call

**A school is located and there is live bait aboard.** This is the
chummed-up-school and picky-school pair of rows in
[yellowfin — situations → techniques](yellowfin-tuna.md#situations--techniques);
the water read and the search that put the boat on the school stay on the
router.

**Bait budget is what shapes the programme.** A private boat carries a two-scoop
maximum against the sportboat's effectively bottomless chum, which is what
pushes the private-boat game toward the scale-down and the bait-net trick rather
than sustained chumming (`8M4QhL-Qb7E`). ⚠ Fact-check (single-source): the
two-scoop figure is precise and sourced only to `8M4QhL-Qb7E`. On a sportboat
the same fish are drawn up by weight of chum instead — a school marked at 40 ft
came into hook range inside roughly two minutes of steady bait (`lxFNVdDhMy4`).
⚠ Fact-check (single-source): the 40 ft / roughly-two-minute figures are
precise and sourced only to `lxFNVdDhMy4`.

**Chum first, hook bait second — always.** Any time a school of yellowfin is
located, the first move is a quick dip of the bait net and a couple of freebies
over the side to fire them up and pull them to the boat, before a hook bait is
ever presented (`8M4QhL-Qb7E`).

**The eager case needs none of the rest of this page.** With good live sardines
aboard and the right school found, a little chum and a hook sardine is the whole
programme — the ladder and the bait-net trick below are the answer to fish that
act "like their bluefin cousins" and go reluctant on a hook bait
(`8M4QhL-Qb7E`).

## How yellowfin changes the execution

**The bait net is a presentation tool, not just a bait-picking tool — the
bait-net trick.** On a school that rolls on onesie-twosie freebies but will not
touch a hook bait, dip the net for a handful of **three to five** baits, pick
one good bait out of it and nose-hook it, then throw the rest of the netful in
as quickly as possible with the hook bait mixed in among them. The stated
mechanism is that the netful reads to the fish as "a little mini bait ball" —
the commotion triggers them, and the hook bait sitting inside the group is
harder to pick out by its line and hook (`8M4QhL-Qb7E`). ⚠ Fact-check
(single-source): the three-to-five-bait count is precise and sourced only to
`8M4QhL-Qb7E`.

**Slow-trolling the bait both locates and entices.** On arrival in the zone,
with the right water, birds and paddies but no committed bite, a nose-hooked
live bait slow-trolled is the way to find schools and to draw a bite out of
picky fish. Free-spool the bait **well back** off the boat and away from the
props so a boat-shy fish will still come to it, then put the boat **just in
gear** — sometimes only **one engine** in gear — so it is barely moving forward
(`8M4QhL-Qb7E`). See [slow-trolling live bait](../techniques/slow-trolling-bait.md).

**Clicker tension is a setting, not a switch.** With the boat in gear, set the
clicker **just heavy enough that the bait cannot pull line by itself**. That
tension is what lets the bait swim freely while still telling you what it is
doing — a skittish bait is audible on the clicker, and a bite rips it
outright. The exact setting depends on the reel (`8M4QhL-Qb7E`).

**On the bite: count ten in free spool, then strike and wind tight.** Keep the
boat in gear, go back to the rod and drop it into **free spool**, letting the
fish take the bait for a deliberate **10-count** — counted out, not estimated —
before going to **strike** and simply winding tight. On circle hooks nothing
more is needed; there is no swing (`8M4QhL-Qb7E`). ⚠ Fact-check
(single-source): the 10-count figure is precise and sourced only to
`8M4QhL-Qb7E`.

## Rigs & gear

- **Entry point: a 30 lb setup with a 2/0 circle.** A **2/0 circle hook**
  (Gamakatsu Nautilus named on camera) on a **30 lb** setup is the default,
  chosen because it is well matched to the size of a healthy live sardine —
  hook to bait, not hook to fish (`8M4QhL-Qb7E`). ⚠ Fact-check (single-source):
  the 2/0-on-30-lb entry point is precise and sourced only to `8M4QhL-Qb7E`.
  Class terms in
  [gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md); hook style and sizing generally in
  [hooks](../tackle/hooks.md).
- **The scale-down ladder, and the climb back.** When a school reacts to chum
  but will not bite the hook bait, drop from the 30 lb setup to a **20 lb** or
  even **15 lb** leader on a **size 1 thin-wire circle**. The stated mechanism
  is the wire gauge as much as the leader: thin wire "lets the bait swim a lot
  more freely and kind of hides itself even better." Then work the balance back
  up — once they are biting the 15, try **25**, then try to get back to **30**
  (`8M4QhL-Qb7E`). ⚠ Fact-check (single-source): the 20/15 lb, size 1
  thin-wire, and 25/30 lb climb-back figures are precise and sourced only to
  `8M4QhL-Qb7E`. The step down is a concession to the bite, not a setting to
  start on.
- **Picky-bite leader and hook: three figures, one situation.** The decision
  turns on how far down you have to go to get bit, not on which source you
  follow — go in at 30 lb and a 2/0 circle, step down only until the bite comes,
  then climb back (`8M4QhL-Qb7E`). The three recorded stepping-off points are
  **15–20 lb leader with a size 1 thin-wire circle** (`8M4QhL-Qb7E`), a **25 lb
  fluorocarbon leader with a thin-wire size 2 circle**, with 20–25 lb fluoro
  running on multiple rods the same day (`AIHvJj-paoo`), and **20 or 25 lb
  fluorocarbon** for a still-forming school at the Coronado Islands
  (`NN5MWeRA28o`, promotional, low confidence). All three give the same reason
  where they give one: thinner wire and lighter leader let the bait swim free
  and hide the terminal end. **Decision frame:** the order they are written in
  is not a ranking — take the lightest step that gets bit and go back up.
- **Bait:** live **sardine**, picked rather than grabbed — no scales off, no red
  spots (`8M4QhL-Qb7E`). Bait keeping and selection depth are in
  [fishing live bait](../bait/fishing-live-bait.md).
- ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: a rod, reel or main-line build for this
  programme beyond the "30 lb setup" framing, and no leader length or knot for
  the scaled-down builds.

## Differs from the general method

Against [flyline](../techniques/flyline.md), which owns the unweighted
presentation, hook position and bait keeping:

- **Chum sequencing is part of the technique, not a preamble.** The general note
  starts at the cast; here the first act on locating a school is a netful of
  freebies, and no hook bait goes in the water until the fish are up and rolling
  on them (`8M4QhL-Qb7E`).
- **The hook/leader is a ladder, not a size.** The general note sizes the hook
  to the bait in your hand and leaves it there; this programme starts at 2/0 on
  30 lb for exactly that reason, then walks down to a size 1 thin-wire circle on
  15 lb and deliberately climbs back — the size is a reading of the bite, not a
  property of the bait (`8M4QhL-Qb7E`).
- **A netful of chum is thrown *with* the hook bait.** The general note's chum
  bucket takes the culled baits; here three to five good baits are spent on one
  cast so the hook bait arrives inside a mini bait ball (`8M4QhL-Qb7E`).
- **The slow-troll carries a stated clicker spec.** The general note names the
  nose hook as slow-trollable and stops there; the yellowfin version pins the
  boat to just-in-gear (sometimes one engine), the bait well back off the props,
  and the clicker to the exact tension at which the bait cannot pull line on its
  own (`8M4QhL-Qb7E`).
- **The bite gets a counted wait before the reel is touched.** The general note
  answers a bite with a set drag and a circle hook that finds the corner on its
  own; here the reel goes to free spool for a counted 10 first, and only then to
  strike and wind tight (`8M4QhL-Qb7E`).
