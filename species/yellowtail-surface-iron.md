# Yellowtail — surface iron

The long rod and a Tady 45 or Salas 7X is the first outfit off the rack when
yellowtail show on top, and the reason is reach: it puts a big profile on fish
that will not let a boat get close. Mechanics of the cast, the kick and the
grind are universal and live in
[surface iron](../techniques/surface-iron.md); this page is what changes when
the fish under the jig is a yellowtail.

## When this is the call

**Fish showing on the surface.** Boiling or breezing fish, or fish worked under
a [kelp paddy](../conditions/kelp-paddies.md), are the iron's water. When
yellowtail are staging up rather than boiling they are still surface-iron and
light-bait fish rather than yo-yo fish; the moment they boil, go all-in on the
surface iron (`0DKmYId1NN4`). The router's full ranking against flylined bait
and stickbaits is in [yellowtail](yellowtail.md).

**It stops being the call when the wind stands the water up.** After a run of
surface days, hard wind at **20–25 kt+** with fish metered rather than showing
sends the fleet bite mostly to [yo-yo iron](../techniques/yo-yo-iron.md); the
surface iron still picks a fish, so this is a re-rank rather than an
abandonment (`ejUTAnONf7o`).

**A trolled rod getting bit is an iron cue.** Yellowtail school, so a troll
strike means more fish are under the boat — cast an iron into the same water
while the first fish is fought (`jo8lMdFZ1bk`).

## How yellowtail changes the execution

**They eat it at the boat, so the retrieve is not over until the jig is out of
the water.** Yellowtail often eat the jig **right next to the boat or kayak**
after trailing it in — keep working the retrieve, rod tip down, all the way
back (`VpW91AKOFVQ`). The general tip-down rule exists so the iron kicks side
to side; what is specific here is that the last five feet are a real bite zone
rather than the end of a cast.

**Sink it before you wind.** Letting the iron fall before starting the retrieve
covers depth instead of only fishing the top (`ntQXxcH5sjI`). On yellowtail
this is a named producer on both sides of the fishery: about **30 ft** of sink
before winding drew a first-cast strike at Catalina
(`oadK6zIYyCo`), and the same move on a Tady 45 was framed as a
near-"yellowtail guarantee" at [Cedros](../locations/cedros-island.md)
(`9tIp4n7q850`).

**Speed is a yellowtail dial, but the fish is not the reason.** A high-gear
jig-stick reel (~**42 in per crank**, Tranx-500-class HG) covers slow grind to
fast yellow speed on cadence alone. Against that sits the corpus's flat denial
of species-specific retrieves: "I was using a yellowtail retrieve, a calico
bass retrieve, a barracuda retrieve — no. When the lure swims right, fish bite
it" (`Ec2GaNpiOHI`). Both hold — the *speed* changes with the fish, the
*action* is the only thing that matters, and you cannot out-wind a fish either
way. Decide on the swim, not on the species.

**Bait size beats fish size when picking the jig.** A smaller iron fished for
**small anchovy** is not only a small-grade-fish pattern: even bigger
yellowtail key onto small anchovy and can be out-produced by the smaller jig,
strongest **earlier in the year** (`JgXMpjxRxmo`). In wind, step up to a
heavier iron (Salas 7X class) to punch the cast rather than to match a bigger
fish.

**Colours are a short, agreed list.** Scrambled egg, bird shit, mint, and
blue/white are the yellowtail iron colours (nate); the full colour doctrine,
including what light and water clarity do to the choice, is in
[surface iron — colour](../techniques/surface-iron-color.md).

## Rigs & gear

- **Rod and reel: jig-stick class.** The long rod is chosen for casting
  distance — the reason the iron reaches fish a boat cannot approach
  (`lYyi_Yh3S5g`). Why high-ratio reels are wrong for the grind is argued in
  [surface iron](../techniques/surface-iron.md#reel--why-high-ratio-reels-are-wrong-for-this).
- **Gear ratio: 7:1 as the middle ground** for landing big yellowtail —
  torque against speed rather than the fastest available
  ([Tackle Express](../tackle/tackle-express-baitcaster-gear-ratio-yellowtail.md)).
- **Jigs: Tady 45 / Salas 7X class**, named together as "the number one"
  surface iron and singled out for yellowtail at
  [Cedros](../locations/cedros-island.md) and the San Benito Islands
  (`lYyi_Yh3S5g`) — "surface jigs that swim really well with a slow retrieve."
  Model detail: [iron jigs](../lures/iron-jigs.md).
- **Line:** straight mono in the **40–50 lb** band; a trophy yellowtail is
  possible at any moment, so the heavier end runs when the water allows it.
  Class lexicon: [gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md).

## Differs from the general method

Against [surface iron](../techniques/surface-iron.md), which owns the cast, the
kick, cadence and the colour argument for every species that eats an iron:

- **The bite zone extends to the rail.** Calico and barracuda iron work does
  not carry the at-the-boat rule that yellowtail do (`VpW91AKOFVQ`).
- **Sink-then-wind is elevated from an option to a named producer**, with two
  independent zone citations behind it (`oadK6zIYyCo`, `9tIp4n7q850`) rather
  than the general "it covers different depths."
- **Jig size is set by bait size, including for big fish** (`JgXMpjxRxmo`) —
  the general note treats size as a distance-and-conditions choice.
- **The colour set is narrow and specific** where the general note keeps the
  full colour argument open.
- **The general note's "no species-specific retrieve" claim is preserved
  here, not overturned** (`Ec2GaNpiOHI`): this page changes speed and reads,
  never the assertion that action beats species.
