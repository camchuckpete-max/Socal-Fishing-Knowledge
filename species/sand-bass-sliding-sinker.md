# Barred Sand Bass — sliding sinker

**One species, one technique.** A live anchovy on a small sliding egg sinker is
the sand bass live-bait program, and every number in it is scaled down to the
bait rather than to the fish: a **1/2 oz** lead, a **size 2 J hook**, **20 lb**
fluorocarbon, and a rod chosen for a whippy tip (`P2OzCf2CwXI`). Reach for it
when the tank is full of anchovy and the fish are hovering above or within the
structure, because the cast is a slow sink through the *whole* column rather
than a bait parked at one depth. The free-sliding rig format and how a fish
takes line through the lead are in
[sliding sinker](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md); this page is what changes
because the fish is a barred sand bass and the bait is an anchovy.

## When this is the call

**Live anchovy aboard, fish above or within structure.** This is the row the
router ranks the live-bait presentation first for, against plastics, the dropper
loop and the winter crankbait program — see
[barred sand bass — situations → techniques](sand-bass.md#situations--techniques).
A tank loaded with good-sized live anchovy on a twilight or PM half-day boat is
itself the signal (`P2OzCf2CwXI`); why the bait is there is in
[barred sand bass — presence & forage](sand-bass.md#presence--forage).

**It is already producing when the plastics come out.** Once a spawning summer
school is fired up, the slider and the dropper loop are both getting bit;
plastics are the choice made on top of that, for a more entertaining way to
catch fish that are already eating, rather than a switch made because the bait
rig stopped working (`P2OzCf2CwXI`).

**It is not the call when they are locked to the bottom.** Winter fish sitting
on deep structure want the bait delivered to their level and moved, which is the
[dropper loop](../techniques/dropper-loop.md) row in the router — see
[barred sand bass — feeding triggers](sand-bass.md#feeding-triggers).

## How barred sand bass changes the execution

**The cast is a slow sink through the entire column, not a delivery to a
depth.** Cast the bait out and let the anchovy sink slowly through the column;
sand bass are structure-oriented but not locked to the structure the way a
lingcod or cabezon is, so they hover slightly above or within the reef, wreck or
kelp, and a rig that covers the entire column is what puts the bait in front of
them wherever in that band they are sitting (`P2OzCf2CwXI`). The sonar picture
behind this — no mark while they sit on the bottom, and fish rising a long way
to eat — is in
[barred sand bass — finding them](sand-bass.md#finding-them-sign--sonar).

**The lead is sized off wind and current, not off the bottom.** **1/2 oz** is
the default; size up when current or wind is on the drift, and size down on a
really nice glassy day (`P2OzCf2CwXI`). The variable being managed is how fast
the anchovy falls through the column, which is why the rule is stated against
surface conditions rather than against depth.

**The whole outfit is downsized to the bait.** Anchovies are small baits, and
the gear has to be downsized to match them or the bait cannot be fished
effectively; the specific requirement is a rod with **whippiness in the tip**
(`P2OzCf2CwXI`). This is the mechanism behind the rod rating below — the tip is
chosen for what it does to a small bait, not for what it does to a 6 lb fish.

**Leverage comes from the reel handle, because the fishing is vertical.** Sand
bass pull harder than rockfish and harder than calico, and much of this program
is fished straight up and down over structure, so an oversized handle on a
narrow star-drag reel is what gets a hooked fish up out of the rocks and into
open water (`P2OzCf2CwXI`). The rest of the fight and release handling is in
[barred sand bass — landing & handling](sand-bass.md#landing--handling).

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the source hedges the calico half of
this comparison — "They pull harder than the rockfish. In my opinion, they
pull harder than calico bass" — and the note states both comparisons as flat
fact, dropping the "in my opinion" the speaker attaches to the calico claim.

**Leader class is set by the fish's size, and the fish is small.** A big barred
sand bass is about **6 lb**, so 20 lb fluorocarbon is more than enough to land
one and still light enough to keep getting bit when the bite goes scratchy
(`P2OzCf2CwXI`).

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: bite handling on this rig for sand bass — no
source states a drop-back count, or when to come tight, for the slider. The
watch-the-free-spool-then-go-into-gear instruction the corpus does carry is
stated for plastics on the sink, not for the live-bait slider.

## Rigs & gear

- **Terminal:** a **1/2 oz egg sinker** and a **size 2 J hook** — sized up in
  current or wind, down on a glassy day (`P2OzCf2CwXI`). Build order (sinker →
  bead → swivel → leader → hook) is in
  [sliding sinker](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md#building-the-rig).
- **Leader: about 10 ft of 20 lb fluorocarbon** (`P2OzCf2CwXI`).
- **Main line: 30 lb braid** (`P2OzCf2CwXI`).
- **Rod: 8–8.5 ft rated 12–30 lb, with a deliberately whippy tip** to match a
  small anchovy (`P2OzCf2CwXI`).
- **Reel: a small, narrow star drag with an oversized handle** for vertical
  leverage (`P2OzCf2CwXI`).
- **Class term:** this is the
  [finesse fly-line class](../tackle/gear-classes.md), and the outfit is not
  sand-bass-specific — the same build is the general small-live-bait inshore
  finesse rig (`P2OzCf2CwXI`).

## Differs from the general method

Against [sliding sinker](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md), which owns the
free-sliding Carolina format, the weight ladders for other species, and how to
feed a bite:

- **It is not a bottom rig.** The general note's default is the sinker settling
  to the bottom and held in light contact, with the leader length setting how
  far the bait rides behind and above the lead. Here the presentation is the
  fall itself — one slow sink through the whole column — because the fish are
  above or within the structure rather than on it (`P2OzCf2CwXI`).
- **The sinker-sizing rule inverts.** The general rule is the least lead that
  will still tick bottom, i.e. sized to depth and drift; this build sizes off
  wind and surface conditions to control the *rate of fall* (`P2OzCf2CwXI`).
- **The weight sits far below most builds on the general note.** 1/2 oz against
  2–4 oz on the San Diego yellowtail build and 8–24 oz on the Baja grouper
  builds; it belongs to the note's lightest tier, alongside the 1/4–1/2 oz
  sheephead cap and the 1/8–3/4 oz white seabass ladder, and it is light because
  the bait is an anchovy (`P2OzCf2CwXI`).
- **The leader is long, not short.** ~10 ft, against the ~6 ft Baja grouper and
  snook leaders and the 4–5 ft build the general note itself calls "way too long
  for most people" (`P2OzCf2CwXI`).
- **The hook is sized to an anchovy.** A **size 2 J**, against the general
  note's octopus-offset default and the 2/0–4/0 J on the yellowtail build
  (`P2OzCf2CwXI`).
- **Column coverage is achieved differently than on the white seabass build.**
  The general note works the column by cycling — free-spool, engage, crank the
  sinker back up near the bait, free-spool again. This program covers it in a
  single uninterrupted slow fall (`P2OzCf2CwXI`).
- **The gear class is NOT a delta on its own.** The finesse fly-line outfit is
  already the general note's stated sand-bass/finesse tier; what earns this page
  is the parameter set — sinker weight rule, hook size, leader length and class,
  rod action and its mechanism, and the fall-through-the-column presentation.

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the comparison figures cited to
`P2OzCf2CwXI` throughout this section — 2–4 oz yellowtail, 8–24 oz Baja
grouper, 1/4–1/2 oz sheephead, 1/8–3/4 oz white seabass weight ladders; the
~6 ft grouper/snook and 4–5 ft general leaders; the octopus-offset default;
the 2/0–4/0 yellowtail hook — are not in this source; `P2OzCf2CwXI` is the
sand bass studio video and never mentions yellowtail, grouper, sheephead, or
white seabass. Those figures match [sliding sinker](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md),
which attributes them to other video ids; only this page's own sand-bass
numbers (1/2 oz, ~10 ft/20 lb leader, size 2 hook) are supported by the cited
source.
