# Spotted Bay Bass — structure pitching

**One species, one technique.** Pitching a **3–4 in** swimbait or dart-spin on a
**3/8–1/2 oz** jighead into docks, pilings and mooring blocks is the harbor
structure program, and every number in it is set by one constraint: the fish
must be turned away from the cover the instant it is hooked, because the cover
is sometimes only **2–3 ft** away (`46ha7J1Fc6A`). Reach for it when the fish
are pinned to hard cover rather than roaming grass or open channel edges. The
general swimbait rigging, retrieve and hookset are in
[swimbaits](../techniques/swimbaits.md); this page is what changes because the
fish is a spotted bay bass sitting inside a dock field.

## When this is the call

**Fish tight to docks, pilings and mooring blocks.** This is the row the router
ranks for hard cover with almost no room to work a hooked fish — see
[spotted bay bass — situations → techniques](spotted-bay-bass.md#situations--techniques).
Anything solid in a harbor counts: dock pilings, the concrete or engine blocks
anchoring mooring buoys, and the shadow side of a boat's hull
(`46ha7J1Fc6A`); why that structure holds fish is in
[spotted bay bass — presence & forage](spotted-bay-bass.md#presence--forage).

**Clear water pushes the fish onto this cover.** The router carries a
clear-water switch that abandons the grass plan for the docks; that row and its
reasoning stay in
[spotted bay bass — situations → techniques](spotted-bay-bass.md#situations--techniques).

**It is not the call on open grass flats or channel edges.** Fish roaming the
eelgrass or hunting in the open at slack want water covered rather than cover
picked apart, which is the
[inshore crankbait](../techniques/inshore-crankbaits.md) row in the router.

## How spotted bay bass changes the execution

**The gear ratio is bought for head-turning speed, not cranking power.** A
**6.6:1** reel is the stated choice because it gives enough speed to turn the
fish's head around and get it out of the structure, and to pick up slack fast
enough to stay tight — docks and pilings are sometimes only **2–3 ft** apart, so
the fish has to be coming your way immediately (`46ha7J1Fc6A`). The grade
justifies the concern: these are not big fish, but one will
[turn a 14 ft skiff on a run](spotted-bay-bass.md#landing--handling)
(`46ha7J1Fc6A`).

**Wind, and keep the rod loaded — "whine on that fish."** Once the hook is set,
grind the fish out of the structure on a continuously loaded rod. Pumping the
rod and dropping the tip is how you lose them: that momentary slack is when the
fish gets back into the piling or the grass (`46ha7J1Fc6A`).

**The leader is stepped up for a fish that is not the target.** Big **halibut**
hang in the same dock and mooring zones as the spotties, and the leader class
below is sized for the halibut that eats the same bait rather than for a
2.8–3.5 lb bass (`46ha7J1Fc6A`) — see
[California halibut](california-halibut.md).

**The rod is chosen for feel first and backbone second.** The blank is sensitive
enough to feel a 3 in swimbait's tail kicking on the retrieve, which is how the
bait is tracked through a dock field with no bottom contact to read, while still
carrying enough backbone to pull a hooked fish clear (`46ha7J1Fc6A`).

## Rigs & gear

- **Rod: an all-graphite, super light and sensitive casting rod rated
  12–20 lb** — 7 ft 6 in is the stated size, and the same blank is fished for
  calico and off break walls (`46ha7J1Fc6A`).
- **Reel: a low-profile 200-size**, stepped to a **300-size** in heavier piling
  work, at a **6.6:1** ratio (`46ha7J1Fc6A`).
- **Main line: 20–30 lb braid** — very strong and with no stretch, so nothing is
  given up on the pull, but poor in abrasion resistance, which is why a leader
  is always run (`46ha7J1Fc6A`).
- **Leader: fluorocarbon stepped up to about 15 lb** in the shallower structure
  water, for the halibut sharing the zone (`46ha7J1Fc6A`).
- **Baits: 3 in and 4 in swimbaits, and dart-spins, on a 3/8–1/2 oz jighead**
  (`46ha7J1Fc6A`); models are in
  [bay bass plastics](../lures/bay-bass-plastics.md).
- **Class term:** the
  [structure-casting class, 12–20 lb](../tackle/gear-classes.md).

## Differs from the general method

Against [swimbaits](../techniques/swimbaits.md), which owns the weedless kelp
rigging, the fast-wind retrieve and the set-on-the-pause hookset:

- **The whole build sits below the general note's lightest tier.** 3–4 in baits
  on a 3/8–1/2 oz jighead, against a 7 in weedless slug on a 3/8–3/4 oz
  keel-weighted hook and the 3/4–1½ oz leadheads with 4–8 in plastics on the
  long-range build (`46ha7J1Fc6A`).
- **Line drops by roughly a factor of three.** 20–30 lb braid to a ~15 lb
  fluorocarbon leader, against the general note's 50–65 lb braid to 40–60 lb
  fluoro or mono for kelp and rock (`46ha7J1Fc6A`).
- **The leader is stepped up for a different reason than in kelp.** The general
  note frames heavy leader as a structure decision — chafe and braid-up against
  kelp and rock, not fish size. Here the step-up is a bycatch decision: a big
  halibut on the same dock, on a bait a spotty would eat on lighter line
  (`46ha7J1Fc6A`).
- **The reel ratio is bought for a spatial constraint, not for slack pickup
  behaviour.** The general note's ≥6.2:1 floor is argued from calico swimming
  *at* the boat after eating; the 6.6:1 here is argued from the **2–3 ft**
  between pilings — the head has to come around before the fish covers that
  distance (`46ha7J1Fc6A`). The numbers agree; the mechanisms are independent.
- **The rod requirement inverts.** The general note wants a soft glass tip that
  bends and buys the split second before a set-on-the-pause hookset; this build
  wants sensitivity plus backbone, because the set is immediate and the fight
  starts inside the structure (`46ha7J1Fc6A`).
- **Grind-don't-pump is the same rule with the margin collapsed.** The general
  note states it for a calico diving back into kelp; in a dock field the fish
  needs only 2–3 ft of slack to be back in the cover, so it is not a
  fish-handling preference but the whole program (`46ha7J1Fc6A`).
