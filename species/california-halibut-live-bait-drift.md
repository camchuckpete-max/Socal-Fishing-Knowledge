# California Halibut — live-bait drift

Drifting a live bait on the bottom is the halibut program that scales from
**8–10 ft** of bay water to the Channel Islands on the same rig: a trap rig on
a short fluorocarbon leader, hung off a three-way with a breakaway sinker so
the weight changes with the depth and the current (`a5u8BaYzw8c`). Everything
that makes it work for this fish is small and specific — the leader length, the
shape of the lead, the hook pairing, and a bite you feel as almost nothing. The
general rig format and how a free-sliding weight feeds a bite live in
[sliding sinker](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md); the two-hook build lives in
[trap rig](../rigging/trap-rig.md); this page is what changes because the fish
is a halibut.

## When this is the call

**A located structure edge with current on it, and fin bait or squid
available.** The rig is the default presentation on the two situations the
router ranks it first for — a structure edge in current at the islands, and a
bay or harbor channel edge in the **8–10 ft** water Mission Bay and San Diego
Bay fish (`a5u8BaYzw8c`) — with the ranking against
drop-shot, hardbait and swimbait programs in
[California halibut](california-halibut.md#situations--techniques).

**It is a drift program, so the drift has to be fishable.** The spot is worked
in short passes *along* the structure rather than across it, which is what
separates it from a long random sand drift (`OpcKQPA3vAI`); the drift-line
geometry and the give-it-20–30-minutes rule are in
[California halibut — finding them](california-halibut.md#finding-them-sign--sonar).

**Squid on the island beds is the same drift with a different hook.** When
squid is what the fish are on, the trap comes apart and a single J carries the
bait — see [Rigs & gear](#rigs--gear).

## How California halibut changes the execution

**The rig is fished straight up and down, not trailed.** Maintaining bottom
contact with the rig vertical under the rod tip is called the basis of the
whole build, because it is the only way the bite registers (`a5u8BaYzw8c`).
That is the opposite of the general slider's wafting bait riding back behind
the lead.

**A short fluorocarbon leader — 2½ to 3 ft at most, of 25–30 lb** (15 lb in
the bay) — is called very important (`a5u8BaYzw8c`). The stated evidence is
outcome, not mechanism: across many trips "the only guy that to not catch a
fish was the one guy using straight mono," with no explanation offered beyond
the fluorocarbon being stiffer and doing "something" to the bait's
presentation (`a5u8BaYzw8c`).

**Sinker shape is a halibut-specific choice: ball or cannonball over torpedo.**
Torpedo sinkers are the habit; ball weights are the borrowed commercial
practice, because they bounce on the bottom rather than burying in the mud,
disrupt the bottom, and "kick that halibut up off the mud" (`a5u8BaYzw8c`).
The source flags the kick-them-up half as possibly just his own theory and
fishes them anyway.

**Sinker weight is set by depth and current, not by a default.** **1–1½ oz** in
the bay; **4–6 oz** as the offshore starting point; up to **10 oz** when the
current is ripping and that is what it takes to stay on the bottom
(`a5u8BaYzw8c`). The breakaway clip exists so the swap takes seconds.

**The bait tells you before the fish does.** A sardine going "real nervous" on
the rig is usually followed by the bite, so any variance in the bait's action
is the cue to pay attention (`a5u8BaYzw8c`).

**The bite itself is a pressure change, not a tap.** The take often feels
exactly like dragging into a patch of kelp — about **60%** of the time it *is*
kelp, but a good **25–30%** of the time it is a halibut that has picked the
bait up and is moving along with it, with no tap felt at all (`a5u8BaYzw8c`).

**Lift and wind; never swing.** Respond to any change in pressure by lifting
the rod and simply continuing to wind until it comes tight — "you never want to
smack into it like a spotty or a largemouth" (`a5u8BaYzw8c`). The fight after
that (one long bolting run, high rod tip, light drag, head kept underwater) is
species-general and lives in
[California halibut — landing & handling](california-halibut.md#landing--handling).

## Rigs & gear

- **Terminal order:** three-way swivel (a swivel-into-a-swivel style is
  preferred as tangle-resistant) → breakaway clip → sinker on one leg, and
  **2½–3 ft of 25–30 lb fluorocarbon** to the hooks on the other
  (`a5u8BaYzw8c`).
- **Fin bait — the trap rig:** an inexpensive bait hook up front (a Mustad
  94150 in this build) plus a **3–4X-strong treble** stinger behind it, so a
  fish that only grabs the back half of a mackerel or a big sardine is still
  landed. Sizes follow the bait: **size 2 front / size 6 rear** on the offshore
  build, **size 2 front / size 8 rear** in the bay (`a5u8BaYzw8c`). Bait
  placement and the slack-to-the-stinger judgment are in
  [trap rig](../rigging/trap-rig.md).
- **Squid — no stinger:** a single large **4/0–5/0 J** hook, set for solid
  purchase in the top of the squid's nose; the treble comes off because squid
  will not hold it (`a5u8BaYzw8c`).
- **Sinkers:** ball/cannonball preferred, **1–1½ oz** bay, **4–6 oz** offshore
  start, to **10 oz** in ripping current (`a5u8BaYzw8c`).
- **Boat rod — [30 lb bait class](../tackle/gear-classes.md)** with **30 lb
  mono** for stretch on the bolting run, framed as the outfit for an overnight
  or two-day trip to the Channel Islands (`a5u8BaYzw8c`).
- **Bay rod — a 10–25 lb inshore rod on a 200-size reel**, the same outfit
  fished for spotted bay bass, with **15 lb fluorocarbon** and the identical
  trap rig one hook size smaller (`a5u8BaYzw8c`). Class terms:
  [gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md).
- Line class here is about bait presentation and bottom contact rather than
  strength — halibut do not pull hard (`a5u8BaYzw8c`).

## Differs from the general method

Against [sliding sinker](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md), which owns the
free-sliding Carolina format and how to feed a bite, and
[trap rig](../rigging/trap-rig.md), which owns the two-hook build:

- **The weight is not free-sliding.** It hangs off a three-way on a breakaway
  clip, so the swap is a clip change and the bait rides on its own leg rather
  than behind a sinker running on the main line (`a5u8BaYzw8c`).
- **The rod attitude is vertical, not trailed.** The general note's leader
  length sets how far the bait rides *behind and above* the lead; here the
  whole point is the rig hanging straight up and down under the boat
  (`a5u8BaYzw8c`).
- **The bite is not fed.** The general note's advantage is the free take — give
  slack, let the fish turn. On this build the instruction is to lift and keep
  winding on the first pressure change (`a5u8BaYzw8c`). The two are not
  reconciled here: they are different rig formats, and the free-take logic
  depends on a weight the fish can pull line through.
- **Sinker shape is argued the other way.** The Sea of Cortez grouper build on
  the general note prefers sliding egg sinkers and treats a torpedo on a short
  breakaway as the fallback; the halibut build prefers ball/cannonball for the
  mud-bounce (`a5u8BaYzw8c`). Different bottoms, different fish, kept side by
  side.
- **It supplies the hook sizes the rig note records as unknown.** Size 2/6
  offshore and 2/8 in the bay, plus the 4/0–5/0 single for squid, against
  [trap rig](../rigging/trap-rig.md)'s halibut build, which names the hook
  types but no sizes (`a5u8BaYzw8c`).
- **Leader class and length are stated here and nowhere else for this fish** —
  2½–3 ft of 25–30 lb fluorocarbon, 15 lb inside a bay (`a5u8BaYzw8c`).
