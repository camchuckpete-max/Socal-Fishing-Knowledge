# Yellowfin Tuna

SoCal's summer-into-fall bread-and-butter tuna: **8–10 lb up to 40–50 lb, with
15–25 lb the average grade** (`8M4QhL-Qb7E`). Two things set the programme
apart from the [bluefin](bluefin-tuna.md) fishery it overlaps — yellowfin are
markedly more cooperative on lures, and they want the **warmest × cleanest**
water, where the bluefin alongside them sit in the cooler zone (`CMQkHQMxbXM`,
`OYOda6T3f-8`). A surface
yellowfin bite is also a routing signal: when yellowfin are up fighting lures,
the bluefin nearby become more cooperative too (`CMQkHQMxbXM`). Baja grade is a
different fish entirely — 40–70 lb at Guadalupe (`Sx40JvCXFuA`), 70–100 lb at
Alijos Rocks (`_r08B4bItAY`, `6kpWn2sXokI`), and 140–350 lb on the lower banks
in November (`ASitOLYzFEA`).

## Where & when

**Season — keyed to the [water regime](../conditions/water-regimes.md).**
Yellowfin arrive with the late warm-stable regime, typically **August**, and
stay through **fall fragmentation, September–October**
([August](../seasonal/august.md), [September](../seasonal/september.md),
[October](../seasonal/october.md)). SST climbs into August–September for two
stated reasons: ordinary seasonal warming, and hurricanes tracking up from
farther south in Baja pushing a pulse of warm water north (`fgTmUq78ofQ`).

**Volume sits near the border and slides north with the warm band.** In
mid-September the bulk is still down toward San Diego and the border while
sportboats farther north only start picking them up (`5to3Q5P7w90`). Once
fragmentation sets in, typically October, the concentrations track the warmest
water on the coast — the West End of [San Clemente
Island](../locations/san-clemente-island-front-side.md) down through Mackerel
Bank to the 181/182 Ridge, with the bigger 50 lb fish off the front side of
Clemente (`OYOda6T3f-8`).

**Water — warmest × cleanest.** Find the warmest water you can, then cross it
against your chlorophyll chart for the cleanest water; that SST-max ×
chlorophyll-min combination is where they are most likely (`OYOda6T3f-8`). See
[water temperature](../conditions/water-temperature.md),
[water color](../conditions/water-color.md). A SoCal fall bite marked by white
terns sat over **72 °F** water (`SczdZIq3UmE`).

**Structure.** They show under [kelp
paddies](../conditions/kelp-paddies.md) mixed in with other schools
(`8M4QhL-Qb7E`), in open-water schools over the offshore banks
([zone lexicon](../locations/zone-lexicon.md)), and suspended beneath
travelling dolphin pods (`fK2AT460xW4`).

**Baja — a different grade ladder, north to south.**
[Guadalupe Island](../locations/guadalupe.md) runs **40–70 lb** in the 2009
records and **50–150 lb** by 2022, reached on five-, six- and seven-day trips
at 215 mi to the north end and 235 mi to the south end from San Diego
(`Sx40JvCXFuA`, `_r08B4bItAY`, `ETw_3AFxEcM`).
[Alijos Rocks](../locations/alijos-rocks.md) and "the Ridge" (upper high spot:
Uncle Sam Bank) are the late-September/early-October seven-day alternative,
with **100 lb** fish at the Rocks and a **70–80 lb** grade recorded there
(`_r08B4bItAY`, `6kpWn2sXokI`) — see [wahoo](wahoo.md#where--when) for the
shared run distances. The Baja **"lower banks" in late November** are the
giant-tuna ground: **140–350 lb** on a school a boat anchored on for four days
(`ASitOLYzFEA`, `Fq4aRI3YrKE`) — ⚠ Fact-check (single-source): the species is
never named on camera in the dock-day footage and is read from the crew's "cow"
language.
At the [East Cape](../locations/east-cape.md) the bite is called consistent year-round
along the coast, with fish moving between locations through the year and grade
running **37–46 lb** in 2016 up to **50–72 lb** in 2019–2020 (`_c6UI3lGBVg`,
`mG8ZZLFGlT8`, `GptrotE0x5M`). Off
[Ensenada](../locations/ensenada.md) they show **30–40 mi out**
(`0dIwWiOc1NY`), and off [Cedros](../locations/cedros-island.md) **20–30 mi
offshore** on an October troll (`YUdbrIm9vrE`).

**Presence is not catchability.** Small spots of yellowfin boiling at the 14 in
August refused surface lures before wind chop shut down spotting altogether
(`CMQkHQMxbXM`); the Ensenada offshore fish 30–40 mi out were a guide's morning
report that the crew declined to run at (`0dIwWiOc1NY`). Zone-by-zone trip
reality lives in the [zone guides](#zone-guides) below and the
[locations](../locations/README.md) gazetteer.

## Presence & forage

**They hold in the warmest, cleanest water available** — the SST-max ×
chlorophyll-min cross above is a presence rule, not a bite rule (`OYOda6T3f-8`).

**Dolphin pods are a forage relationship, not a companionship.** A travelling
pod drives and scatters bait balls, and the yellowfin school is hunting those
scattered balls: fish suspend **underneath** a pod that is merely travelling
and mostly do not surface-swim with it, which is why the school can look empty
from above (`fK2AT460xW4`); once the pod breaks a baitball up, the yellowfin
show **on the flanks and out in front** of it instead, with terns working
50 yd ahead (`ouBrIdO7d4k`). Common dolphin and spotted spinner dolphin are the
two SoCal pod types that hold them (`fK2AT460xW4`). What that read does to the
troll — which water the lure works, and the approach that gets it there — is in
[yellowfin — trolling](yellowfin-tuna-trolling.md#how-yellowfin-changes-the-execution).

**Forage: small anchovies, and flying fish where they occur.** Yellowfin feed
on the same "little tiny anchovies" bluefin bust on, which is what the small
long-cast lure classes are matched to (`VgpgJ8IAyJo`). At the East Cape a ~50 lb
fish cleared the water by about 5 ft chasing a flying fish (`BQ2U1PqxWi8`). A
large bait ball sitting directly under the boat is enough to hold a giant-tuna
school in place for days (`Fq4aRI3YrKE`).

## Feeding triggers

- **Chum is the switch, and it works on a marked school in about two
  minutes.** Chumming a school marked at 40 ft drew it up into hook range
  inside roughly two minutes of steady bait, with the side scan filling as it
  came (`lxFNVdDhMy4`). A chum line can pull the bite all the way to the hull,
  with free-swimming fish charging alongside (`6zYRI1ZQU3c`). On locating a
  school, throw a few freebies before you ever present a hook bait
  (`8M4QhL-Qb7E`).
- **Small chum bait triggers harder than big.** The stated mechanism is prey
  density: a scoop of small bait raises the density of prey in the water and
  fires a reaction, so the working split is big bait on the hook, small bait
  over the side (`qv0QbLgp72o`).
- **The full moon costs you the morning.** Yellowfin, bluefin and albacore have
  been seen moving **20 mi** after a full-moon night, on the stated mechanism
  that a bright moon gives a school the confidence to travel and feed in the
  dark; the day after, they bite poorly first thing and better late
  (`fri_BWI-VA0`). See [moon](../conditions/moon.md).
- ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: any tide, slack, gray-light or
  current-speed trigger for yellowfin specifically, and no mechanism for why
  warm clean water switches them on beyond the fact that they hold in it. See
  [tide and slack](../conditions/tide-and-slack.md),
  [current structure](../conditions/current-structure.md).

## Finding them (sign & sonar)

- **Sonar signature: school marks up in the column, commonly ~40–50 ft down,
  and they rise to chum** (`lxFNVdDhMy4`). Grade a paddy's mark fast and move if
  it is dry. Set the sounder range **manually** rather than on auto for these
  shallow marks in deep open water (`SgF5hRlEGqU`) — method:
  [electronics & sounder](../planning/electronics-and-sounder.md).
- **Visual sign: working birds (terns best), boils, and paddies.** Working
  birds over surface boils marked a ~30 lb school offshore of San Clemente
  Island (`3T4c3Zez_DM`); white terns marked a bite over 72 °F water near the
  209 (`SczdZIq3UmE`). See [bird reading](../conditions/bird-reading.md),
  [search & glassing](../planning/search-and-glassing.md).
- **Choosing between dolphin pods: take the one carrying its own bird.** With
  several pods around, even a single bird sitting on a pod is the tell that that
  group is holding fish, over unmarked pods nearby (`SgF5hRlEGqU`). A feeding
  pod and a travelling pod present differently — see
  [Presence & forage](#presence--forage) for where the fish sit in each case.
- ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: how the 40–50 ft mark shifts with sea
  state, time of day or grade, and any sonar signature for the Baja giant-tuna
  schools.

## Gear summary (class terms)

- **Bait:** **30 lb flyline class**, circle hooks **2/0 down to size 1** with
  leader **30 lb down to 15 lb** when they get picky — thin wire lets the bait
  swim free and hides better (`8M4QhL-Qb7E`).
  [Gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md); the ladder itself, the bait-net
  trick and the slow-troll variant are in
  [yellowfin — flyline](yellowfin-tuna-flyline.md).
- **Surface lures:** **jig-stick class**;
  [poppers & stickbaits](../lures/tuna-poppers-and-stickbaits.md) and small lead
  baits in the 120–150 mm range; a light popper for 10–20 lb fish.
- **Troll:** light **troll class**;
  [tuna feathers & skirts](../lures/tuna-feathers-and-skirts.md) and
  [cedar plugs](../lures/cedar-plug.md) at ~6–6.5 kt. The pod lure build, the
  line classes and the second speed band are in
  [yellowfin — trolling](yellowfin-tuna-trolling.md#rigs--gear).
- **Baja big grade:** a **50–60 lb two-speed** outfit fly-lining a sardine for
  100 lb-class Alijos Rocks fish (`uWGGeDOprsE`), and a **50–60 lb top shot over
  80 lb braid** on a 30–60 to 40–100 rated rod for Guadalupe (`ETw_3AFxEcM`) —
  full ladders in [Searcher big/giant tuna rig
  ladder](../tackle/searcher-big-tuna-rig-ladder.md) and [Searcher five-outfit
  quiver](../tackle/searcher-alijos-rocks-ridge-7-day-quiver.md). The jig stick
  travels too: irons are cast for tuna at Guadalupe and "a lot of guys leave
  their jig sticks at home" (`rziFyx7SRGI`). See
  [surface iron](../techniques/surface-iron.md).

## Regulations

- **Mexico / Baja California Sur — circle hooks are mandatory for
  sportfishing.** Stated on camera at the East Cape, where a large share of the
  corpus's yellowfin records come from (`GptrotE0x5M`). As of 2019-02;
  **verify current rules before fishing.** See
  [flyline](../techniques/flyline.md).
- **Mexico / federal fishing permit — required to fish Baja and Sea of Cortez
  waters.** A San Diego operator's first Guadalupe Island trip in 2009 was run
  only after obtaining Mexican fishing permits (`Sx40JvCXFuA`). As of 2009;
  every Baja ground in this note sits under that requirement. **Verify the
  current permit and entry paperwork with CONAPESCA before fishing** — see
  [Mexican waters](../locations/mexican-waters.md).

## Doctrine & conflicts

Section order below is not a ranking.

**The water read is settled: warmest × cleanest.** Yellowfin want the warmest
water available crossed against the cleanest water on the chlorophyll chart
(`OYOda6T3f-8`), and in warm-water years chlorophyll and water colour carry at
least as much weight as SST, with "dirty green" water not fished for gamefish
at all (cameron). Nothing in the corpus disputes this.

**Yellowfin activity is a bluefin signal, not the reverse.** Yellowfin are more
cooperative on lures than bluefin, and when they are up fighting lures on the
surface the bluefin nearby become more cooperative as well (`CMQkHQMxbXM`).
One charter sequence ran the other way — an 80–90 lb bluefin morning switching
into a wide-open yellowfin bite (`tU4jhAkdzNw`) — which establishes neither
fish's activity as causing the other's and does not overturn the doctrine.

**Picky-bite leader and hook: three figures, one situation.** How far down you
go is set by how far the bite forces you, not by which source you follow — start
at 30 lb with a 2/0 circle, step down only until they commit, then work back up
to 25 and 30 (`8M4QhL-Qb7E`). Three sources give three different stepping-off
points for that same scaled-down flyline bite; all three, with the decision
frame, are on [yellowfin — flyline](yellowfin-tuna-flyline.md#rigs--gear)
(`8M4QhL-Qb7E`, `AIHvJj-paoo`, `NN5MWeRA28o`), which holds the whole live-bait
programme.

**Guadalupe line class runs 20 lb to 60 lb; grade and line-shyness pick the
build.** One build runs a
**50–60 lb top shot over 80 lb braid** on a 30–60 to 40–100 rated rod for
50–150 lb fish (`ETw_3AFxEcM`); a second calls the same island a **light-line**
fishery on **size 2 and 4 hooks**, with a 45 lb fish "frightfully tough" on that
gear and no line number given (`EfaxxszOYFI`); a third puts **20 lb fluorocarbon
with size 1–2 hooks** on "very, very line shy" 30–40 lb fish (`WxlDxFjB8oQ`).
**Decision frame:** the split tracks grade and line-shyness, not disagreement —
the heavy build is for the 50–150 lb class, the 20 lb build for a shy 30–40 lb
class, and the middle account names no line class at all, so it cannot be
placed. Take the grade the boat is on and the water clarity as the input.
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-internal): three builds for one ground from one
channel, spanning 20 lb to 60 lb with no stated reason for the spread.

**Private-boat and sportboat programmes diverge on bait budget.** A private boat
carries a two-scoop maximum against the sportboat's effectively bottomless
chum, which is what pushes the private-boat game toward the scale-down and the
bait-net trick rather than sustained chumming (`8M4QhL-Qb7E`).
