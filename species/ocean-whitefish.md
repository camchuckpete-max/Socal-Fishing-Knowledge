# Ocean Whitefish

The ocean whitefish (*Caulolatilus princeps*) is a tilefish, not a rockfish,
and it fishes as a school over hard bottom and sand-edge transitions: at the
Channel Islands they "school up like crazy up here — you'll catch a hundred of
them," and a good one is a legit 30-lb-class dropper-loop fish
(`6-8KfjEg0x8`). It eats the same dropped squid as the reds on the same
island and bank high spots, but holds higher off the rock and along the sand
seam, so the decision it forces is where in the column you put the bait rather
than what you tie on. It is a prime Channel Islands variety target and a
by-catch everywhere the bottom program runs, from an offshore-bank drift in
150 ft (`fDSd9kqwYW0`) to a float tube inside [La Jolla](../locations/la-jolla.md)
(`ouBrIdO7d4k`).

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the source says "this is like a legit
30-lb dropper loop rig" — the RIG's line class, not the fish's weight. The
note's own Gear summary reads it correctly as a 30 lb bait-class outfit, and no
weight is given for any fish on camera; the only size language is "they get
freaking big up here. This is kind of an average size one" (`6-8KfjEg0x8`).

## Where & when

**Where.** Ocean whitefish come off hard bottom and hard-to-sand seams across
three water types the corpus records:

- **Channel Islands hard bottom and sand-edge transitions** — Santa Cruz,
  Santa Rosa, [Santa Barbara Island](../locations/santa-barbara-island.md) and
  the outer island reefs, on the same shallow **100–250 ft** island high spots
  that produce the [rockfish and lingcod](rockfish-lingcod.md) variety bag.
  ⚠ Fact-check (single-source): one Channel Islands variety day is the corpus's
  only SoCal-specific account of whitefish as a target rather than a by-catch,
  and it carries the "catch a hundred" and 30-lb-class claims
  (`6-8KfjEg0x8`).
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): that day names no individual island —
  only "the Channel Islands" and "just outside of Ventura" — and gives no
  whitefish depth: the whitefish stop is called only "the shallows," and the one
  depth figure in the video, **175 ft**, belongs to the separate rockfish rock
  the boat ran to afterwards (`6-8KfjEg0x8`).
- **Offshore banks.** [Cortez and Tanner](../locations/tanner-bank.md) produce
  a large grade — "the white fish are absolutely Jurassic" (`Y1xeieQI3B4`) —
  and a ~**150 ft** ridge drift there takes them alongside reds and
  [sheephead](sheephead.md) on squid (`fDSd9kqwYW0`). This is an offshore
  bank, not an island, and the same hard-bottom rule carries.
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): "Tanner" appears nowhere in the
  "Jurassic" source, which names only Cortez Bank (`Y1xeieQI3B4`).
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the ~150 ft ridge drift produced reds;
  the captain's own summary puts the whitefish and sheephead on a separate,
  shallower stop — "we fished out there at the edge of the bank and we ended up
  catching our limit on the Reds … we moved into the shallows we picked up some
  sheep head and some white fish" (`fDSd9kqwYW0`).
- **Nearshore coast and inshore island rock.** At [La Jolla](../locations/la-jolla.md)
  a float-tube angler's catch is "mostly white fish" (`ouBrIdO7d4k`), and small
  whitefish are part of the resident fauna over inshore bass structure
  (`kwMIgkCtFUE`). On the front side of Catalina an anchored calico stop
  produces whitefish to about **10 lb** off the same rock (`HGyL7pXy3Ts`) —
  see [Catalina Island — Front Side](../locations/catalina-island-front-side.md).
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the 10 lb figure is one sarcastic aside
  about a single fish — "after he stoled your 10 pound white fish" — not a
  stated grade for the spot, and "front side" is never said: the captions render
  the place as "just inside of what they call white scope" (`HGyL7pXy3Ts`).
- **Baja Pacific.** A high spot roughly **7–8 mi** off [Ensenada](../locations/ensenada.md)
  that rises to about **25 ft from roughly 200 ft** gave a whitefish as the
  first fish of the day (`CKq0Z6ExVs4`).

See [island structure](../locations/island-structure.md) and the
[zone lexicon](../locations/zone-lexicon.md).

**When.** No month prior applies — whitefish are an all-season bottomfish
wherever the hard bottom is fishable, and the
[seasonal calendar](../seasonal/README.md) matters only for the island or bank
trip that gets you there. **Presence is not access:** in California the
whitefish bite opens with the groundfish season, and charters name
"rockfish, lingcod, whitefish and sheephead" as the opener's package
(`46ha7J1Fc6A`) — see [Regulations](#regulations). In late October at
Catalina they were biting alongside calicos and bonito in what was called
steady wintertime fishing (`OYOda6T3f-8`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): in that 2022-10-20 report whitefish
appear only inside a second-hand fly-guide sentence, and "good steady wintertime
fishing" summarises the whole Catalina picture — bonito, bass and yellowtail —
not a whitefish bite (`OYOda6T3f-8`).

**Conditions.** At the islands current is as important as the bait you are
fishing, and conditions make or break the day (`6-8KfjEg0x8`); a slack spot
meters fish that will not eat. See
[current structure](../conditions/current-structure.md).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the trip that states the current
doctrine had little current at the whitefish stop and caught whitefish there
anyway, so the "won't eat" half is not what that day showed
(`6-8KfjEg0x8`).

## Presence & forage

- **They school, and a school is worth staying on.** Once one comes over the
  rail the rest of the drop is usually more of the same — the reason the
  program is keep dropping rather than move (`6-8KfjEg0x8`).
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the schooling quote is verbatim, but
  the drop-continuation rule and the keep-dropping-rather-than-move program are
  not in the source — its only "we didn't move very much" line is the day's
  summary of the separate rockfish rock (`6-8KfjEg0x8`).
- **They share hard bottom with the whole variety bag** — reds, blue bass,
  bocaccio, lingcod and sheephead over the same island and bank rock
  (`6-8KfjEg0x8`, `fDSd9kqwYW0`, `CKq0Z6ExVs4`) — but hold **higher off the
  bottom and along the sand edge** rather than pinned to the pinnacle, which
  is what separates the two programs on one spot.
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): none of the three cites states that
  whitefish hold higher off the bottom or along a sand edge — no sand-edge
  language occurs in any of them — and in both the Channel Islands and the
  Cortez Bank days the whitefish came off a separate, shallower stop than the
  reds rather than off shared rock. The claim carries the front-matter
  `sonar_depth` field, the sonar bullet in Finding them, and the routing
  rationale in Doctrine & conflicts.
- **Small whitefish are resident over inshore bass structure**, mixed into the
  blacksmith and blue perch that make up most of what a meter shows there
  (`kwMIgkCtFUE`).
- ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: what ocean whitefish eat naturally.
  Squid is the hook bait everywhere in the corpus, and no source names a
  forage species, a bait-driven arrival, or a temperature that moves them.

## Feeding triggers

- **Current gates the island bite.** Current at the islands is as important as
  the bait, and the conditions make or break the day (`6-8KfjEg0x8`).
  ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: mechanism — no source says whether current
  positions the fish on the seam, moves the bait to them, or simply lets the
  presentation work.
- **They are a fast, aggressive eat on a bottom bait.** A jig allowed to sit on
  the bottom is picked up by whitefish and small cod before a yellowtail can
  find it (`8XIy7hPsFkQ`) — the retrieve that answers it is in
  [yo-yo iron](../techniques/yo-yo-iron.md#mechanics). The same speed shows on
  the dropped squid:
  the eat comes on the drop rather than after a soak (`6-8KfjEg0x8`).
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the source shows a fast hookup — "And
  he's on. And we're bit. Just like that." — but never distinguishes the drop
  from a soak (`6-8KfjEg0x8`).
- ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: a light window, tide or slack gate, or
  moon effect on the whitefish bite.

## Finding them (sign & sonar)

No surface sign — this is a meter-and-chart fishery, like the rockfish
complex.

- **Hunt the hard bottom and the hard-to-sand transition.** Whitefish paint as
  scattered mid-column marks and arches over hard bottom and along the sand
  edge — the "scratchy marks coming through throughout the column" you see
  while set up on an island rock, higher off the bottom than the rockfish stack
  sitting tight to the pinnacle (`6-8KfjEg0x8`). Run a **dual-frequency split
  screen**, low frequency to find structure and bait and high to pick the
  gamefish out of it, and waypoint the seams. Full method:
  [electronics and sounder](../planning/electronics-and-sounder.md),
  [search and glassing](../planning/search-and-glassing.md).
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the "scratchy marks" quote is verbatim
  ("some kind of scratchy marks coming through throughout the column") but the
  speaker never says those marks are whitefish — he says it while explaining why
  he is adding a sliding sinker — and "arches", "hard bottom" and "sand edge"
  appear nowhere in that transcript (`6-8KfjEg0x8`).
- **The meter does not name the species.** Marks hanging above a piece of
  structure can be vermilion, bocaccio or whitefish; you drop a bait to find
  out which (`_ZThckj2TIM`).
- **Over inshore bass structure, assume perch first.** Most of what a meter
  shows on a nearshore reef is blacksmith and blue perch, with the occasional
  small whitefish or sheephead in the mix, so a wall of marks there is not a
  whitefish school (`kwMIgkCtFUE`).
- **The read that says whitefish:** stacked, willing mid-column marks that bite
  immediately on a dropped squid, rather than the picking, one-at-a-time
  bottom contact of a lingcod stop (`6-8KfjEg0x8`).
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): no whitefish-versus-lingcod mark or
  bite contrast exists in the source; lingcod appear only as a fish that was
  caught and measured short (`6-8KfjEg0x8`).

  A single all-purpose **island rod fished in the middle of its line rating**
  covers calicos, whitefish and sheephead off the same structure
  (`7L7OVoXE7KU`, `DfLcSS-J3g4`) — see
  [all-purpose rod line rating](../tackle/all-purpose-rod-line-rating.md) and
  [gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md).
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): no reel of any kind is described in
  that transcript — "level-wind" is not in the source, here or in the Situations
  table row (`6-8KfjEg0x8`).
- **Terminal:** [dropper loop](../techniques/dropper-loop.md) or
  [sliding sinker](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md). Squid is the staple bait;
  cut bait and [gulp/soft-plastic grubs](../lures/soft-plastic-swimbaits.md)
  work when you are not carrying squid.
- **Hooks and leader:** carry **size 1 and 3/0 light-wire J hooks** in the box
  for the shallow cod, whitefish and sheephead stop (`3xx_vES0kdo`) — see
  [hooks](../tackle/hooks.md). The same source's **25 lb fluorocarbon** figure
  is stated for the flyline setup, not for the bottom hooks (`3xx_vES0kdo`),
  and no corpus source gives a whitefish-specific leader — see

## Doctrine & conflicts

Section order below is not a ranking.

- **Whitefish route separately from [rockfish and lingcod](rockfish-lingcod.md)
  even when they share a spot.** What the choice turns on is column position,
  not tackle: whitefish are tilefish (Malacanthidae), not *Sebastes*, and hold
  higher off the bottom and along sand edges rather than pinned to the rock,
  so a stop that
  looks like a mediocre sand-edge seam can be the right stop for them and the
  wrong one for reds. Terminal tackle is shared, so the two notes cross-link
  rather than merge, and a variety-day plan can call whitefish out explicitly
  instead of burying them inside the rockfish stack.
- **Target or trash depends on what you came for.** On a variety day whitefish
  are a prime Channel Islands species and "pound for pound, highly underrated"
  on the table (`6-8KfjEg0x8`); on a yellowtail day the same fish is the thing
  that eats a jig left sitting on the bottom and costs you the drop
  (`8XIy7hPsFkQ`). Both statements are about the same behaviour — a fast eat on
  a stationary bottom presentation — and the decision is set by the target, not
  by disagreement between sources.

## Landing & handling

- **Do not hand-grab a whitefish: the gill plates are sharp** (`fDSd9kqwYW0`).
  General method: [dehooking and release](../fish-care/dehooking-and-release.md).
- **Sea lions take hooked whitefish at anchored island spots.** A roughly
  **10 lb** fish was taken off the line by a sea lion at Catalina, and one was
  saved only by getting it aboard quickly (`HGyL7pXy3Ts`) — the handling rule is
  to keep the fish moving up rather than letting it hang in the column.
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the source's one whitefish/sea-lion
  passage says both "we got it away just in time" and "after he stoled your 10
  pound white fish" in the same breath, so it establishes neither two fish nor a
  lost one; the later unambiguous theft — "the sea lion got it broke you off" —
  names no species (`HGyL7pXy3Ts`). The wind-fast handling rule is separately
  well supported.
- **They are a table fish, not a throwback** — pound for pound highly
  underrated eating (`6-8KfjEg0x8`); the value is table quality, not fight.
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the source says "pound for pound,
  whitefish highly underrated species" and never mentions eating, meat or table
  quality; its only "very tasty" line is about the blue rockfish at a different
  stop. The same "on the table" gloss is carried in Doctrine & conflicts
  (`6-8KfjEg0x8`).
- ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: whether whitefish brought up from the
  **150–250 ft** band suffer barotrauma, and whether venting or a descending
  device is used on released fish. General care:
  [fish care](../fish-care/README.md).
