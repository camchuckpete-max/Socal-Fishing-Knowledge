# Cabrilla (Leopard Grouper)

Cabrilla is the leopard grouper of the Sea of Cortez (`dEPuDrhoClM`), fished
from pangas as a cast-and-burn program: the fish sits on rock and is pulled
off it by a jerkbait or an iron moving fast enough to trigger a reaction, not
by a soaked bait (`EmZO8QiOfik`). Grade on a documented Bahía de los Ángeles
trip ran **5–10 lb** with **15 lb** fish in the same schools and a **17 lb**
best on a spring scale (`0dNfsle54Wo`). The program is high-volume casting —
**2–3 casts per minute, 500–700 casts a day** — and the fish's first move is
back into the rock, which is what sets the drag, leader and rod choices below
(`EmZO8QiOfik`).

## Where & when

Cabrilla are fished from pangas in the Sea of Cortez, principally out of
[Bahía de los Ángeles](../locations/bahia-de-los-angeles.md) on the
multi-day mothership trips (`EmZO8QiOfik`, `dEPuDrhoClM`), and south into the
[Loreto](../locations/loreto.md) island country (`n4nMCLLW3tY`). Zone
lexicon and structure: [Sea of Cortez](../locations/sea-of-cortez.md).

**Bait size, not calendar date, is the seasonal variable the corpus records.**
From roughly **October** into winter, Sea of Cortez baitfish drop to about
**1–1.5 in** and cabrilla key hard on that micro-bait, so a fall or winter trip
carries downsized lures; in spring and summer the same downsize is the fallback
when the bite goes slow (`EmZO8QiOfik`).
⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: a month-by-month presence or temperature
pattern for cabrilla anywhere in the Cortez.

**Presence is not the bite, and cabrilla are the fallback when the pelagic
bite quits.** At a Bahía de los Ángeles tournament in **May** the cabrilla
fishing out-produced the yellowtail fishing on the day the yellows went off
(`6P34jI9q1tE`); a dedicated cabrilla program ignores yellowtail and dorado
entirely and fishes structure all day (`EmZO8QiOfik`).

## Presence & forage

- **They hold on the structure, not in the water column.** Cabrilla sit behind
  a boulder or against a vertical wall and ambush what passes, the way a
  [calico bass](calico-bass.md) works structure (`6P34jI9q1tE`). The bottom
  to look for is **snaggy, not sandy or flat** — big bull rocks, pinnacles and
  scattered boulder/slide areas (`EmZO8QiOfik`).
- **Fall and winter forage is micro-bait at about 1–1.5 in**, and the fish key
  on it hard enough that matching the hatch is the whole program in that window
  (`EmZO8QiOfik`).
- **Sierra mackerel share the same surface schools** when cabrilla come up to
  feed on top (`0dNfsle54Wo`).
- **The same structure holds a wide bycatch list**, which is why the tackle is
  sized above the target: yellowtail, roosterfish, dorado tight against the
  rocks, sierra, and snappers all eat the cabrilla program (`EmZO8QiOfik`);
  the downsized micro-lure version adds spotted bay bass, pompano and lane
  snapper (`EmZO8QiOfik`).
- ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: the forage species cabrilla eat by name,
  or what they feed on outside the fall/winter micro-bait window.

## Feeding triggers

- **Light level sets both aggression and colour.** In low light — early
  morning and late afternoon — the fish feed aggressively, and white, bright
  and dark colours all get bit. Once the sun clears the hills and light
  penetrates, bright, metallic and flashy colours get bit better and the fish
  turn finicky (`EmZO8QiOfik`, `M8hOYQ_6rSg`). The lure-size answer to that
  light change — the 170/150 ladder and the disagreement over it — is in
  [cabrilla — jerkbait](cabrilla-jerkbait.md#doctrine--conflicts).
  ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: mechanism — why light penetration flips
  the colour preference.
- **Speed is the trigger, not the presentation.** A bait moving fast draws the
  bite; a rolling or slow-rolled one does not (`EmZO8QiOfik`). The action test
  a bait has to pass and the no-pause, no-jerk retrieve cadence are in
  [cabrilla — jerkbait](cabrilla-jerkbait.md#how-cabrilla-changes-the-execution).
- **Sun height moves them down the wall**, so the trigger moves with them: see
  the depth ladder in [Finding them](#finding-them-sign--sonar).
- ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: tide, slack-water, current or moon-phase
  gating for the cabrilla bite.

## Finding them (sign & sonar)

- **Structure is the search, and a boiler rock is the easiest target.** Look
  for bottom that is not sandy or flat; a **boiler rock** — one that shows
  itself breaking the surface — is the easiest thing to cast at, and
  **vertical walls** fish the same way (`EmZO8QiOfik`, `6P34jI9q1tE`).
- **Read bottom colour changes** to identify what you cannot see directly: a
  colour change separates sandy bottom from reef from a submerged boiler rock
  (`EmZO8QiOfik`).
- **The dark spot holds the bigger fish once the sun is up.** In low light the
  fish sit tight and shallow on visible shoreline rock; as the sun climbs they
  back off it, and a **dark patch in the water** a little further out is
  usually submerged rock invisible in low light (`EmZO8QiOfik`).
- **Split the cast.** With two anglers, one casts at the visible shoreline
  structure while the other casts parallel to work the submerged rock further
  out (`EmZO8QiOfik`).
- **Depth ladder through the day:** fish rise **30–40 ft** to eat a jerkbait on
  the surface early; by late morning (**~9–10 am** on) they have dropped to
  **60–80 ft**; under high sun (**~11 am–3 pm**) the bigger fish push to
  **80–150 ft** (`EmZO8QiOfik`). The shallow extreme is real too — cabrilla
  came off **5–10 ft** of water on a jerkbait at Loreto (`n4nMCLLW3tY`).
- **Surface foam is a cabrilla sign in its own right.** A school of roughly
  **30 fish, every one above 5 lb and running 5–15 lb**, swirled on the surface
  "like they were a school of carp," worked under booby birds and gulls with
  sierra mackerel in the same water (`0dNfsle54Wo`) —
  [bird reading](../conditions/bird-reading.md),
  [search and glassing](../planning/search-and-glassing.md).
  ⚠ Fact-check (single-source): one trip is the corpus's only record of
  cabrilla foaming on the surface, and no mechanism is stated for what puts
  them up.
- ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: a sonar signature or arc depth for
  cabrilla. The corpus describes the visual and structure read only, so
  silence here is a source gap, not a statement that cabrilla cannot be
  metered. General method:
  [electronics and sounder](../planning/electronics-and-sounder.md).

## Gear summary (class terms)

- **Jerkbait outfit — two rods fished side by side**, a **medium-light** and a
  **medium-heavy**, both effectively an oversized, super-stiff jerkbait rod
  ("a crankbait rod on steroids"), **7'10"–8 ft, ~50 lb class**
  (`EmZO8QiOfik`). See
  [rod & reel selection](../tackle/rod-and-reel-selection.md) and
  [gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md).
- **Reel — the jerkbait program's gear ratio and its level-wind requirement**
  are in [cabrilla — jerkbait](cabrilla-jerkbait.md#rigs--gear).
- **Line — lighter outfit 65 lb braid to a 50 lb mono top shot; heavier outfit
  80 lb braid to an 80 lb leader** (`EmZO8QiOfik`). Run **6–8 ft of leader** —
  close to a rod length — so that a fish that gets into the rocks is pulling
  against leader rather than braid (`dEPuDrhoClM`).
- **Yo-yo iron — stout 40–60 lb class rod with a high-speed reel**; the
  depth-keyed iron weights are in
  [cabrilla — yo-yo iron](cabrilla-yo-yo-iron.md#rigs--gear), the general
  method in [yo-yo iron](../techniques/yo-yo-iron.md).
- **Hooks/hardware for jerkbaits:** see
  [hooks](../tackle/hooks.md#baja-sea-of-cortez-sizing) for the Baja / Sea of
  Cortez jerkbait hook upgrade.
- **Spare line is a packing item on this program**, because the cast volume
  wears braid inside a day: parameters in
  [braid wear from cast volume](../tackle/line-and-leader.md#braid-wear-from-cast-volume--carry-a-spare-spool).
- **Size for the bycatch, not the average fish.** Jerkbait tackle built for
  cabrilla routinely hooks yellowtail, roosterfish, dorado, sierra and snappers
  tight against the rocks (`EmZO8QiOfik`), so hooks, leader and drag are sized
  for the cabrilla program.
- **Lure attrition is a gear-planning number** — how many jerkbaits a session
  and a multi-day trip actually consume is in
  [cabrilla — jerkbait](cabrilla-jerkbait.md#rigs--gear).

## Doctrine & conflicts

Section order below is not a ranking.

- **Drag setting is chosen against rod action, not on its own.** The shared
  premise is that a cabrilla given line turns its head and goes into the rock,
  and that a fish allowed to pull line is lost more often than landed
  (`EmZO8QiOfik`, `6P34jI9q1tE`). What splits the two positions is where the
  shock absorption comes from. **Cesar** runs the star drag to its stop —
  essentially locked — and pairs it with a **slower, softer-bending rod** whose
  bend absorbs the strike the drag no longer does. **Ray Sharifi** backs the
  drag off and pairs it with a **faster-action rod**, taking his give from the
  drag and from running braid straight to fluoro (`EmZO8QiOfik`). Pick the pair,
  not one half of it: a locked drag on a fast rod leaves nothing to absorb the
  head-turn and pulls hooks.
- **The jerkbait doctrine conflicts are kept in the sub-article, not here** —
  whether a Current-Sniper-class bait kicks tightly enough for cabrilla at
  all, whether the sun-up bite is answered by dropping to the 150, and the
  light vs heavy hook-wire trade: all three are held side by side in
  [cabrilla — jerkbait](cabrilla-jerkbait.md#doctrine--conflicts).

## Landing & handling

- **Release is the default and a couple of fish are kept for the table.** A
  tournament crew at Bahía de los Ángeles kept two cabrilla for dinner and
  released the rest (`T3cEQUqiH8E`); a separate Bahía de los Ángeles trip kept
  one or two between two anglers and released everything else (`wX7jUycIJ-U`).
- **Bleed the kept fish at the boat rather than at the end of the day** — a
  cabrilla panga session bled its catch at midday before running in
  (`ZYAILGyrkRk`). General method: [fish care](../fish-care/README.md).
- **Cabrilla have teeth worth respecting on the unhook** (`n4nMCLLW3tY`).
- ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: whether cabrilla brought up from the
  **80–150 ft** midday depth band suffer barotrauma, and whether venting or a
  descending device is used before release — the corpus documents both the
  deep bite and the release ethic and never connects them.
