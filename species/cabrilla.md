# Cabrilla (Leopard Grouper)

**Region: Baja — Sea of Cortez / Bay of LA (BOLA), panga fishery.** Cabrilla
(leopard grouper) are a structure-ambush fish: they sit tight to rock and
reef and get pulled off it with a fast, reaction-triggering retrieve rather
than a soaked bait. The doctrine below is a single dedicated cabrilla
breakdown (Cesar, 25+ yr Sea of Cortez cabrilla angler and rod-company rep,
via Dirty Hookers, EmZO8QiOfik, 2025-03-04) — a registered voice
([source registry](../sources/source-registry.md)), but this is the first
committed cabrilla-specific doctrine from him, so it sits at `medium`
pending repetition from other Cesar/Dirty Hookers cabrilla videos in the
corpus; specific lure/reel model picks stay `low` per his product-endorsement
caveat.

## Where & when

Fished from pangas out of Sea of Cortez / Bay of LA trip bases (e.g. Tony
Reyes-style multi-day trips). No month-by-month location pattern is given in
this source beyond a bait-driven seasonal shift: **fall into winter,
roughly October on**, Sea of Cortez baitfish shrink to about **1–1.5 in**,
and cabrilla key hard on that micro-bait — carry downsized lures for that
window (see [Situations → techniques](#situations--techniques)). Outside
that window (spring/summer), downsizing is still a reliable fallback any
time the bite goes slow.

## Finding them (sign & sonar)

**⚠ Flagged stub — no sonar signature in the corpus yet.** This source
describes the visual/structure read only and gives no sonar-arc or depth
signature for cabrilla specifically. Cabrilla are a structure-ambush fish, so
a meter signature over the rock is a real thing to look for — the gap is in
the sources, not the fishery. Flagged so silence doesn't read as "you can't
meter them." General method:
[electronics & sounder](../planning/electronics-and-sounder.md).

- **Structure is everything — target "snaggy," not sandy.** Look for
  bottom that is **not** sandy or flat: big bull rocks, pinnacles, and
  scattered boulder/slide areas. The easiest visual target is a **boiler
  rock** (a rock that shows itself breaking the surface).
- **Read bottom color changes** to identify what you can't see directly —
  a color change signals sandy bottom vs. reef vs. a submerged boiler rock.
- **The "dark spot" — submerged structure once the sun is up.** In low
  light, fish sit tight and shallow on visible shoreline rock. Once the
  sun climbs, fish back off that tight shallow structure; a **dark patch
  in the water** a little further off the visible rocks is usually
  submerged rock invisible in low light, and it holds the **bigger fish**
  once the sun is up.
- **Split the cast:** with two anglers, one casts at the visible shoreline
  structure while the other casts parallel to work the submerged rock
  further out.
- **Depth moves through the day:** fish will rise **30–40 ft** to eat a
  jerkbait fished on the surface early; by late morning (~9–10 am on)
  they've dropped to **60–80 ft**; by midday sun-high (~11 am–3 pm) the
  bigger fish push to **80–150 ft**. General search/electronics method:
  [search and glassing](../planning/search-and-glassing.md),
  [electronics and sounder](../planning/electronics-and-sounder.md) — this
  source gives no sonar-arc description for cabrilla specifically, only the
  visual/structure read above.

## Situations → techniques

| Situation (conditions) | Do this (ranked) | Gear class | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Low light — early morning / late afternoon, tight to shoreline structure | [Jerkbaits](../lures/jerkbaits.md) — 170-size, cast tight to the rock | Jerkbait-class casting outfit, 50 lb class | White, bright, or even dark colors all get bit in low light; fish are feeding aggressively and eating the larger bait |
| Sun up, still shallow-to-mid structure | [Jerkbaits](../lures/jerkbaits.md) — drop to 150-size (or the Mag Minnow), switch to bright/metallic/flashy colors | Same jerkbait-class outfit | Once light penetrates, work off the tightest rock toward the "dark spot" submerged structure |
| Late morning to lunch (~9–10 am on), fish off the top, 60–80 ft | [Yo-yo iron](../techniques/yo-yo-iron.md) — Salas 6X, 6X Jr, 60 g jig paras, or Krocodile | 40–60 lb class stout rod, high-speed reel | Cast out, let it hit bottom, then **burn it straight back to the boat** — not a hop-on-bottom rockfish-jig retrieve |
| Midday, sun high (~11 am–3 pm), bigger fish pushed deep, 80–150 ft | Heavier [yo-yo iron](../techniques/yo-yo-iron.md) — Salas 6X, Tady 40, or a 7X-class iron | Heavier 40–60 lb+ class | Burn ~30–40 cranks off the bottom, let it settle back to bottom, repeat; bites usually come within 5–10 cranks of picking the iron back up |
| Fall/winter (Oct+) micro-bait (~1–1.5 in) match-the-hatch; also a year-round slow-bite fallback | Downsize both the jerkbait and iron programs to micro/small-profile lures — small jig paras and small-profile jerkbaits (asr-uncertain: two additional micro-lure names in this source could not be confidently reconstructed — see log) | Same outfits, smaller lures | Also draws incidental bycatch on the same small lures — spotted bay bass, pompano, lane snapper |

## Gear summary (class terms)

- **Jerkbait outfit:** two rods fished side by side — a **medium-light** and
  a **medium-heavy**, both effectively an oversized, super-stiff jerkbait
  rod (a "crankbait rod on steroids"), **7'10"–8 ft, ~50 lb class**. Reel:
  a **low-profile, level-wind baitcaster at a 6.5:1–7:1+ gear ratio** — the
  fast retrieve is required to swim the baits correctly, and a level-wind is
  needed because the program is high-volume casting (**~2–3 casts/min**); a
  reel you have to hand-guide the line back on fatigues the angler and
  starts producing backlashes over a session. Line: lighter outfit **65 lb
  braid to 50 lb mono top shot**; heavier outfit **80 lb braid to 80 lb
  leader**. See [rod & reel selection](../tackle/rod-and-reel-selection.md)
  and [gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md).
- **Yo-yo iron:** stout **40–60 lb class** rod with a high-speed reel — see
  [yo-yo iron](../techniques/yo-yo-iron.md).
- **Hooks/hardware for jerkbaits:** see
  [hooks](../tackle/hooks.md#baja-sea-of-cortez-sizing) for the Baja/Sea of
  Cortez jerkbait hook upgrade.
- **Bring a spare spool of braid** (at least a one-shot 300 yd spool) and
  extra top shot — high cast/retrieve volume in a day wears braid through
  the guides.
- **Size for bycatch:** jerkbait tackle built for cabrilla routinely hooks
  large incidental bycatch tight against the rocks (yellowtail, roosterfish,
  dorado, sierra, snappers) — size hooks, leader, and drag for the cabrilla
  program, not the average bycatch.

## Doctrine & conflicts

- **Drag philosophy — kept side by side, not reconciled (EmZO8QiOfik):**
  Cesar fishes his jerkbait drag **essentially locked down** (star-drag run
  to its stop) — his reasoning is that a cabrilla given an inch of line
  turns its head and starts taking line, so the drag should give nothing; he
  pairs that locked drag with a **slower, softer-bending rod** to absorb the
  strike shock the drag no longer does. **Ray Sharifi** (co-host,
  unregistered channel, medium confidence) fishes the drag **backed off**
  instead, paired with a **faster-action rod**. Both are attributed
  drag/rod philosophies for the same cabrilla fishery.
- **Retrieve action:** cabrilla want a **fast, tight wobbling action with a
  little "hunting"** (slight off-track wander); a **rolling/slow-rolling**
  action does not draw bites — Cesar's stated, unconfirmed exception is that
  lethargic spawning fish might eat a slow-rolling bait.
- **Hook wire on Current Sniper-class jerkbaits is a situational trade,
  not a conflict:** a lighter Z-wire hook (Owner STX, size 1) keeps the
  swimming action for a finicky bite; a heavier wire hook (Owner ST-66, 1X,
  size 1) holds up better in a hot/wide-open bite but dampens the action —
  see [hooks](../tackle/hooks.md#baja-sea-of-cortez-sizing).
