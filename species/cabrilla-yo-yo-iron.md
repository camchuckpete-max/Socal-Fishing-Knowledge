# Cabrilla — yo-yo iron

Once the sun is up and cabrilla have backed off the shallow rock, the iron
takes the day over from the jerkbait — but it is fished as a **cast-and-burn**,
not as a vertical drop on a metered school (`EmZO8QiOfik`). Which lure program
to run at all is the [cabrilla](cabrilla.md) router's call, and the general
method lives in [yo-yo iron](../techniques/yo-yo-iron.md); this page is the
retrieve, the bite window and the iron weight for this fish.

## When this is the call

**When the fish drop below the jerkbait's running depth.** Cabrilla rise
30–40 ft to eat a jerkbait on the surface early; by late morning (**~9–10 am**
on) they have dropped to **60–80 ft**, and under high sun (**~11 am–3 pm**)
the bigger fish push to **80–150 ft** (`EmZO8QiOfik`). The iron is what
reaches them through both of those bands. The full ranking against the
jerkbait program, the surface-iron program and the foam stop is in
[cabrilla](cabrilla.md#situations--techniques).

**The target is still structure, not a mark.** Cabrilla hold against rock and
wall rather than suspending in the column, and the corpus records no sonar
signature for them, so the iron is cast at the same boiler rock, pinnacle and
vertical wall the jerkbait was thrown at — see
[finding them](cabrilla.md#finding-them-sign--sonar).

## How cabrilla changes the execution

**Cast, let it hit bottom, then burn it straight back to the boat.** The
retrieve is a straight fast wind off the bottom — explicitly **not** a
hop-along-the-bottom rockfish-jig retrieve (`EmZO8QiOfik`).

**In the deep band the wind is partial, not full-depth.** In the **80–150 ft**
midday zone, burn the iron **30–40 cranks** off the bottom, let it settle back
to the bottom, and repeat, rather than winding it all the way up
(`EmZO8QiOfik`). That count sits beside the shorter crank-and-freespool
cadences the general note carries — see
[Differs from the general method](#differs-from-the-general-method).

**The bite window is the first few cranks off the bottom.** Bites usually come
within **5–10 cranks** of picking the iron up off the bottom (`EmZO8QiOfik`),
which is what makes the settle-and-restart cycle worth the arm rather than a
single full wind-up per cast.

**A hooked fish turns for the rock.** The cabrilla program's drag, leader and
rod choices exist because the fish's first move is back into structure; the
drag-versus-rod-action doctrine is in
[cabrilla](cabrilla.md#doctrine--conflicts).

## Rigs & gear

- **Rod and reel — stout 40–60 lb class with a high-speed reel**, stepping up
  to the heavier end of that class for the 80–150 ft midday zone
  (`EmZO8QiOfik`); class lexicon in
  [gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md), general builds in
  [yo-yo iron](../techniques/yo-yo-iron.md#gear-class).
- **Iron weight is keyed to the depth band.** For the **60–80 ft**
  late-morning zone: a Salas 6X, a Salas 6X Jr., a **60 g** jig para, or a
  Krocodile-class casting spoon. For the **80–150 ft** midday zone: step up to
  a Salas 6X, a Tady 40, or a 7X-class iron (`EmZO8QiOfik`). Bait specs:
  [iron jigs](../lures/iron-jigs.md).
- **Line** — the cabrilla program's braid/leader pairs (65 lb braid to a 50 lb
  mono top shot on the lighter outfit, 80 lb braid to an 80 lb leader on the
  heavier; 6–8 ft of leader so a fish in the rocks pulls against leader rather
  than braid) are in [cabrilla](cabrilla.md#gear-summary-class-terms).
- **Downsizing for the fall/winter micro-bait window** — small jig paras in
  place of the full-size iron — is the router's call, not a separate program:
  see [cabrilla](cabrilla.md#situations--techniques).

## Differs from the general method

Against [yo-yo iron](../techniques/yo-yo-iron.md), which owns the vertical
drop, the depth-banded sizing and the colour doctrine:

- **It is a cast at structure, not a drop on a mark.** The general method
  positions up-current of a metered school or the bottom and free-spools
  straight down; the cabrilla version casts at visible or read rock and burns
  the iron back to the boat (`EmZO8QiOfik`).
- **The cadence count is its own.** The general note carries a 10-crank
  working-water cycle, a 5–6-crank under-the-boat cycle and a half-depth wind;
  the cabrilla deep band runs **30–40 cranks** before settling back
  (`EmZO8QiOfik`). Nothing is reconciled — the counts come from different
  fisheries and each is stated for its own.
- **The bottom-hop prohibition arrives by a different route.** The general note
  rules out short hops on the bottom because that motion draws rockfish instead
  of yellowtail; the cabrilla source rules out the same rockfish-jig motion in
  favour of the burn, with speed as the stated trigger rather than bycatch
  (`EmZO8QiOfik`) — see
  [feeding triggers](cabrilla.md#feeding-triggers).
  ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: mechanism — why the hop specifically fails
  on cabrilla.
- **The working band sits at the shallow end.** The general method's common
  bands are 120–180 ft nearshore and 150–250+ ft on offshore banks and islands;
  the cabrilla program runs **60–150 ft** (`EmZO8QiOfik`), inside the
  smaller-iron rungs of the general sizing ladder — see
  [jig size & depth](../techniques/yo-yo-iron.md#jig-size--depth).
