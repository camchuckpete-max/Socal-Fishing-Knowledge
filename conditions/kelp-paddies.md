# Kelp Paddies

Drifting kelp paddies are floating structure that hold bait, shade, and
gamefish — yellowtail, dorado, paddy bluefin, tripletail, and bycatch — out
over open water where there is no bottom structure to concentrate them. Two
things drive a paddy program: **geometry** (paddies line up along the current
or a bank contour, so a productive paddy points you to the next one) and
**freshness** (only some paddies hold fish on a given day, and that
population is fixed until dark).

## Paddy geometry — search the line, not the compass

Paddies are not scattered at random; the current that moves them lines them
up (`DnSqw4r7A1s`):

- **Open water:** under the summer uphill current, paddies align roughly
  **north–south**. Find one, then search its **N–S line** for the next.
- **Near a bank edge:** paddies align **along the contour** instead — follow
  the contour, not the compass.

Plan a **paddy line** — a route that walks paddy to paddy — rather than
working a single stakeout.

If another boat is already parked on a paddy, don't crash it; look **up and
down its line** for un-pressured paddies feeding the same drift instead. Fish
on a worked paddy are the least willing; ones a quarter mile up the line have
not seen a bait (`-JZpyWLdKlk`).

## Paddy pressure gradient (SoCal geography)

Paddies are not equal across the Bight (`HzE4FHHcvTk`):

- The closer to a dock and the more boat traffic, the less willing the fish.
- Paddies are scarcer and hold less north of Dana Point.
- The best paddy water is south — south of Santa Catalina / San Clemente
  Island, and from Dana Point to Oceanside southward.

Weight a paddy program toward that southern water when boat range allows.

## Paddy freshness — check fast, then move

Fish move paddy-to-paddy only at night; during the day the population on a
given paddy is fixed, so a dry paddy stays dry all day — do not sit on a
barren paddy hoping it turns on (`4xzK7YaXK5s`).

The discipline:

1. **Approach directly — don't sneak up.** Cutting the stereo and bait pump
   and drifting in quietly is the wrong instinct for a kelp paddy: unlike
   structure fish over a reef, a paddy isn't spooky. Drive up to the edge of
   it "like it's the dock" (`ZFqe49jRgA0`, `8XrMkWPRxgs`). Look under the
   paddy for visible fish before committing bait — if fish are visible, it's
   on today (`8XrMkWPRxgs`).
2. **Check fast.** Look in the water for visible fish (dorado, yellowtail),
   then check the fathometer for boomerang-shaped arcs, typically **90–120
   ft** down, if nothing shows on the surface. Give it about a minute, maybe
   two — a look, a sounder pass, one bait (`ZFqe49jRgA0`).
3. If nothing shows, move to the next paddy on the line.
4. Paddies restock overnight, so a paddy dry yesterday can be loaded this
   morning; moon-bright nights drive bigger redistribution (see the
   [moon layer](moon.md)). The driver of an "afternoon bite" is not time of
   day — it is simply when an angler finds an already-loaded paddy. A paddy
   sat on for hours without a bite will not turn on by waiting, because
   restocking only happens at night (`ZFqe49jRgA0`, `8XrMkWPRxgs`).

This is why the paddy line matters more than any single paddy — it is a
numbers game across many quick checks.

Paddy size doesn't disqualify it from a check — even a marginal-looking
piece is worth the minute it costs (`8THSuqoPI_Q`).

A paddy showing skipjack isn't the same as a dead paddy: better-grade tuna
can move in later and push the skipjack out, so a skipjack-marked patty is
worth staying on rather than leaving. This is a nuance on the
check-fast-then-move rule above, which is about a patty with no life on it
at all (`Ow3an9lSVh4`).

A paddy that announces itself from a distance — fish already jumping,
visible through binoculars from several miles out — collapses the check step
to zero; run straight to the edge and pitch baits (`HcEh5KOYTH4`).
Conversely, a paddy with no visible surface activity at all likely holds
nothing and can be written off without a cautious approach (`CrLDC4O8qS8`).

If a paddy has already produced a fish and the boat then drifts off it,
circle back and work it again rather than treating that one catch as the end
of the stop — a paddy that has already proven itself is a different case
than one still unproven (`-bw1KDfDjv4`).

## Working the paddy — stand off for tuna

Fish holding around a paddy, especially tuna, sit **outside** the piece
rather than directly under it — stay roughly **100 yards off** rather than
fishing tight against the kelp (`A6DJoXbID4c`). This is a positioning
nuance, not a reversal of the direct-approach check above: the direct
approach is about confirming a paddy is loaded without spooking it; once
fish are confirmed and working (a bird pile, boils, a bait ball chased away
from the kelp), the fishing happens in the water around the paddy, not
snagged tight to it. Watch the full 360° around the boat rather than
fixating on the paddy itself — fish and the birds marking them can show up
anywhere in that radius (`A6DJoXbID4c`).

## Finding paddies

- Glass down-swell on gray-calm mornings and watch paddies roll over the
  swell crests (`DnSqw4r7A1s`); general method in
  [search and glassing](../planning/search-and-glassing.md).
- Gulls mark paddies — "good sign but dumb," a general measure of life, and
  reliable for locating floating kelp even when they aren't marking gamefish
  (cameron). See [bird reading](bird-reading.md) for the full bird model.
- Once in the right water, the school is not sitting directly under the
  paddy — set the drift up so the boat's current carries onto the fish, not
  just onto the visible kelp. Mark the school on the sounder first, then
  position the boat so the drift carries to it; sometimes the school drifts
  to the boat, other times the boat has to work toward the school, and it
  varies drift to drift. Electronics are an aid, not sufficient alone —
  reading current to predict the drift, plus eyeballs on deck covering the
  boat's full 360°, still carries the search. Glass for splashes/breaking
  surface, birds cycling up and down over bait ("work birds"), and the
  paddy's own discoloration spot on the water — normally a visible brown
  patch, though under overcast skies the same paddy shows only as a plain
  dark spot (`VWClGAn2WEw`).
- **Drone.** An aerial alternative to binocular glassing and the fathometer
  check: fly a drone over a paddy to look for fish holding underneath it
  before committing bait — the same look-before-you-commit motion as the
  visual/sounder check above, done from the air instead of the deck
  (`9jDy4gUUyJk`).

## How to use it in planning

- Once a paddy produces, work its line — north–south in open water, along
  the contour near a bank edge — rather than treating it as a single stop.
- Weight the search south of Dana Point / south of Catalina–San Clemente
  Island when boat range allows; paddy density and willingness both drop
  north of Dana Point.
- Budget each paddy about a minute or two: a look, a sounder pass for
  90–120 ft arcs, one bait. Move on if nothing shows — it will not turn on
  before dark.
- After a moon-bright night, expect more restocking/reshuffling among
  paddies than after a dark night.
- On tuna, hold roughly 100 yards off the paddy rather than working tight to
  the kelp, and watch the full 360° around the boat.
- Glass down-swell in gray, calm conditions and watch for gulls to locate
  paddies before committing gear.
