# Moon

Moon phase is a **probability adjustment** to the search and timing plan, never a
hard gate on whether to go. The patterns below come from a full 2022 season of
dated reports rather than single-trip observations, so treat them as shifts to a
search prior and to timing — never as on/off switches. Full and new moons cluster
documented bite windows for several species, and the moon peak itself behaves more
like a **relocation event** than a bite-death.

## Bite windows — full/new ±1 week, leading days strongest

Treat full and new moon **±1 week** as elevated-probability windows: the **days
leading into the peak run strongest**, and the peak day itself is better read as a
relocation event than a shutdown (see [below](#post-moon-relocation--disappearance)).

- The days leading into the full moon are the strongest documented **bluefin**
  windows: "if we've learned anything over the last six, seven years"
  (`XLVUhV8DW64`).
- **White seabass** bites at the islands cluster on both the full
  (`DnSqw4r7A1s`, `-JZpyWLdKlk`) and new moon in season; a new-moon night on
  2022-08-31 produced a 22-fish day for one boat (`HnqiE05vdXs`). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus
  source: mechanism — whether the moon acts through the squid spawn cycle, the
  tidal range it sets, or night light (same gap noted on
  [white seabass](../species/white-seabass.md#feeding-triggers)).
- Late-November **bluefin** day windows tied to the **new moon** in one season
  (`Z3rZqy2Pi8E`).
- **Swordfish** bite activity rises around both the new and full moon and the
  weeks flanking them. ⚠ Fact-check (unverifiable): legacy claim carried through
  the v2 migration; no source id in this note's set ties swordfish bite timing to
  moon phase.

Capt. Dave Hansen (Your Saltwater Guide) puts the strongest window
differently: **7 days before AND 7 days after** the peak, with the peak day
itself the **travel period, not the bite period** — doctrine handed down to
him early in his career by captains he worked for, not a measurement
(`fri_BWI-VA0`, `LEiyB9QNzHY`). He names **bluefin** specifically for the
post-peak week ("that stuff really gets going") and calls the peak day itself
**"not good"** for the bite, tempered by having also seen bites land on a full
moon — a lean, not an absolute (`fri_BWI-VA0`).

## Post-moon relocation ≠ disappearance

On a bright moon night, fish travel and schools scatter to relocate rather than
going dormant: moonlight gives them enough confidence to move at night, when
daytime predation risk keeps them from covering the same ground
(`LEiyB9QNzHY`). Two independent distance estimates, kept side by side rather
than averaged: open-water bluefin not holding structure move **10–15 nm
overnight** (`Blh2BA-7Ono`); Capt. Dave Hansen puts a wider estimate on
schools — named as **albacore, bluefin, and yellowfin** — moving **20 miles**
after one full-moon night (`fri_BWI-VA0`).

After the October full moon, "the tuna are gone" was a coverage illusion, not an
actual disappearance — the fleet lost the fish and stopped reporting them
(`XLVUhV8DW64`). Post-moon discipline:

- **Heavily discount report persistence** — a 3-day-old fish position is stale.
- Treat **absence of reports** as coverage bias, not absence of fish.
- Treat the **moon peak itself as a relocation event**, not a bite-death.
- **Widen the search prior** toward same-date prior-year locations
  ([year-anniversary prior](../seasonal/year-anniversary-prior.md)).

Hansen ties a same-day bite-timing pattern to the same travel mechanism: the
morning after a full-moon night runs slow — either the school hasn't
relocated onto bait yet, or it fed all night and isn't inclined to feed hard
again right away — with the bite typically rebuilding late in the day
(`fri_BWI-VA0`). His practical guidance: if a trip on the full moon itself is
unavoidable, fish it late rather than push the early-morning window
(`fri_BWI-VA0`).

## Full-moon daytime bass suppression

On clear full-moon nights, [calico bass](../species/calico-bass.md) and
[sand bass](../species/sand-bass.md) gorge after dark and go quiet by
mid-morning — full fish shut down until the bite rebuilds late in the day
(`Blh2BA-7Ono`, `-JZpyWLdKlk`, `XLVUhV8DW64`). In winter the daytime window
narrows further to **reaction bites only**: fish that gorged all night on a
clear full moon aren't hungry, so a bait has to be worked as a reaction trigger
rather than fished to a feeding fish (`5D1vx29LVpI`). Fish bass segments at
first light or in the afternoon on a full moon; midday is the weakest window.

## Kelp paddies restock only after dark

Fish move paddy-to-paddy exclusively in darkness, so a paddy dry during the day
stays dry until night no matter how long it's worked — check it fast (look, one
sounder pass, one bait) and move to the next paddy on the line rather than
staking out (`4xzK7YaXK5s`; see [kelp paddies](kelp-paddies.md)). Moon-bright
nights are read to drive bigger paddy-to-paddy redistribution than dark
nights — the same night-travel-confidence mechanism described in
[post-moon relocation](#post-moon-relocation--disappearance) above, though no
source in this note's set ties that mechanism to paddies specifically.

## Lobster: darkest, no-moon nights are best

Unlike the pelagic/bass doctrine above,
[California spiny lobster](../species/california-spiny-lobster.md) runs the
**opposite** moon lean. Lobster are nocturnal because "everything wants to eat a
lobster," and a bright moonlit night reads to them the same as daylight — they
travel and crawl less under it (`fri_BWI-VA0`). Capt. Dave Hansen's stated
best hoop-netting conditions are the **darkest, no-moon nights**, including
rough/rainy/stormy dark nights — "the dark, dark, darkest nights are the
best" (`fri_BWI-VA0`) — with sea state, not the moon, as the hard go/no-go
call. He carries his own caveat: full-moon hoop-netting has "never done very
good" for him personally, but has still produced lobster, and he knows others
who caught "lots of lobsters" on a full moon too — a lean, not a shutout
(`fri_BWI-VA0`).

## Tide amplitude coupling

Spring tides — the biggest swings — fall on the full and new moon; neap tides,
the smallest, fall on the quarters. A moon window is therefore also a
big-swing [tide window](tide-and-slack.md).

## Arrival events

The moon marks arrivals as well as bites: a new wave of bluefin was tracked
arriving "out of the west" on the moon into the Ensenada zone, distinct from
the fish already holding in U.S. waters (`Blh2BA-7Ono`).

## How to use it in planning

- Treat full/new moon **±1 week** as an elevated-probability window for
  bluefin, white seabass, and swordfish — not a go/no-go gate — and weight the
  search prior toward the days leading into the peak.
- After a full-moon peak night, discount stale reports and widen the search
  toward the [year-anniversary prior](../seasonal/year-anniversary-prior.md)
  rather than assuming the fish left.
- On the full moon itself, plan the trip late in the day rather than pushing
  the early-morning window.
- For calico and sand bass on a full moon, fish first light or the afternoon
  and expect a dead midday, sharpest in winter when the day may be reaction
  bites only.
- On paddies, expect a bigger overnight reshuffle after a moon-bright night —
  don't assume yesterday's dry paddy stays dry, or a loaded one stays loaded.
- For lobster, book around the darkest, no-moon nights when the calendar
  allows; sea state, not the moon, is still the hard go/no-go call.
- A moon window is also a big-tide-swing window — stack the
  [tide and slack](tide-and-slack.md) plan against it.
