# Bird Reading

Birds are the fastest long-range fish detector on the water — a working flock
is visible for miles, well before sonar or surface commotion confirms fish.
Each species reads differently (terns and shearwaters are the reliable ones;
gulls and pelicans mostly are not), and the same behavior distinction —
locked-in on bait vs. actively pushed by fish underneath — repeats across all
of them. Use with [search and glassing](../planning/search-and-glassing.md)
for the glassing protocol and effective ranges.

## The core question for every bird

The question is never just whether birds are present — it is **whether they
are on bait alone, or on bait being pushed by fish underneath**. Locked-in,
stationary behavior reads as bait only; moving, pushing, or racing behavior
reads as fish working that bait — read behavior, not just presence.

## Terns

The single best bird: constant, high-eyesight surface hunters that are always
flying and looking, so they surface anything edible fast (`Blh2BA-7Ono`).

- **Locked-in, repeated dives on one spot = bait only.** Bait fish under
  predation pressure look down, not up, so surface bait undisturbed by
  anything below it sits still and lets terns hit the same spot over and
  over without moving (`Blh2BA-7Ono`).
- **A moving dive-line = bait being pushed by fish underneath** — terns come
  in, dive, fly ahead a few feet, and dive again because the bait itself is
  fleeing something below it. That is the one to run on (`Blh2BA-7Ono`).
- **Cast under the bird the moment it dives**, not at its general position: a
  hovering tern is waiting on a school to push bait to the surface beneath
  it, and the dive is the signal that bait just broke the surface directly
  under it (`JiJYamo0QHk`).
- **Read the individual bird, not just the flock.** Birds actively
  diving/working on a school mean it is feeding at the surface; birds off the
  fish but still nearby mean the school hasn't left — it is holding just
  under the surface without feeding on top ("puddling") rather than gone
  (`A6DJoXbID4c`). A finer-grained read than the moving-vs-locked dive-line
  rule above, kept alongside it, not a replacement.
- On-the-water reports corroborate the tern-first read directly: anglers new
  to tuna fishing are told to key on the white terns over pelicans and gulls,
  in 72°F water (`SczdZIq3UmE`).
- Terns are Cameron's best bird too — he will stop and cast on working terns,
  and offshore he follows terns to find fish even with no other birds working
  (cameron).

## Shearwaters

Cameron does not currently factor shearwaters into his own bird model (as of
2026-08) (cameron) — the corpus doctrine below fills that gap.

- **Rafted (sitting in a tight group)** = sitting over deep bait, waiting for
  the tide to bring it within diving range (`Blh2BA-7Ono`).
- **Drive-through test:** run the boat straight through a raft. Circling
  straight back = fish underneath; scattering and landing elsewhere = bait
  only (`Blh2BA-7Ono`).
- **Rafts hopscotching 20–30 ft at a time** = tracking fish holding deep
  (`Blh2BA-7Ono`).
- **Racing off low in one direction from a working boil** = the birds are
  running to the head of the school — follow them; the boil they left is the
  tail (`Blh2BA-7Ono`).

## Gulls

Unreliable — a gull is as happy fighting over fast-food scraps as it is
feeding on bait, so it will eat garbage, dead bait, or anything thrown
overboard (`Blh2BA-7Ono`). Only meaningful when locked in at an island or a
kelp edge with nothing better working (`Blh2BA-7Ono`). For Cameron, gulls are
mainly useful as [paddy-finders](kelp-paddies.md) — a general life/floating-
kelp indicator, not evidence of gamefish underneath (cameron).

## Pelicans

Worthless as a gamefish indicator — no observed case of a diving pelican
marking fish (`Blh2BA-7Ono`). Cameron calls them "dirty birds": diving
pelicans usually mean he is in the wrong area, and casting there typically
produces mackerel — a useful *bait* tell, not a gamefish one (cameron).

## Cormorants

**Diving *in kelp* = actively feeding = a bass indicator**, a better inshore
read than gulls (`Blh2BA-7Ono`). Cormorants normally perch on rocks with
wings spread rather than swim, so one diving in the kelp is doing something
it does not do by default — a mechanical tell that it is actually feeding,
not just present (`Blh2BA-7Ono`). Cameron previously wrote cormorants off as
bait stealers; he is now weighing them as a bait-depth indicator (cameron) —
an open item, not yet doctrine — which is the same read the corpus mechanism
above formalizes.

## Boobies

Mostly seen at the Coronado Islands; Cameron considers them worth paying
attention to (cameron). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: what booby behavior
distinguishes bait-only from fish-underneath, and no corpus doctrine on this
bird at all.

## Mixed piles and birds on the water

- **Mixed piles** (gulls + pelicans + terns + cormorants) **at open-water
  current breaks = bait only — skip** (`Blh2BA-7Ono`). **Exception:** a mixed
  pile **on kelp/structure** can be a real bait event worth working — a
  grunion blitz in the kelp produced bass on every cast in dirty water
  (`OYOda6T3f-8`).
- **Birds sitting still on the water still mark the zone.** Trolling past
  sitting birds, then hopscotching rafts, produced immediate bites with
  nothing flying (`HzE4FHHcvTk`).
- Riprap version: birds sitting on the rocks mark the
  [thumb-over-hose deviation](../locations/breakwalls-jetties-riprap.md#birds-mark-the-deviation)
  where current forced past a jetty/breakwall accelerates.

## Bird radar

On a T-top bird-mode radar, run gain near max and cap range at **4–5 mi** —
a shorter range gives a clean return on bird schools instead of drowning them
in clutter (`HWx1jDTGsng`). Bird schools paint as **red blotches**, sometimes
boat-sized (`HWx1jDTGsng`). That 4–5 mi span is the working detection
envelope to expect from bird-mode radar.

## Quick reference

| Bird | Behavior that means *fish* | Behavior that means *bait / skip* |
|---|---|---|
| **Terns** | moving dive-line; diving/working the flock | locked dives on one spot; off the fish and puddling |
| **Shearwaters** | circle back on drive-through; hopscotching rafts; racing low off a boil | rafted and scatter-off; no movement |
| **Gulls** | locked in at island/kelp edge (weakly) | anywhere else — "parking-lot" / paddy-finder |
| **Pelicans** | — (worthless) | diving = wrong area = mackerel water |
| **Cormorants** | diving *in kelp* = bass below | elsewhere |
| **Mixed piles** | on kelp/structure = possible bait event | open-water break = bait only, skip |

## How to use it in planning

- Glass for birds before running gear — they convert a search box into a
  specific stop faster than sonar; see
  [search and glassing](../planning/search-and-glassing.md) for range and
  method.
- Prioritize terns and shearwaters when choosing where to close in; treat
  gulls and pelicans as weak/negative signals unless nothing else is working.
- Set radar gain near max with range capped at 4–5 mi to pick up bird schools
  as red blotches before they are visible by eye.
- A locked/rafted read (terns hitting one spot, shearwaters rafted and
  scattering on a drive-through) means bait only — hold position or keep
  searching. A moving read (dive-line, drive-through circle-back,
  hopscotching, racing low) means fish are pushing that bait — close and cast
  now.
- On kelp/structure, a mixed pile is still worth a look; the same pile over
  open water at a current break is not.
