## Bait holds in the dirtier water; predators work the edge

- `HzE4FHHcvTk` (Erik Landesfeind, BDOutdoors, 2022-08-18, "Southern
  California Bight FISHING REPORT 08/17/22"; Avalon Bank / 152 Ridge): on a
  Saturday trip out of Newport for the Balboa Angling Club Lady Anglers
  tournament, marlin jigs were run on the edge of bait near Avalon Bank in
  water described as "dirtier" but "not super dirty" — as the boat trolled
  into the adjacent clear blue water where marlin would more likely live,
  the bait disappeared; narrator's read is that most of the bait that week
  was hanging in the off-color water rather than the clean blue.
- `HWx1jDTGsng` (Capt. Duane Diego Mellor, BDOutdoors, 2020-06-08, "Captain
  Duane Diego Talks San Diego Bluefin Tuna Fishing Tactics"; duane-diego-mellor
  registered voice — bluefin doctrine): names chalky water-color transition
  breaks as a key factor he looks for alongside temperature breaks; states
  bluefin have no fixed color preference — some days they sit in the
  dirtiest, coldest water, other days in clean blue/green water — and that
  the dirty water commonly holds the bait that draws them in.

## Lure brightness and bite depth by water clarity (coastal bass)

- `LqW32NSkObY` (Tommy Ponce, kayak guide, Dana Point, Fisher Newb channel,
  2019-03-13, "Fred Hall Show Seminars - Saltwater Bass Tactics (Calico Bass
  and Sand Bass)"; unregistered channel — medium confidence): close-range
  kayak/skiff doctrine for coastal calico/sand bass — murkier water calls
  for a brighter, higher-flash lure (blade-style, e.g. 1 oz+ spinnerbait);
  names rain runoff as the local driver of dirty water at Dana Point, and
  ties heavy-rain-muddied water to bass hunkering on bottom and largely off
  the bite, versus bright/clear/sunny days pulling bass up to feed on bait
  near the surface.
