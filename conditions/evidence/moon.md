# Evidence — Moon

Per-source provenance backing [Moon](../moon.md).

## Bite windows

- `XLVUhV8DW64` (BDOutdoors, Southern California Bight report, 2022-10-13):
  "if we've learned anything over the last six, seven years," days leading
  into the full moon read as the strongest bluefin window.
- `HnqiE05vdXs` (BDOutdoors, Southern California Bight report, 2022-09-01,
  reporting the 2022-08-31 new-moon night): the Aloha Spirit landed 22 fish
  (white seabass) on the new moon.
- `-JZpyWLdKlk` (BDOutdoors, Southern California Bight report, 2022-08-11,
  the "pink moon" full moon that night): the Mirage boat had 46 sea bass
  (white seabass) coming into the full moon, called "pretty much in line"
  with prior full-moon reports; the same report notes bluefin "went nuts"
  over the full moon and forecasts them dropping back off after it, and
  separately predicts a weaker bass weekend on the coming full moon.
- `Z3rZqy2Pi8E` (BDOutdoors, Southern California Bight report, 2022-11-24):
  a late-November bluefin bite window tied to going into the new moon, with
  the bite expected to carry into the following full moon in mid-December.
- `fri_BWI-VA0` (dave-hansen, Your Saltwater Guide, "Moon Phases and how It
  affects your fishing," 2022-02-12) and `LEiyB9QNzHY` (dave-hansen,
  re-uploaded/re-cut by BDOutdoors as "Offshore Fishing During Full Moon? Is
  The Fishing Any Good?", 2020-03-09): same captain's doctrine across two
  channels and dates — best window 7 days before and 7 days after the full
  moon, taught to him early in his career by captains he worked for; bluefin
  ("this stuff") "really gets going" in the post-peak week; the full moon
  peak itself is "not good," softened by "I've seen a bite in a full moon
  also."

## Post-moon relocation ≠ disappearance

- `Blh2BA-7Ono` (BDOutdoors, Southern California Bight report, 2022-07-21):
  open-water bluefin not holding hard structure can move 10-15 miles
  overnight, scattering boats that were on them because the search area
  balloons; long-range boats with time to re-search eventually reconnect.
- `fri_BWI-VA0` (dave-hansen, Your Saltwater Guide, 2022-02-12): puts the
  distance at 20 miles after one full-moon night, naming albacore, bluefin,
  and yellowfin as species he has seen do it; explains the mechanism as
  moonlight giving the fish confidence to travel at night without the
  daytime predation risk that normally keeps them from covering ground.
- `XLVUhV8DW64` (BDOutdoors, Southern California Bight report, 2022-10-13):
  after the October full moon, "the tuna are gone" reporting was fleet
  coverage loss, not an actual disappearance.
- `fri_BWI-VA0` (dave-hansen, Your Saltwater Guide, 2022-02-12): the morning
  after a full-moon night is typically slow because the school either
  hasn't relocated onto bait yet or fed all night and isn't inclined to
  feed hard again right away, with the bite picking back up late in the
  day; his practical guidance is to fish a full-moon day late rather than
  push the early-morning window.

## Full-moon daytime bass suppression

- `Blh2BA-7Ono` (BDOutdoors, Southern California Bight report, 2022-07-21):
  a Catalina trip coming off a full moon caught bass well early, went dead
  by roughly 9 am, and the crew left the island by 11 am; the same report
  states the general full-moon bass pattern (good early, slow for a big
  part of the day, not tied to moon visibility/cloud cover) and recommends
  not launching before noon-1 pm on a full moon if an early start isn't
  possible; separately notes a coastal sand bass slowdown that report
  attributes to a cold-water upwelling swell coincident with the same full
  moon, not to the moon itself.
- `-JZpyWLdKlk` (BDOutdoors, Southern California Bight report, 2022-08-11):
  forecasts a weaker bass weekend on the coming full moon ("they don't
  really seem to like that a lot").
- `5D1vx29LVpI` (BDOutdoors, Southern California Bight report, 2022-12-15):
  a winter full-moon trip out of Alamitos found bass marking well on
  numerous spots but refusing a variety of dropped lures; stated mechanism
  is that the fish gorged all night on a clear full moon and weren't hungry
  during the day, so the crew switched to working reaction bites (small
  crankbaits worked as a trigger) rather than fishing to a feeding fish.

## Kelp paddies restock only after dark

- `4xzK7YaXK5s` (source shared with [kelp paddies](../kelp-paddies.md)):
  paddy-to-paddy fish movement happens only at night; a paddy dry during
  the day stays dry until dark regardless of soak time.

## Lobster: darkest, no-moon nights are best

- `fri_BWI-VA0` (dave-hansen, Your Saltwater Guide, 2022-02-12): stated
  mechanism is that lobster are nocturnal because "everything wants to eat
  a lobster," and a bright moon reads to them the same as daylight, so they
  travel and crawl less. Best conditions named are the darkest, no-moon
  nights, including rough/rainy/stormy dark nights ("the dark, dark,
  darkest nights are the best"), with sea state overriding the moon on
  safety grounds. Self-reported record: "never done very good during a full
  moon" hoop netting personally, but has caught lobster on one, and knows
  others who caught "lots of lobsters" on a full moon too.

## Arrival events

- `Blh2BA-7Ono` (BDOutdoors, Southern California Bight report, 2022-07-21):
  a new wave of bluefin was reported arriving "out of the west" on the
  moon into the Ensenada zone, described as distinct from the fish already
  holding in U.S. waters.
