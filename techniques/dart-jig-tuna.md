# Dart Jig — Daytime Finesse Tuna

A castable, daytime-only presentation for [bluefin](../species/bluefin-tuna.md)
and [yellowfin tuna](../species/yellowfin-tuna.md) keyed on small bait and
refusing the bigger, "crazy looking" lures fished at night: free-spool the
sink and crank hard the instant it stops (`TU5quAG4atM`). There's no bottom
under tuna, so a jig that stops sinking means a bite, not a bottom mark
(`TU5quAG4atM`). Distinct from [yo-yo iron](yo-yo-iron.md) — the boat's drift,
not a structure mark, sets the drop angle, and the lure class is lighter and
specifically castable — and from [foamer casting](foamer-casting.md), which
targets fish actively breaking on top rather than fish marked or suspected
around the boat and biting poorly.

## Reach for this when

- Tuna are keyed on **small bait** and refusing bigger presentations — the
  lure class this note covers runs roughly **50 g up to 100 g, maybe 125 g
  at the most**, sized well below night-bite jig weight for that reason
  (`TU5quAG4atM`).
- It's **daytime** specifically — the finesse counterpart to the bigger
  lures thrown after dark (`TU5quAG4atM`).
- Fish are **around the boat but not biting well** — one of the most
  effective ways to connect on a day when the fish are around the boat and
  not biting well, for bluefin and yellowfin later in the SoCal season
  (`TU5quAG4atM`).
- **You have to be able to cast** for this to fish effectively — it's a
  jig-stick/casting presentation, not a straight-down drop (`TU5quAG4atM`).

## Mechanics

Two ways to work it end in the same rule: free-spool the sink, then engage
the reel and crank hard and fast the instant it stops. There's no bottom
under tuna, so a jig that stops sinking didn't hit bottom — a tuna took
it — and the window to come tight is only a few seconds because "these don't
taste very good" and get spit fast (`TU5quAG4atM`). Don't set the hook like a
TV angler; putting the reel in gear and cranking hard and fast is what comes
tight (`TU5quAG4atM`).

- **Windy-side drop.** Walk to the windward side of the boat (typically the
  bow), free-spool the reel, and let the jig sink as the wind pushes the boat
  off the sinking line. Manage the drop so it tracks as straight down as
  possible despite the boat sliding away from it, and always mark the line
  (`TU5quAG4atM`). Past about **300 ft**, wind back rather than let it keep
  dropping: tuck the rod under the left armpit, point the tip down, and wind
  fast — winding up to roughly **50 ft** down saves energy before dropping it
  again, no need to wind all the way to the surface (`TU5quAG4atM`). As the
  boat drifts further from the drop point, reposition (wind in and reset) to
  keep the line running vertical instead of swinging out at an angle
  (`TU5quAG4atM`).
- **Bow-casting technique.** From the bow, cast at an angle out in the
  direction the boat is drifting — no need for a long cast — and let it sink
  in free-spool; roughly **40% of all bites come on the sink**, so stay
  attentive through the drop (`TU5quAG4atM`). Timed right, the jig runs close
  to straight down and sits around **250–300 ft** by the time the line
  crosses the boat's anchor stanchion; engage the reel and wind back fast,
  only about halfway to the boat. No bite: free-spool it and let it sink
  again. Move to the windy-side rail as the boat continues to drift and
  repeat the sink/wind-halfway cycle from there; on the next drop (now
  angling out the other side), wind all the way back to the boat and restart
  the cycle from the bow — a continuous bow → windy rail → full retrieve loop
  while the boat drifts (`TU5quAG4atM`).

## Sizing, color, and depth

An earlier citation of the same lure class names its own sizing, color, and
depth parameters — sitting beside, not replacing, the casting mechanics above
(`m2g97MxmAGI`; provenance and confidence-tier detail in the
[evidence file](evidence/dart-jig-tuna.md)):

- **Match the jig to the size of the bait the fish are actually eating** —
  the whole "trick" to this presentation. Carry two sizes: the larger (about
  as large as this jig class ever gets fished, rigged sometimes with a treble
  hook, sometimes a single hook) and a smaller size that is often the
  **only** one that draws a bite (`m2g97MxmAGI`).
- **Color matters less than size** for this jig class (`m2g97MxmAGI`) —
  distinct from, and not merged into, the forage-matched color doctrine
  already logged for [iron jigs](../lures/iron-jigs.md) and [surface iron —
  color](surface-iron-color.md), which covers a different lure class.
- **Drop it to whatever depth the captain calls** — examples given: **80 ft,
  100 ft, or 150 ft** — so the jig sits at the same depth as the bait and the
  tuna, rather than being worked near the surface; it then blends in with
  the other bait fish at that depth (`m2g97MxmAGI`). No casting or
  drift-angle mechanic is given in this citation, unlike the
  windy-side/bow-cast mechanics above.

## Gear class

The lure is the **"Dart" jig / "Colt Sniper"-class jig** documented at
[iron jigs](../lures/iron-jigs.md); full rod/reel/line/leader/hook builds are
tackle-level detail kept in the linked rig-ladder notes rather than
duplicated here.

- **Light rig:** the primary outfit runs a Penn Fathom 30 reel, 65 lb
  braid topped with a 50 lb monofilament top shot, speed/gearing not stated
  on camera (`TU5quAG4atM`); full build in [Searcher big/giant tuna rig
  ladder](../tackle/searcher-big-tuna-rig-ladder.md#dartcolt-sniper-finesse-jig-rig--daytime-tuna-steve-carson-pennsearchersportfishing-2024-06-13).
  An earlier, independently corroborating citation of this same outfit names
  a **Penn Fathom II 30, two-speed**, on an **8 ft rod rated 30–60 lb test**
  ("Carnage Three" West Coast rod — brand/model asr-uncertain) — the same
  rod-length/casting-weight window as the [surface-iron/jig-stick
  class](../tackle/gear-classes.md#rod--reel-classes), but two-speed, unlike
  that class's standard single-speed pairing. Full parameters in [Searcher
  daytime dart-jig outfit
  ladder](../tackle/searcher-daytime-dart-jig-outfit-ladder.md)
  (`pCd6QykcZ0w`). The same video names bluefin, yellowfin,
  yellowtail, and occasionally [opah](../species/opah.md) as catches on this
  presentation (`TU5quAG4atM`).
- **Heavy rig — bigger/deeper daytime fish (100 lb-plus):** Penn Fathom 40
  Narrow, 6.5–7 ft rod rated 30–80 lb, 80 lb braid topped with a 60 lb top
  shot bumped to 80 lb once fish run over 100 lb — closer to the
  [rail-rod/heavy-tuna class](../tackle/gear-classes.md#rod--reel-classes).

## Common failures

- **Setting the hook instead of cranking through it** — with a tuna, the cure
  for a drop that suddenly stops is to engage the reel and grind, not strike
  like a bass angler; a TV-style hookset wastes the few seconds before the
  fish spits the jig (`TU5quAG4atM`).
- **Fishing it straight down instead of managing the drift angle** — both
  variants depend on keeping the sink tracking vertical (windy-side) or
  timed to the boat's drift (bow cast); a drop that swings out sideways
  fishes a different, less-vertical part of the column than intended
  (`TU5quAG4atM`).
- **Undersized hooks** — stock hooks on these lures are not tuna-rated (thin,
  weak); see the hook swap in [iron jigs](../lures/iron-jigs.md#hook-style--assist-vs-treble)
  and the rig-ladder links above (`TU5quAG4atM`).
