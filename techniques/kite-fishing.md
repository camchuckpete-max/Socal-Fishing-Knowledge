# Kite Fishing

Execution for flying a rigged flying fish on the surface under a kite — the
standard SoCal method for the big bluefin grade (**100 to 300 lb** fish). The
kite holds the bait's [harness](../rigging/flying-fish-harness.md) up off the
water so only the flyer touches down, skipping and splashing like a fleeing
flying fish. Where/when to reach for it, and how it slots against the other
bluefin approaches, is the router: [bluefin tuna](../species/bluefin-tuna.md).

## Reach for this when

- There is **enough wind to fly a kite**, and bluefin in the 100–300 lb class
  are up top (foamers or high sounder marks) — the highest-percentage way to
  convert that grade. See [bluefin tuna](../species/bluefin-tuna.md).
- When the wind won't fly a kite, cover water and pull bites with
  [speed trolling](speed-trolling.md) instead.

## Deploying the kite

The kite flies on its own dedicated line, separate from the fishing line. A
ring tied onto the fishing line ahead of the terminal tackle carries a rubber
band running to the kite line's release clip — no knot joins the fishing line
directly to the clip, so the rubber band is the weak link that lets the
fishing line pop free of the kite line on the bite (`Ix0gG0-l3v0`). Full
kite-line build detail (the quick-release, payout control, and boat-handling
around it) is in the [evidence file](evidence/kite-fishing.md).

Fly the kite as high as the crew can still see the flying fish skipping on
the surface — visibility, not a fixed height, sets the ceiling
(`rsCAh-QyK60`). Adjust the clip and kite so the flyer just touches the
surface: it should skip and splash on top, never drag under and never fly
clear of the water. Flying the bait through the air like the kite itself,
rather than skipping wave-to-wave the entire time, is a common mistake — the
fish are in the water, not the sky, so the bait has to stay in constant
contact with the surface to draw a strike (`JiJYamo0QHk`). That same
splashing contact reproduces the **~8.5 kt** trolled-flyer speed logged in
[flying fish harness](../rigging/flying-fish-harness.md#artificial-alternative--durable-rubber-flyer-for-trolling)
— a second data point for that figure (`JiJYamo0QHk`).

Keep the bite leader **short** so a hooked fish can be gaffed once it's
fought to the swivel — see [the bite and the gap](#the-bite-and-the-gap)
below; the harness note carries the leader-length and wing-angle numbers.

## Bait and rig alternatives

- The default rig is a pre-rigged dead [flying fish](../rigging/flying-fish-harness.md)
  on its harness.
- A **live mackerel, back/dorsal-hooked**, is a simpler live-bait alternative
  for the same 100–300 lb bluefin grade; yellowtail and calico bass have also
  eaten a mackerel fished this way off the kite. Hook position and
  mackerel-selection detail: [fishing live
  bait](../bait/fishing-live-bait.md#mackerel-butt-hook-no-weight-vs-nose-hook-any-weight-vs-backdorsal-hook-kite-only)
  (`w5_x6kkN-xE`).
- For giant-grade tuna, whole **squid** rigged on a **double-hook rig** is
  also fished under the kite — left to drift with the current and wind,
  presented as almost unresisted on the take (`pk2blktDQ9Y`).
- One report adds a **2–4 oz [sliding sinker](sliding-sinker.md)** ahead of a
  kite-flown bait, on the reasoning (secondhand, from other anglers) that it
  keeps the bait in the water better; offered as untested by the speaker's
  own account, and it stands unreconciled against the bare-flyer dapping
  doctrine above (`jH0q4UPjwC0`).

Full rigging and session detail for each of these is in the [evidence
file](evidence/kite-fishing.md).

## Trolled rubber flyer vs. dead-boat dead flyer (a wind-driven choice)

- **~8 kt of wind or more — troll a rubber flyer.** A durable rubber
  flying-fish lure (the "Yummy Flyer") is dragged under the kite at **~8.5
  kt** instead of sat dead-boat. Trolling covers more water while searching
  and converts a bite to a hookup at a better ratio than dead-boat fishing
  does (source states the outcome, not the mechanism). Once trolling raises a
  school, mark it on the GPS and keep working that spot with the rubber flyer
  (`2y0VznL2qk8`).
- **Under ~8 kt of wind, with fish already located** (surface sign, or heavy
  marks on the fish finder) **— go dead-boat with a dead flyer instead.**
  Stop trolling and deploy (typically **two**) dead flying fish under the
  kite, or under a balloon, and drift them like float fishing. Never deploy a
  dead flyer speculatively with no fish around; it's fished over confirmed
  sign, not blind water (`2y0VznL2qk8`).
- **Deploying the dead flyer:** drive forward a little to open distance
  between the boat and the bait, then lower the flyer slowly onto the
  surface. Keep it **dapping** — constantly in contact with the water,
  bouncing with the swell, never fully submerged and never airborne clear of
  the surface (`2y0VznL2qk8`).
- **Hookset on a dead-boat bite:** the take shows as a surface explosion on
  the flyer. Put the boat in gear and drive **forward gently — about 4–5 kt,
  not a hard throttle** — to keep the rod bent and constant pressure on the
  fish as it takes off, driving the hook home without shock-loading it
  (`2y0VznL2qk8`).
- A rubber flyer suspended under a small balloon instead of a kite has also
  taken bluefin, worked dapping/skipping rather than retrieved; this report
  doesn't say whether a kite was up too, so it doesn't resolve whether the
  balloon adds lift or is only a strike marker (`wYeKJLoKo4g`). A separate
  report flew a balloon together with the kite itself, in fishable wind, not
  as a light-wind fix — the same open question, still not settled either way
  (`usHl-4SfqDA`).

**A different lure, a different threshold direction, than the Mad Mac
speed-troll numbers:** this is a different lure (rubber flyer vs. Mad Mac) at a different troll
speed (8.5 kt vs. 13–15 kt), and the same ~8 kt wind mark cuts the opposite
way in each note. Here, inside the range where the kite still flies, 8 kt is
the threshold between trolling to search and dead-boating over confirmed
fish. In [bluefin trolling](../species/bluefin-tuna-trolling.md), the same
mark is the threshold *below which the kite won't fly at all* — that failure
is what triggers the switch to speed trolling as a different technique, not
a variant of this one.

## The bite and the gap

- On the eat, the line **pops out of the release clip** and the angler comes
  tight directly to the fish — no clip in the fighting system.
- Lay the short leader out flat on the surface to bring the fish into gaff
  range at the swivel; a leader run too long stops the fish short of the
  gaff.
- A kite-hooked 100 lb+ bluefin has been fought **40–45 minutes** before
  landing, in a session that also converted fish on flyline gear fished
  alongside the kite in the same window — kite and flyline aren't mutually
  exclusive presentations in one spread (`zBd1mayUt_I`).

## Wind window

- The kite needs wind to stay aloft. Marginal, gusty, or dying wind makes the
  flyer inconsistent on the surface — that is the cue to switch to
  [speed trolling](speed-trolling.md), which needs no wind.
- **Helium-balloon assist** is the general fix for holding a kite up in light
  wind: the balloon twists into a dedicated clip with no knot, the clip
  rubber-bands to the kite, and a taped fishing-line tether backs up that
  rubber band so a failure leaves the balloon and kite cradled together
  instead of lost (`vCskOx6N-XM`). **Cameron's panga program rules helium
  out** — too expensive and no room to carry a tank on the boat — a platform
  constraint of his setup, not a rule of the fishery; on a bigger boat with
  room for a tank, the light-wind window reopens (cameron).

## Gear class

- A **heavy fighting-rod class** — these are 100–300 lb fish and the rod is
  pure leverage, not a casting tool. Rail rods built for this program run
  **7 ft 6 in, rated 80–200 lb-plus (unlimited class)**, so a big fish can be
  fought off the rail without breaking the rod down (`RM7cBgCDWFA`). See
  [gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md) — the nearest documented row is
  the rail-rod/heavy-tuna class, but kite outfits run heavier than that
  row's 60–130 lb baseline.
- **Reel:** a two-speed conventional. Dedicated boat kite programs typically
  field **50-class** reels — **Accurate ATD 50** or twin-drag reels and a
  **Shimano Tiagra 50 Wide** — against a lighter, rental-grade **Shimano
  Talica 25** (25-class, spooled with 80 lb line) also offered as a
  kite/fighting outfit (`fgTmUq78ofQ`, `lf3S28nh-kk`, `RM7cBgCDWFA`,
  `shZCjX2-fkI`). A personal kite outfit, if not using the boat's own, wants
  at minimum an **80-lb-class two-speed reel** on a big rail rod
  (`shZCjX2-fkI`).
- **Main line and top shot:** reported spools run **130–200 lb braid or
  spectra**, either tied straight to a heavy mono leader or joined to a
  **~15 ft top shot of 130–200 lb mono**; a personal outfit's stated floor is
  **100 lb** top shot (`RM7cBgCDWFA`, `fgTmUq78ofQ`, `lf3S28nh-kk`).
- **Bite leader:** heavy fluorocarbon, **200 lb-plus, 400 lb common**, with a
  big J hook up front and a strong treble stinger on the tail — construction
  lives in [flying fish harness](../rigging/flying-fish-harness.md); a
  **12/0 stainless Mustad** J plus treble stinger is one documented spec
  (`fgTmUq78ofQ`).
- Reel picks reported to split on **budget, not capability** — a higher tier
  (Accurate, Shimano Talica) against a workhorse tier (Penn International,
  Shimano Tiagra); all named reels are called adequate for the job
  (`shZCjX2-fkI`).

Full per-outfit specs (exact reel/rod pairings, knot names, swivel ratings)
are in the [evidence file](evidence/kite-fishing.md).

## Common failures

- **Wings set too far out** on the flyer — a swell knocks the bait off and
  ruins the presentation. Rig the wings tighter (harness note).
- **Leader too long** — the fish hits the swivel before it's in gaff range.
- Letting the flyer **sink or drag** instead of skipping — the splashing
  surface action is the whole trigger.
