# Sliding Sinker

Execution for the sliding-sinker (Carolina) rig: the **weight rides free on the
main line above a swivel, and a leader runs from the swivel to the hook**, so a
fish can pick up the bait and swim off **without feeling the lead**. The line
slides through the sinker — the resistance the fish would otherwise feel is
gone, which is what makes it deadly on wary, soft-bottom feeders. Where these
fish sit is the router; this note owns the rig and how you feed the bite.

## Reach for this when

- **Sand or soft bottom** where the bait should waft naturally just off the
  deck — [California halibut](../species/california-halibut.md) is the headline
  target; also [white seabass](../species/white-seabass.md) and
  [yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md) on the drift or the anchor.
- **Sheephead and other structure/artificial-reef fish over a chum line** — cap
  the slider at **1/4-1/2 oz** (never heavier) and suspend the bait off bottom
  rather than laying it on the deck: sheephead feed with a forward-facing mouth,
  and a bottomed bait draws rays and sharks instead (`OSbAHdB4uPs`). See
  [species/sheephead.md](../species/sheephead.md).
- The fish are **wary** and drop a bait the instant they feel weight — the
  free-sliding lead removes that tell.
- You are **drifting bait** and want the presentation pinned near bottom while
  it still moves with the current.

## Building the rig

- **Egg sinker → bead → barrel swivel → leader → hook.** The bead protects the
  knot from the sliding sinker and the swivel stops the weight; the leader length
  sets how far the bait rides behind and above the lead — run it long enough that
  the bait moves freely, short enough to stay in the strike zone.
- Match the sinker to the drift and the depth — just heavy enough to tick bottom.
  A fish that grabs the bait **pulls line through the sinker and feels nothing**;
  that is the whole point of the rig.

**A long-leader, no-cast build** (`O49WsHP4Zh0`). No species or location is
stated; water depth at the time of filming was about **9 ft**, with no place
named.

- **Leader: 4, 4½, or 5 ft** — longer than the general guidance above; the
  presenter calls it **"way too long for most people"** (`O49WsHP4Zh0`).
- **Drag: kept loose**, "not too pinched down" — looser than the locked-down
  drag on the Lopez Mateos snook/grouper build below (`O49WsHP4Zh0`).
- **Deployment: no cast.** Flop the bait out alongside the boat and let the
  boat's own movement carry it out, rather than casting (`O49WsHP4Zh0`).

## The squid version

For Channel Islands squid fishing, run a small sliding egg sinker straight to a
Gamakatsu offset octopus hook, sinker preferably **glow color** — it matches
the squid and adds a light attractant on the common **night** bite
(`apyGy3XKlss`).

- Rig the squid through the top of the mantle: slide it up onto the barb and
  shank, pop the point out the back, then slide the mantle over the eye and
  knot to lock it in place. **Fish two** squid — it mimics the spawning ball
  the fish are there to feed on (`apyGy3XKlss`).
- On the water, the egg sinker bounces off the bottom and the bait wafts
  behind it in the current, which triggers bites — strong on both the anchor
  and the drift (`apyGy3XKlss`).

## Baja, Sea of Cortez grouper leader

A grouper/cabrilla bottom rig built on this note's sliding-sinker format (egg
sinker riding free above a swivel, leader down to the hook) rather than a
dropper loop (`M8hOYQ_6rSg`):

- **Sinkers:** carry at least 8 oz, going to a minimum of 16 oz, heavier still
  if the current calls for it — consistent with the 16-24 oz Baja weight range
  logged on [dropper loop](dropper-loop.md) from a different source/trip (that
  entry never confirms its own rig format). **Sliding egg sinkers are
  preferred over torpedo sinkers** for grouper here; sometimes two or three
  8 oz egg sliders are run together rather than stepping up to one bigger
  sinker. A **torpedo sinker (16-20 oz) on a short ~6 in breakaway** is the
  fallback (`M8hOYQ_6rSg`).
- **Leader:** about 6 ft long — no need to run it longer. **300-400 lb**
  leader material, a swivel on one end (a 350 lb-rated ball-bearing swivel in
  this build), a big hook on the other — a **12/0-class** hook (asr-uncertain:
  captions render this "12 oz hook," almost certainly a mishearing of
  "12 aught" given hooks are sized in aught throughout; the brand is spoken
  clearly, twice, as "Jobu," carried at low confidence per the sponsored-claim
  caveat). The sliding egg sinker rides up against the swivel (`M8hOYQ_6rSg`).
- **Why 6 ft and not longer:** running straight braid to a grouper leader is
  the failure mode — hook a grouper and it runs for a rock, gets past a short
  leader, and cuts you off on the rocks. The fix is **not** a longer bite
  leader; it's a **long mono top shot above the leader** — minimum 30 yards,
  ideally at least 100 ft of 100-130 lb mono — so the abrasion-and-stretch
  section that has to survive rock contact is the mono top shot, not the
  fluoro/heavy-mono bite leader itself. **Mono over fluoro** is preferred here
  specifically for the stretch, though fluoro is fishable if that's what's on
  hand (`M8hOYQ_6rSg`).
- **Crimp stiff, don't snell** the hook: run the tag through one side of the
  eye, wrap around the shank a couple of times, then crimp through the
  opposite side — keeps the hook rigid so it can't pivot when a grouper hits,
  rather than folding over the way a snelled hook can. The presenter has a
  hard time snelling at 300 lb test and crimps for that reason; a shop can tie
  a stiff snell instead if you don't crimp your own (`M8hOYQ_6rSg`).
- **Working the bottom:** drop to bottom, then wind up **three or four turns**
  so the bait sits roughly **10 ft off bottom** rather than dragging and
  snagging — most Sea of Cortez rigs are fished this way rather than
  bait-on-bottom (`M8hOYQ_6rSg`).
- **Alternate rig for long/live bait:** see the [Baja grouper trap-hook
  variant](../rigging/trap-rig.md#baja-sea-of-cortez-grouper-variant) — nose +
  mid-body hook — for small yellowtail or barracuda live bait, used when a
  sub-100 lb grouper is grabbing the tail rather than swallowing a whole bait.

## San Diego local/long-range yellowtail — 2-4 oz, no leader shown

Framed as "one of the favorite ways to fish for yellowtail with live bait on
the Searcher," for yellowtail in the **12-22 lb** range (`yGXSrUauo2w`):

- **Egg sinker sliding straight down to the hook — no bead, swivel, or
  stopper shown or described.** A leaner, single-piece build than this note's
  general Carolina-rig format above (egg sinker → bead → swivel → leader →
  hook) (`yGXSrUauo2w`).
- **Weight: 2, 3, or 4 oz** (asr-uncertain: a garbled fragment just before
  these figures may indicate a lighter ~1 oz option was also shown — too
  unclear to add as a fourth number), scaled up for more wind or deeper fish,
  and adjusted through the day as the bite changes. Above 4 oz, a different
  rig is preferred (not detailed on camera). Notably heavier than the
  ~1/4-1/2 oz slider weights other SoCal/Baja island trips have produced
  yellowtail on (see [evidence](evidence/sliding-sinker.md)) — a San Diego
  local/long-range dockside briefing rather than a single-trip observation
  (`yGXSrUauo2w`).
- **Reach for this over a flylined bait** to get the bait away from the boat,
  and — when bonita or similar bycatch are working the surface — down below
  them and in front of the yellowtail more quickly (`yGXSrUauo2w`).
- **Hook: J hook, not circle**, roughly 2/0 up to 4/0 for a larger bait — see
  [hooks](../tackle/hooks.md#circle-vs-j--the-core-decision-swrsycmt4tw-m2q22sppkem)
  for the full circle-vs-J doctrine this corroborates (`yGXSrUauo2w`). ⚠
  Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the transcript's auto captions render
  the sizes as "about a 20... to maybe a 40," not "2/0"/"4/0" — the same
  aught-vs-digit ASR ambiguity already caveated for the 12/0 grouper hook

## Fishing it — feed the bite

Let the sinker settle to the bottom and keep it in light contact. When a fish
loads up, **let it take** — because the line runs through the lead, you can
give slack and let the fish turn before you come tight, rather than pulling
against a weight it can feel.

## Bluefin — slow drop, slow wind, more time in the strike zone

For bluefin, when not flying a [kite](kite-fishing.md), a sinker rig worked
slowly through the whole water column on both ends of the presentation, not
soaked or fished fast (`46ha7J1Fc6A`):

- **Drop the bait down nice and slow**, deliberately passing the fish at
  every depth on the way down rather than free-spooling straight to bottom —
  the stated reason is not missing fish that are holding somewhere other than
  where you expect (`46ha7J1Fc6A`).
- **Wind it back up on a very slow, steady retrieve** rather than a fast
  crank — the slow wind makes the bait **"look as natural as possible"**
  through the retrieve (`46ha7J1Fc6A`).
- Working both the drop and the retrieve slowly gives the bait **more total
  time in the strike zone** on every single presentation, tied directly to a
  better chance at a bite (`46ha7J1Fc6A`).

Unlike the deadstick default in *Fishing it* above, this actively works the
bait on both the drop and the retrieve — reach for it when the fish's depth
or grade is uncertain, not when they're pinned to the bottom.

## Tangle prevention — rail-rod rental rig

Demonstrated on a Fisherman's Landing rental outfit — **100 lb test**
monofilament main line, a **two-speed reel**, and a **rail rod** (a graphite
Calstar rated **60-120 lb**) — "the outfit you would use with a sinker rig"
(`zkA1jqHXXD0`). Hook, leader, and connection-knot build are in [essential
SoCal knots — surgeon's knot, mono-to-fluoro
connection](../rigging/essential-knots.md#when-to-use-it); the
tangle-cause/fix content below is this video's actual subject.

- **Sinker rides above the leader/main-line connection, not near the hook** —
  it slides down the main line until it hits the knot joining it to the
  fluorocarbon leader, and stops there, which keeps it from sliding all the
  way down onto the hook (`zkA1jqHXXD0`).
- **Tangle diagnosis** (stated as a guess, not confirmed on camera): if the
  sinker rig comes up tangled around the main line, the likely cause is
  **letting the rig down too quickly** — free-spooling it straight down
  rather than feeding it out at a controlled speed (`zkA1jqHXXD0`).
- **Fix:** let the rig down a little slower, and measured to the correct
  depth, rather than in full free-spool (`zkA1jqHXXD0`).
- **Bait hooking is the other half of the fix: nose-hook the bait.** A bait
  that's belly-, collar-, or shoulder-hooked won't sink as easily on this rig
  and will probably pull off — an independent citation of the
  any-weighted-rig → nose-hook doctrine already logged in [fishing live
  bait](../bait/fishing-live-bait.md#hook-position-by-situation) from other
  sources, here attributed to "won't sink as easily / will pull off" rather
  than the "unnatural silhouette dragged backward" mechanism given elsewhere
  (`zkA1jqHXXD0`).

## Gear class

[30 lb bait class](../tackle/gear-classes.md) is the SoCal default — enough
backbone to feel a bottom bite through a free-sliding weight without
overpowering the light bait presentation. Sand-bass/finesse work drops to the
[finesse fly-line class](../tackle/gear-classes.md). Baja Pacific grouper and
snook builds move up to the [Baja Pacific grouper/snook bottom
class](../tackle/gear-classes.md) — reel, line and leader specs on
[snook — sliding sinker](../species/snook-sliding-sinker.md). Octopus offset
hooks by default; often run alongside soaking [dropper-loop](dropper-loop.md)
rods.

## Common failures

- **Leader too short** — the bait sits on top of the weight instead of wafting,
  and the fish feels the lead.
- **Too much weight** — drags out of the zone in current and telegraphs
  resistance; use the least lead that holds bottom.
- **Coming tight too soon** — the rig's advantage is the free take; don't rush
  the hookset and undo it.
