# Bait-and-Switch (Striped Marlin)

The sighted-fish/raised-fish play for [striped marlin](../species/striped-marlin.md):
raise a fish on the trolled spread, then feed it a live bait — a live dropback
**mackerel** in the SoCal tanker-lanes/offshore-bank fishery. A same-trip Baja
comparison rates the dropback over a trolled-lure strike for hookup
reliability (`Mwx5AAXNMvE`). This note owns the tease/switch mechanics and the
dropback bait execution; teaser handling and hookset specifics are flagged
gaps below.

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-internal): this note's front-matter `regions`
carries `cortez-north`, which [striped marlin](../species/striped-marlin.md) —
the only species this note serves, and the router it hangs off — does not
(`socal-bight`, `baja-pacific-north`, `baja-pacific-south`, `cortez-south`).
Both of this note's sources are SoCal (`3qSY328fFYo`) and Cabo
(`Mwx5AAXNMvE`), so nothing here supports the wider gate, and a northern
Sea-of-Cortez plan can surface this play for a species the router gates out of
that region. Flagged here only — the router's narrower set is the supported
one.

## Reach for this when

- A **tailing / finning marlin is sighted** — the single highest-value marlin
  encounter in SoCal; seconds matter.
- The trolled spread raises ("lights up") a fish that won't eat the lures —
  including a fish that's just sleeping, tailing, or slashing/showing up
  behind the spread without ever touching a jig.

## Method skeleton (unsourced — carried from the initial build)

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: this tease/switch skeleton was written into
the marlin router at the initial build with no source; treat it as unverified
until a dedicated teaser/pitch clip lands.

- **Tease:** keep the trolled spread swimming — the raised fish is committed to
  the teaser, not to any hook.
- **Switch:** **pitch a live bait** to the raised fish; the pitched bait is
  what it eats. For a tailer spotted off the spread, **cast the bait ahead of
  the fish's line of travel**.

## Dropback bait execution

Keep a live **green-back mackerel** rigged and ready as the dropback bait
through the whole troll (`3qSY328fFYo`). The instant a marlin shows anywhere
around the spread — even without a confirmed jig strike — get the dropback
bait in the water as fast as possible rather than waiting to confirm the hit
first (`3qSY328fFYo`). Leave the boat in gear while feeding the bait back
instead of stopping to pitch (`3qSY328fFYo`).

If the bait is already staged on an outrigger clip, pull it and throw it off
to the side of the boat, working it back toward where the jig is running —
the goal is to pull the fish off the jig and onto the bait. Hold the rod tip
up so you can see where the line enters the water and place the bait right in
front of the fish (`3qSY328fFYo`). The play is reactive, not scripted: react
the instant a rigger goes off rather than following a pre-set plan
(`3qSY328fFYo`) — trip detail is in the
[evidence file](evidence/bait-and-switch.md).

**Hook placement.** Hooking the mackerel through the nose is common, but the
hook can turn under load and pull back out; hooking it higher keeps it pinned
better, especially on an aggressive cast needed to reach the fish
(`3qSY328fFYo`) — a single captain's stated preference, not tested
head-to-head on camera, kept alongside the nose-hook default in
[flyline — hook position by situation](flyline.md#hook-position-by-situation).

## Gear class

- [Flyline / live-bait class](../tackle/gear-classes.md) rod rigged and ready
  to pitch the instant a fish lights up — the pitch rod sits ready the whole
  troll; heavy leader per [wind-on leader](../rigging/wind-on-leader.md).
- A Baja/Cabo data point ties the dropback rig to **80 lb "Soft Steel Ultra"
  monofilament** and a **#9 circle hook** (`Mwx5AAXNMvE`) — sponsor-named
  line, region-labeled rather than treated as the SoCal class default.
  ⚠ Fact-check (single-source): this exact line/hook pairing comes from one
  Baja/Cabo trip only, not corroborated elsewhere in the corpus.

## Common failures

- **Missing the raise entirely.** A fish can whack a jig without popping the
  outrigger clip or the rubber band, so a moment's inattention on the spread
  reads as a kelp snag instead of a shot at a fish — costs four or five fish
  over a day (`3qSY328fFYo`).
- **Low conversion is normal, not a sign of an error.** Even with the play run
  correctly, one experienced captain put raised-fish-to-hookup at roughly one
  in five or six (`3qSY328fFYo`).
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): `3qSY328fFYo` scopes this figure to
  jig-strike conversion — "to get a jig fish is usually get one out of five
  or six if you're lucky" — not a raised-fish-to-bait-and-switch-hookup rate,
  and drops the source's own hedge ("if you're lucky").
- **A hooked fish can still shake free.** Same session, a second angler's
  marlin came unbuttoned on a trolled teaser lure shortly after the strike
  (`3qSY328fFYo`).
