# Bait-and-Switch (Striped Marlin)

The sighted-fish/raised-fish play for [striped marlin](../species/striped-marlin.md):
raise the fish on the trolled spread, then feed it a live bait — a live
**dropback mackerel** in the SoCal tanker-lanes/offshore-bank fishery. The
core teaser/switch mechanics below are still an unsourced skeleton carried
from the initial build (per the router rule, a method real in the fishery
must not be silent); the **dropback bait execution** section is now sourced
from an on-the-water SoCal marlin trip. Remaining gaps are flagged.

## Reach for this when

- A **tailing / finning marlin is sighted** — the single highest-value marlin
  encounter in SoCal; seconds matter.
- The trolled spread raises ("lights up") a fish that won't eat the lures —
  including a fish that's just sleeping, tailing, or slashing/showing up
  behind the spread without ever touching a jig.

## Method skeleton (unsourced — carried from the initial build)

**Provenance warning:** this skeleton was written into the marlin router at
the initial build without a corpus source. Treat the two lines below as
unverified until a dedicated teaser/pitch source lands.

- **Tease:** keep the trolled spread swimming — the raised fish is committed to
  the teaser, not to any hook.
- **Switch:** **pitch a live bait** to the raised fish; the pitched bait is
  what it eats. For a tailer spotted off the spread, **cast the bait ahead of
  the fish's line of travel**.

## Dropback bait execution

**Observed** (Jimmy Decker via StokedOnFishing, 2014-11-08, Catalina tanker
lanes/277 bank — `3qSY328fFYo`; unregistered channel — medium confidence):

- **Bait:** keep a live **green-back mackerel** rigged and ready as the
  dropback bait through the whole troll.
- **Trigger:** the instant a marlin shows anywhere around the spread — even
  if you never actually see it hit a jig — get the dropback bait in the water
  as fast as possible; don't wait to confirm a jig strike first.
- **Boat handling:** leave the boat in gear while feeding the bait back
  (don't stop to pitch).
- **Delivery:** if the bait is already staged on an outrigger clip, pull it
  and throw it off to the side of the boat, working it back toward where the
  jig is running — the goal is to pull the fish off the jig and onto the
  bait. Hold the rod tip up so you can see where the line is entering the
  water and place the bait right in front of the fish. On camera this was
  described as reactive, not scripted — "you think you have a plan but then
  as soon as one of the riggers goes off... it all goes out the window."
- **Hook placement on the dropback bait:** hooking the mackerel through the
  nose is common, but the hook can turn and pull back out; Decker prefers
  hooking it higher, especially when an aggressive cast is needed to reach
  the fish, saying it stays pinned better that way. Kept side by side with,
  not reconciled against, any nose-hook default elsewhere in this KB — this
  is a single captain's stated preference, not tested head-to-head on camera.

## Gear

- A **flyline / live-bait class** rod rigged and ready to pitch the instant a
  fish lights up — the pitch rod sits ready the whole troll; heavy leader per
  [wind-on leader](../rigging/wind-on-leader.md).
