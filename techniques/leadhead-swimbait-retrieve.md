# Leadhead Swimbait Retrieve — Depth Control by Feel

A count-and-pause retrieve for a leadhead + soft-plastic swimbait that keeps
the bait working just off the bottom without a depth finder or a
line-counter reel — bottom contact is read off the **line falling slack**,
not off a sounder mark. A second, distinct open-water retrieve family for the
same lure class works a sonar mark or squid nest for a reaction bite rather
than reading bottom contact by feel. Bait/head selection lives in
[soft-plastic swimbaits](../lures/soft-plastic-swimbaits.md); this note owns
the retrieve and its hookset.

## Reach for this when

- Fishing a **leadhead swimbait** and you want to hold the bait tracking near
  the bottom on a controlled drift or a stationary cast, rather than bouncing
  it randomly or winding it back blind.
- **California halibut** in water past the depth a shallow-running hardbait
  can work without digging into the sand: at **10 ft of water**, a leadhead +
  swimbait is more effective than a hardbait (`E4CDqBFOaP8`; see [California
  halibut](../species/california-halibut.md#situations--techniques)). ⚠
  Flagged gap — no corpus source: mechanism for why 10 ft is the threshold;
  the retrieve below is the general technique, not a claim that 10 ft is
  specifically where it starts working.
- The count-and-pause rhythm is described as broadly useful "for a lot of
  presentations," beyond just this bait — no other presentation is named on
  camera (`OURS05qEGcA`).

## Mechanics — harbor bottom contact

1. Make the cast.
2. **Sink the bait out on totally free spool** — let it fall all the way to
   the bottom before doing anything else.
3. Put the reel in gear and take **three turns of the handle**, at a
   moderate pace — no RPM or per-second count given beyond "about that fast"
   (`OURS05qEGcA`).
4. **Pause. Keep the line tight and watch it.**
5. The bait keeps falling after you stop winding; when the **line goes
   slack**, that is the tell you're back on the bottom — not a manual count
   or a feel through the rod.
6. **Take three more turns** and repeat the wind-three/pause/watch-it-fall
   rhythm continuously, all the way back to the boat, seawall, or beach
   (`OURS05qEGcA`).

## Hookset

Two distinct hooksets apply to this presentation, scoped by range and
current — not variations of one rule.

### Long range, current running: grind to the drag, don't swing

Demonstrated from a boat with **150 ft of line out** (`TzK4iuVmUSE`). Scaled
up from the harbor halibut program above — the example given is a **30 lb
gamefish** eating an **8-in lure**; species and location are not named on
camera.

- **Rod grip on the sink:** thumb over, forefinger under the rod — the same
  grip used bass fishing — to feel bites through the blank on the sink,
  "especially when you're out of gear" (free spool). Even a 30 lb fish can
  register as barely more than a surprisingly small "thump" (`TzK4iuVmUSE`).
- **Do not swing on the bite.** With 150 ft of line out and current running,
  the line isn't straight or tight, so a swing doesn't transmit.
- **Instead: turn the handle fast and point the rod, cranking until the drag
  slips** (direction not specified on camera) — not until you physically can't
  turn the handle anymore. If it feels like you can't turn it, keep trying;
  you should still be able to turn it right up to the point the drag gives.
  Once the drag slips, fold the rod up into the fight.
- The fish "freaks out" and takes a run right as the drag slips — expect to
  follow it around the boat (`TzK4iuVmUSE`).

This hookset is a distinct mechanism from winding-not-pumping while fighting
an already-hooked kelp bass out of structure (see
[swimbaits](swimbaits.md#fighting-fish-out-of-the-kelp-dave-hansen-your-saltwater-guide))
— that rule addresses an already-hooked fish; this one is the hookset
itself.

### Short range, pier/boat inshore: wind to a tight line, don't grind

Baseline rig: a **½–¾ oz** leadhead with a **3–4 in** swimbait on a long cast
(`yTPtYL9QAsw`, `yl-AkO5S64o`).

- **Grinding the handle flat-out and waiting for the drag to slip as the cue
  to set the hook is a mistake here** — it misses fish consistently.
- **The fix: wind only until the line comes tight, then set the hook** — for
  any single-hook lure fished on the bump-bottom/turn-the-handle rhythm
  above (`yTPtYL9QAsw`).

The two hooksets are not reconciled into one rule: on a long cast with
current running, winding alone can't straighten the line, so grinding to the
drag is the workaround; on a short pier/boat cast, winding to a tight line is
achievable directly and is the correct read. The same clip also fishes a
treble-hook minnow lure on a **Major Craft Sienna 962M** (9½ ft) surf rod;
that hookset is documented in [inshore crankbaits](inshore-crankbaits.md#execution),
not repeated here (`yTPtYL9QAsw`).

## Bait sizing by water

- A **3-inch swimbait** (e.g. a War Baits head with a Sudden Impact 3-inch)
  covers roughly **90%** of the technique's use fishing deeper
  (`OURS05qEGcA`).
- **Smaller baits are favored specifically in the harbor** (`OURS05qEGcA`).

## Open-water casting retrieves — erratic vs. smooth

A second, distinct retrieve family from the harbor count-and-pause rhythm
above: casting a fluke-style leadhead off a drifting boat over a sonar mark
or squid nest in open water/around the Channel Islands — same leadhead +
soft-plastic class, different mechanics and goal (working the lure for a
reaction bite, not reading bottom contact by feel) (`hjZ3hgq0Bzg`).

- **Chop-glide:** after the cast, sink the lure to the bottom, take up
  slack, then work the rod tip in **half handle-turns ("chop the handle")**
  followed by lifting the rod tip — the chop drags the lure across the
  bottom like a squid, then the tip-lift makes it glide gently.
- **Sweep-and-drop:** sweep the rod, then give slack; the lure sweeps across
  and then sinks in a spiral on the slack.
- **Crack-and-slack:** the rod input gives the lure its action, the slack
  lets it react. Crack, slack, crack, slack; near bottom this walks the lure
  roughly **6 ft** in either direction. Follow a crack-slack sequence with a
  pause suspending the lure **~5 ft** off the bottom, giving a curious fish
  that followed it in a chance to eat it.
- **Bottom-check-and-suspend:** periodically just check bottom, take a
  handle turn, and let the lure sit — the same "walk it 6 ft either way,
  then suspend it" idea framed as its own move rather than a follow-on to
  crack-slack.
- **Test a new lure before committing to a retrieve plan:** in clear water,
  drop the lure ~10 ft down alongside the boat and watch what each planned
  retrieve actually does to it before fishing it blind.
- **Don't reset once the cast swings vertical.** A cast that's drifted
  straight up and down under the boat is not "toast" — keep working it
  (lift-drop, crack-slack) rather than winding straight in to recast. Fish
  follow the lure back and get curious about something lingering in one
  zone; many bites come on a lure fished straight up and down, including
  from anglers who aren't strong casters and simply drop it vertically
  (`hjZ3hgq0Bzg`).

**Species split, same retrieve family:** white seabass and yellowtail are
described as more curious and mobile, and respond to the **hard crack**
(erratic, darting action). Halibut will come a long way for the lure but
prefer a **smoother, steadier action** — bottom contact, then lift and
tight-line it so it glides across the bottom rather than the erratic
crack-slack action (`hjZ3hgq0Bzg`).

**White seabass has its own program inside this retrieve family** — the
countdown-to-bottom cast pair, the midwater-column swim tried roughly every
third cast, and the slow current-worked walk that conflicts with the hard
crack above — in [white seabass — leadhead
swimbait](../species/white-seabass-leadhead-swimbait.md)
(`hjZ3hgq0Bzg`, `qLKLlZdiflA`).

## Where it doesn't work

- **Not an effective beach presentation with a leadhead specifically**: the
  leadhead always wants to return to the bottom too quickly from the beach,
  so it never gets the **mid-column swim** a suspending presentation would
  give it. This is a limitation of the leadhead/swimbait combination in that
  scenario, not a rejection of the retrieve rhythm itself (`OURS05qEGcA`).
  See the beach-halibut alternatives in [California halibut — situations →
  techniques](../species/california-halibut.md#situations--techniques) (a
  shallow-running minnow, and a fast-retrieve hardbait) — different lure
  classes solving the same shallow-beach problem.
- **Rod-tip height is a complementary hand-position adjustment, not a fix
  for this limitation** — see [beach depth
  control](beach-lure-depth-control.md): it doesn't stop the leadhead
  returning to bottom fast, it only raises or lowers where that fall happens
  in the column.

## Common failures

- **Swinging on the bite with slack in the line** (long range, current
  running): a swing doesn't transmit through slack, unstraightened line;
  grind the handle until the drag slips instead of striking (`TzK4iuVmUSE`).
- **Grinding to the drag on a short, direct cast** instead of winding to a
  tight line: consistently misses fish. The grind-to-the-drag hookset is
  scoped to long-range fishing with current running the line off-straight;
  on an everyday pier/boat cast, wind only until the line comes tight, then
  set (`yTPtYL9QAsw`).
- **Fishing a leadhead for a mid-column swim from the beach**: the leadhead
  always falls back to bottom too fast from the beach to hold a suspended
  presentation; rod-tip height only shifts where that fast fall happens, it
  doesn't stop it — switch lure class for a beach mid-column presentation
  instead (`OURS05qEGcA`; see [beach depth
  control](beach-lure-depth-control.md)).
