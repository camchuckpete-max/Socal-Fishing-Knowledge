# Leadhead Swimbait Retrieve — Depth Control by Feel

A count-and-pause retrieve for a leadhead + soft-plastic swimbait that keeps the
bait working just off the bottom without a depth finder or a line-counter reel —
you read bottom contact off the **line falling slack**, not off a mark. Taught by
Shannon Gallagher (Tackle Express) as his number-one halibut technique
(`OURS05qEGcA`, 2026-01-14; unregistered channel, single mention — medium
confidence). Bait/head selection lives in
[soft-plastic swimbaits](../lures/soft-plastic-swimbaits.md); this note owns the
retrieve only.

## Reach for this when

- Fishing a **leadhead swimbait** and you want to hold the bait tracking near
  the bottom on a controlled drift or a stationary cast, rather than bouncing it
  randomly or winding it back blind.
- **California halibut** in water past the depth a shallow-running hard bait can
  work without digging into the sand — his stated example is switching to a
  leadhead and swimbait at **10 ft of water**, calling it "much more effective"
  than a hardbait there (see
  [California halibut](../species/california-halibut.md#situations--techniques)).
  No further mechanism for that specific depth threshold is given on camera; the
  retrieve below is the general technique, not a claim that 10 ft is where it
  starts working.
- He frames the rhythm itself as broadly useful "for a lot of presentations,"
  beyond just this bait — no other presentation is named on camera.

## Mechanics

1. Make the cast.
2. **Sink the bait out on totally free spool** — let it fall all the way to the
   bottom before doing anything else.
3. Put the reel in gear and take **three turns of the handle**, at a moderate,
   demonstrated-on-camera pace (no RPM or per-second count given beyond "about
   that fast").
4. **Pause. Keep the line tight and watch it.**
5. The bait continues to fall after you stop winding; when the **line goes
   slack ("falls")**, that is the tell you're back on the bottom — not a manual
   count or a feel through the rod.
6. **Take three more turns** and repeat the wind-three/pause/watch-it-fall
   rhythm continuously, all the way back to the boat, seawall, or beach.

## Bait sizing by water

- A **3-inch swimbait** (his example: a War Baits head with a Sudden Impact
  3-inch) covers roughly **90% of the technique's use fishing deeper.**
- **Smaller baits are favored specifically in the harbor.**

## Where it doesn't work

- **Not an effective beach presentation with a leadhead specifically** — his
  stated reason: the leadhead always wants to return to the bottom too quickly
  from the beach, so it never gets the **mid-column swim** a suspending
  presentation would. This is a limitation of the leadhead/swimbait combination
  itself in that scenario, not a rejection of the retrieve rhythm.
  Kept side by side with, not reconciled against, the other beach-halibut
  presentations logged in
  [California halibut](../species/california-halibut.md#situations--techniques)
  (a shallow-running minnow, and a fast-retrieve hard bait) — those are
  different lure classes solving the same shallow-beach problem.

## Gear class

Leadhead + soft-plastic swimbait class — see
[soft-plastic swimbaits](../lures/soft-plastic-swimbaits.md) for head/weight
selection and [gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md) for rod/reel.

**Rod flexibility (same presenter, Tackle Express w/ Shannon Gallagher,
`Oi5n-uVpY9g`, 2025-05-02; unregistered channel, single mention — medium
confidence):** no specialized rod is required for this presentation — a
calico bass rod, a rod custom-built for the technique, a **big spinning rod**
(his pick specifically for **California halibut in shallow water**, matching
this note's own shallow-water halibut use case above), or a plain jig stick
all work. His framing: "there's not a whole lot you can't get away with" on
rod choice here.
