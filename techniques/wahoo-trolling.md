# Wahoo Trolling — Speed, Leader, and Fight Mechanics

Dedicated trolling execution for wahoo on the heaviest outfit aboard — distinct
from [wahoo bomb & Raider jig casting](wahoo-bomb-casting.md), which is a
cast-and-burn presentation on much lighter gear. Species pattern and where
this fits among wahoo's other techniques routes from
[wahoo](../species/wahoo.md); the on-the-water Alijos Rocks trolling account
(hookup-ratio reasoning for trolling a bomb over casting one) is in
[trolling — Alijos Rocks](trolling.md#alijos-rocks-baja--bomb-lure-trolling-for-wahoo).

Source: Searcher Sportfishing, "Tackle Talk Live Recap (Wahoo 5.5.20),"
`4uNPLknRAQg`, 2020-05-06; `searcher-sportfishing` registered channel/series —
single-video citation for the parameters below, medium confidence.

## Reach for this when

- Arriving at a dedicated wahoo stop (Alijos Rocks or "the Ridge") to get the
  boat's spread started and "get the lay of the land" before switching to cast
  or live-bait presentations.
- You want lines working while running between grounds or crossing structure —
  the heaviest outfit aboard stays out on the troll by default on a
  multi-day trip built around wahoo.

## Mechanics

- **Troll speed: 7–8 knots** as the standard pace. Once the boat is in the
  right water — **72°F or warmer** — speed increases to **10.5 knots** running
  marlin-style trolling lures, on the reasoning that warm water is when wahoo
  are actively feeding aggressively enough to catch a fast-moving bait.
- **Searching more water on a miss — free-spool-and-extend.** If your lure
  didn't get bit on the way out, put the reel in **free spool and count to
  10 or 15** as the boat's forward slide carries the lure out **another
  50–60 ft**, then put the reel back in gear and wind it in. The stated
  reasoning: this covers more water with the same lure on the retrieve, in
  case multiple fish are working the area, giving a second chance at a bite on
  the way in rather than just re-trolling the same track.
- **Fighting a hooked wahoo — high gear first, then low once you stop gaining
  line.** On a two-speed reel, leave it in high gear at the start of the fight
  so you're constantly gaining line and not giving a fast-running fish any
  slack; once you're in high gear and stop gaining line, that's the cue to
  shift to low gear to keep working the fish in. **Don't pump — just wind the
  handle continuously.** Pumping the rod gives the fish a moment of slack on
  the down-stroke, which is enough for a wahoo to throw the hook; the boat's
  regulars enforce this loudly ("Art from the upper deck" yells at anglers who
  pump). This matches the same wind-continuously, no-pumping fighting
  discipline already logged for the trolled-bomb fight in
  [trolling — Alijos Rocks](trolling.md#alijos-rocks-baja--bomb-lure-trolling-for-wahoo)
  — corroborating, repeated Searcher doctrine on this specific point.
- **Before pulling a trolling lure clear of the water** to recast or rack the
  rod, let it settle and count **four or five** first — see
  [wahoo handling & deck safety](../fish-care/wahoo-handling.md) for the full
  reasoning (a following wahoo can come aboard).

## Rigging

- **Wire: 200 lb** (a second demonstration in the same session shows
  **200–250 lb**), uncoated, finished with the appropriate crimp sleeves —
  **buy the sleeve size printed on the wire's own packaging**; going up one
  sleeve size from what the wire calls for leaves the crimp too loose and
  risks losing the fish and the lure on the hookset.
- **Loop size: about 1 in, and no shorter.** The exact loop length "doesn't
  really make that much difference" longer, but going shorter than about an
  inch is called out as costing the lure some of its swimming action.
- **Swivel: heavy black, not chrome.** A chrome swivel gets bitten — "we see
  that a lot" — so the standing recommendation is a heavy black swivel at the
  wire-to-mainline junction.
- **Leader length: 30–36 in.** On camera, one leader on hand measured only
  24 in and was called too short ("I like them a little longer"); the stated
  recommendation is 30–36 in.
- **Main line: 80 lb test** on the heaviest outfit aboard.
- **Sharpen every hook out of the package** — trolling lures included —
  before running them; wahoo have "a very, very bony jaw," and a factory hook
  that isn't sharpened well enough often won't penetrate it. A demonstrated
  lure with visible bite marks and a used, sharpened hook was cited on camera
  as evidence the sharpening pays off. This corroborates, with an added
  mechanism (the bony jaw), the wahoo-bomb-hook sharpening practice already
  logged in [hooks — factory sharpness](../tackle/hooks.md#factory-sharpness--surfaceyo-yo-iron-trebles-vs-wahoo-bomb-hooks-needing-a-touch-up-searchersportfishing-tackle-talk-live-49jokhd7umc-2020-06-03-searcher-sportfishing-registered-channel-single-mention--medium-confidence)
  — same channel, this citation dated about a month earlier, and broader in
  scope (trolling lures, casting lures/bombs, and live-bait hooks, not just
  the wahoo bomb specifically).
- **Wire vs. straight heavy leader — a live debate, not resolved.** The
  stated preference on this boat is to wire every trolling lure and casting
  lure, reasoning that the cost of losing a $40–50 trolling lure (or a
  cheaper $25 casting lure) to a bite-off isn't worth saving the tie time,
  and that going without wire is not believed to draw meaningfully more
  bites. Named as a counter-opinion in the same session: some anglers try a
  **couple hundred pound fluorocarbon or mono leader** instead of wire on
  trolling lures. See [wahoo — doctrine & conflicts](../species/wahoo.md#doctrine--conflicts)
  for the full side-by-side with a second, named data point on this same
  question (Howard Fulmer).

## Lure choice

- **Yo-Zuri Marauder** — a large trolling plug, shown in the **"Tony the
  Tiger"** color pattern (this same color name and its long-running
  popularity is independently corroborated in
  [wahoo bomb & Raider jig casting — lure choice](wahoo-bomb-casting.md#lure-choice)),
  rigged with big, sharp hooks and a split ring.
- **Nomad-brand diving plug** — shown in **pink**, called "the new hot wahoo
  lure here recently" as of this 2020-05-06 session; a plastic bib/blade on
  the nose is what makes it dive. This is consistent with, and a further data
  point on, the pink-Nomad-as-recent-hot-color doctrine already logged in
  [DTX Minnow — wahoo trolling application](../lures/dtx-minnow.md#wahoo-trolling-application-dtx-200),
  though the specific model/size shown here is not confirmed as the DTX 200.
- **Large Rapala-class plug** — a hard-bodied swimmer that can be tuned
  (bent up/down/side to side) by hand so it swims straight and true.
- **Hook size:** trolling lures run **9/0 or 10/0** single hooks, or bigger.

## Gear-class detail

- **Rod/reel:** the boat's **heaviest outfit** — an **80 lb class**, two-speed
  conventional. Named models the crew runs for this: **Penn International**,
  **Accurate** twin-drag models, and **Shimano Talica**.
- **Braid main line:** **100–135 lb**, solid or hollow — solid is stated as
  fine for wahoo trolling; hollow braid matters mainly if you also want to
  crimp a wind-on leader connection for big tuna, not a wahoo-specific
  requirement.
- This 80 lb heaviest-outfit spec is the same class already summarized in
  [wahoo — gear summary](../species/wahoo.md#gear-summary).

## Common failures

- **Leader too short (≈24 in)** — called out on camera as undersized; run
  30–36 in instead.
- **Chrome swivel** at the wire-to-mainline junction — gets bitten through.
- **Wrong crimp-sleeve size for the wire test** — a sleeve one size too big
  crimps loose and costs you the fish and the lure.
- **Trolling too slow in warm water** — once water is 72°F or warmer and
  wahoo are actively feeding, staying at 7–8 kt instead of stepping up toward
  10.5 kt may leave bites on the table (stated as the boat's own practice,
  not tested head-to-head in this source).
