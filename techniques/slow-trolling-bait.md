# Slow-Trolling Live Bait

Pull a **live bait** behind a moving boat slowly enough that it swims naturally
rather than being dragged. It is the bridge between
[flylining](flyline.md) (same bait, same tackle, boat stopped) and
[trolling](trolling.md) (boat moving, but hard baits and a search purpose). The
bait is the presentation; the boat's job is to cover ground and to set the hook.

Doctrine here is **Nate Winnicki** (BDOutdoors, `HTowqnwAMeA`, filmed mid-August;
`nate-winnicki` is a registered voice in the
[source registry](../sources/source-registry.md), but this is the first commit
of these specific claims → `medium` pending repetition). **Named rod, reel,
hook, and line models sit at `low`** per the sponsored-claim rule; the
parameters and the reasoning qualify normally.

## Reach for this when

- [Yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md) show on the meter as a **stratified
  column** — marks running from the bottom up through mid-column, sometimes to
  the surface — rather than a ball glued to the bottom. That shape reads as
  **fish actively hunting bait**, and it is the stated trigger to put slow-troll
  baits out.
- You have live bait and want to keep covering water instead of committing to
  an anchor. See the anchor-versus-move judgment recorded in
  [yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md#doctrine--conflicts).
- The same outfit and bait you would flyline is already rigged — this is a
  change of boat behaviour, not a change of tackle.

## Speed — 1.5 to 3 mph, chosen by the bait

The range is wide on purpose, and **the bait picks the number**:

- **Small-grade sardine** (the kind that needs a size 1 or 2 hook) — go **as
  slow as you possibly can**. A small bait cannot swim against much.
- **Mackerel, or any bait big enough for a 3/0–5/0 hook** — you can pull it
  **faster** within the range.

The governing test is not the speedometer: the bait must **look natural**, and
the failure is a bait that reads as *being dragged by a machine*. Set speed by
watching the bait, and use the range as a sanity check.

## Setback, drag, and letting the boat set the hook

- **Distance back: 100–150 ft.** Explicitly *not* far — a long setback is not
  the goal here.
- **Fish a light drag and let the boat drive the hook.** On a lever drag, set
  it around the **"2"** mark: enough pressure that forward motion of the boat
  seats the hook the moment the fish eats, but not so much that it snaps you
  off or overruns the reel. Whether you fish in gear at all is presented as
  angler preference; the light-drag setting is the specific recommendation.
- **The reason the drag cannot be pinned:** a yellowtail eating a slow-trolled
  bait will take **50–100 yards immediately**, and more if it is any size. A
  locked drag at that moment either breaks the line or backlashes the reel.

## Line, leader, and hooks

- **Line class: 25–40 lb** for local yellowtail, and **always carry a lighter
  option** — 20–25 lb — even on a day that starts on 40. A full day of bites on
  40 lb is described as luck rather than the norm on the SoCal local coast.
  *"You'd be really surprised how hard you can pull on 25 lb"* — provided the
  drag is set for it.
- **Fluorocarbon at the hook, matched to the mainline, not dropped off a
  cliff.** Run a short (~6 ft) fluoro section sized to your mainline — 30 lb
  main gets 30 lb fluoro — and **at most halve it** when fish are finicky
  (30 lb main down to 25 lb fluoro). The stated warning is explicit: **do not
  go much lighter than that.** The stated payoff is abrasion resistance right
  at the hook, where you otherwise get bitten off or rubbed off on rock.
- **Hooks: a J hook in the 3/0 range as the working default**, with **1/0, 1,
  and 2** carried for two distinct situations — **small bait** and **finicky
  fish**. The downsizing is a paired move: drop line class *and* hook size
  together to get a bite, not one or the other.
- **Full setup as described:** an **8 ft fiberglass or composite rod**, a
  **size-25 reel**, 50 lb braid with a 30–40 lb mono top shot, then the short
  fluoro to the hook. See
  [rod & reel selection](../tackle/rod-and-reel-selection.md) and
  [line & leader](../tackle/line-and-leader.md).

## Common failures

- **Pinning the drag** — see above; it costs the fish on the first run.
- **Setting the bait too far back.** Past ~150 ft you lose the hook-setting
  benefit of boat motion and gain nothing.
- **Over-dropping the fluoro.** Halving the mainline class is the floor, not a
  starting point.
- **Running one speed all day.** The speed is a function of what bait is in the
  tank, and it changes when the bait does.
- **Treating educated fish as naive.** Fish on the heavily-worked local coast
  north of the border are described as having seen a great many slow-trolled
  baits and being able to tell a bad presentation from a good one — the source
  flags this as possibly anthropomorphising, and it is recorded as his stated
  view rather than as established fish behaviour.

## Cedros Island, Baja Pacific — mackerel bait-and-switch, a break from casting

**Tackle Express, `_C7TJoGT0nw`, "What You ACTUALLY Need for Cedros Fishing
Trips | The Gear Vault," 2025-09-26; unregistered channel — medium
confidence; region: Baja Pacific, Cedros Island.** A different use case from
the rest of this note: not a search tool, but a **rest-break substitute for
casting iron all day.** After several yellowtail, the stated reason to switch
was arm fatigue ("your arms hurt a little bit") — the operator (Toro) runs
its own **bait dock/bait bar** stocked with mackerel and sardines, so the
boat can slow-troll instead of keep casting. Mackerel ran big on this trip —
**15-in** fish — fished on **Owner ringed offshore hooks sized 10, 30, and
50** to match whatever bait size was on the dock that day (full hook detail
in [hooks](../tackle/hooks.md#owner-ringed-offshore-hook--cedros-mackerelsardine-slow-troll-sizing-by-bait-size-tackle-express-_c7tjogt0nw-2025-09-26-unregistered-channel--medium-confidence-region-baja-pacific-cedros-island)).

**Observed, secondhand (same source, relayed from another angler on the
trip, not filmed):** downriggers rigged with **2 lb max weights**, slow-
trolled in **30 ft of water**, reportedly hooked two big fish and landed
neither — "they were just giants." Kept as a secondhand, unconfirmed data
point, not folded into the mackerel-hook doctrine above.
