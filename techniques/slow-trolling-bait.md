# Slow-Trolling Live Bait

Pull a **live bait** behind a moving boat slowly enough that it swims naturally
rather than being dragged. It bridges [flylining](flyline.md) (same bait, same
tackle, boat stopped) and [trolling](trolling.md) (boat moving, but hard baits
and a search purpose): the bait is the presentation, and the boat's job is to
cover ground and set the hook.

## Reach for this when

- [Yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md) show on the meter as a **stratified
  column** — marks running from the bottom up through mid-column, sometimes to
  the surface — rather than a ball glued to the bottom. That shape reads as
  fish actively hunting bait, and it is the trigger to put slow-troll baits
  out (`HTowqnwAMeA`).
- Live bait is already rigged and you want to keep covering water instead of
  committing to an anchor. See the anchor-versus-move judgment in
  [yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md#doctrine--conflicts).
- The outfit and bait you'd flyline is already rigged — slow-trolling is a
  change of boat behavior, not a change of tackle.

## Speed — 1.5–3 mph, chosen by the bait

Speed is scaled to what's in the tank, not fixed to a target species
(`HTowqnwAMeA`):

- **Small-grade sardine** (the kind that needs a size 1 or 2 hook) — go **as
  slow as you possibly can**; a small bait can't swim against much speed.
- **Mackerel, or any bait big enough for a 3/0–5/0 hook** — pull it **faster**
  within the range.

The test is the bait, not the speedometer: watch it swim, and slow down the
moment it starts reading as dragged rather than swimming (`HTowqnwAMeA`).

## Setback and drag — let the boat set the hook

- **Setback: 100–150 ft.** Not far — a long setback isn't the goal here
  (`HTowqnwAMeA`).
- **Fish a light drag and let the boat's forward motion drive the hook.** On a
  [lever drag](../tackle/star-drag-vs-lever-drag.md), set it around the **"2"**
  mark — enough pressure to seat the hook the instant a fish eats, not so much
  it snaps the leader or overruns the reel on the strike. Whether to fish in
  gear at all is angler preference; the light-drag setting is the specific
  call (`HTowqnwAMeA`).
- **Why the drag can't be pinned tighter:** a yellowtail eating a slow-trolled
  bait takes **50–100 yd immediately**, more on a bigger fish — a locked drag
  at that moment either parts the line or backlashes the reel (`HTowqnwAMeA`).

## Line, leader, and hooks

- **Line class: 25–40 lb** for local yellowtail; carry a lighter option — **20–25 lb**
  — even on a day that starts on 40. A full day of bites on 40 lb is
  described as luck rather than the norm on the local SoCal coast —
  *"you'd be really surprised how hard you can pull on 25 lb"* once the drag
  is set for it (`HTowqnwAMeA`).
- **Fluorocarbon leader matched to the mainline, not dropped off a cliff.** Run
  a short (~6 ft) fluoro section sized to the mainline — 30 lb main gets 30 lb
  fluoro — and at most halve it when fish are finicky (30 lb main → 25 lb
  fluoro floor). Going much lighter than that costs fish to abrasion right at
  the hook, where bite-offs and rock rub happen (`HTowqnwAMeA`).
- **Hooks: J hook, 3/0 as the working default**, with **1/0, 1, and 2** carried
  for two situations — small bait and finicky fish. Downsize line class and
  hook size together to draw a bite; downsizing only one rarely works
  (`HTowqnwAMeA`).

## Gear class

**[Flyline / soft-rod class](../tackle/gear-classes.md)** tackle throughout —
the same soft-tipped rod, live-bait reel, and light leader used to
[flyline](flyline.md#gear-class) the same bait. Slow-trolling changes the
boat's behavior, not the tackle (`HTowqnwAMeA`).

- **Full outfit as described:** an **8 ft fiberglass or composite rod**, a
  **size-25 reel**, **50 lb braid** to a **30–40 lb mono top shot**, then the
  short fluoro section above to the hook. The braid capacity matters because
  the initial 50–100 yd run (above) has to come off the spool before the
  light drag can be advanced. See
  [rod & reel selection](../tackle/rod-and-reel-selection.md) and
  [line & leader](../tackle/line-and-leader.md) (`HTowqnwAMeA`).
- **Cedros mackerel/sardine slow-troll:** Owner ringed offshore hooks, sized
  10, 30, and 50 to match whatever bait size is on the dock that day — full
  hook detail in
  [hooks](../tackle/hooks.md#owner-ringed-offshore-hook--cedros-mackerelsardine-slow-troll-sizing-by-bait-size-tackle-express-_c7tjogt0nw-2025-09-26-unregistered-channel--medium-confidence-region-baja-pacific-cedros-island)
  (`_C7TJoGT0nw`).

## Common failures

- **Pinning the drag** — see Setback and drag above; it costs the fish on the
  first run.
- **Setting the bait too far back.** Past ~150 ft, the hook-setting benefit of
  boat motion is lost and nothing is gained.
- **Over-dropping the fluoro.** Halving the mainline class is the floor, not a
  starting point.
- **Running one speed all day.** Speed is a function of what bait is in the
  tank, and it changes when the bait does.
- **Treating educated fish as naive.** Fish on the heavily-worked local coast
  north of the border have seen a great many slow-trolled baits and can tell
  a bad presentation from a good one — a single source's stated view, not
  measured fish behavior (`HTowqnwAMeA`).

## Cedros Island, Baja Pacific — a rest-break substitute for casting

A different use case from the rest of this note: not a search tool, but a
rest-break substitute for casting iron all day. After several yellowtail, the
stated reason to switch was arm fatigue — the operator runs its own bait
dock/bait bar stocked with mackerel and sardine, so the boat can slow-troll
instead of keep casting. Mackerel ran big on this trip — **15 in** fish
(`_C7TJoGT0nw`).
