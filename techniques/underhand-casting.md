# Underhand Casting

The underhand cast is a simple lob toss for delivering live bait off a
sportboat rail or bow — an easier motion to learn than an [overhead
surface-iron cast](surface-iron-casting.md), and useful whenever the extra
distance an overhead swing buys isn't needed. It lands a bait more gently
than an overhand throw, which matters for a bait that needs to swim away and
stay lively (`QHY5kmU7OTU`). Certain hook placements call for it outright: a
hard overhand swing can tear a bait off a soft hook point (`SAltQjih0ms`).

## Reach for this when

- You haven't learned an overhead cast yet — this is the simpler motion to
  start with (`0hQJxESLTm4`).
- You're standing in a corner of the rail or bow with no room to bring an
  overhead cast out cleanly (`0hQJxESLTm4`).
- You're fishing a lighter bait — the source's example is an anchovy on a
  small rubber-core sinker — rather than a heavier iron or weighted rig
  (`0hQJxESLTm4`).
- The bait is hooked through a soft spot — a shoulder hook or belly hook —
  where a hard overhand swing risks tearing it off (`SAltQjih0ms`).
- Landing the bait gently and letting it swim off on its own matters more
  than maximizing distance (`QHY5kmU7OTU`).

## Mechanics

- Keep the bait clear of other passengers while setting up.
- Put the reel in free spool, thumb on the spool.
- Bring the rod up to the rail.
- Toss the bait out with a simple underhand lob — no wind-up, no overhead
  swing (`0hQJxESLTm4`).

## Pulling slack for a light bait

A lighter bait — an anchovy on a small rubber-core sinker, in the source's
example — often isn't heavy enough to pull line smoothly off the reel by
itself. Pull a bit of line off the spool by hand before the toss to make the
cast easier (`0hQJxESLTm4`).

The same pull-slack motion works more generally, independent of bait weight:
rest one finger on the spool, use the pointer finger to pull some line off by
hand, then lift the rod tip and guide the bait out. This cast doesn't need
much distance when the goal is just getting bait away from the boat
(`7iSGGb9ueAk`).

## Avoiding backlash

- **Lob it, don't whip it.** Bring the bait up to about where the reel sits,
  then lob it out with a controlled motion — not a hard whipping cast
  (`Ixyi1mY3Qeg`).
- **Thumb the spool again just before the bait hits the water** — the same
  thumb-control principle as the free-spool/thumb-on-spool setup above,
  applied a second time at the other end of the cast (`Ixyi1mY3Qeg`).

If a backlash happens anyway, see [clearing a
backlash](clearing-a-backlash.md) for the hand-tension recovery method rather
than cutting the tangle out.

## Underhand vs. overhand casting

Asked directly which he prefers for casting live bait, Carson's answer is
underhand: it takes more timing and care to execute well, but for live bait
it matters far more that the bait lands gently on the water and swims away
on its own than it does to maximize casting distance (`QHY5kmU7OTU`). This is
a stated preference, not a rule — an angler who needs more distance than an
underhand lob gives should cast overhand instead (`QHY5kmU7OTU`).

## Soft hook placements need the gentler swing

A shoulder hook (between the dorsal fin and the gill plate) and a belly hook
(just behind the underbelly fins) are both soft spots in a live bait —
thinner tissue than the cartilage at the nose. Underhand cast a bait hooked
either of those two ways rather than swing it overhand: a hard overhand cast
risks tearing the bait off the hook at a soft hook point (`SAltQjih0ms`). See
[hook position by situation](flyline.md#hook-position-by-situation) for the
shoulder/belly placements themselves; a nose-hooked bait sits in cartilage
and can take a firmer cast.

## Gear class

Not a distinct rod/reel class of its own — this cast is thrown with whichever
live-bait outfit is already rigged, most often the [flyline / soft-rod
class](../tackle/gear-classes.md) for an unweighted bait or a [30 lb bait
class](../tackle/gear-classes.md) outfit fishing a small sinker. The only
equipment detail the technique itself depends on is a reel that free-spools
cleanly and lets the angler get a thumb back on the spool at both ends of the
toss (`0hQJxESLTm4`, `Ixyi1mY3Qeg`).

## Common failures

- **Whipping the cast overhand-style** instead of a controlled lob — the
  named cause of backlash on this cast (`Ixyi1mY3Qeg`).
- **Not getting the thumb back on the spool just before the bait lands** —
  the same free-spool/thumb-control lapse at the other end of the toss
  (`Ixyi1mY3Qeg`).
- **Overhand-casting a bait hooked through a soft spot** (shoulder or belly)
  — tears the bait off the hook; underhand cast those placements instead
  (`SAltQjih0ms`).
- **Skipping the pull-slack step on a light bait** — the bait alone often
  isn't heavy enough to pull line smoothly off the reel, and the cast comes
  up short or hangs up (`0hQJxESLTm4`).
