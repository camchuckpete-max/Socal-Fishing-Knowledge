# Surface Iron — Colour

**The short answer from the most experienced voice in the corpus: colour is
not the variable you think it is. Swim is.** This note exists because
[surface iron](surface-iron.md) carried a self-declared "thin area — no
systematic colour doctrine" for the whole of batches 1 and 2, and it now has
one — a *negative* doctrine plus two narrow exceptions.

## The doctrine

**Dan Wade** (Strictly Irons, `Ec2GaNpiOHI` 2019 and `yn1suHH6b1Y` 2026 —
two channels, seven years apart, same answer; 60+ years fishing iron, personal
best 68 lb on the surface iron at Guadalupe on 40 lb line):

> *"Does colour matter?" — "Only for you."*
> *"I use colours that I can see. Lighter colour, white, yellow, mint."*
> (`yn1suHH6b1Y`)

The supporting argument is historical:

> *"If you go back to when these jigs were first invented, all those jigs in
> their original form were aluminium — no paint on them. Then the makers were
> going 'we're not selling as many jigs as we used to'… and somebody invented
> a thing called a blue stripe. Anybody that's done any amount of jig fishing,
> you've got a jig or two where about 85% of the paint is gone. It's just
> patinaed aluminium, and the dumb thing works just fine."*
> — *"Still gets bit. It's how it swims. It's a reaction bite."* (`yn1suHH6b1Y`)

**Practical reading:** pick a colour **you can see on the water**, so you can
watch the jig swim and read whether it's working. That is the actual function
colour serves in his program. Rank of what matters:
**swim → size/weight → colour, last.**

**Confidence is a real input, though.** The same voice, on tackle generally:
*"There is some substance to the confidence in what you're doing. If you're
sitting there thinking 'I'm never gonna catch a fish on this lure' — well
guess what, you're not"* (`Ec2GaNpiOHI`). That squares the negative doctrine
with the confidence-colour reports below rather than contradicting them.

## The two exceptions — calico bass

The only situation where Wade says he thinks about colour at all
(`Ec2GaNpiOHI`):

| Where you're fishing | Colour |
| --- | --- |
| **Up in the kelp and canopy** | **Yellow** |
| **Out in the open, off the kelp** | The **"bleeding soldier" / "GI Joe"** pattern |

> *"That's the only two times I get into the colour question for calico bass."*

Note also his qualifier that a repaint "depends on what you painted over" —
the base metal underneath is part of what you end up with.

The reports below were logged before the doctrine above existed. They are
**not** reconciled into it — several are single-angler confidence colours,
which the doctrine explicitly predicts you would see.

angler's stated confidence color for surface iron is **mint** — most of his

**Color-by-light framework, tuna lead jigs (Cesar, Eric's Tackle, via Dirty
Hookers, F7jLrt2j2X8, 2023-10-09; Cesar is a registered voice, source-registry
mechanism/parameter caveat applies — first commit of this claim, medium
confidence pending repetition):** the folklore that "color doesn't matter"
is framed here as a yellowtail saying — asked whether it holds for tuna,
Cesar's answer is that tuna color plays a bigger role. First choice is
**chrome, or chrome and blue**; second choice is **anything with white in
it** — blue and white, black and white, mint and white — as long as there's
a contrasting color against the blank. **Bright, clear, sunny, middle-of-the-
day conditions favor chrome.** **Overcast days, early morning, late evening,
or rough/dirty water favor anything with white**, on the stated mechanism
that in low light a chrome finish has no light left to reflect and just
mirrors the water (loses definition), while a white-accented finish holds
contrast regardless of light. This is jig-color doctrine for the tuna
lead-jig class specifically (see [tuna poppers and
stickbaits](../lures/tuna-poppers-and-stickbaits.md) and [iron
jigs](../lures/iron-jigs.md)) — kept alongside, not merged into, this note's
"no systematic color doctrine" framing above, since it answers the question
for that lure class rather than for the classic surface-iron blank.
