# Surface Iron — Colour

Colour is the least-leveraged variable on a swimming surface iron. The
corpus's most experienced voice on the technique states flatly that swim
design and retrieve control decide bites, not paint job, with two narrow
exceptions for calico bass and a separate framework for tuna lead jigs. This
note holds that doctrine; the many single-angler confidence-colour reports in
the corpus (mint at Cedros, Wounded Warrior for calico, and the rest) are
logged as [evidence](evidence/surface-iron-color.md), not doctrine, because a
personal confidence colour is exactly what the doctrine below predicts you'll
see. See [surface iron](surface-iron.md) for the cast and retrieve this note
assumes.

## Reach for this when

- Choosing a surface-iron colour before rigging up — read the doctrine below
  first: swim and retrieve speed decide the bite, not colour (see [surface
  iron — retrieve & cadence](surface-iron.md#retrieve--cadence)).
- Fishing calico bass in kelp/canopy versus open water off the kelp — the two
  situations where colour is worth choosing deliberately.
- Fishing tuna on a lead jig in variable light — colour is a bigger lever
  there than for a classic yellowtail/calico surface iron.
- Picking a colour you can watch on the water, to judge whether the swim is
  right.

## The doctrine

Colour is not the variable: the same answer, from the same voice, on two
channels seven years apart — "Does colour matter?" — "Only for you… I use
colours that I can see. Lighter colour, white, yellow, mint." (`yn1suHH6b1Y`;
corroborated `Ec2GaNpiOHI`, 2019). Dan Wade — 60+ years on the surface iron,
personal best 68 lb at Guadalupe on 40 lb line — is a registered voice on the
[source registry](../sources/source-registry.md), and this is repeated
doctrine, not a single mention.

The historical argument for it: original surface irons were bare aluminium,
no paint — makers added colour ("a blue stripe") to sell more jigs, and a jig
that has lost 85% of its paint down to patinaed aluminium still gets bit,
"because it's how it swims. It's a reaction bite." (`yn1suHH6b1Y`)

The practical rule: pick a colour **you can see on the water**, so you can
watch the jig swim and judge whether the retrieve is right — that is colour's
actual function in this program. Priority order: **swim → size/weight →
colour, last.**

Confidence is a real, separate input: "There is some substance to the
confidence in what you're doing. If you're sitting there thinking 'I'm never
gonna catch a fish on this lure' — well guess what, you're not." (`Ec2GaNpiOHI`)
That is why single-angler confidence-colour picks recur throughout the corpus
(logged in the [evidence file](evidence/surface-iron-color.md)) without
contradicting the no-systematic-colour doctrine above — confidence in the
tool, not the tool's colour, is the stated mechanism.

## Calico bass — the two exceptions

The only situation where Wade chooses colour on purpose (`Ec2GaNpiOHI`):

| Where you're fishing | Colour |
| --- | --- |
| Up in the kelp and canopy | Yellow |
| Out in the open, off the kelp | **Bleeding soldier** / **GI Joe** pattern |

A repaint's result depends on what's underneath — the base metal shows
through and becomes part of the finish (`Ec2GaNpiOHI`).

## Tuna — colour by light, not species folklore

"Colour doesn't matter" is a yellowtail saying; for tuna on a lead jig,
colour plays a bigger role (Cesar, Eric's Tackle, `F7jLrt2j2X8`). First
choice: **chrome, or chrome and blue**. Second choice: anything with white in
it — blue/white, black/white, mint/white — any colour that contrasts against
the blank. **Bright, clear, sunny, midday conditions favour chrome; overcast
days, early morning, late evening, or rough/dirty water favour anything with
white** — the mechanism given is that in low light chrome has no light left
to reflect and just mirrors the water, losing definition, while a
white-accented finish holds contrast regardless of light (`F7jLrt2j2X8`).
This is tuna lead-jig doctrine specifically — see [tuna poppers and
stickbaits](../lures/tuna-poppers-and-stickbaits.md) and [iron
jigs](../lures/iron-jigs.md) — held beside, not merged into, the surface-iron
colour doctrine above, since it answers the question for a different lure
class.

## Gear class

[Jig-stick class](../tackle/gear-classes.md) surface iron, per [surface
iron](surface-iron.md#gear-class) — colour is a finish choice on that same
jig, not a separate gear decision. Carrying a spread of colours only buys
options you can see in different light and water clarity; it does not change
rod, reel, or line class.

## Common failures

- **Cycling colours instead of checking swim or retrieve speed.** The
  doctrine ranks swim and size/weight above colour: if a jig isn't producing,
  check whether it's swimming right and whether the retrieve speed matches
  the bite before changing colour (`Ec2GaNpiOHI`, `yn1suHH6b1Y`; see [surface
  iron — retrieve & cadence](surface-iron.md#retrieve--cadence)).
- **Fishing a colour you can't see.** A colour that disappears in the glare
  or the water column can't be read for swim quality — the corpus rule is to
  pick something visible, not a specific hue (`yn1suHH6b1Y`).
- **Doubting the lure instead of fishing it.** Confidence measurably matters;
  swapping colours out of doubt rather than a diagnosed problem burns fishing
  time on a change that the doctrine says isn't the lever (`Ec2GaNpiOHI`).
