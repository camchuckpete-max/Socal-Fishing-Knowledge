# Spinning-Reel Wind Knots — Close the Bail and Come Tight Immediately

A casting-technique cause of wind knots on a spinning reel, distinct from the
spool-setup causes covered in [spooling line — tension and
twist](../tackle/spooling-line-tension-and-twist.md). Those spool-setup
causes are line twist from spooling and reverse line-lay taper; this note is
the casting-side cause instead. Source is a single Tackle Express short,
"Stop ruining your fishing line with these wind knot mistakes"
(`WeabGOvhgto`, uploaded 2026-06-18); unregistered channel, single mention —
medium confidence.

## Reach for this when

- You're fishing a **spinning reel loaded with braid** (the source's example
  is eight-carrier braid) and getting wind knots after the cast, even when
  the spool itself is properly loaded.
- **Stated as reel-type-specific:** learning a spinning reel with
  eight-carrier braid is a different discipline from learning a baitcasting
  reel — this habit is the spinning-reel side of that difference.

## The stated cause

Casting, then leaving the reel in free spool with slack line blowing around
in the wind **before coming tight**, produces wind knots "regardless of line
twist, regardless of anything that's going on" — i.e. this cause operates
independently of the spool-mechanism causes logged in the sibling note above.

## The fix — the moment the lure lands

- **As soon as the lure lands, close the bail** (put the reel in gear) —
  don't leave it in free spool while the line blows around.
- **Sweep the rod to the side to come tight**, so that:
  - the line between the reel and the first guide is tight, and
  - the line from the tip-top to the lure is tight.
- **Then sink it out** — let the lure sink under that tension, rather than
  free-spooling slack line down to it.
