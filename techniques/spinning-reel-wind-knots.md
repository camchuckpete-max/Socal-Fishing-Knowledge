# Spinning-Reel Wind Knots — Close the Bail and Come Tight Immediately

Closing the bail and coming tight to the lure the instant a cast lands
prevents the wind knots a spinning reel loaded with braid gets when slack
line is left blowing in the wind before the retrieve starts (`WeabGOvhgto`).
The cause is casting-side, distinct from the spool-loading causes in
[spooling line — tension and twist](../tackle/spooling-line-tension-and-twist.md):
slack blowing loose ties itself into a knot regardless of line twist or
anything else going on (`WeabGOvhgto`). A second, construction-side factor
compounds the problem independently of casting technique — eight-carrier
braid under 6-lb test wind-knots almost no matter what (`w5iryVkSe-0`).
Mastering the habit is what lets a spinning outfit run light, high-quality
eight-carrier braid for its full presentation without giving the line up to
wind knots (`nlrSBi_hrrg`).

## Reach for this when

- Fishing a spinning reel loaded with braid — eight-carrier is the tested
  example (`WeabGOvhgto`) — and getting wind knots after the cast even
  though the spool itself is loaded correctly.
- Learning a spinning reel running eight-carrier braid is a different
  discipline from learning a baitcasting reel; this habit is the
  spinning-reel side of that difference (`WeabGOvhgto`).

## The fix — the moment the lure lands

- Close the bail as soon as the lure lands — don't leave the reel in free
  spool while the line blows around (`WeabGOvhgto`).
- Sweep the rod to the side to come tight, so the line between the reel and
  the first guide is tight and the line from the tip-top to the lure is
  tight (`WeabGOvhgto`).
- Sink the lure out under that tension rather than free-spooling slack line
  down to it (`WeabGOvhgto`).
- Don't wind the loose slack straight onto the spool either: after the cast
  there's a bit of loose line hanging below the first guide, and closing the
  bail and winding it on without first coming tight lets it wrap in
  underneath the spool — a bigger tangle than the wind knot itself
  (`cAZRaGB_RRM`).
- Two ways to take up that slack before winding normally: close the bail,
  grab the line taut just above the normal fishing hand position, and wind
  the loose line tight until it comes tight against the lure before
  releasing and continuing to wind — or simply raise the rod tip to take up
  the loose line, then wind and continue fishing (`cAZRaGB_RRM`).

## Recovery — freeing a loop that already formed

Distinct from the prevention habit above: this is the by-hand fix once loose
line is already hanging after a cast with a loop already in it, worked free
with no tools (`ntS17IEKyJ0`).

- Close the bail first, then grab the line just above the reel handle and
  wind it in under a little tension (`ntS17IEKyJ0`).
- When a loop is reached, don't wind it onto the spool as is — pull it apart
  by hand while continuing to wind under tension, so no twist or knot goes
  into the reel (`ntS17IEKyJ0`).
- If a loop hangs up and won't pull apart right away, work it loose by hand
  before continuing to wind, rather than forcing it onto the spool
  (`ntS17IEKyJ0`).
- Once the line comes tight against the lure, let go and fish as normal
  (`ntS17IEKyJ0`).

This complements the dental-pick fix in [reel & rod maintenance — on-the-water
tackle box](../tackle/reel-maintenance.md#on-the-water-tackle-box--field-tools-and-repairs)
(for a loop that's already cinched down tight and needs picking loose) and
[clearing a backlash](clearing-a-backlash.md) (for a full baitcaster/
conventional bird's nest, not an isolated spinning-reel loop).

## Braid construction — carrier count and breaking strength

A construction-side cause sits alongside the casting-side cause above and the
spool-setup causes in the sibling spooling note: wind knots are "nearly
impossible" to prevent on eight-carrier braid rated below 6-lb test, and the
failure gets worse as an eight-carrier line gets lighter than that
(`w5iryVkSe-0`). The stated fix is stepping down to a four-carrier braid
instead — a 5-lb, .4 PE, four-carrier line (Major Craft Dangan) run on a
500- or 1,000-size spinning reel (`w5iryVkSe-0`). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus
source: mechanism for why carrier count interacts with breaking strength to
drive wind-knot frequency.

The claim is demonstrated for trout on light freshwater spinning gear
(`w5iryVkSe-0`); a SoCal saltwater extension — e.g. finesse spinning for
[bay bass](../lures/bay-bass-plastics.md) — is not stated by the source, only
plausible from the same carrier-count/breaking-strength mechanism, and no
other source in this KB corroborates it. See [hollow vs solid
spectra](../tackle/spectra-hollow-vs-solid.md) for the broader carrier-count
construction axis (that note's kelp-cutting context doesn't cover this
wind-knot claim).

## Gear class

Applies to any [spinning reel](../tackle/gear-classes.md) loaded with braid —
the light-spinning and minnow-bait spinning classes both commonly run
eight-carrier braid and get the same habit. A baitcast or conventional
(revolving-spool) reel doesn't wind-knot the same way; see [clearing a
backlash](clearing-a-backlash.md) for that reel type's own tangle recovery
instead.

## Common failures

- Free-spooling the post-cast slack straight onto the spool instead of
  coming tight first — it wraps in underneath the spool and creates a bigger
  tangle than an ordinary wind knot (`cAZRaGB_RRM`).
- Winding a loop straight onto the spool instead of working it apart by hand
  under tension — the loop cinches into a knot inside the reel instead of
  clearing (`ntS17IEKyJ0`).
- Running eight-carrier braid under 6-lb test — wind knots become "nearly
  impossible" to prevent as the line gets lighter, independent of casting
  technique (`w5iryVkSe-0`).
