# Live-Bait Pendulum Cast

The cast for getting an **unweighted**, fly-lined bait out and away from the
boat: with no sinker to load the rod, a pendulum swing supplies the momentum
weight would otherwise provide (`Zhn-VDrlaLM`). A companion technique to
[underhand casting](underhand-casting.md) (a simpler lob, typically fished
with some weight in play) and distinct from the [surface iron pendulum
cast](surface-iron-casting.md#mechanics) (an iron, swung for maximum
distance rather than a short toss).

## Reach for this when

- You're [flylining](flyline.md) live bait — **no weight** on the line — and
  need a swing to send the bait out, since there's no sinker to help load
  the cast.
- You want the bait landing **away from the boat** rather than close to the
  hull, without muscling a long overhead throw.
- Deciding overhead vs. the low swing below — see [Hook position decides
  which cast to use](#hook-position-decides-which-cast-to-use).

## Handling the bait before the cast

Don't hold the bait tightly while hooking it — a common casting error
(`Zhn-VDrlaLM`). Hook it (e.g. sideways through the nose), then **let go of
the bait and hold the line above the hook** instead of continuing to grip
it. Less handling means a stronger bait going into the water.

The same hold-the-line-not-the-bait principle appears in [fishing live
bait](../bait/fishing-live-bait.md).

## Mechanics — the pendulum

- **Match bait height to the reel.** Let line out until the bait hangs at
  about the same height as the reel before starting the swing (`Zhn-VDrlaLM`).
- **Free-spool, with a light thumb on the spool** (`Zhn-VDrlaLM`).
- **Swing it in toward you, then let it swing back out** — a pendulum
  motion. A gentle in-and-out swing sends the bait roughly **20 ft** away
  from the boat; a strong, lively bait keeps swimming from there on its own
  (`Zhn-VDrlaLM`).
- **Fish it from the windward side of the boat**, wind in your face, and
  account for it in the swing (`Zhn-VDrlaLM`).
- **A second distance benchmark and a shore-practice drill** (`tpmOYXYQwhU`):
  the stated target is **25 to 30 ft** out from the boat so the bait swims
  into the bite zone — a further data point alongside, not a correction of,
  the ~20 ft figure above (different source, same order of magnitude).
  Getting there takes practice: rehearse the cast at home — in the backyard
  or an alley — using a **clothespin or a couple-ounce torpedo sinker** in
  place of a live bait, on **20 or 25 lb test**, to dial in the casting
  motion before it has to work on an actual fragile bait.

## Overhead variant

Same setup (bait at reel height, free spool, light thumb on the spool), but
the pendulum swings up and over the head instead of staying low
(`Zhn-VDrlaLM`):

- Swing the bait in toward you, then pendulum it **up and over the top of
  your head**.
- **Follow the bait down with the tip of the rod** until it hits the water.

## Hook position decides which cast to use

- **Nose-hooked or shoulder-hooked bait:** overhead casting is fine
  (`Zhn-VDrlaLM`).
- **Belly-hooked bait: do not overhead cast it** — the bait rips off the
  hook. Use the low, underhand-style pendulum swing instead (in toward you,
  then out, roughly 20 ft from the boat). A belly-hooked bait fished this
  way should swim down, ideally into the bite zone (`Zhn-VDrlaLM`).

This sits alongside the hook-position doctrine in
[flyline](flyline.md#hook-position-by-situation) (a belly hook sends the
bait down and away) — this note adds the casting-style consequence of that
hook choice.

## Gear class

Same [flyline / soft-rod class](../tackle/gear-classes.md) outfit used for
any flylined bait — no dedicated rod or reel for the pendulum cast itself. A
soft, moderate tip loads through the swing without whiplashing the bait off
the hook or stunning it dead on landing (`48ZFXnCTTQE`; see [flyline — gear
class](flyline.md#gear-class)). The shore-practice drill above is run on
**20 or 25 lb test** (`tpmOYXYQwhU`).

## Common failures

- **Gripping the bait while hooking it**, rather than letting go and holding
  the line above the hook — more handling than the bait needs, weakening it
  before the cast (`Zhn-VDrlaLM`).
- **Overhead-casting a belly-hooked bait** — rips it off the hook; use the
  low pendulum swing instead (`Zhn-VDrlaLM`).
- **Not matching bait height to the reel** before starting the swing —
  throws off the pendulum's timing (`Zhn-VDrlaLM`).
- **Skipping practice before it has to work on a live bait** — the drill
  (a clothespin or a couple-ounce torpedo sinker, 20–25 lb test, rehearsed at
  home) exists to dial the motion in before it has to land a fragile live
  bait without stunning or tearing it off the hook (`tpmOYXYQwhU`).
