# Live-Bait Pendulum Cast

The cast for getting an **unweighted**, fly-lined bait out and away from the
boat: with no sinker to load the rod, a pendulum swing supplies the momentum
weight would otherwise provide. A companion technique to [underhand
casting](underhand-casting.md) (a simpler lob, typically fished with some
weight in play) and distinct from the [surface iron pendulum
cast](surface-iron-casting.md#mechanics) (an iron, swung for maximum
distance rather than a short toss).

## Reach for this when

- You're [flylining](flyline.md) live bait — **no weight** on the line — and
  need a swing to send the bait out, since there's no sinker to help load
  the cast.
- You want the bait landing **away from the boat** rather than close to the
  hull, without muscling a long overhead throw.
- Deciding overhead vs. the low swing below — see the hook-position rule.

## Handling the bait before the cast

Don't hold the bait tightly while hooking it — named as a common error.
Hook it (e.g. sideways through the nose), then **let go of the bait and hold
the line above the hook** instead of continuing to grip the bait. Less
handling means a stronger bait going into the water.

This is the same hold-the-line-not-the-bait mechanic already logged from two
earlier Searcher Tackle Tip Thursday episodes in [fishing live
bait](../bait/fishing-live-bait.md) — a third occurrence of the doctrine from
the same registered channel/series, which the source registry's
repeated-doctrine rule reaches `high` confidence for.

## Mechanics — the pendulum

- **Match bait height to the reel.** Let line out until the bait hangs at
  about the same height as the reel before starting the swing.
- **Free-spool, with a light thumb on the spool.**
- **Swing it in toward you, then let it swing back out** — think of a
  pendulum. A gentle in-and-out swing sends the bait roughly **20 ft** away
  from the boat. A strong, lively bait will keep swimming from there on its
  own.
- **Wind check:** demonstrated fished from the **windward side** of the
  boat, wind in your face — account for it in the swing.

## Overhead variant

Same setup (bait at reel height, free spool, light thumb on the spool), but
the pendulum swings up and over the head instead of staying low:

- Swing the bait in toward you, then pendulum it **up and over the top of
  your head**.
- **Follow the bait down with the tip of the rod** until it hits the water.

## Hook position decides which cast to use

- **Nose-hooked or shoulder-hooked bait:** overhead casting is fine.
- **Belly-hooked bait: do not overhead cast it** — the bait will rip off the
  hook. Use the low, underhand-style pendulum swing instead (in toward you,
  then out, roughly 20 ft from the boat). A belly-hooked bait fished this
  way should swim down, ideally into the bite zone.

This sits alongside the hook-position doctrine in
[flyline](flyline.md#hook-position-by-situation) (a belly hook sends the
bait down and away) — this note adds the casting-style consequence of that
hook choice.
