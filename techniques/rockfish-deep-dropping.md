# Rockfish Deep-Dropping

Put a bait or jig on hard bottom in deep water, hold the zone against the drift,
and keep off the snag. This note owns the execution — bait rigs, jig cadence,
weight, and reading the meter; the reverse map of species is in **Linked from**.

## Reach for this when

- [Rockfish and lingcod](../species/rockfish-lingcod.md) on deep structure —
  reds, bocaccio (salmon grouper), coppers, lings, whitefish, and the Channel
  Islands variety (blue bass, johnny bass, sheephead) that come with them.
- You want harvest fish off a marked hard-bottom spot without carrying bait —
  a rolled-up gangion and a few sinkers live in the rod locker, ready any trip.
- For a sportier presentation on the same fish, see
  [slow-pitch jigging](slow-pitch-jigging.md).

## Rigs — three ways to fish the bottom

- **Dropper-loop gangion** (bait/Gulp): 40 lb main line, **two dropper loops
  ~1.5 ft apart**, a small swivel at each end so it rolls up clean. Add **6/0
  Gamakatsu octopus circle** hooks looped on, then thread a **5 in Gulp grub**
  (nuclear chicken / gold) onto each like a swimbait — the tail wafts and the
  scent fishes as well as live bait even after the tail's chewed off. See the
  [dropper-loop](dropper-loop.md) note for tying the loops. Below the bottom
  hook, tie **~1 ft of lighter 12–15 lb line** to the sinker as a breakaway.
- **Shrimp fly / bait gangion:** same idea with tied flies over squid or Gulp when
  you want to sort species or the bite wants a smaller offering.
- **Leadhead + plastic (jigging):** a 3 oz leadhead with a swimbait/grub for
  shallower deep-drops (100–300 ft), or an 8 oz jig head + 8 in Gulp grub for the
  bigger reds. A free-swinging head fishes suspended fish; a fixed head lets you
  drop through a red already limited-out without hooking it, then sink the rest of
  the way to a copper or ling on the bottom.

## Weight & staying off the snag

- **Thin braid, not heavy** — **30 lb braid** (a fathom-400 / squall-20 level-wind
  holds plenty for 600–900 ft) sinks straight with far less scope than the old
  80 lb Dacron; you can hold bottom in 300 ft with a 3 oz head. A **24 oz torpedo
  sinker** gets a gangion down and holds it — no need to carry a range of weights.
- **Breakaway sinker** (the light dropper above) means a snagged sinker breaks off
  alone and you keep the gangion, hooks, and fish.
- On a jig, keep **light thumb tension on the spool** during the drop so the jig
  falls straight and fast rather than scoping away; ease off near the bottom to let
  it flutter into the zone.

## Jig cadence

- Drop to the bottom, put the reel in gear, **wind 1–3 turns up out of the rocks**,
  then work **big sweeps of the rod, letting the jig flutter back down**, keeping
  bottom contact. On a slack-line drop, most bites come **on the first fall or
  right when you kick it back into gear** off the bottom.
- These fish chase up — reds and lings will come **30 ft off the bottom** to eat.
  Bring the jig as far as ~30 ft up, then flutter it back down. On a short-biting
  ling, drop straight back to the bottom and start hopping again — they'll chase
  it back down and re-eat.

## Line class and spooling (Dave Hansen)

A second, independently attributed line-class figure for the same problem
(Dave Hansen, Your Saltwater Guide, zVIfArUrpDI, 2021-06-15; dave-hansen
registered voice, single mention — medium confidence), for a **rock-cod
quick gangion** built and fished at **400–600 ft**:

- **Fill the reel to the rim with braid.** A reel that isn't spooled full
  reads as "out of line" once you're fishing — you want every inch of
  capacity available for the drop and the fight.
- **Line class by reel size:** **40 lb braid** on a smaller reel, **65 lb
  braid** on a bigger reel — a different pairing from the **30 lb braid**
  figure already logged above (a different captain, not the same channel
  updating its own doctrine); preserved side by side rather than reconciled.
  He notes 40 lb braid is already hard to break by hand — a break-off on
  40 lb reads as a genuine snag, not an undersized line problem.
- **Why braid over mono at this depth (mechanism):** monofilament "has a
  tendency to want to float," so at 400–600 ft it won't get a bait straight
  down to the fish fast enough — and the spots fished are often very small, so
  a slow, scoped drop can miss the zone entirely on the drift. See
  [line & leader](../tackle/line-and-leader.md#when-braid--and-how-heavy) for
  the general braid-vs-mono buoyancy breakdown this sits alongside.
- **No hookset on the bite** — consistent with the slow-steady-wind retrieve
  already logged above (U-dGRQ0X-Mc): on a self-hooking circle hook, you
  don't swing, you just turn the handle and wind up the fish.

## Reading the meter (Furuno bottom discrimination)

- Approach the spot on the chartplotter, flip on the sonar, and idle onto the
  structure — many spots are a **distinct hump coming up 20–30 ft** off the
  surrounding bottom. Drop your autopilot on it and mark a **waypoint**, then work
  the **track line** back and forth.
- Run a **dual-frequency split screen.** **Low frequency** paints everything and
  runs gainy — good for the bait cloud sitting up over the hump. **High frequency**
  distinguishes the game fish: **arches over hard bottom** are your rockfish. Fish
  the hard-bottom "highways" where you see arches, not the open bait cloud. See
  [electronics and sounder](../planning/electronics-and-sounder.md) for the general
  method.
- Islands note: current matters as much as bait — a spot with squid but no
  [current](../conditions/current-diagnostics.md) fishes soft; combine a
  dropper loop with a [sliding sinker](sliding-sinker.md) to cover the whole
  column when marks are scattered.

## Common failures

- **Heavy braid / too much sinker** — scopes out, drags off the spot, snags more.
  Go thin and just heavy enough to hold bottom.
- **No breakaway** — one snag costs the whole rig instead of a sinker.
- **Fishing the bait cloud instead of the hard bottom** — stop and drop only on
  marks you can grade as fish over structure.
