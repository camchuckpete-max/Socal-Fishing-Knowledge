# Ned Rig

Execution for the Ned rig in the bays: a **small soft stickbait on a
mushroom-head jig, hopped subtly along the bottom, with the bite read in the
line before you feel it in the rod.** A finesse presentation for pressured,
not-giant bay fish that refuse bigger, faster baits. Where these fish sit in
the [bays and harbors](../locations/bays-and-harbors.md) is the router; this
note owns the rig and the cadence.

## Reach for this when

- **Pressured [spotted bay bass](../species/spotted-bay-bass.md)** in the back
  bay — and anything else bottom-oriented that bites; the rig catches
  "basically anything" (`um5MAeCjNDg`).
- You want a **finesse** presentation: small profile, light line, subtle action
  for fish that won't commit to a full-size swimbait.

## Building the rig

- **Two parts only:** a **mushroom-style Ned head** (a small lead head) and a
  small soft bait — a **ZMan finesse TRD** is a favorite because the elastic
  plastic **stretches and survives — you can catch 50 fish on one bait**
  (`um5MAeCjNDg`), which matters when the back bay hands you fish after fish.
- The TRD has a **top and a bottom**: it is **slightly thinner toward the top
  and tapers to a thicker bottom** (which helps it sink better), and markings
  on top show where the hook exits. Thread it by **stretching the plastic
  farther than you normally would, then bringing it back** so it seats
  straight on the head (`um5MAeCjNDg`).
- Fish it on **light spinning: 10 lb braid, 8 lb leader, a 4-to-10 lb rod**
  (`um5MAeCjNDg`). The fish aren't giants — if you hook a big one, **let it run
  and play it out** on the light tackle.
- **A second combo for the same TRD-on-Ned-head presentation:** a 6'6"
  medium-heavy, fast-action spinning rod (rated 8–15 lb line, 3/16–3/4 oz lure
  weight) and a 2500-class spinning reel, 10 lb mainline to a 10 lb
  fluorocarbon leader (`6L8nIFeqvkw`).

## Cadence — and watch the line

- **Let it sink all the way to the bottom, watching your line the whole way
  down.** Pick up the slack once it lands (`um5MAeCjNDg`).
- **Bounce it** off the bottom, then **let it fall back to the bottom** — a
  subtle hop-and-drop, not a steady swim; the TRD has no tail, so a straight
  retrieve doesn't work it (`um5MAeCjNDg`).
- **Read the bite in your line.** You **see the bite in the line before you
  feel it in the rod** — when the line **taps and then starts moving off
  faster**, that's the eat (`um5MAeCjNDg`).
- **Close the bail the instant the bait hits the water** — don't let it
  free-spool to bottom. Bites often come on the initial drop, and
  free-spooling misses them; engaging the reel immediately makes the bait
  fall at a slight angle instead of straight down, so a fish can still be
  felt taking it on that angled fall. The line — straight from rod tip to the
  surface while it's falling — visibly bows once the bait reaches bottom
  (`6L8nIFeqvkw`).
- **Two retrieve styles once it's down** (`6L8nIFeqvkw`):
  - **Reel-only hop:** hold the rod level to slightly up (~20°) and barely
    move it; give the reel a **half crank** to lift the bait off bottom, let
    it drop back, and let it sit 2–4 seconds before the next half crank.
    Speed up to a full or double crank to cover more water when the
    half-crank pace isn't producing.
  - **Rod-lift-and-slack-pickup:** in grass, or wherever the bait keeps
    snagging, lift the rod tip (roughly 20° up to 45–80° depending on how big
    a hop you want) to pop the bait up and clear of the grass, then take up
    the resulting slack with the reel as it falls — this pulls the bait up
    and out of standing grass instead of dragging it through, and doubles as
    a way to feel out where the grass actually is.
- **Don't work the last quarter to third of the retrieve.** Once the bait is
  coming up off bottom toward the boat near the end of a cast, stop imparting
  action and just wind it in — bites concentrate on bottom contact, not on
  the rise back to the boat, so working that last stretch mostly costs time
  (`6L8nIFeqvkw`).

## Bite detection & hookset

- **Don't set the hook — reel into it.** On light line with a thin-wire Ned
  hook, swinging on a tap rips the hook out of the fish's mouth, bends it
  out, or snaps the line. Instead, start reeling; the fish loads the rod and
  hooks itself. By the time you feel the tap-tap, the fish is usually
  already hooked and you're just winding it up (`6L8nIFeqvkw`).
- **Set the drag below your line's rated strength** — e.g., on 10 lb line,
  run the drag around 7–8 lb — so an instinctive hard hookset can't snap the
  line outright. Contrasts with a heavier-tackle habit (65 lb braid to 15 lb
  fluorocarbon, drag near max, swing hard on the bite) that doesn't transfer
  to this finesse gear (`6L8nIFeqvkw`).
- **Learn the difference between bottom contact and a bite** by keeping the
  bait on the bottom and feeling out the normal nicks, bumps, and scrapes of
  rock and eelgrass — a bite reads as the bait suddenly *not* touching bottom
  anymore, or as a different kind of tap than a snag (`6L8nIFeqvkw`).

## Gear class

[Light spinning / finesse-bottom class](../tackle/gear-classes.md) — light
spinning on 10 lb-class braid keeps the tiny TRD casting well and matches
the thin-wire Ned hook; heavier tackle fished with the drag near max invites
a hard hookset that tears the hook out or bends it, exactly what the
drag-below-rating and reel-into-it rules above are working around
(`6L8nIFeqvkw`). Heads and plastics in
[bay bass plastics](../lures/bay-bass-plastics.md).

## Common failures

- **Retrieving it like a swimbait** — the tailless TRD wants a bottom hop,
  not a straight wind.
- **Not watching the line** — the take is visual and light; wait to feel it
  in the rod and you miss it.
- **Rigging it crooked or on the wrong end** — seat it off the thicker
  bottom with the hook exiting the marked top so it sinks and sits right.
- **Setting the hook on the tap** — on thin-wire hooks and light line, a
  hard swing rips the hook free or snaps the leader; reel into the bite
  instead.
