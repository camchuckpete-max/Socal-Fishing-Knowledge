# Evidence — Yo-Yo Iron

Trip reports and per-source provenance backing [yo-yo iron](../yo-yo-iron.md).
One line per observation or source, grouped under the parent note's section
headings, in its order. Observations never change the parent's stated
doctrine; a contradicting one is also traced beside that doctrine in the
parent.

## Reach for this when

- `ntQXxcH5sjI` (StokedOnFishing, "Deep Dive into Yo-Yo Fishing," 2024-08-29,
  Tanner Bank, SoCal): a bluefin bite on the drop read as the rod suddenly
  going heavy — "almost like it got stuck on the bottom" — just before
  hookup; unregistered channel — medium.

## Mechanics

- `ecJPMTCi-gw` (StokedOnFishing, 2019-06-19, San Clemente Island, SoCal):
  switching to a smaller yo-yo iron drew a yellowtail bite the same session
  that surface iron up top and yo-yo iron down both produced, with a Simrad
  sounder showing fish holding below even as some anglers got bit on the
  surface; no bait-size trigger stated. One iron named on camera renders as
  "a little tatty nine" (asr-uncertain — possibly a Tady 9, consistent with
  the Tady→"Tatty" caption-corruption pattern in this corpus); unregistered
  channel — medium.
- `HQC-NwW4018` (Tackle Express, "Colt Sniper or Yo-Yo Jig: Which one lands
  more fish?," 2026-05-12): on a slower first day of the same trip, bites
  favored smaller-profile baits — the Colt Sniper and a Salas 6X Jr. are
  named as the smaller-profile picks that produced; no reason given for the
  size preference. asr-uncertain: the auto-captioned sentence tying the two
  lures together ("They were more keyed in on the Colt Sniper baits, your
  Salas 6X Jr., so your smaller profile") is garbled; read as both lures
  cited together as the smaller-profile options that outproduced bigger
  profiles that day; unregistered channel — medium.

## Cadence variants

  Salas 6X Jr (captioned "little Sal 6X Junior") as free-spool to the bottom,
  crank hard ("reel like the devil") about halfway up, out of gear and fall
  back to the bottom, crank hard about halfway again, fall back a second

  unambiguously identified on camera (captain Bobby Tapp/Taft or host Shay

## Common failures

- `HTowqnwAMeA` (Nate Winnicki, BDOutdoors): `nate-winnicki` registered
  voice, first commit of this claim — medium; the light-current/small-jig vs
  wind-or-current/upsize sizing rule and its stated time-budget mechanism —
  "you don't want to spend the majority of your time in free spool waiting
  for your lure to hit bottom when you could be actively working up and down
  in a zone."
- `6Z6Dht20kTA` (Tackle Express, 2026-03-24): unregistered channel — medium;
  the don't-jig-in-place-inside-a-marked-school doctrine — let the jig reach
  bottom and bounce it there, or burn it up on a fast continuous retrieve,
  rather than working it with an active continuous motion in place.
- `S0-BCQWA0H4` (Tackle Express, "Stop JIGGING Suspended Fish! Pro Blade Bait
  Secret Revealed," 2026-03-14, ten days before the `6Z6Dht20kTA` clip above;
  same-channel re-statement, not independent confirmation): unregistered
  channel — medium; the same rule framed for a school picked up on
  up-and-down or forward-facing sonar — thumb the spool and drop as fast as
  possible into the column, go through the school to the bottom first, then
  either rip it up or reel very fast (like a yo-yo) back through the column
  and let it fall again to repeat.
- `SH5CZQi9ATw` (Tackle Express, "Turn Slow Days into Limit Days: The Secret
  to Firing Up Dormant Fish!," 2026-03-23): unregistered channel — medium;
  the stirring-a-dormant-stacked-mark mechanism behind the rip-it-up/burn
  fix — a heavy mark held at the thermocline may not be a true, cohesive
  school, just where fish are stacked/holding; casting through and ripping
  the jig stirs the fish into chasing. Two practical add-ons: put two or
  three anglers on the boat working the same retrieve at once to multiply
  the stirring effect, and keep the lures in the water rather than pulling up
  between drops.
- `Ob45hK4HSWc` (Jared Saaib, Strictly Irons, 2019-02-07): registered voice,
  single session — medium; a yellowtail day planned around yo-yo iron and
  surface iron was abandoned to bonito — "we tried yo-yo fishing, didn't go
  well — a lot of bonitos." The fix was not a different iron: dead sardines
  got the yellowtail going, and the crew finished hand-lining them once the
  bite went wide open. The fish in question were sitting on tuna pens,
  straight down at about 120 ft — see [yellowtail](../../species/yellowtail.md)
  for the tuna-pen row, including the unresolved question of which pens.
