# Evidence — Fighting a Big Bluefin

Trip reports and per-source provenance backing
[fighting a big bluefin](../fighting-big-bluefin.md). One line per
observation, grouped under the parent note's section headings, in its order.
Observations never change the parent's stated doctrine; a contradicting one is
also traced beside that doctrine in the parent.

## The gear-tap: winching the fish in with the boat

- `xzIaUEDklrE` (StokedOnFishing, 2022-12-14, ~55 mi offshore SoCal;
  unregistered channel): a fish hooked trolling a Mad Mac — the crew's own
  estimate that ~80% of the fight ran on the bump-in/bump-out cadence,
  watching rod-tip tension as the cue; once the fish came straight up and
  down under the boat, the crew pulled the rod from the holder and hand-fought
  the rest ("railroad it" — the final-stretch sense of the term). Going to
  neutral was also driven by keeping the line clear of the props.
- `mDmbGdQAy-4` (StokedOnFishing, 2025-10-06 upload, SoCal, NE of Santa
  Barbara Island, "Catching Monster Bluefin Using a Flying Bait"; unregistered
  channel; same operation/trip as the `usHl-4SfqDA` rail-mechanic entry below,
  likely overlapping footage): names the whole beltless, boat-leverage,
  rail-locked style "railroading" — a different sense of the word than the
  final-hand-fought-stretch-only sense in the `xzIaUEDklrE` entry above.

## Walk it down-swell, avoid the death circle

- `8jC61LzQoxU` (Your Saltwater Guide / Dave Hansen, "How to Fight a Giant
  Bluefin | Yummy Flyer," 2018-08-07; dave-hansen registered voice — high
  confidence): the doctrine source for the gear-tap, down-swell walk,
  death-circle avoidance, head-shake doctrine, and gaff hand-off. States the
  program's average fight (~15 min, 25 min longest) and that a 218 lb fish,
  landed 2018-08-05 (two days before upload), took 17 min — attributed to the
  constant down-swell walk.
- `rsCAh-QyK60` (StokedOnFishing, 2017-06-06, ~40 mi off San Diego, aboard a
  22 ft center console; unregistered channel): fighting a big bluefin doing
  "giant circles," the crew's on-camera framing was that a smaller,
  maneuverable boat (their 22 ft center console) works a circling fish better
  than a yacht or bigger long-range boat, which they characterized as "racing
  around" rather than staying with the fish — a boat-class opinion, not
  independently corroborated.
- `84XPJAeH0Rw` (StokedOnFishing, 2019-02-10, East Cape, Sea of Cortez, Baja
  California Sur, Hotel Buena Vista Beach Resort trip; unregistered channel):
  species/weight not confirmed on camera as the SoCal bluefin grade (the
  day's tuna recapped as mostly 60s–70s lb). Coaching treated a fish circling
  boatside as the normal end-of-fight pattern — "circle them up... bring them
  around this way, pull them back and reel" through two more circles, cue
  that the fish was ready being that no more line was going out. No
  boat-driving, drag, or line class given; no bite-through/break-off outcome
  reported.
- `fgTmUq78ofQ` (Lori Sackow, Turner's Outdoorsman, via SearcherSportfishing,
  "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 147 — Live at Turner's Outdoorsman," 2021-05-14;
  `searcher-sportfishing` registered channel, single mention — medium
  confidence): rod-length-as-death-circle-cause framing, with the >=7.5 ft
  sportboat-minimum fix and the ~8 ft 2 in upper bound before the rod becomes
  unwieldy ("seesaw") for the angler.
- `9JnIS8HkZlA` (Steve Carson, Penn, San Diego Rod and Reel Club, 2023-11-14;
  unregistered channel/speaker, single mention — medium confidence):
  reel-side counter-move on a two-speed reel — continuous low-gear winding
  instead of pump-and-reel — cross-referenced in full at
  [two-speed low-gear fight](../two-speed-low-gear-fight.md).
- `xzIaUEDklrE` (StokedOnFishing, 2022-12-14, ~55 mi offshore SoCal;
  unregistered channel): boatside rail positioning once the fish was close —
  rod butt tucked under the armpit, pull as the boat travels down the back of
  a swell, ease off as it rises on the next.
- `usHl-4SfqDA` (StokedOnFishing, 2024-11-13, SoCal, NE of Santa Barbara
  Island, "Fishing, Cleaning & Cooking Pacific Giants"; unregistered channel):
  second, independent trip corroborating the same rail mechanic, called
  "wrap system pipes" at the rail on this boat — rod butt under the left
  armpit, squat down and stand up using the boat as leverage, no fighting
  belt/harness used ("you're off the belt"). Two visiting East Coast anglers
  used to a harness/belt found the beltless style "awkward" on their first
  fish; one wedged the rod between his legs instead of under the armpit.
  Reel on this program's kite/fighting outfits: Okuma Makaira 50 Wide.
- `vCskOx6N-XM` (StokedOnFishing, 2022-03-23 upload, trip resolved to late
  October 2021, Tanner Bank, aboard Captain Andrew's own boat, Stoked On
  Fishing Charters; unregistered channel): third, independent rail-fight data
  point — live coaching for anglers new to bluefin: kneel, rod butt under the
  left armpit, left hand on the reel, hold still as the boat rises on a
  swell, crank as it drops; even a half-crank on the way down still counts as
  gained line. With a heavier-than-usual swell running, anglers were coached
  to pace themselves and save energy rather than force it.

## Handing off a tiring angler

- `_c6UI3lGBVg` (StokedOnFishing, 2019-02-18, East Cape, Sea of Cortez, Baja
  California Sur, near Cabo (exact locality asr-uncertain), Hotel Buena Vista
  Beach Resort trip, "Non-Stop Tuna And Dorado Part 2"; unregistered channel):
  live mid-fight coaching on a big tuna — "if you have to hand off, hand
  off... don't screw around with that, these fish are big and they beat you
  down." Same session: a small (sardine-bait-sized) hook pulled from a big
  tuna mid-fight, framed as a known risk that steered the crew back toward

## Sea lion depredation at the boat — the free-spool trick (cross-species)

- `Klfb433I3Uk` (StokedOnFishing, 2019-10-04, San Clemente Island, "A Day of
  San Clemente Island Yellowtail Fishing"; unregistered channel): preventive
  tactics — get a seal looking the wrong way and fish from the opposite side;
  put the rod down out of sight once a fish is on, on the belief seals read a
  bent rod as a hooked fish. Preemptive free-spool coached live to an angler
  ("Odyssey") with a seal near his line; outcome not confirmed on camera
  ("we'll see").
- `d0yGBQDeY_4` (StokedOnFishing, 2019-03-05, Cedros Island, Baja, "Big
  Yellowtail Part 1"; unregistered channel): a yellowtail already boated and
  being bled over the rail was grabbed by a sea lion ("the Lobo") and pulled
  back into the water — "you took it right out of his hand" (00:21:45). No
  free-spool counter-move applies once a fish is off the line.
- `6EDQtQHEwFE` (StokedOnFishing, October 2024 trip per on-camera reference,
  2025-03-07 upload, Cedros Island, Baja, "This Year's Yellowtail Bite Has
  Been the Best Ever"; unregistered channel): a hooked yellowtail grabbed
  boatside by a seal through the gut before it reached the boat — fish lost;
  no counter-move described.

## Doctrine & conflicts

- `mdhoEQPqpng` (Your Saltwater Guide / Dave Hansen, "Yellowtail Tuna BITE!
  Sea Lion Tries to STEAL Fish," 2019-11-07; dave-hansen registered voice):
  live coaching mid-fight — wind continuously, never pump, since every pump
  pauses the fish's progress and lets the sea lion grab it; once the sea lion
  had the fish by the tail, free-spool/give total slack, which reads to the
  sea lion as the line breaking; fish landed.
- `nsUdT-zXI8s` (StokedOnFishing, 2022-12-07 upload, front side of San
  Clemente Island, veterans-charity overnight trip aboard the El Dorado;
  unregistered channel): opposite live coaching — keep enough pressure on a
  hooked yellowtail to make the sea lion drop it, then wind fast; the sea
  lion won this instance, fish lost.
- `XwwIvPFxRiQ` (StokedOnFishing, 2015-01-17, Cedros Island, Baja;
  unregistered channel): a seal grabbed a hooked yellowtail by the tail; the
  angler kept winding (no free-spool/slack move); the seal let go once,
  bringing the fish to near gaff range, then came back and took it — fish
  lost. Crew moved off the spot despite the bite staying active.

## Mola mola (sunfish) interference

- `sHnqSIOjTdM` (StokedOnFishing, August trip (year not stated), 2026-04-24
  upload, offshore San Clemente Island, "Night Bluefin to Day Yellowtail to
  Night Bluefin," 2.5-day Eldorado charter; unregistered channel): incident 1
  — a fish cranked in fast was bombarded by a mola mola near the boat and the
  hook pulled; crew's own read was that bringing it up too fast may have
  drawn the sunfish in, unconfirmed; a "hot 90-pounder" was landed on the next
  hookup at the same stop. Incident 2 — a second-night bluefin fought
  unusually weak ("dead like a kite"), found tail-wrapped at the surface;
  once at the boat it "woke up" and broke 100 lb with one kick before the
  gaff, taking the angler's jig (West Coast Jiggerz) with it; crew again
  blamed a mola mola for harassment through the fight.

## Tough hookup-to-landing ratio on 40 lb tackle, bigger-grade bluefin (Cortez Bank)

- `nQvJnfb5jQ4` (StokedOnFishing, trip dated August 2024, "our annual trip in
  August," part 2 of a multi-part Cortez Bank series, day one of a 2.5-day
  charter aboard the 85 ft El Dorado, Captain TJ Will, out of Long Beach,
  2025-04-30 upload, "2.5 Days. 25 People. A Boat Full of Bluefin Tuna and
  Yellowtail"; unregistered channel): evening ("Sundowner") bite on
  bigger-grade bluefin — ~6 hooked, 1 landed on 40 lb line/tiny circle hook,
  called "really tough" on camera. On the fish lost after a long fight, the
  hook "shot through" the 40 lb and wasn't seated right — described as
  looking like "a firecracker went off." Fishing method (kite vs. flyline)
  not clearly resolved on camera.

## Under-gunned on light tackle vs. a bigger-than-expected grade

- `r4J5nP5Bkl4` (StokedOnFishing, trip framed as "this past June," resolved
  from the 2024-04-26 upload to June 2023, backside San Clemente Island, 85 ft
  El Dorado, Captain TJ, "Summer Bluefin Bite on the ElDorado"; unregistered
  channel): a sundown bite turned up 70–90 lb-plus bluefin against gear set
  for 50–70 lb; multiple "casualties" including at least one leader broken
  boatside; the captain framed it on camera as "not ideal, but we're making
  it work" rather than swapping gear mid-bite. Same session: live coaching
  "spin them up," "get a bend on it," "walk him down" at the rail, and — on
  the trip's night-jig stretch — "wind down when we come down with the
  swell," corroborating the down-swell rail cue logged in the parent note.
