# Evidence — Slow-Pitch Jigging (SPJ)

Per-source provenance backing [slow-pitch jigging](../slow-pitch-jigging.md).

## Reach for this when

- `7T6dIYqr3KI` (SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 141," 2021-04-01; unregistered channel — medium confidence): names sand bass and sculpin, alongside rockfish/lingcod, as small-to-medium fish SPJ is "unbelievable fun and very effective" for. No conditions/situation given beyond the species list. Separately names calico bass — not part of the "very effective" list, but the source's second-biggest-ever personal catch on SPJ gear; see [evidence — calico bass](../../species/evidence/calico-bass.md) for the catch.
- `6SClBs16L2Y` (Tackle Express, 2025-03-10; unregistered channel — medium confidence): general activity-level axis — lethargic/less-active fish call for slow-pitch over speed jigging, offshore or nearshore alike, not tied to one water type. Very likely a re-cut of the fuller 37-minute `ZrcwcugaEe4` interview with Cesar Chang (2025-03-07, three days earlier) — treat the two ids as one source appearance, not independent repetition, for confidence purposes (2026-08-21 source-identification correction).
- `ZrcwcugaEe4` (Cesar Chang, Tackle Express, "Slow Pitch vs. Speed Jigging: Basics & Gear Breakdown with Ceasar Chang!," 2025-03-07; medium confidence): the named kelp-paddy exception — fish present but not actively feeding (likely already full/sated, not lethargic from cold or season) still take a slow-worked jig a speed jig won't draw, on a spot type anglers might otherwise default to speed jigging on reflex.
- `M8hOYQ_6rSg` (Cesar, via Dirty Hookers, 2025-05-20; Cesar is a registered voice, single mention — medium confidence): Baja/Sea of Cortez current-and-activity decision axis (light current/less-active fish → slow-pitch; more current/more-active fish → speed jig).
- `0dIwWiOc1NY` (StokedOnFishing, 2026-02-17 upload, "What It's Like Fishing in Ensenada, Mexico," Pacific side; unregistered channel — medium confidence): after the yellowtail stop went quiet, the crew moved to a sounder-marked rocky bottom drop-off at roughly 300 ft and worked slow-pitch jigs there alongside a second group fishing a "classic" dropper-style dead-bait rig off the rocks — see [rockfish & lingcod](../../species/evidence/rockfish-lingcod.md) Ensenada entry for the catch mix. The on-camera mechanics (drop to bottom, slow half-turns, gentle lifts so the jig flutters down like a wounded baitfish) match this note's standing fall-flutter doctrine and are described as drawing bites from fish not actively chasing fast-moving prey — the same slower/less-active-fish case as the Sea of Cortez entry above, on the Pacific side.

## Mechanics — the pitch and the fall

- `Et2TUskzCSs` (Benny Ortiz, Pacific Coast Sportfishing Show 2024 "Jigging 101" panel, uploaded 2024-03-16; unregistered channel/panel — medium confidence): the handle-turn-counting method (~3 ft/turn = one "pitch") for positioning the jig on a called depth, and the rod-position switch from under-forearm (jigging fulcrum) to under-armpit (fight position) the moment a fish is hooked — the same fight-off-the-reel doctrine elsewhere in this note, restated as a physical rod-position switch, not just a mindset change.
- `-nIhadZwxAA` (Benny Ortiz, CustomRodandReel seminar, 2025-05-20; East Coast/Gulf-based instruction, general SPJ mechanics, not species-specific — unregistered channel, medium confidence): cadence-scales-with-current guidance (0–1 kt small/subtle lifts, 1–4 kt faster/punchier, multi-layer current needs a stronger punchy pitch); the auditory rationale for bouncing bottom (fish hear ~20–2,000 Hz, a multi-hook assist rig doesn't spook by sound, bottom-bounce produces a lateral-line-detectable thud read as curiosity-triggering); the boat-control ranking (locked-in-place > sea anchor > free drift, bow-into-current, repeat offset passes over stacking the same track).
- `M8hOYQ_6rSg`: the reading-the-drop bottom-vs-bite mechanic (line speeds up/slows in rhythm on the sink; a dead stop means bottom or a bite; working up off bottom in half-turn increments) (Cesar, via Dirty Hookers, 2025-05-20; registered voice, single mention — medium confidence).

## Jig weight & selection

- `vqsD0qpwcJA`: BOLA yellowtail zone reference — 150–300 ft on high spots, fishing observed at 150–180 ft, 130–250 g wing-fall jigs eating well. One trip only; a second id this was previously credited to (`Jtf-bU4aM-c`, see front matter) is the same recording re-uploaded, and the narrator self-declares as new to slow-pitch on camera. Attributed data point, not established BOLA doctrine.
- `-nIhadZwxAA` (Benny Ortiz, CustomRodandReel seminar, 2025-05-20; East Coast/Gulf-based instruction — unregistered channel, medium confidence): the granular jig-profile-by-current-speed thresholds (0–0.5 kt wide flat-sided wing-fall, 0.5–1.5 kt thicker rounded/convex profile, 1.5–3 kt longer flat-bottomed profile); the alternate depth-in-meters × 2 weight formula with a current-speed multiplier, framed by the source as a starting point to adjust from, not a fixed number; the spawn-cycle jig-selection heuristic (longer profile pre-spawn, smaller profile post-spawn), explicitly not tied to a specific SoCal/Baja species or calendar — the source's own suggestion is to ask the boat/captain whether cleaned fish are showing eggs to gauge where the local population sits in the cycle.
- `05uB5V_jWTg` (Tackle Express, 2026-04-10; unregistered channel, single mention — medium confidence): the 60 g/120 g rockfish weight-by-depth pairing, explicitly conditions-dependent (the 120 g jig's own sweet spot narrows back to roughly the 60 g jig's range in calm weather).

## Gear class

- `7T6dIYqr3KI` (SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 141," 2021-04-01; unregistered channel — medium confidence): a 250 g-rated rod paired with a Penn Fathom 8 star-drag conventional (a compact/small reel) spooled with 300 yd of 30 lb braid, matched to a 250 g jig, called capable of landing a 25 lb yellowtail with no trouble if hooked; a 450 g-rated rod paired with a Penn Fathom Extra Narrow two-speed conventional ran a 280 g (~10 oz) jig. Both jigs carried Owner dual "dancing stinger" assists front and back for yellowtail/tuna; for rockfish, the tail hook was pulled off entirely to stop it from snagging bottom. Also flags a rod-blank storage/transport accessory: a padded lure wrap around the jig to keep it from rattling loose and denting the exposed carbon blank in transit.
- `Et2TUskzCSs` (Benny Ortiz, PCS Show 2024 panel; medium confidence): the three-rod SPJ series (330 g / 400 g / 500 g max jig rating), picked by the jigs planned to fish, not by target fish size ("I'm picking a rod based on the jigs that I'm fishing, not the fish that I'm fighting"); nearly all dedicated SPJ rods built as conventional, not spinning, because gaining line by lifting/pumping twists the blank toward failure ("turn them into a two-piece rod pretty quickly"); higher-end blanks add internal reinforcement (branded on camera as "High Power X") to resist that twisting/torquing failure mode. Also sources the ball-bearing-swivel rotation-during-the-fight rationale for bottom fish worked up off structure (grouper, rockfish) — lets the fish spin freely on the way up without twisting the main line, where a solid ring doesn't; the failure mode it avoids (a wind knot on the next cast) shows up later, not immediately. Contrast with [knife/speed jigging](../knife-jigging.md#gear-class), where the same source prefers a solid ring so the jig stays in-plane on the rip. Also sources the twin-vs-single hook-size axis for slow-pitch bluefin gear (twin hooks share the fight load across four points, allowing a lighter gauge per hook; singles, larger gauge, bury deeper in the mouth for the biggest fish) and the top-and-bottom placement rationale (fish strike the jig head-first and the jig can slide either direction as it works). ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the transcript attributes this specific passage to panelist James Kikkawa answering the moderator's question, not to Benny Ortiz — the rest of this source's claims in this note (rod series, conventional-vs-spinning, ball-bearing-swivel rationale, 90%-on-the-fall) are confirmed as Benny Ortiz's.
- `ZrcwcugaEe4` (Cesar Chang, Tackle Express, 2025-03-07; medium confidence): the level-wind ("double-wind") reel as a good starting option for an angler intimidated by a true small/narrow jigging conventional — lays line back and forth automatically, pairs well with thin braid; a spinning reel as a workable, arguably easier starting choice (no birds-nesting risk on the drop) but gives up some drop control; caution against too high a gear ratio on a spinning reel (a fast retrieve gives up torque). Also sources the Asia-fleet spinning-for-speed-jigging pattern (spinning line pickup beats any conventional's) paired with a short 4.5–5 ft rod tucked against the gut/under the arm; tradeoff that a hooked fish on spinning can't be racked on the rail the way a West Coast conventional can. Also sources the braid-class-by-structure-vs-open-water doctrine (30 lb braid in non-structure water; 50–65 lb around heavy structure) for the same light SPJ outfit used for deep rockfish.
- `zVMY4fZydRQ` (Cesar Chang, Tackle Express, "Spinning vs Baitcasting for Jigging? Ceasar Breaks It Down," 2025-06-09, ~3 months after the `ZrcwcugaEe4` breakdown — a re-cut of the same doctrine, not independent confirmation; medium confidence): restates the level-wind pick as scoped explicitly to slow jigging ("not so much for speed jigging"), and adds the mechanism for the spinning/short-rod speed-jig pairing — the shorter rod lets the angler keep up with the swing of the jig, and matching the rod's swing to the jig's swing is what makes the jig "dance" and lets the faster spinning pickup translate into a faster-worked jig.
- `-nIhadZwxAA` (Benny Ortiz, CustomRodandReel seminar, 2025-05-20; East Coast/Gulf-based instruction — unregistered channel, medium confidence): the four-strand-vs-eight-strand braid rule of thumb (four-strand sheds a smaller turbulent eddy and scopes out less; on a boat with someone running eight-strand, watch whose line scopes out first — it won't be the four-strand angler); four-strand also bites into fluorocarbon leader better on a wrapped PR/RP-class knot and is more abrasion resistant; eight-strand reserved for casting. Also sources the jig-connection detail: leader to a ball-bearing swivel (his preference over a solid ring, letting a spinning fish unwind independently) tied with a cow-hitch-started alternating-half-hitch wrap he calls an "AG chain knot," rated a 100% connection by the source; a split ring off the swivel's far eye connects to the jig. And the whole "Fighting & landing (light tackle)" section: drag-at-a-third-of-breaking-strength, effective-drag-climbs-off-a-full-spool, always-be-gaining-line, fight-with-your-body, direct/straight-line control, and two-speed low-gear-for-standoff guidance.
- `M8hOYQ_6rSg` (Cesar, via Dirty Hookers, 2025-05-20; Cesar is a registered voice, single mention — medium confidence): the single-top-vs-double hook choice by whether the jig is constantly knocking bottom vs. fish suspended; the Baja/Sea of Cortez gear-class reference (5.5–6 ft rod rated 100–300 g, most commonly 100–250 g, 200 g cap absent unusually heavy current; 30 lb Spectra main stepping to a 60 lb top shot; 80–150 g jigs fished).
- (cameron) — gear-acquisition status, not doctrine: shopping a dedicated SPJ / speed-jig setup to cover night [bluefin](../../species/bluefin-tuna.md) knife jigging and the September BOLA bottom-yellowtail program, ideally one setup for both; not yet owned, so gear resolves from class terms until it lands.
