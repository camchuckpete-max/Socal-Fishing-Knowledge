# Evidence — Trolling

Per-source provenance backing [trolling](../trolling.md).

## Reach for this when — Cedros Island, Baja: live-bait slow-troll for home-guard yellowtail

- `_C8w6zeVPak` (StokedOnFishing, 2015-01-20, Cedros Island, Baja, "Shogun Skiff Trip part 3"; unregistered channel): with bait and birds showing and no bite on surface iron/yo-yo iron for yellowtail, trolling mackerel/sardine-pattern Rapala-class hardbaits around the bait produced giant yellowtail — a fallback for a picked-at surface bite, not tested head-to-head against the iron.
- `BvT560Nblqo` (StokedOnFishing, 2015-01-20, Cedros Island, Baja, same trip, different skiff, "Shogun Skiff Trip part 2"; unregistered channel): repeats the pattern — after no bites on surface iron, switching to a trolled hardbait captioned "rala" (asr-uncertain, likely Rapala-class) produced two personal-best yellowtail for the group; a line class captioned "40b" is heard mid-fight (asr-uncertain, likely 40 lb).
- `QSmE3mdEL28` (StokedOnFishing, 2021-10 trip/2022-06-05 upload, Cedros Island, Baja; unregistered channel): a full-day program built around slow-trolling live mackerel over shallow structure (the standing method, not a switch-to) on an 80–100 lb line class and circle hooks; no-strike/wind-tight hookset, "railing" a big fish over the gunnel in shallow structure, deliberately backing the boat down on a hooked fish, and running through building wind/whitecaps because the technique doesn't depend on a clean cast; bait set "not too far" back over ~40 ft.
- `I-QBxuV2p7M` (StokedOnFishing, 2021-10 trip/2022-06-15 upload, Cedros Island, Baja, day two of the same trip; unregistered channel): slow-trolled live mackerel again over ~30 ft on 65 lb braid/80 lb fluorocarbon leader — no yellowtail that day; a mackerel bait out on the water hooked a large calico bass instead, landed on a visually confirmed circle hook and released.
- `brx6Ie_L2FM` (StokedOnFishing, 2021-10 trip/2022-06-22 upload, Cedros Island, Baja; unregistered channel, sponsor-heavy): dock advice for dorado at Cedros was to "slow troll" a bait captioned "maguro" (asr-uncertain, read as mackerel given the trip's live-mackerel program); no independent dorado hookup on the troll was captured in this footage.
- `9tIp4n7q850` (StokedOnFishing, 2023-05-17 upload, Cedros Island, Baja; Cedros Outdoor Adventures/Jose charter; unregistered channel): a further Cedros slow-troll session giving numeric parameters — ~3 mph troll speed ("putt putt putt"), live mackerel (captioned "cavala," presumed caballa/mackerel) flylined 50–75 ft back off the stern on a 40 lb fluorocarbon top shot; the bite ran mixed with bonito.
- `Zo92MG459gQ` (StokedOnFishing, 2023-05-24 upload, Cedros Island, Baja, continuation of the trip above; unregistered channel): the crew spent parts of two days catching bait mackerel, fishing deeper to avoid seals stealing bait at the surface; landed bait was ~2 lb mackerel on a circle hook; the resulting yellowtail was credited to straight 80 lb line (some in the group recommend stepping up to 100 lb for bigger bites), a shallow-water fight landed as a 45 lb personal best.

## Reach for this when — Cedros Island, Baja: dorado and offshore tuna trolling

- `YUdbrIm9vrE` (StokedOnFishing, October trip/2024-03-20 upload, offshore Cedros Island, Baja; Cedros Outdoor Adventures charter with Jose; unregistered channel): day one of a 5-day trip ran offshore (~20–30 mi) for yellowfin/dorado ahead of the resident yellowtail; spread of Rapala-class hardbaits run one short/one long plus a later-swapped surface popper on 60 lb line; running the same lure color (purple/purple) on both lines produced simultaneous doubles, corroborated by the captain's report that a different color two days earlier did not produce the same result; after birds marked a dorado school ~2–3 mi away, a flylined sardine drew hookups until the bait stopped working, and swapping back to feathers (the same combination that produced that morning's tuna bite) restarted the bite, this time on dorado; a bonita was also boated on the troll.
- `A8SuzB5qiKE` (StokedOnFishing, October trip/2024-04-11 upload, Cedros Island, Baja; Cedros Outdoor Adventures charter, sponsor-heavy; unregistered channel): rounding the point back toward the harbor after a yellowtail session, the crew put out trolled feathers on the outside rim, deliberately following visible bait, for a planned 20–30 minutes; connected quickly, landing a dorado on a lure/leader captioned "purple people leader" (asr-uncertain) — an artificial-feather variant alongside the live-mackerel slow-troll logged above, a close-to-port opportunity worked on the run in.

## Running the spread — Cabo San Lucas: working a current break by trolling

- `dgauGbNxP84` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2021-01-28, Cabo San Lucas, Baja, "Fishing 'Current Break' IT Holds FISH! Cabo San Lucas"; presenter not named on camera, inferred from channel self-references): once a current break is located, work it trolling back and forth across it — inshore side, then offshore side — rather than a single straight pass; fish shifted from one side of the break to the other with the bait, and the advice given on camera was to stay locked onto the break rather than leave it to search elsewhere.

## Marlin trolling — Catalina, Tanker Lanes/277 Bank

- `3qSY328fFYo` (StokedOnFishing, presenter/angler Jimmy Decker, 2014-11-08 upload, SoCal, "Marlin Fishing with Jimmy Decker"; unregistered channel): a half-day striped marlin troll — 19.2 mi at 193° to the tanker lanes (~17 mi out), then on toward the 277 bank at the bottom of the ridge off Catalina's east end (~35 more minutes); target water temp 68°F hunted specifically; spread of a flying-fish swimmer, an unnamed "NE"-captioned swimmer, a Hawaiian-style lure, and a small pink lure nicknamed "the little pinky" that had produced a fish the week before; mid-morning the boat crossed a ~0.2°F temperature break, a nearby boat reported a bite/fish-up on the colder side, and this boat worked the cold side after; the stop produced a hooked and released striped marlin estimated at ~160 lb, hooked in the corner of the mouth on the flying-fish swimmer — the angler said mid-fight "now I wish it was 50 lb tackle," implying the outfit in use was lighter than 50 lb without naming the class.

## Backside Catalina — porpoise-associated yellowfin finesse trolling

- `ouBrIdO7d4k` (SD Fish and Sips, "Pacific Coast Sportfishing Show 2025," 2025-03-08 upload, filmed 2025-03-06, booth interview at the OC Fairgrounds trade show; speaker is an unnamed SoCal charter captain, transcript not speaker-diarized; unregistered channel/speaker, single mention): recapping half-day charter trips run mostly around Catalina during lobster opener (late Sept/early Oct), the captain described the King-Buster-derived finesse-troll method (see main note) producing 60–70 lb yellowfin on the dolphin/porpoise pod; he also volunteered, as a non-SoCal contrast, that the same small trolled-feather approach is a Hawaii skipjack staple.

## Cabo San Lucas — offshore marlin, bite-but-lost

- `Mwx5AAXNMvE` (StokedOnFishing, 2014-12-03 upload, offshore Cabo San Lucas, "Stoked On Cabo San Lucas, Part 2"; same Hurricane Fishing charter as the Melton Tackle dockside interview in the main note; unregistered channel): two big blue marlin struck trolled lures but didn't stay hooked — one lure heard as "Mean Joe" in green/black (asr-uncertain), a second in red/black (model not named); a raised-but-lost data point, not a recommended pattern.

## Ensenada, Baja — reef/point live-bait slow-troll plus cast jigs

- `0dIwWiOc1NY` (StokedOnFishing, 2026-02-17 upload, a point/reef a few miles down the coast from Ensenada, Baja; guide Alejandro, a local full-time commercial fisherman; unregistered channel): the crew combined slow-trolling live mackerel from the stern with casting jigs from the bow at the same structure — the stated logic being that the jig splashes draw attention and pull fish toward the trolled bait rather than competing with it; the stop ran a couple of hours without a yellowtail (the stated target, described as constantly moving around the structure), landing a calico bass and a small bonito instead.

## Ensenada, Baja — dolphin-school troll/foamer conversion and offshore temp-break search

- `0bcDBGzQnGw` (StokedOnFishing, 2018-12-17 upload, offshore Ensenada, Baja, "Fishing In Baja with Navico & Tito Ortiz, Part 1"; annual September Navico supplier-conference trip out of Hotel Coral; unregistered channel): the crew slow-trolled cedar plugs around a large dolphin/porpoise school hoping for tuna holding with it ("the name of the game is finding life") after a couple hours with no action; the school broke into a full foamer, the crew switched to flylined sardine plus chunking, lost several fish ("about three or four") before boating one (species not stated); a striped marlin was also seen jumping right at the boat during this stop.
- `0bcDBGzQnGw` (StokedOnFishing, 2018-12-17 upload, offshore Ensenada, Baja, same trip, separate stop): the day plan was to run ~20–30 mi due west to a temperature break, then continue north or west by sea conditions (a fallback 20–30 mi north was also named), deliberately working away from the rest of the conference fleet (placed 45–50 mi out); the search produced a floating object/school marker captioned "Cal Pal" (asr-uncertain) ~35 mi from Ensenada, with skipjack and yellowfin sign nearby, ahead of a multi-angler yellowtail bite.

## La Bocana, Baja — 25-mile offshore troll for tuna/marlin/wahoo

- `Cobp85UvHmM` (StokedOnFishing, 2018-02-07 upload, La Bocana, Baja California Sur, "Stoked On La Bocana," part 2; unregistered channel): the crew ran 25 mi offshore, past the "barracuda grounds," onto "tuna grounds," trolling tuna feathers and Rapala-class hardbaits, naming tuna, marlin, and wahoo (heard "wasps," asr-uncertain) as targets; one hookup was lost when a tuna-rigged hook popped, with the crew speculating without confirming the fish may have been a marlin; working birds later led to a dorado school (see dorado.md), and the day finished on inshore rock structure for yellowtail on surface iron.

## Alijos Rocks, Baja — bomb-lure trolling for wahoo

- `97clKtVsEOs` (StokedOnFishing, "Intrepid Style 8 Day, part 2," 2015-10-29 upload, Alijos Rocks, Baja, ~400 mi south of San Diego; aboard the long-range boat Intrepid, Captain Kevin Osborne; unregistered channel, sponsor-heavy): after a Benito's Island yellowtail leg, the boat ran to Alijos Rocks (unfished ~5 days beforehand) specifically for wahoo; one angler ran a bomb-style trolled lure rather than jig-and-reel, reasoning that wahoo characteristically strike the back half of a bait and the bomb's hookup ratio is better for that bite pattern (a single angler's stated reasoning, not corroborated elsewhere in this corpus); tackle was a two-speed Okuma "Andros 12" prototype on 40 lb Okuma Soft Steel Ultra (sponsor product, low confidence on the specific model), with a stated preference for high gear on the strike then low gear once the fish settles; standing fight instruction was to never pump the rod, wind continuously; only one wahoo bit that day despite the multi-day no-pressure expectation, and the crew moved on rather than keep grinding. See [wahoo trolling](../wahoo-trolling.md) and [wahoo bomb & Raider jig casting](../wahoo-bomb-casting.md) for the dedicated technique notes this account fed.

## East Cape, Baja — Buena Vista Beach Resort offshore troll

- `mG8ZZLFGlT8` (StokedOnFishing, 2016-12-22 upload, Buena Vista Beach Resort, East Cape, Baja, "Stoked On Hotel Buena Vista Beach Resort, Part 3"; first annual Stoked On Fishing Fiesta, 2016; unregistered channel): roughly 5 mi offshore the crew hooked and lost an estimated 400–500 lb blue marlin twice in quick succession, then connected a third time on the same water after a ~40 min run-back; the fight ran as a team relay (captain to leader four times, then a friend finished it) over ~1.5 hr combined; the day's broader recap named five tuna plus a garbled second species for one boat and four tuna for another.
- `tcso7Lpm_Xs` (StokedOnFishing, 2018-03-09 upload, Buena Vista Beach Resort, East Cape, Baja, "Stoked On Hotel Buena Vista Beach Resort"; unregistered channel): first-morning jigs were in the water ~15 min before the first hookup; the session produced tuna (species unspecified), ~15–16 dorado (most released), ~15–20 skipjack, one unidentified "snapper," and an unconfirmed big marlin (jump described as not looking like a striped marlin, species never confirmed on screen); a second morning produced multiple wahoo (garbled running count, likely three-plus boated with a fourth hooked).
- `6j7V34GYzzw` (StokedOnFishing, 2018-03-12 upload, Buena Vista Beach Resort, East Cape, Baja, "Fishing Baja - Hotel Buena Vista Beach Resort | Part 2"; unregistered channel): on the offshore troll, an angler ("Ryan") fought a large unconfirmed gamefish through a long run, boat put in free-spool and worked back, landed and released, credited to a circle hook for the solid hookup; separately, a promised beach/rooster-fish session instead appeared as a panga slow-troll of a pink "pinky" lure/skirt through water heard as "Kabul Eidos" (asr-uncertain, possibly Cabo Pulmo), drawing repeated strikes on circle hooks, including a debated jack-vs-rooster fish and an unlogged African pompano caught after an ad cutaway.
- `APsnsunT4gM` (StokedOnFishing, 2020-04-08 upload, Buena Vista Beach Resort, East Cape, Baja, "Hot Bite in Baja"; 5th annual Fiesta trip; unregistered channel, sponsor-heavy): the first day's offshore troll produced "solid grade A" yellowfin plus wahoo, including one angler's personal-best wahoo on a circle hook, described (per garbled captions) as the fleet's first wahoo of the season; a marlin bite produced one fish boated and a second that spooled the angler; no leader, lure, or troll-speed detail given in the captions.

## Magdalena Bay, Baja — inshore/offshore troll

- `4t_Z75shK_E` (StokedOnFishing, 2020-09-25 upload, Magdalena Bay, Baja, presenter Shea aboard the 37 ft "Pro Cat," with local marlin-lure-company owner/guide Toby of Mag Bay Lures; unregistered channel): after ~3 days of wind settled then picked back up, no boats had run for a couple of days before this trip; Toby framed cedar plugs as the crew's standard tuna go-to in a dockside interview (general practice, not confirmed as an event of this specific day); the dead-bait mouth-tie and wahoo-transit-lure tips are logged in the main note; a second dead bait dragged ~50 ft behind the boat during an already-hooked fight, to try for a double, didn't connect.
- `isXJONlpUP4` (StokedOnFishing, 2020-10-07 upload, Magdalena Bay, Baja, Mag Bay Lodge, presenter Shea, "Surprisingly Great Inshore Fishing in Magdalena Bay"; unregistered channel): after getting beat up by wind/chop offshore for striped marlin the prior day, the crew and lodge switched to the inshore estuary/mangroves; the local guide (Enrique) called the channel depth near the mangroves at ~25–30 ft; trolling small lures tight to the mangrove edge produced fast, repeated action on palometa (pompano/jack family) and a lost fish guessed as snook; later, with a school of pargo showing on top, the crew switched to casting a 3/4 oz Cast Master on light spinning tackle; falling/low tide produced "a lot of fish" and the guide's framing was that once the tide started flooding the open-estuary bite would normally slow but the fish move inside the mangroves rather than stop biting, so the crew returned to trolling tight along the mangrove edge — corroborating, from an independent crew/guide, the Lopez Mateos ebb/flood doctrine already logged in flyline.md.
- `OVwqUKim9Pc` (StokedOnFishing, 2020-10-14 upload, Magdalena Bay, Baja, Mag Bay Lodge, presenter Shea, continuing the same trip; unregistered channel): before committing offshore, the crew idled out past the estuary mouth to check wave state, choosing between the longer run to "the Ridge" (depth captioned "23 fetus," asr-uncertain, likely ~23 fathoms) if waves looked manageable, or the closer "Modesto Main" bank if not; ran offshore targeting wahoo but found a "full speed" dorado bite instead (specific bank not named on camera); the trip's final day returned to the estuary instead of another offshore run, for a multi-fish snook bite.

## La Paz, Baja California Sur — live-bait slow-troll for yellowtail/jurel

- `ORC1A68cEeM` (StokedOnFishing, 2023-10-25 upload, La Paz, Baja, Baja Rancheros fleet, guides Pedro and Sergio, "Stoked on La Paz: World-Class Fishing"; unregistered channel): guide Pedro framed the day's plan as chasing "jurel" (asr-uncertain, read as the local name for yellowtail) and cabrilla; Sergio's crew fished 40 lb line with a captain-prompted drag-test ritual before starting the troll, circle hooks with a 3-second count before engaging the reel (a distinct, explicitly-counted variant of the Cedros no-strike/wind-tight phrasing above); the troll started in just 12 ft of water tight to visible rock structure, hooked fish repeatedly ran back toward the rocks; doubles close to 30 lb, one fish 25–30 lb; bait species (sardine vs. mackerel) for that fish was left an open question on camera.
- `92y14x33etQ` (StokedOnFishing, 2023-11-09 upload, La Paz, Baja, Baja Rancheros fleet, guide Felipe, "Beyond Expectations: Stoked On La Paz," third/final video of the same trip as `ORC1A68cEeM` and the day-2 flyline entry `qM7iOO7fOBw`; unregistered channel): after a quiet bottom stop for snapper/cabrilla at Isla Espíritu Santo, the fleet ran to the bank "El Bajo" (shallowest ~50 ft on low tide, ≤80 ft on high tide); dropped mackerel bait on a "D-rigger" for amberjack/yellowtail, then slow-trolled the same mackerel over the bank — 10 yd of line let out, weight dropped to bottom, then lifted off bottom by the troll's own speed rather than cranked — producing multiple yellowtail on 65 lb braid/60 lb fluorocarbon top shot; the crew stopped keeping fish and released the rest once the box (three bags' worth) was full.

## Drill-powered crank — field reports

- `xzIaUEDklrE` (StokedOnFishing, 2022-12-14, ~55 mi offshore SoCal; Okuma product staff on camera, sponsor-adjacent segment): introduced the drill-powered "Makaira Rapid Crank" reel-crank attachment, which chucks into a cordless drill and spins the reel handle directly, for fast wind-in after a kelp pickup on a far-back-set line.
- `U1AgwmlY5bI` (StokedOnFishing, 2024-08-14 upload, Baja Blue Fin Tuna Tournament, offshore Ensenada, Baja; unregistered channel): a second, different boat/crew ran the same tool (captioned "MRA rapid crank"), credited to a "pro development guy" (name garbled) who built it around the 50-Wide reel specifically; practical advice to keep the drill's spare batteries charged, since a long hand wind-in can take up to 10 minutes; separately on the same boat, a trolled line picked up kelp and was dragged an unknown stretch before being noticed.
- `elBPRrdkugU` (StokedOnFishing, 2022-12-22 upload, offshore San Clemente/Catalina, SoCal; unregistered channel): a third mention of the same tool (captioned "Makira rapid crank"), used here to speed up checking a rod after a hook pulled mid-fight rather than after a kelp pickup; standing crew advice to run the drill on low speed, not high — high speed was called out as getting "out of control" and putting extra tension on the reel.
