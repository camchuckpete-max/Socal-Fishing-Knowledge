# Evidence — Swimbaits

Per-source provenance backing [swimbaits](../swimbaits.md).

## Bait vs. plastic

- `ZoIzpyyS9xw` (n6yun panel, part 1, Fred Hall Show, 2013-03-09, unnamed
  SoCal island; speaker "Jack" — surname uncertain in ASR, `asr-uncertain`;
  unregistered channel — medium confidence): a client kept re-pinning cut
  squid onto a swimbait hook against the presenter's stated preference, and
  the boat drew a heavy bite on it that day at that spot — no stated reason
  beyond "it worked there," kept beside the bare-hook doctrine rather than
  generalized from it.

## Weedless rigging

- `_r_qKX_7080` (StokedOnFishing, "MC Swimbaits Skiff Trip part 2," 2014-11-13,
  Chester's Rock, south end of Cedros Island, Baja; unregistered channel —
  medium confidence): a weedless bait rigged on a Trokar keel-weighted hook
  (captions render "TR car") with its 3/8 oz weighted belly keel screwed into
  the bait was called out on camera as running clean through "the thick of
  the heel [kelp] grass" with no fouling — matching the Gamakatsu/Trokar
  3/8 oz keel-weighted-hook spec in the main note.

## The retrieve and the hookset

- `L3tkGVu516A` (StokedOnFishing, "MC Swimbaits Skiff Trip part 1," 2014-11-13,
  Dono, Baja; unregistered channel — medium confidence): a very slow wind —
  "as slow as I can turn the handle" — produced a sheephead on the wind-up;
  later the same session an angler was coached to "slow roll on the bottom"
  because "they want it slow right now" — the bite's preferred speed that
  particular day, not a fixed rule.
- `iQLyBzhOSi8` (StokedOnFishing, "California Offshore Challenge, part 1,"
  Northwest Harbor / front side, San Clemente Island, day one; this video's
  on-camera slate reads "2012" for the fourth-annual event, vs. the
  2014-11-11 YouTube upload date used elsewhere in this KB for the same
  tournament — see the note in [calico bass](../../species/calico-bass.md);
  unregistered channel — medium confidence): once a producing spot was
  located, the angler dropped straight to the bottom, wound 10–15 turns up,
  then free-spooled back down and repeated — lure type not named on camera; a
  higher turn count than, and not confirmed identical to, the 10-crank yo-yo
  cadence in [yo-yo iron](../yo-yo-iron.md#mechanics) (there for yellowtail).
- `iQLyBzhOSi8` (same session, closing toward a five-fish limit): the crew
  cast up onto exposed rocks and let the bait sit in the wash, then worked it
  as it dropped off the rock shelf into the water — bites typically came
  right as it dropped off the shelf. No lure, leader, or rod detail given.
- `IATPg9110CE` (StokedOnFishing, "California Offshore Challenge, part 3,"
  Santa Barbara Island, event ~early November 2014 per the 2014-11-11
  upload; unregistered channel — medium confidence): a plastic worked tight
  to rock structure was let sink close to bottom, then wound on a real slow
  roll along the bottom with periodic stops to let it fall again — bites
  came as the bait fell after the pause.
- `IATPg9110CE` (same location): anglers discussed rod length as a
  distance-vs-leverage trade-off — 8 to 8½ ft rods cast farther for reaching
  into structure but "wear you down" fished all day, while a 7 ft rod was
  described as having "tons of leverage" pulling fish out of rock/kelp.
- `PKf7G3uL4io` (StokedOnFishing, "California Offshore Challenge, part 2,"
  Santa Barbara Island, same 4th-annual tournament and 2014-11-11 upload as
  the `IATPg9110CE` entries above, day two; unregistered channel — medium
  confidence): the same crew's retrieve was called "just like yesterday" — a
  slow crawl, "creeping it across the bottom" while staying out of "the
  stuff" (structure) — continuing the slow-roll-on-the-bottom pattern into a
  second day, fished right up into skinny water (fish holding "in the foam,"
  inches of water off the beach) with long casts down the beach.
- `P6Slg6RQiXw` (StokedOnFishing w/ Capt. Benny Florentino, San Clemente
  Island, December 2013; date resolved from the crew's reference to Aaron
  Martens' upcoming "classic" the following February, i.e. the 2014
  Bassmaster Classic): mid-retrieve, Florentino actively varied the cadence —
  fast, slow, fast, slow — hunting a faster strike than his usual pace,
  working the bait through a patch of boiling rock structure, noting recent
  trips to the same spot had fish keying on a fast "burned" retrieve.
  Separately, breeze was called out as helping the bite late in the session —
  wind puts more current on the water and a surface texture that benefits
  weedless and other surface presentations.

## Kelp canopy — punching gaps under the canopy

- `P6Slg6RQiXw` (StokedOnFishing, San Clemente Island, December 2013; guest
  angler Aaron Martens, 2013 Bassmaster Angler of the Year, fishing with
  Capt. Benny Florentino — single-session data point, not established
  doctrine): partway through a wide-open bite, Martens — drawing on
  freshwater bass-flipping technique — called for "a heavy head flip": a
  heavy leadhead pitched on a short, accurate cast into gaps in the kelp
  itself ("punching the kelp"). Fish were found up underneath the floating
  canopy, working the dropped bait before eating it; once it started
  producing, the crew adopted it as the session's pattern ("Aaron said flip,
  so we're flipping"). No leader, hook, or weight spec given on camera.

## Fighting fish out of the kelp

- `L3tkGVu516A` (StokedOnFishing, 2014-11-13, Dono, Baja, "MC Swimbaits Skiff
  Trip part 1"): a fish came unbuttoned boatside, and the crew explained why
  on camera — kelp had slid all the way down the weedless hook's shank, and
  kelp on the shank "pops it right out of the fish's mouth." The described
  counter (`asr-uncertain`, and it is not clear on camera whether it actually
  saved this particular fish): slack the line and drop the tip fully so the
  fish's head falls back underwater, let it shake its head, then resume
  winding.

## Boat work — covering water

- `PKf7G3uL4io` (StokedOnFishing, "California Offshore Challenge, part 2,"
  event ~early November 2014 per the 2014-11-11 upload, Catalina Island;
  unregistered channel — medium confidence): after getting blown off a West
  End run and retreating to a sheltered "plan B" spot (see [island
  structure](../../locations/island-structure.md)), the crew worked the
  approach deliberately quiet — they let the wind drift the boat down
  through the spot instead of running the trolling motor the whole way, shut
  off the electronics, the bait pump, and the livewell pump, and lifted the
  trolling motor coming into the spot. Stated on camera to matter more over
  hard rock bottom, which "echoes," than over sand, where "you get away with
  a little better."

## Multi-bait rigs

- `tJFSV3AcIdE` (StokedOnFishing, 2016, San Clemente Island, SWBA California
  Offshore Challenge tournament, day two; unregistered channel — medium
  confidence): a hooked three-to-four-pound calico drew followers on the way
  up, and a second angler pitched a bait right next to the still-hooked fish
  — an immediate bite from a follower. The originally hooked (bigger) fish
  then spit the hook and the smaller follower was landed instead, narrated on
  camera as a "50/50 decision": leaving a hooked fish in the water longer to
  draw more followers risks losing it before it's netted.
