# Evidence — Two-Speed Reel Technique

Per-source provenance backing
[two-speed reel technique — low-gear grind vs. pump-and-reel](../two-speed-low-gear-fight.md).

## Lead / doctrine source

- `9JnIS8HkZlA` (Steve Carson, Penn, San Diego Rod and Reel Club talk,
  2023-11-14; unregistered channel/speaker, single mention — medium
  confidence): the doctrine source for the gear-tap-adjacent low-gear grind,
  the pump-and-reel problem, the fix, and the after-the-fight shift-back-to-
  high rule.

## Reach for this when

- `G1YIlakiRqg` (Capt. Matt, SD Fish and Sips, uploaded 2024-03-13;
  unregistered channel/speaker — medium confidence): he did not used to run
  two-speed reels on his 25 lb-class flyline setup, reasoning a fish that
  size didn't need one — that's changed with SoCal daytime bluefin trips on
  25 lb test now regularly hooking 40, 50, even 60–80 lb fish.
- `cpUq7Z3UOwU` (Nathan Winnicke, Penn Reels, via SearcherSportfishing,
  "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 117 (Favorite Set-up)," 2020-09-17;
  `searcher-sportfishing` registered channel/series — medium confidence,
  single mention of this specific claim): offshore tuna worked up and down
  like to stay down and circle once hooked; on smaller reel sizes run with
  40 or 50 lb test, the two-speed starts "bunching them up."
- `rxoaPT5Zaog` (Tackle Express, "180 Pounds of Fury Landed in SIX
  Minutes?!," uploaded 2026-02-09; unregistered channel, single mention —
  medium confidence; no species, location, or on-camera date given, and the
  hookup depth is stated only as "about 450" with no unit named): two
  self-reported fights on speed-jigging setups, contrasted directly — a 180
  lb fish landed in 6 minutes on a two-speed reel spooled with 80 lb braided
  line (the only fish for which "braided line" is stated on camera), versus
  a 140 lb fish that took 15 minutes on a single-speed reel spooled with 65
  lb (line type not restated for this second fish). No reasoning is given on
  camera for which reel was matched to which fish — an outcome, not stated
  doctrine.

## Timing the shift

- `vn4fmPxUqsU` (Shane, SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 172
  (Engaging Two-speed Reel)," 2022-03-10; `searcher-sportfishing` registered
  channel — medium confidence, single mention of this specific "stay high
  through the run" reasoning): names the reason to stay in high gear
  through the run (can't retrieve fast enough in low if the fish charges the
  boat) as well as the reason to drop to low; coins "pop in, low; pop out,
  high."
- `DuNkl8F45NE` (Tackle Express, "How to Catch a Trophy Bluefin Tuna on a
  Jig," James, uploaded 2022-06-04; unregistered channel — medium
  confidence, single mention): the same sequence on jig-hooked triple-digit
  bluefin specifically, naming the end-stage circle the "pinwheel" and
  giving it a 100–200 ft depth band. Full rigging/reel/rod detail in
  [trophy bluefin jig
  outfit](../../tackle/tackle-express-trophy-bluefin-jig-outfit.md).
- `ejkr3z7xkJA` — see the Mechanism entry above; the same clip also frames
  the high-early/low-late timing generically (no species or line class) and
  adds the short-rod-pumps end-game description, not reconciled in the
  parent note with the continuous-grind motion described elsewhere.
