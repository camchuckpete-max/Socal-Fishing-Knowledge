# Evidence — Wind in Your Face Rail Positioning

Per-source provenance backing [wind-in-your-face rail
positioning](../wind-in-your-face-positioning.md). One line per source,
grouped under the parent note's section headings. Observations never change
the parent's stated doctrine.

## The mechanism / Mechanics

- `pQ9kGqgsX8I` (Captain Mark, SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol.
  180 (Wind in Your Face)," 2022-05-05): source for the core windward-side
  rule, the bow-catches-more-wind mechanism, the walk-with-your-line/reset
  cycle mechanics, and the 2–5 minute stern-to-bow timing.
  `searcher-sportfishing` is a registered channel/series per the source
  registry.

## Reach for this when

- `rNiQKb3sCh4` (Laurie, Turner's Outdoorsmen tackle department, via
  SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 56 (Long-Range Tips),"
  2019-07-10): earlier, thinner corroboration of the core windward-side rule,
  given in a short "how to prepare for a sport boat" segment as a rule of
  thumb for a first-timer who doesn't know which side of the boat to fish —
  "remember facing the wind... if you don't know what side to get on the boat,
  make sure the wind is blowing in your face" — with the stated reason being
  to end up "in the right area and not messing everybody up." No mechanism
  (bow-catches-more-wind, walk-with-your-line, timing) is given here — that
  detail comes only from Vol. 180 above — but this independently confirms the
  core rule 2.5 years earlier, from a different on-camera speaker within the
  same registered channel/series. This repeated-doctrine-within-a-registered-
  channel/series pattern is why the parent note stands at `high` confidence
  per the source registry's repeated-doctrine rule.
- `S9sQ8Vg8-5o` (Tackle Express, 2025-03-28; unregistered channel — medium
  confidence on its own): extends the rule to a rock-cod/dropper-loop
  bottom-fishing drift — "the standard format on nine times out of 10
  rockfish boats" per this source — framed the same way ("if the wind is in
  your face, you're in the right place") because a leeward bottom rig trails
  underneath the drifting boat. ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): in the
  transcript, "nine times out of 10" (and, restated earlier, "a good 90% of
  the time") describes how often these boats drift rather than anchor —
  "nine times out of 10... going out on these Rockfish boats we are
  typically drifting and not on anchor" — not the dropper-loop rig/format's
  standing; the rig itself is separately called only "one of the most simple
  and basic rigs... probably one of the most productive," with no figure
  attached.
- `eqRMpfcuM2s` ("Why the wind is actually your best friend while fishing for
  Rockfish," uploaded 2026-04-16, 71 s): a re-cut of the same `S9sQ8Vg8-5o`
  passage, word for word — not an independent sighting; `duplicate-of
  S9sQ8Vg8-5o`, logged separately here per conservation.
