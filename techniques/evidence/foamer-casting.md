## The approach

- `A6DJoXbID4c` (StokedOnFishing, 2016-10-06 upload, offshore San Diego,
  bluefin on a kelp paddy): three-point trip-tips summary consistent with, and
  adding detail to, the ahead-of-travel rule — (1) watch the terns and try to
  read the school's direction of travel; (2) don't run straight up on the
  fish — swing out and around to come to rest ahead of its travel, then shut
  down and let the fish drift into casting distance; (3) match the hatch of
  whatever bait the fish are on (that day, anchovies) — see the [Savage Gear
  Squish jig](../../lures/tuna-poppers-and-stickbaits.md#savage-gear-squish-jig-anchovy-match-the-hatch)
  entry for the lure pick that produced. Point (2) is a wide-swing-then-drift
  variant of "set up ahead of the school," not a contradiction of the
  stop-hard-and-cast-immediately mechanic in the main note — framed as a
  stealthier arrival for a school still under way. Unregistered channel —
  medium confidence.
- `84XPJAeH0Rw` (StokedOnFishing, 2019-02-10, East Cape, Sea of Cortez, Baja
  California Sur, Hotel Buena Vista Beach Resort trip): while running toward a
  known pack of working boats, the crew spotted a smaller, separate patch of
  surface activity ("a little something something") off to the side and
  veered off to make a couple of casts at it first, rather than continuing
  straight to the main pack — a detour estimated to cost "only... a couple of
  minutes." That smaller patch turned out to be biting ("it never hurts to
  take a shot, take a quick look"). Separately in the same episode, a small
  popper cast at bait breaking the surface ("puddles") drew an immediate
  blow-up. Baja tuna, species not named on camera — outside this note's SoCal
  bluefin/yellowfin foamer program; logged as a side-by-side
  scouting-philosophy data point, not a retrieve/lure/hookup detail.
  Unregistered channel — medium confidence.
- `e73wPONTOJU` (StokedOnFishing, 2022-10-06 upload, SoCal island zone):
  working a string of individual bluefin foamers rather than one big school —
  get in front of a spot, the school sinks out before or as the boat arrives,
  move on to the next spot that's popped up — described on camera as "like
  playing Pac-Man and asteroids and a little pinball machine." Fish keyed on
  baby anchovies about 2–3 in long. Corroborates the short-lived,
  move-fast-between-schools framing (budget ~2–3 casts per school) and the
  tiny-anchovy downsizing note in [bluefin tuna](../../species/bluefin-tuna.md)
  rather than adding a new mechanism. Unregistered channel — medium
  confidence.

- `M7BtON4GZgQ` (StokedOnFishing, "Bluefin Fishing on the El Dorado," trip
  dated August 2020 — limited-load, pandemic-era charter; video uploaded
  2021-02-03, west end of Catalina Island, SoCal, aboard the 85 ft El Dorado,
  Long Beach Sportfishing): sliding up on a showing school, the crew's call
  was to bomb a Colt Sniper into the middle of the fish and drag it through,
  with a flylined bait rigged as a backup rod ready on the same push,
  switching over to the flyline once the boat started to drift on the fish.
  That trip ran a lot of run-and-gun, not much drifting, framed on camera as
  the pattern for the whole day — consistent with, not new beyond, the
  run-and-gun approach documented in the main note. Unregistered channel —
  medium confidence.
- cameron, fall 2025, late-season bluefin within panga range: an epic finish
  to the season fished run-and-gun with no bait — couldn't hold enough of the
  only bait available (big sardines) to chum effectively, and skipping the
  30-gallon tank cut water weight for the high-mileage running days. About
  2 to 3 casts per school before moving on. Most fish came on Colt Snipers,
  many of them actually snagged — the smaller presentation fished better.
  Landed fish were 25 to 40 lb; saw 80 to 140 lb foamer schools but never
  hooked one. The no-bait/no-tank choice is a platform decision for that
  program, not general doctrine — see [boat](../../profiles/cameron/boat.md).
