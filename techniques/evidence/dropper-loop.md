# Evidence — Dropper Loop

Trip reports and per-source provenance backing [dropper loop](../dropper-loop.md).

headings, in its order. Observations never change the parent's stated
doctrine; a contradicting one is also traced beside that doctrine in the
parent.

- `JaKSGkZ6CAc` (StokedOnFishing, "Top Gun 80 5-Day Fun, Part 2," aired
  2015-01-18; unregistered channel — medium confidence): San Benito Island —
  on-camera crew framed the rig as "dropper loop on the bottom, 100 lb line
  minimum" for the day's 30 lb-and-better yellowtail bite, fished on squid
  in 30–40+ fathom water; one angler's first-ever yellowtail came dropped
  straight to the bottom and worked by hand rather than off a rod. Same trip
  as the `SH7zOA9ZF3o` entry logged in
  [yellowtail](../../species/evidence/yellowtail.md) — not independent
  confirmation.
- `RgtkbmBFUXI` (StokedOnFishing, "Guadalupe Island Yellowtail vs Man,"
  2012-03-27; unregistered channel — medium confidence): Guadalupe Island —
  for the island's resident big yellowtail (crew nickname "water buffalo"),
  the on-camera rig was a 100 lb leader tied on the bottom on a dropper
  loop, fished on a drift swinging across steep rock structure from about
  120 ft out to 250 ft where the bottom drops off like a wall. Even at
  100 lb, hooked fish diving back for the rocks cut the leader through — the
  angler put his own loss rate at about eight out of ten hookups lost that
  way.
- `CdjT_I_PBHQ` (Steve Carson, Penn Fishing Tackle, via SearcherSportfishing,

  2021-12-29; presenter self-identifies as "Dwayne Diego Malloy," an ASR
  mis-transcription of Capt. **Duane Diego Mellor** — `duane-diego-mellor`
  registered, this is his named Dockside Tutorial dropper-loop video per
  `sources/source-registry.md` — high confidence): the source for the whole
  note's core rig doctrine — the dropper-loop knot/spider hitch preference
  for a longer loop, the 6-10 oz sinker with sinker-down/bait-wafting
  presentation, the rock-cod-heavier/seabass-lighter/current-heavier
  weight-tracking rule, the double-dropper-loop build (straight braid to
  barrel swivel to fluorocarbon to two octopus-offset hooks) and its
  double-hooking-squid holding-power rationale for this section, and the
  one-to-two-even-three-rods deadstick default logged under *Fishing it*.

## Fishing it — deadstick vs. work it

- `mUrihh0V59M` (BDOutdoors, "Dockside Tutorial: Dropper Loop Rig,"
  2021-12-29; Capt. Duane Diego Mellor — high confidence): the
  one-to-two-even-three-rods deadstick default, also logged under *Building
  the rig* and *Single vs. double dropper* above.
- `S9sQ8Vg8-5o` (Tackle Express, 2025-03-28; unregistered channel — medium
  confidence): periodic re-drop reasoning (drift/bottom-contour mechanism,
  ~90% of trips drifting). Footage also re-cut and re-uploaded as the short
  `nJPQVouJQ0g`, "Stop losing your gear! The secret to mastering rock
  contours," 2026-03-26 — same claim word for word, not independent
  corroboration.
- `U-dGRQ0X-Mc` (Capt. Dave Hansen, Your Saltwater Guide, San Clemente
  Island, 2022-02-11): the slow-steady-wind retrieve instruction.
- `HMdrP4-i9MM` (StokedOnFishing, 2022-10-11, off North County San Diego
  near Camp Pendleton, SoCal; kids/family Okuma trip aboard a Dana Wharf
  Sportfishing charter, Dana Point; unregistered channel — medium
  confidence): crew set kids up on a dropper loop specifically to avoid
  casting — drop straight down and reel — over hard bottom with light kelp
  not reaching the surface, in ~40–42 ft of water (see [calico
  bass](../../species/calico-bass.md#where--when) for the full structure
  detail). Baits included live sardine and a dropper-style strip of squid or
  sardine, producing calico and [sand bass](../../species/sand-bass.md).
- `Y2bXn44lfqo` (StokedOnFishing, Catalina Island, 2023-08-30; unregistered
  channel — medium confidence): white seabass overnight soak for
  broodstock-program fish (see [white
  seabass](../../species/white-seabass.md#doctrine--conflicts)) — an
  anchored crew ran four dropper-loop rods with circle hooks, two off the
  bow and two off the stern, each set to a different depth off the bottom
  (2, 4, 6, and 8 cranks up) so a squid swimming through at any level in the
  column gets covered. Line class split fore and aft: 40 lb on the stern
  rods, 60 lb up front on the bow rods — the heavier bow line explained as
  giving a better chance of landing a fish if the line gets wrapped around
  the anchor rode. Circle hooks here contrast with the same crew's own
  description of the popular dropper-loop rig strung with an Ahi twist
  (J-style) hook, which they said guts noticeably more fish — kept side by
  side rather than reconciled; see [leadhead
  mods](../../rigging/leadhead-mods.md#rigging-a-leadhead-with-squid-for-white-seabass-xr4nurk-z48)
  for the actively-fished alternative from the same trip. This footage is
  also the basis of the queued white-seabass-dropper-loop spin-out in
  `sources/relocation-queue.md`.
