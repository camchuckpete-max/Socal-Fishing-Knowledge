# Evidence — Kite Fishing

Trip reports and per-source provenance backing [kite fishing](../kite-fishing.md).
One line per observation, grouped under the parent note's section headings,
in its order. Observations never change the parent's stated doctrine; a
contradicting one is also traced beside that doctrine in the parent.

## Deploying the kite

- `Ix0gG0-l3v0` (StokedOnFishing, 2024-08-21, 2nd annual Baja Blue Fin Tuna
  Tournament, Ensenada, Baja; unregistered channel, medium confidence):
  kite-line build — ring-plus-rubber-band breakaway to the fishing line, a
  loop-flip quick release on the kite line itself, star-drag payout control
  in place of thumbing the spool, a knot marking ~75 ft out during payout
  (exact function unresolved), a heavy-duty snap swivel (caution against
  over-cranking it), straight-braid kite line with no separate leader
  section, roughly a couple hundred yards spooled out once set, boat run on
  a single center engine at ~2 kt while working the spread, gyro-stabilized
  Fuji binoculars called mandatory both for spotting fish and for watching
  the flyer stay upright (same doctrine as [search and
  glassing](../../planning/search-and-glassing.md#the-glassing-protocol),
  with a kite-specific second use case), and a tagline rigged and called
  mandatory at ~30 ft (asr-uncertain: exact attachment point/mechanism not
  resolved; read as this tournament's own boatside-handling requirement, not
  confirmed as a jurisdiction regulation — general procedure in [fighting a
  big bluefin — hand-off to the
  gaff](../fighting-big-bluefin.md#hand-off-to-the-gaff)).
- `rsCAh-QyK60` (StokedOnFishing, 2017-06-06, launched Shelter Island/San
  Diego Bay, fished ~40 mi off San Diego, 22 ft center console; unregistered
  channel, medium confidence): kite run 120–150 yd out, shifting with wind,
  drift, and the boat's heading relative to kite height; troll speed called
  8 mph (~7 kn) on the GPS while working the kited spread (crew's own
  mph-to-knots conversion is garbled on audio — asr-uncertain).

## Bait and rig alternatives

  added to a kite-flown bait on secondhand advice ("that's what they were

## Trolled rubber flyer vs. dead-boat dead flyer (a wind-driven choice)

- `wYeKJLoKo4g` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2018-09-04, San Clemente Island): a
  foaming/breezing bluefin spot went quiet by the time the boat closed in
  slowly rather than running-and-gunning; a Yummy Flyer (rubber
  flying-fish lure) suspended under a small yellow balloon, kept
  dapping/skipping rather than retrieved, drew several missed strikes before
  a hookup signaled by the balloon going under and staying down; doesn't
  state whether a kite was also up.
- `usHl-4SfqDA` (StokedOnFishing, 2024-11-13, SoCal, NE of Santa Barbara
  Island; unregistered channel, medium confidence): a hand-inflated balloon
  flown together with the kite itself in fishable wind ("one rod with a
  kite with a balloon on top"), not framed as a light-wind workaround —
  balloon's purpose relative to the kite's own lift not explained on camera.
  Same trip: an Okuma Makaira 50 Wide observed on the kite/fighting outfit.
- `UuyqTE21-kc` (StokedOnFishing, 2015-01-18 upload, lower banks near Mag
  Bay, Baja; unregistered channel, medium confidence): a helium balloon
  rig — no kite mentioned in this clip's audio — held a flyer bait on the
  surface, floated downwind of the boat; multiple hookups came on flyers
  fished this way while targeting giant yellowfin (150–300-plus lb).
- `3tQ1_xiqwVU` (StokedOnFishing, "Intrepid Long Range Sportfishing | Part
  2," 2012-05-01 upload, transiting from near Mag Bay toward Alijos Rocks,
  Baja; unregistered channel, medium confidence): stated plan, not shown
  executed, to fish the kite, helium balloon rigs, and 50 lb flyline for
  "quality" 60–90 lb (possibly a few over 100 lb) yellowfin at the next
  stop — a lighter target grade than the Mag Bay balloon observation above,
  kept side by side as a different leg of the same trip.

## Wind window

- `vCskOx6N-XM` (StokedOnFishing, 2022-03-23 upload, trip resolved to late
  October 2021, Tanner Bank, SoCal, aboard Captain Andrew's own boat, Stoked
  On Fishing Charters; unregistered channel, medium confidence):
  balloon-to-clip connection (twists in, no knot), clip-to-kite (rubber
  band), and a taped-fishing-line backup tether (about three wraps, "a
  little safety harness") so a rubber-band failure leaves balloon and kite
  cradled together rather than lost — the crew reported this exact failure
  happening on a prior trip, prevented that time by the taped backup;
  launch: hand the kite up, it flies straight up off the stern, then carries
  away from the boat once the boat turns onto an angle.
