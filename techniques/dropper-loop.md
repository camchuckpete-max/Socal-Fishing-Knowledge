# Dropper Loop

Execution for the classic dropper-loop rig: a **loop knot tied into the leader
holds the hook above a sinker that rides on the bottom**, so the bait wafts in
the current a set distance up while the lead pins your presentation to the
structure. A multi-species bottom rig — the target only changes the sinker
weight and the depth you fish it. Where each species holds and why is the router;
this note owns the rig and the retrieve.

## Reach for this when

- The fish are **on or near the bottom** and you want a bait presented on the
  bottom rather than swimming through the column — [rockfish and
  lingcod](../species/rockfish-lingcod.md), bottom-holding
  [white seabass](../species/white-seabass.md),
  [yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md) sitting deep, and
  [California halibut](../species/california-halibut.md) on hard bottom.
- You want **lines soaking in the rod holders** (drift or anchor) while your
  hands are busy on a jig or another rig.

## Building the rig

- Tie the hook off a **dropper loop knot or spider hitch** formed in the
  leader — tying mechanics for both, plus a stated reason for reaching for
  the spider hitch on hard-pulling fish, are in their own note: [Dropper-Loop
  Knot & Spider Hitch](../rigging/dropper-loop-knot-and-spider-hitch.md).
  A **longer loop gives the bait more action** — err long.
- Sinker at the very bottom, **6 to 10 oz**, hook(s) on the loop(s) above it.
  The bait then presents **sinker down, bait wafting out** above the lead.
- Weight and depth track the target: **rock cod → heavier sinker, deeper
  water; seabass → lighter sinker**; up the weight when the **current is
  heavy** to hold bottom. It is the same rig across species — only the lead
  changes.
- **Cut-loop variant** — the loop as a means to two legs, not a hook mount:
  tie a 3-to-4-turn surgeon's loop, then cut it off-center into a short leg
  (to the hook) and a long leg (to the sinker) instead of leaving it intact —
  full build in [Cut-Loop Surgeon's Loop](../rigging/cut-loop-dropper.md),
  shown for Guadalupe Island yellowtail and white seabass but framed as
  general-purpose (`rFmWrp-Vndo`).
- **Single-hook rockfish version:** a simple one-hook rockfish dropper, loop
  tied with a **surgeon's knot** instead of the dropper-loop knot/spider
  hitch above — see [essential knots](../rigging/essential-knots.md) for the
  mechanic. Keep the hook **close to the sinker — no more than about 2½ ft**
  of leader between the loop and the weight, for a flowing leader.
  **Sinker weight tracks drift speed, not just species/depth:** on a
  low-drift day, a **6 oz** sinker held bottom — light end of the 6–10 oz
  SoCal range above, since low drift needs less weight to get down and hold;
  step the weight up as drift/current picks up (`eEcRPEoG4DQ`).
- **White seabass loop height:** hook **12 to 18 inches above the sinker** so
  the squid rides clear of the bottom over/near a squid nest — the same
  "seabass → lighter sinker, kept off bottom" principle above, with a
  concrete figure (`ShSxNKAcUB4`). See also the [sliding-sinker
  suspend-and-retrieve
  alternative](sliding-sinker.md#white-seabass-weight-ladder--suspend-and-retrieve-your-saltwater-guide)
  for a cast-and-retrieve option on the same fish.
- **A second white seabass build, kept side by side, not reconciled:** a
  single **Owner Ai/Oy Twist** hook, size **50–70** — a long-shank J sized to
  pin a whole fresh dead squid; asr-uncertain — a follow-up clip from the same
  channel renders the hook name two ways in the same video, "Owner Oy twist"
  and "Owner Octy twist" (`d93vB_EBu30`), which does not resolve which is
  correct. Sinker: **8–12 oz torpedo**, sized to depth and current, fished
  typically in **60–90 ft** close to bottom over a squid bed. **Leader from
  sinker to hook: about 4 ft**, sized for bycatch avoidance (bat rays,
  sharks) rather than presentation, though it can also net a yellowtail or
  halibut on the same leader. **Hook-to-loop leg: 8–16 in** (a wider window
  than, but overlapping, the 12–18 in figure above), reasoned as letting the
  squid frolic in the current for a seabass working the bottom slowly.
  **Rigging the squid:** pass the hook through the squid's tail three times,
  flipping the squid between passes, then pull the tail up snug over the knot
  so the bait sits pinned and doesn't slide (`Eo_bA8IWvyU`).
- **A wider torpedo-sinker range for the same Channel Islands white seabass
  drop:** **6 oz up shallow to 12–16 oz**, scaled to how fast the drop needs
  to get down to capitalize on a bite — a broader range than, and not
  reconciled to, the 8–12 oz figure just above (`nRIAgz5G_Bc`; the same range
  is repeated verbatim in a re-cut short, `wl27BWAWpq0` — same footage, not
  independent confirmation). See also [hooks](../tackle/hooks.md) for the
  same short's hook-sizing portion.
- **Baja, Sea of Cortez reference weights:** bottom fishing for cabrilla/
  grouper there runs well past the SoCal 6–10 oz range — up to **16 oz**,
  stepped to **24 oz** for bigger baits or a ripping current; the exact rig
  format (dropper loop vs. straight bait-to-bottom) is not confirmed on
  camera, so treat this as a regional weight reference rather than confirmed
  dropper-loop parameters (`dEPuDrhoClM`).
- Heavier line classes are also documented for two specific Baja island
  fisheries — a **100 lb line minimum** for 30 lb+ yellowtail at San Benito
  Island (`JaKSGkZ6CAc`) and a **100 lb leader** routinely lost to rock
  cutoffs at Guadalupe Island (`RgtkbmBFUXI`) — kept side by side as
  different-island, different-variable data points rather than one
  reconciled number.
- A same-region Baja/Sea of Cortez grouper leader instead runs a **sliding
  egg sinker riding against a swivel, leader below to the hook** — that is
  the [sliding-sinker](sliding-sinker.md#baja-sea-of-cortez-grouper-leader-cesar)
  rig format (hook below a fixed sinker), not a dropper loop, and does not
  confirm which format the San Benito/Guadalupe reference weights above used
  (`M8hOYQ_6rSg`).
- **Long-range Baja Pacific yellowtail:** on 6-day-or-longer trips down the
  Baja Peninsula, an **80–100 lb line class** rig runs a **16–24 oz+**
  sinker (sized to bait/wind/current) and a **~2–2½ ft dropper loop** holding
  a **6/0, up to 8/0–9/0** hook — the same 16 oz/24 oz sinker figures as the
  Sea-of-Cortez cabrilla/grouper rig above, but a different fishery and
  region (Pacific-side long-range vs. Cortez), so a numeric match rather
  than one doctrine. Three ways to attach the hook to the loop are covered in
  full at [Surgeon's Loop (Giant Yellowtail,
  Long-Range)](../rigging/surgeons-loop.md) rather than duplicated here
  (`CdjT_I_PBHQ`).

## Heavy-tackle build — blood-knot loop

No target species or location is named on camera — a generic heavy-tackle
bottom rig: a **two-speed reel** spooled with **80 lb test**, a **16 to 20 oz
torpedo sinker** to reach bottom, and a hook sized **6/0 to 9/0** depending on
bait size, fished on a live mackerel, nose-hooked. Heavier across the board
than the 6–10 oz SoCal reference above — kept side by side as a heavy-tackle
variant, not reconciled (`KLmNyflzsQ0`).

- **Tying the loop (blood-knot method):** start with **24 in of line** to
  leave a long loop. Twist the doubled line **about half a dozen times**,
  leaving one twist running to the rod side and one to the sinker side. Bring
  the bottom of the loop back up through the middle between the two twists,
  then cinch the twists tight by pulling both standing lines — leaves
  roughly a **12 to 14 in loop** out of the 24 in of line used.
- **Attaching the hook — two ways:** (1) double the loop, pass it through the
  hook eye, then pass the hook back through the loop — simple, but holds
  less well; or (2) tie a [San Diego jam
  knot](../rigging/san-diego-jam-single-vs-double.md) or [improved clinch
  knot](../rigging/improved-clinch-knot.md) to the hook instead of the
  loop-through-eye method, for better hold. Either way, trim the tag end so
  the finished loop above the hook runs **8 to 10 in**.
- **Sinker placement:** about **36 in (3 ft) below the hook**, tied on with a
  straight improved clinch knot, no separate loop.
- **Fishing it:** nose-hook a live mackerel or sardine, drop to the bottom,
  put the reel in gear and wind up slightly so the sinker isn't sitting
  directly on bottom, and fish an **extremely snug drag**. On the bite, shift
  to low gear and horse the fish up and away from the structure right away
  to avoid losing it back to the bottom.
- **Corroborating build, same tying method:** join the two line ends to form
  a loop, twist the doubled line **about five or six times**, then pass the
  tag end back through and cinch down — framed on camera as the knot to tie
  when deep-dropping for a yellowtail, "maybe a Guadalupe" (read as the
  Guadalupe Island fishery above but not further specified on camera, so the
  referent stays unresolved rather than confirmed) (`PS8CRYwTPiU`).

## Deploying it — drop, detect bottom, read the bite

The name is the instruction: **drop it straight down over the side, never
cast it out.** The dropper loop is fished on marks the sounder shows directly
under the boat — casting it away from the boat carries the bait off the fish
(`OIqdmhKfuOc`).

- **Bait:** a live mackerel, nose-hooked side to side through the nostrils —
  the standard nose-hook placement covered in [fishing live
  bait](../bait/fishing-live-bait.md), reconfirmed here for the dropper loop
  specifically.
- **Hand position on the drop:** index finger on the spool, thumb on the
  trigger, left hand always in front of the reel — the same position the
  rock-cod quick gangion build below uses, and the same grip already logged
  from a different source in [drag setting](../tackle/drag-setting.md)
  (`F0g5r9Rkrd4`); that note's hookset is wind-only, no swing, while this one
  calls for an active two-crank-and-lift set below — the grip repeats, the
  hookset does not, kept side by side rather than reconciled. Holding the
  rod any other way means missing bites that come on the drop.
- **Detecting bottom:** the line stops paying out once the sinker reaches
  bottom, confirmed by slack forming at the rod tip. Take a **half crank up**
  off the bottom, then put the reel in gear. Keep re-checking through the
  soak that the mackerel is still riding on bottom, re-dropping and
  re-detecting the same way, rather than letting the weight swim up in the
  column.
- **Reading the tip:** a steady light **tap-tap-tap** is the live mackerel
  swimming on the loop. The tip pulling down and staying down under a
  **steady pull** is the bite.
- **Hookset:** on the pull-down, take **two cranks and lift up hard** to
  drive the hook out of the mackerel and into the fish's mouth, then keep
  winding until the fish is up — a hard, driven lift-set reading as a
  **J-hook** technique (a circle hook is meant to self-set on the wind, not
  get horsed). Contrast the circle-hook no-swing set in the rock-cod quick
  gangion below and the slow-steady-wind retrieve in *Fishing it* below
  (`U-dGRQ0X-Mc`) — three different hook/rig combinations on the same rig
  family, kept side by side rather than reconciled to one retrieve.
- **When the mackerel is too strong for the sinker** and keeps dragging the
  weight off bottom, clip a portion of its tail fin (top or bottom lobe)
  with line clippers. It doesn't kill the bait — it still swims and kicks —
  but it can no longer drag the weight around, so it stays pinned on the
  bottom (`OIqdmhKfuOc`).
- **A fourth hookset variant, personal preference rather than a rule:**
  fishing the standard rock-cod double-dropper loop dangling in the rod
  holder, reel in gear, the bite reads as the tip starting to **bounce,
  jiggle, and dance**. One angler's move is to give himself **a little swing
  as he turns the handle**, driving the hook home and taking up slack in the
  same motion, while noting other anglers just straight-turn the handle with
  no swing — a fifth data point in this rig family's already-unreconciled
  hookset spread (J-hook driven lift-set, circle self-set no-swing,
  slow-steady-wind, two-crank-and-lift), kept side by side rather than
  merged into one method (`S9sQ8Vg8-5o`).

### Hook orientation on the loop

Which side of the hook eye the loop is fed through changes how the hook sets
under load (`_ZThckj2TIM`):

- Pass the loop **into the eye from the point side** so the standing line
  runs off the **back** of the hook. Under load this **digs the point
  farther into the fish** as you pull — the recommended orientation.
- Feeding it the other way (loop entering from behind the eye, standing line
  off the point side) still hooks fish, but the geometry **pulls the point
  back out** under load — more lost fish, especially costly on a long crank
  up from 200 ft.
- Whichever way it's tied, **check both legs of the loop pull evenly taut**
  on the hook before fishing it — one leg tighter than the other
  concentrates load on a single strand instead of sharing it.

### Weak link at the bottom dropper loop

A second way to sacrifice minimum gear on a bottom snag, distinct from a
light breakaway sinker: fish **30–40 lb mainline** (25–30 lb if you can't
break 40 lb by hand) straight through a double dropper loop, no separate
light dropper to the sinker. When the rig hangs up, lock the reel down (not
free-spool) and let the boat's drift load the line until it parts. It
normally **breaks at the bottom dropper loop's tag point** — the short tag
nearest the sinker — rather than up the mainline or at the sinker knot,
because the long loaded section of mono stretches under the pull while the
short tag at the loop takes the shock straight. This recovers the gangion
and hooks above the break; only the sinker is sacrificed. See [rockfish
deep-dropping](rockfish-deep-dropping.md) for the contrasting
light-breakaway-sinker method used on the gulp gangion there — the two are
kept side by side as different mechanisms for the same goal, not merged
(`_ZThckj2TIM`).

## Single vs. double dropper

- **Double dropper loop** shines for rock cod and mixed bottom species —
  reds, chuckleheads, [lingcod](../species/rockfish-lingcod.md). Run
  **straight braid to a barrel swivel, swivel to fluorocarbon, then two
  hooks** (octopus offset class), sinker below both (`mUrihh0V59M`).
- **Leader line size and material, standard rock-cod double dropper loop:**
  **30 or 40 lb, mono by default** — cheap and effective since rockfish
  aren't leader-shy; step up to fluorocarbon only for a tough bite. See
  [rockfish leader
  line](../tackle/tackle-express-rockfish-leader-line.md) for the full
  breakdown.
- Bait each hook by **double-hooking the squid** — don't fuss about the
  upper hook riding above the knot. The double pin gives holding power
  because **rockfish short-bite**, and it keeps the bait on. Two baited
  hooks fishing vertically often come up **two fish at once**
  (`mUrihh0V59M`).
- **Premade alternative to tying either loop build by hand:** a packaged
  [P-Line rock cod rig](../tackle/tackle-express-premade-rock-cod-rig.md)
  ships with the gangion, top swivel, and a clip-on swivel for the torpedo
  sinker already tied — tie the main line to the top swivel, clip on a
  sinker, bait, and drop.
- **Simpler, swivel-free alternative:** rather than a barrel swivel plus two
  hooks, tie two **surgeon's-knot loops** directly into the leader — one,
  then a second **a little lower down** — using the same three-turn
  surgeon's knot as the single-hook version above (see [essential
  knots](../rigging/essential-knots.md)). Sinker goes at the very bottom
  below both loops. Kept side by side with the swivel build above as a
  different construction for the same double-hook-over-the-sinker goal, not
  reconciled to one method (`3gykKyPdOvA`).

## Rock-cod quick gangion

A fast, hands-on-the-rail build for rockfish when the surface bite is off
(`zVIfArUrpDI`). Captions render the rig name as "quick canyon" throughout —
an ASR mishearing of the established term **gangion** (a leader with
dropper-loop hooks), which is what's demonstrably being tied; not carried as
a separate rig name.

- **Sinker:** tie an **8 oz torpedo sinker** straight to the end of the main
  line — no separate light-line breakaway, unlike the **24 oz torpedo
  sinker on a 12–15 lb breakaway** figure already logged in [rockfish
  deep-dropping](rockfish-deep-dropping.md); a different sinker weight and
  depth range, preserved side by side rather than reconciled to one weight.
- **Loops:** tie **two dropper loops** into the leader above the sinker —
  wrap the line around two fingers to form a loop, twist a second, smaller
  loop into it with a half turn, then pass the standing big loop over the
  top and through that hole to cinch — a fast, two-handed loop knot tied at
  the rail (paraphrased; see the source video for the visual).
- **Hooks:** tie a **circle hook** onto each loop using a single-pass,
  7-wrap San Diego jam tie — see [essential knots](../rigging/essential-knots.md)
  for the mechanic (the same tie also attaches the sinker to the main line
  above). One hook fishes low, one up top, matching the double-dropper
  convention above. **Circle hooks here specifically because** most charter
  clients can't feel a rockfish bite in time to swing, so the circle hook
  self-hooks the fish on the wind-up instead — see
  [hooks](../tackle/hooks.md#circle-vs-j--the-core-decision-swrsycmt4tw-m2q22sppkem)
  for the full rationale and its personal-preference caveat.
- **Bait:** sardine, anchovy, or a strip of squid on the circle hook.
- **Fishing it:** drop to the bottom (index finger on the spool, thumb on
  the clicker/trigger controlling the fall), click into gear the instant it
  hits bottom, and on the bite **don't set the hook — just turn the handle
  and wind**, consistent with the slow-steady-wind retrieve below.
- **Why braid, filled to the rim:** built for **400–600 ft** rockfish water
  — see [rockfish
  deep-dropping](rockfish-deep-dropping.md#line-class-and-spooling-dave-hansen)
  for the line-class and spooling detail from the same source.

## Fishing it — deadstick vs. work it

- **Deadstick in the rod holder** is the default: keep **one to two, even
  three** dropper rods soaking on the drift, anchor, or in the holders while
  actively working a [lead head](../lures/soft-plastic-swimbaits.md) or a
  [sliding-sinker](sliding-sinker.md) rig — more lines in the water, more
  bites (`mUrihh0V59M`).
- **Work it** by lifting to keep the sinker ticking bottom as you drift over
  marks, then letting it settle back so the bait re-wafts on the drop.
- **Why re-drop periodically while deadsticking:** the default is bottom,
  into gear, **one to three turns up**, then hang; after a short soak,
  free-spool the sinker back down to re-touch bottom. The stated reason is
  the **drift, not the fish** — on a drifting boat (roughly 90% of trips, by
  estimate) the bottom's contours shift under you as you move, so a rig left
  at a fixed number of turns up off an old bottom reading can end up 20 ft
  off the new bottom without you knowing it. This corroborates, with an
  explicit drift-contour mechanism, the periodic free-spool re-drop in
  [yellowtail — dropper loop](../species/yellowtail-dropper-loop.md) (there
  reasoned from the boat swinging on a drift/anchor rather than the bottom
  itself changing shape) — two different reasons
  converging on the same practice (`S9sQ8Vg8-5o`; the same claim is re-cut
  and re-uploaded word for word as `nJPQVouJQ0g`, not independent
  corroboration).
- **The retrieve once hooked up: slow, steady wind, all the way to the
  surface.** No jerking, no pumping — jerk-and-wind-fast winds the fish
  right off the hook (`U-dGRQ0X-Mc`).
- September's BOLA yellowtail program is fished with dropper loops (and
  [yo-yo iron](yo-yo-iron.md)) for fish holding on the bottom that part of
  the season (cameron).

## Gear class

Bottom / bait-in-the-holder work — see [gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md).
The light-tackle single/double rockfish builds sit in the **30 lb bait
class**. The heavier builds documented above — 40–80 lb SoCal yellowtail and
generic bottom-tackle rigs, up to 80–100 lb+ on Baja/long-range yellowtail and
grouper — run past every application this lexicon currently names for that
line range (yo-yo/heavy-iron class is fast-crank gear for iron, not this
rig's slow-steady-wind retrieve): ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: a named
heavy-bottom/dropper-loop rod-reel class at 40–100+ lb. Octopus offset hooks;
loop knots and the barrel-swivel double covered in [essential
knots](../rigging/essential-knots.md).

## Common failures

- **Loop tied too short** — kills the bait's action; the long loop is the point.
- **Under-weighting in current** — the sinker drags off the structure and the
  bait leaves the strike zone. Up the lead until it holds bottom.
- **Single-hooking squid on the double rig** — short-biting rockfish strip it;
  double-pin each bait.
