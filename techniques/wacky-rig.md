# Wacky Rig — Reading the Bite in a Slack Line

Execution for fishing a wacky-rigged stick worm (a soft stickbait hooked
through the middle so both ends fall and wiggle freely — the rig's own
construction, hook style, and weight are not described in this source): the
whole technique lives in **leaving the line slack through the fall and
reading the bite off the line itself, not the rod.** No species or location
is named on camera (Tackle Express, `xFW2002SaQk`, 2026-04-17; unregistered
channel, single mention — medium confidence).

## Reach for this when

- Fishing a wacky-rigged stick worm and you need to detect a light bite
  before you can feel it in the rod — the same watch-the-line principle used
  in [ned rig](ned-rig.md), applied here to a wacky-rigged worm's slower,
  freer fall.

## The core mistake — a tight line kills the wiggle

Named as **the main reason people fail at this technique**: worried about
missing the bite, anglers keep the line tight through the fall. A tight line
kills the worm's wiggle — the free, unweighted wobble of both exposed ends as
it sinks is the whole point of the rig, and tension on the line damps it out.
**Let the line lay slack on the surface instead.**

- **A wind belly in the line is fine and doesn't need fighting** — "if it's
  windy and you got a big circular wind line, that's fine, too."

## Bite detection — read it in the line, not the rod

- Watch the line lying on the surface rather than waiting to feel the bite in
  the rod.
- **Sun line (segmented, colored line) makes this easier:** pick a single
  visible colored segment and track just that piece. Watch it **sinking** as
  the worm falls, then watch it **stop** the instant the worm hits bottom —
  reading the line this closely "really tells you what your worm is doing,"
  both through the fall and at bottom contact.

## Common failures

- **Fishing the line tight through the fall** — kills the bait's action,
  which is the opposite of what a tight line is meant to buy you.
- **Watching the rod tip instead of the line on the water** — the tell here
  is visual, on the surface, not a rod load.
