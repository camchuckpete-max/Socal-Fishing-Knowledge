# Wacky Rig — Reading the Bite in a Slack Line

Execution for fishing a wacky-rigged stick worm — a soft stickbait hooked
through the middle so both ends fall and wiggle freely. The technique is
entirely about leaving the line slack through the fall and reading the bite
off the line itself, not the rod (`xFW2002SaQk`). No species or water body is
named for this method in the source corpus; treat it as general
finesse-plastics execution until a corroborating source scopes it.

## Reach for this when

- Fishing a wacky-rigged stick worm and you need to detect a light bite
  before you can feel it in the rod — the same watch-the-line principle used
  in [ned rig](ned-rig.md), applied here to a wacky-rigged worm's slower,
  freer fall.

## The core mistake — a tight line kills the wiggle

Anglers worried about missing the bite keep the line tight through the fall —
the single biggest reason this technique fails (`xFW2002SaQk`). Tension damps
out the worm's wiggle: the free, unweighted wobble of both exposed ends as it
sinks is the entire presentation, and a tight line kills it. Let the line lay
slack on the surface instead. A wind belly — a big circular loop of slack
blown out by wind — is fine and doesn't need straightening or fighting
(`xFW2002SaQk`).

## Bite detection — read it in the line, not the rod

Watch the line lying on the surface instead of waiting to feel the bite in
the rod. Segmented, colored ("sun") line makes this easier: track one visible
colored segment, watch it sink as the worm falls, and watch it stop the
instant the worm hits bottom — reading the line this closely shows exactly
what the worm is doing, through the fall and at bottom contact (`xFW2002SaQk`).

## Common failures

- Fishing the line tight through the fall — kills the bait's action, the
  opposite of what a tight line is meant to buy (`xFW2002SaQk`).
- Watching the rod tip instead of the line on the water — the tell here is
  visual, on the surface, not a rod load (`xFW2002SaQk`).
