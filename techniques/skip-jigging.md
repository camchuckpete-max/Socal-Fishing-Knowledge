# Skip Jigging

Skip jigging is a topwater cast-and-burn presentation: instead of swimming a
lure through the water column, the lure is made to skip and skitter ACROSS
the surface itself. Billy, Fisherman's Landing Tackle Shop, via
SearcherSportfishing ("Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 120 (Surface Skip Lure),"
`OL8D1l73RVg`, 2020-10-08; `searcher-sportfishing` registered channel — a
single-mention topic on it at first) calls it *"a fairly crude technique...
very easy to do"* and names it as a way to draw **tuna, dorado, and
yellowtail** up from underneath to blast a lure they can see splashing on
top.

**Corroborating repeat, an earlier episode of the same channel/series
(SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 4," `Ud3hi9r1Nr4`, upload
2018-07-12, filed as a same-week Searcher fleet report — "straight from the
Searcher... real-time info"; `searcher-sportfishing` registered channel):**
framed specifically for **bluefin tuna** on the surface (the Vol. 120 source
above names tuna generically, without a species) — cast a **Shimano Colt
Sniper (100 g)** or **P-Line Laser Minnow (6 oz)** into the middle of a
boiling school on **40 or 50 lb test**, and, **as soon as it hits the
water**, put the reel in gear, point the rod tip in the air, and skip it
across the top through the boiling fish — the identical tip-up,
wind-as-fast-as-you-can mechanic documented above. A second registered-channel
episode independently describing the same mechanic is a **repeat**, not a
single mention, which is why this note's confidence is now **high** per the
repetition rule in [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md).

## Reach for this when

- You want a **loud, splashy topwater presentation** rather than a
  subsurface swim — the visual commotion on top is the draw, not a swimming
  kick.
- [Dorado](../species/dorado.md) or [yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md)
  (and tuna — species not specified in the Vol. 120 source, confirmed as
  **bluefin** specifically in the Vol. 4 source above) are around and you
  want to trigger a reaction strike from something they can see skipping on
  the surface above them.

## Mechanics

- **Gear: just a long rod and a lure** — no other tackle is called out in
  this source.
- Cast out.
- **Point the rod straight up in the air** — the opposite of the tip-down
  position [surface iron](surface-iron.md) calls for on its standard
  swimming retrieve.
- **Turn the handle as fast as you can.** The speed is what keeps the lure
  riding up and skipping across the top of the water instead of swimming
  through it.
- The lure skips/skitters across the surface; tuna, dorado, or yellowtail
  come up from underneath and blast it.
- On the bite, **keep winding as fast as you can** to come tight on the fish.

⚠ **Not reconciled with, and in direct tension with, this KB's standing
surface-iron retrieve doctrine.** [Surface iron](surface-iron.md) documents a
tip-down, steady-to-moderate-grind retrieve as the baseline and flags
reeling too fast as *"the single most common mistake."* This source presents
skip jigging as a **deliberately different, tip-up, flat-out-fast**
presentation — a distinct technique choice, not an error in the surface-iron
retrieve. Kept side by side, attributed, rather than merged into either
note's doctrine.

## Hook choice — a single big hook is not an upgrade here (Art Taylor/Mike, SearcherSportfishing, via Turner's Outdoorsman)

**(Art Taylor/Mike, SearcherSportfishing, `fgTmUq78ofQ`, "Tackle Tip Thursday
Vol. 147 — Live at Turner's Outdoorsman," 2021-05-14; `searcher-sportfishing`
registered channel — medium confidence, single mention.)** Asked whether a
big single hook would be an advantage over a treble for bluefin breezing/
eating bait on the surface (a **skip jig** or surface iron scenario): **no**
— his stated reasoning gives the single hook two separate downsides, not a
clean tradeoff against the treble. A single hook is **less likely to hook
the fish in the first place**, and the fish he does hook on a single hook
are **more likely to fall off / throw it** before landing, compared with the
stock treble.
**Color doesn't matter for this presentation** — "it doesn't make any
difference," as long as the lure is skipped; the action, not the finish,
is what draws the strike, corroborating the color-doesn't-matter-for-
action framing already logged in [surface iron —
color](surface-iron-color.md) and [tuna poppers and
stickbaits](../lures/tuna-poppers-and-stickbaits.md).
