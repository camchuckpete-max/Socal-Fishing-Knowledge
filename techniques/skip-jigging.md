# Skip Jigging

Skip jigging is a topwater cast-and-burn presentation: instead of swimming the
lure through the water column, wind it as fast as possible so it skips and
skitters ACROSS the surface — "a fairly crude technique... very easy to do"
(`OL8D1l73RVg`). The commotion draws tuna, dorado, and yellowtail up from
underneath to blast a lure they can see splashing on top. A second,
independent source frames the identical mechanic specifically for bluefin
tuna busting on the surface (`Ud3hi9r1Nr4`).

## Reach for this when

- You want a **loud, splashy topwater presentation** rather than a
  subsurface swim — the visual commotion on top is the draw, not a swimming
  kick.
- [Dorado](../species/dorado.md), [yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md), or
  tuna — confirmed as **bluefin** specifically (`Ud3hi9r1Nr4`) — are around
  and you want to trigger a reaction strike from fish that can see the lure
  skipping on the surface above them.

## Mechanics

- **Gear: just a long rod and a lure** — no other tackle is called out in
  this source (`OL8D1l73RVg`).
- Cast out.
- **Point the rod straight up in the air** — the opposite of the tip-down
  position [surface iron](surface-iron.md) calls for on its standard
  swimming retrieve.
- **Turn the handle as fast as you can.** The speed is what keeps the lure
  riding up and skipping across the top of the water instead of swimming
  through it (`OL8D1l73RVg`).
- The lure skips/skitters across the surface; tuna, dorado, or yellowtail
  come up from underneath and blast it.
- On the bite, **keep winding as fast as you can** to come tight on the fish.
- **For bluefin busting on the surface specifically**: cast a **Shimano Colt
  Sniper (100 g)** or **P-Line Laser Minnow (6 oz)** into the middle of the
  boiling school on **40 or 50 lb test**, and, as soon as it hits the water,
  put the reel in gear, rod tip up, and skip it across the top through the
  boiling fish — the same tip-up, wind-as-fast-as-you-can mechanic
  (`Ud3hi9r1Nr4`).

This is a deliberately different, tip-up, flat-out-fast presentation than the
tip-down, steady-to-moderate-grind retrieve [surface iron](surface-iron.md)
uses as its baseline — a distinct technique choice, not an error in the
surface-iron retrieve. See that note's Doctrine & conflicts section for the
comparison.

## Hook choice

Asked whether a big single hook is an advantage over a treble for bluefin
breezing/eating bait on the surface (a skip jig or surface iron scenario):
**no** (`fgTmUq78ofQ`) — a single hook is **less likely to hook the fish in
the first place**, and fish that are hooked on a single hook are **more
likely to fall off or throw it** before landing, compared with the stock
treble. ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-internal): this note's own Lures section
above states the Colt Sniper/Laser Minnow's stock treble can be swapped for a
single hook to help both action and hook-up (`Ud3hi9r1Nr4`), and
[foamer casting](foamer-casting.md#colt-sniper--sizing-sink-bite-and-hook-swap)
reports "single beats treble" for the same Colt Sniper (`lf3S28nh-kk`) — kept
unreconciled rather than presented side by side with a decision frame.

**Color doesn't matter for this presentation** — "it doesn't make any
difference," as long as the lure is skipped; the action, not the finish, is
what draws the strike (`fgTmUq78ofQ`), corroborating the color-doesn't-
matter-for-action framing already logged in [surface iron —
color](surface-iron-color.md) and [tuna poppers and
stickbaits](../lures/tuna-poppers-and-stickbaits.md).

## Gear class

[Jig-stick class](../tackle/gear-classes.md) — the same long rod
[surface iron](surface-iron.md) uses, needed for the same reason: casting
distance to reach breezing or boiling fish. Beyond "a long rod and a lure,"
the corpus gives no reel, line, or rod-action guidance specific to the
skip-jigging retrieve itself (`OL8D1l73RVg`); the bluefin-specific spec above
— 40 or 50 lb test with a Colt Sniper or Laser Minnow — is a lure/line
pairing, not a rod-class statement (`Ud3hi9r1Nr4`).
