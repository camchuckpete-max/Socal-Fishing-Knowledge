# Speed Trolling

Execution for pulling a fast-tracking hard bait to **locate** bluefin and pull a
bite over water you can't otherwise cover. The workhorse is the
[Mad Mac class](../lures/mad-mac.md), run in the **13 to 15 knot** band
(`z1CmWHqe9uk`). It is not a kite substitute for the biggest grade — it is how
you find fish and get bites when a [kite](kite-fishing.md) won't fly
(`z1CmWHqe9uk`). The WHEN-to-pull-what decisions live in the router:
[bluefin trolling](../species/bluefin-tuna-trolling.md).

## Reach for this when

- The wind won't support a [kite](kite-fishing.md) — too light to fly one, or
  otherwise outside its window — and you need to **cover water, locate fish, or
  pull a bite**. See [bluefin trolling](../species/bluefin-tuna-trolling.md).
- You are searching a zone with no surface sign and want lines in the water while
  you look.

## Covering water

- Pull the lure at **13 to 15 knots** — the speed the Mad Mac class is built to
  track at (`z1CmWHqe9uk`). Too slow and the bait stops swimming and rolls out.
- Work the water the same way you would any trolling pass: sweep the bird and
  bait areas rather than straight-lining away from them (see
  [trolling](trolling.md)); the point of the speed is to search, not to sprint
  past sign.

## Rigging that survives the speed

- Run a **heavy bite leader** (200 lb) **no more than about 3–4 ft long**
  (`z1CmWHqe9uk`) — any longer and the swivel stops at the rod tip before the
  fish is in gaff range.
- Keep the connection **low-profile** so nothing kills the lure's action at
  speed: a small stainless swivel crimped in, not a bulky snap swivel
  (`z1CmWHqe9uk`). Cameron's Mad Mac spec instead crimps the leader straight
  to the lure with **no swivel at all** — a different route to the same
  low-profile result (cameron). Terminal detail for both variants is in the
  [Mad Mac](../lures/mad-mac.md) note.
- **Buffer the crimp at the lure ring.** These lures rattle so hard at 13–15 kt
  that bare crimps are known to **break from the vibration**: connect through
  the lure's **split ring**, or on a lure without one add a **crimp sleeve /
  chafe protector** in the loop before crimping (`z1CmWHqe9uk`).
- **Crimp discipline:** **three crimps** per connection, seated **snug but not
  crushed** — over-crimping can kink or break the mono inside the crimp; use
  **stainless crimps on mono/fluoro** — brass is for wire, since stainless
  won't rust or damage the line in the sleeve (`z1CmWHqe9uk`).
- Tie the leader to straight **braid** (130 lb) for the main line: the thin
  braid cuts the water, telegraphs the strike or a snag straight to the rod
  tip, and — with no stretch — keeps slack out of the system once you come
  tight (`z1CmWHqe9uk`).
- Swap the stock back **J hook for a treble** — most fish are lost when slack
  lets a single J fall out; the extra points hold a fish that only swipes the
  tail (`z1CmWHqe9uk`).

## Hookup and handling at speed

- When a fish eats at 13–15 knots the strike shows first in the **rod tip**;
  knock the boat down and clear the other lines before you fight.
- The braid main line keeps you tight through the deceleration — the moment slack
  enters is the moment a hot fish throws the hook.

## Gear class

- A **heavy rod class** — this is factor 1 of the
  [rod-to-lure pairing framework](trolling.md): the tow and drag load of a
  full-size Mad Mac at speed-troll speeds needs a heavy rod behind it. See
  [gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md#rod--reel-classes) — the nearest
  documented row is the trolling/spreader-bar class, though that row's
  150–200 lb line spec runs heavier than the 130 lb braid main line used here.

## Common failures

- A **bulky terminal connection** (big snap swivel) that impedes the lure and
  kills its swimming action at speed (`z1CmWHqe9uk`).
- Leaving the **single J** on the back — swap to a treble to hold
  short-strikers (`z1CmWHqe9uk`).
- Trolling **too slow**, so the Mad Mac never tracks and swims dead.
- Straight-lining away from sign instead of sweeping it (see
  [trolling](trolling.md)).
