# Cheater Troll

A stopgap trolling trick for a SoCal live-bait sportboat (half-day through
multi-day trips): rig a swimbait/jig — built with a **double-collar keeper**
so the soft-plastic trailer doesn't slide or wrap (see
[bait-keeper collar design](../lures/soft-plastic-swimbaits.md#bait-keeper-collar-design--why-it-matters-robert-schneider-phoenix-baits))
— on a spare rod and troll or slow-drag it off the side or back of the boat
during the boat's dead time: the stretch between a hookup/chum stop and the
deckhand's bait call, when most of the rail is standing idle at the bait tank
waiting to be handed a live bait.

Robert Schneider — name and lure company (Phoenix Baits) as given in the
auto-generated intro narration of a Fred Hall Show seminar, not independently
verified against typed video metadata — describes picking up an occasional
extra yellowtail this way: *"I'm out there when everybody else is waiting for
bait."* He frames it as an efficient way to sneak an extra bite out of
downtime, not a program to run instead of what the boat is actually doing
(`NXvqLUZ6qp8`, 2019; unregistered speaker, single mention — medium
confidence).

## Reach for this when

- You're on a SoCal live-bait boat between stops, most of the rail is idle at
  the bait tank, and you have a spare rod already rigged.
- Also usable as a **cheater trolling rig while working the slide** (a
  drifting presentation) — stick the rod out wide off the rail so the line
  stays clear of everyone else fishing live bait. The source also gestures at
  a second use case here, but the audio is too garbled to extract reliably
  (possibly kite fishing) — not carried forward as a claim.

## Etiquette and failure mode

- **Only do this if you know what you're doing, and stay out of the way of
  the boat's actual trolling pattern and the live-bait fishermen at the
  rail** — captains generally tolerate it on that condition.
- **The live-bait rotation always wins.** The moment the deckhand starts
  passing out bait, get the cheater-troll line in and out of the way before
  the rail gets going.
- **Risk:** run carelessly — especially while working the slide — this rig
  can foul the boat's actual trolling gear when the crew brings it up. That's
  the stated cost of getting it wrong.
