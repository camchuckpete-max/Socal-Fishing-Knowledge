# Panga Team Trolling — Follow-Up Cast on a Troll Hookup

A 3-angler panga program for yellowtail: the moment one of two trolled rods
gets bit, the crew's other anglers work the same water instead of only
fighting the hooked fish. The stated reason: yellowtail school, so a strike
on a trolled line means more fish are almost certainly still around the boat
(Tackle Express, `jo8lMdFZ1bk`, 2026-05-11; unregistered channel, single
mention — medium confidence). No location is named on camera — logged as
Baja panga fishing generally (panga being a Baja/Mexico open-boat type), not
pinned to a specific bank or island.

## Reach for this when

- Trolling for yellowtail from a small Baja panga running **two trolled
  rods** with **three anglers** aboard, and one trolled rod gets bit.

## Mechanics — role split on the hookup

- **The angler on the hooked rod** fights the fish.
- **A second angler grabs the other trolled rod** and winds it in — that rod
  is described as "normally" getting bit too as it comes tight, i.e. worked
  as a second hookup opportunity rather than left to trail passively behind
  the boat.
- **The third angler, with no rod already committed, immediately casts a
  lure into the same water** — a stickbait (named on camera as a
  ["Colt Sniper"-class bait](../lures/tuna-poppers-and-stickbaits.md#colt-sniper-stickbait--jerkbait-yellowtail--a-different-product-from-the-colt-sniper-casting-jig-above))
  or a [surface iron](surface-iron.md) — rather than
  waiting for the fight to finish. Framed on camera as a high-percentage
  cast: "your chances of getting bit are pretty high," since the school is
  still there and already keyed up by the commotion.

## Follow-up jig — the "stupid tube"

A second speaker on the same clip adds a standing go-to for this same
moment — casting right behind any hookup, not only a troll strike — a
**1.5–2 oz** jig he nicknames "the stupid tube." No model or brand name is
given on camera (nothing else survived the auto-captions clearly enough to
carry), so this is logged as the described weight class, not a named
product. His framing: simplicity produces — "as long as they're in the
water, you have a good shot at catching a fish."
