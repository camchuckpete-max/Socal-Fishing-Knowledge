# Deep-Drop Swordfishing (daytime)

Execution for daytime broadbill: a dead bait presented **in or just below the
[deep scattering layer](../conditions/deep-scattering-layer.md)** on deep contour
structure, on a slow drift, with a breakaway lead. Where/when this applies
and how to pick the spot is the router: [swordfish](../species/swordfish.md).
Numbers here are **SoCal**; see the East-coast contrast at the end.

## Reach for this when

- Daytime, deep contour break (150–350 fm) with the DSL present at ≥600 ft
  **and** chunky bait balls at 100–150+ fm on the meter (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
- Fair weather: **wind <13 kt, drift ≤ ~2–2.5 kt** (a **drift sock** extends
  marginal days). Above **15 kt** is a no-go for the whole SoCal fleet
  (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).

## Drift setup

- Set the drift **up-current of the fathom line** so the bait sweeps the
  structure as you drift down onto it; the preferred line is **"down and
  in"** along the contour edge the fish dwell and feed on (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
- Run two rods — a buoy-gear rod and a tip rod — staggered to different
  depths, and budget **roughly 7–9 minutes** to get both baits down and
  fishing (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
- Baits ride the layer and roughly **100–250 ft below** it: SoCal baits
  average **800–1,100 ft**, run shallower when the layer is shallow — a DSL
  read at 850 ft puts the bait at about 900 ft (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).

## Rigging parameters (SoCal)

- **Lead:** breakaway **7–10.5 lb, matched to the drift** — about 7 lb on the
  low end, stepped up toward 10.5 lb as drift and current pick up — on a
  light stick / rubber-band breakaway so it drops on the bite (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-internal): this note (and the corpus) also
  describe the lead as **one stationary weight taken off by hand at the boat
  around 30 ft** during the fight (see Drag & the endgame below), not a
  mechanism that "drops on the bite" — no rigging step in either source video
  describes a load-triggered release at the strike (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
- **Hook:** a **14/0 offset circle hook, very heavy-duty forged**, with a
  deliberately big barb chosen for extra purchase against everything a
  swordfish does at boatside (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`). It is rigged sewn so the hook
  exits the top of the squid's mantle: the fish eats the bait and swims up
  against the weight, and the circle finds the corner of the mouth rather
  than fouling like a J-hook can (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
- **Line & leader build:** **80 lb braid** main line (some crews drop to
  **65 lb** in hard current, trading a little cutting-water advantage for
  less resistance) to a **25 ft, 200 lb wind-on**, then a **10–15 ft leader
  of 200 lb at the least, 300 lb at the most**, swivel to bait — a
  deliberately **short-leader system** overall, so the fish is in gaff range
  once the swivel reaches the rod tip; heavy
  [bite-leader class](../rigging/bite-leaders.md) (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the **65 lb** option under this
  SoCal-labeled build is Rush Maltz's Gulf Stream/East-coast line class
  ("both I'm using 80 and 65 ... if the current's running really hard I
  will drop down to that 65"), not a stated SoCal-crew practice — Duane
  Diego Mellor, the sole SoCal captain in this source, states only **80 lb
  braid** ("we're using 80 pound braid") and never mentions 65 lb
  (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
- **Light:** a single **LP-class clip-on light 10–15 ft from the bait** —
  green or blue is the SoCal standard, switching to **white early and late in
  the day**, run bright to compete with the crowd of other boats' baits on
  the same ground (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`). Fishing with no light is a rigging
  failure, not a variant.
- **Bait:** dead squid, mackerel, or mullet-class, rigged to swim/track on
  the drop (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): mackerel and mullet are listed here
  as bait options, but the corpus's only mackerel trial ended when the bait
  swam and fouled the leader and was abandoned — "other than that it's just
  been squid" — and mullet is never described as a bait choice anywhere in
  either source; it appears only as stomach content pulled from an unrelated
  fish (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).

## Bite handling

- The best hookups come from **rods nobody touches** — the biggest "wolf"
  bites come back on the buoy-gear rod that no one is winding on or teasing,
  landing more fish per bite than the actively-worked rods (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
  Resist grabbing the rod on the first taps.
- The breakaway lead drops on the hookset so you fight the fish clean
  (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
- The circle-hook rig is designed to let the fish hook itself as it swims up
  against the weight; interfering with the bait mid-take is what is believed
  to disrupt the feeding pattern and cost hookups (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).

## Drag & the endgame (SoCal)

- **Drag at strike — the choice turns on how much resistance the rig already
  carries, not on a single correct number.** A build with less inherent load
  (a shorter, lighter leader system) runs drag heavier at strike so the hook
  buries and nothing pulls loose: **Rush Maltz** runs **18–20 lb** at strike,
  backed off after the hookset. A build that already carries more resistance
  — **Duane Diego Mellor**'s heavier **15 lb** lead and a long **150 ft**
  wind-on — starts heavier too but backs off fast, **two or three clicks**,
  down to about **10 lb at the most** pulling on the fish, because the lead
  and leader are already doing part of the work. Both framings agree the
  setting is more a preference than a critical one: a properly buried circle
  hook holds regardless, the same way a buoy sitting on effectively zero
  rod-and-reel drag still lands fish all day (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the 15 lb lead / 150 ft wind-on /
  "twice as much line as you guys have out" description in this source is
  Rush Maltz's Gulf Stream rig, not Duane Diego Mellor's — Duane elsewhere in
  this same video states a 7–10.5 lb lead and a 25 ft wind-on. The speaker
  with the 15 lb lead also says the opposite of "backs off ... down to about
  10 lb at the most": "I can't back off that much because ... I already have
  a 15 pound lead on there" (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
- **Boat stays in gear** through the endgame — a slowly moving boat keeps the
  fish tracking near-motionless alongside, almost "in a trance"; at a
  standstill nearly every fish darts under the boat and into the motors
  (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
- **The fight changes at 30 ft**, when the lead is taken off by hand and the
  load transfers from the weight to the rod tip — the point everyone
  describes as when things "get weird" and the fish can suddenly dart
  anywhere (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
- **Leadering: no-wrap, finger-pull only** — never wrap and pull hard against
  a soft mouth; the standard sequence is boat in gear, leader by hand, no
  wrap (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
- **Finish with the flying gaff/harpoon to the gill plate**, preferably under
  the vertebra and back through, then a couple of gaffs and a tail rope — the
  kill shot goes in the gill plate, not the body (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).

## Gear class

- **Rod/reel — [deep-drop / swordfish class](../tackle/gear-classes.md):**
  heavy stand-up or bent-butt, a soft-enough tip to read a subtle "wolf it"
  take but with backbone to stop a big fish, on a 2-speed lever-drag reel run
  **manual or electric-assist**. Electric-assist is the common SoCal choice
  because most of the fight is the fish swimming up against the lead rather
  than pulling on the rod and reel — the outfit's job is mostly holding
  position until the weight comes off at 30 ft, so saving the angler's energy
  for the actual endgame is the point (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
- **Line:** **80 lb braid** main, chosen to cut the water on a drifty day;
  some crews step down to **65 lb** for less resistance in hard current
  (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): same **65 lb** misattribution
  flagged under Rigging parameters above — that figure is Rush Maltz's Gulf
  Stream/East-coast practice, not a stated SoCal-crew one; Duane Diego
  Mellor states only 80 lb braid (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
- **Terminal:** heavy **[bite-leader class](../rigging/bite-leaders.md)**
  (200 lb+) short-leader system — parameters above.
- A high-ratio casting or spinning reel built for retrieve speed is the wrong
  tool here: nothing in this technique rewards crank speed, since the bait
  sits stationary on the drift and the reel's job is drag control plus
  cranking distance during the leadering finish, not a retrieve.

## Common failures

- Dropping on a **bare DSL with no bait balls** — the bait *in* the layer
  next to a feed, not the layer alone, is the trigger (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
- Too much drift (**>2.5 kt**) — presentation dies; deploy the sock or move
  (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
- Touching the rod early and pulling the bait away from a tentative,
  "wolfing" fish (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).

## East-coast contrast (labeled — not SoCal doctrine)

East-coast/Gulf Stream deep-dropping runs **bottom-oriented (~1,800 ft)**,
against a much faster Gulf Stream drift (~2.5–3 kt versus SoCal's ~1–2.5 kt),
with tip-rod leads running **10–15 lb** (light current to heavy current) and
a buoy-rod lead of **6–8 lb**, and **long leaders** run out to 60–100 ft
instead of SoCal's short-leader system, so the fish is kept farther from the
boat when the weight comes off (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): Rush Maltz's own confirmed rig
elsewhere in this source is a **150 ft wind-on with a short 6–7 ft main
leader** ("we got 150 foot wind on and then ... our six and seven foot main
leaders," tied to the same speaker turn as his already-flagged 15 lb lead
above) — comparable in length to SoCal's system, not long. The 60 ft/"a
buddy ... gone to a hundred foot leader" passage sits in an unlabeled turn
immediately after Duane's confirmed 35 ft-total SoCal answer, and cannot be
confidently attributed to either captain from the transcript alone; as
stated, this contrast's "short SoCal vs. long East-coast leader" framing is
not what the corpus's one identified East-coast captain describes for
himself (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`). Different enough that the numbers do not transfer —
use the SoCal parameters above for the Bight.
