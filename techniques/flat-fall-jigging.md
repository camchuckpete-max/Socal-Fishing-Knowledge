# Flat-Fall Jigging

A free-spool-and-count presentation for large [bluefin tuna](../species/bluefin-tuna.md)
marked deep: drop the jig on slack line, let it fall to the bite zone, and
work it in partial up-and-down cycles rather than a continuous rip —
flat-fall lures are built to be eaten **on the sink** (`an_uw-5pjfw`).
Distinct from [yo-yo iron](yo-yo-iron.md) (a full-speed continuous crank on a
heavy iron) and [knife/speed jigging](knife-jigging.md) (a fast, continuous
rip to the top) — flat-fall is worked passively, without an active jigging
stroke. Lure specs for the class live in [knife jigs](../lures/knife-jigs.md#the-class--models)
alongside the other wing-fall/butterfly profiles. Execution only; where/when
to reach for it is the reverse map in **Linked from** below.

## Reach for this when

- Large bluefin are marked deep and the captain calls the fish's depth over
  the PA — the same called-depth workflow as
  [knife jigging](knife-jigging.md#reach-for-this-when), worked with a
  flat-fall's fall-triggered cadence instead of a continuous rip.
- **Bite zone** runs shallow to moderately deep depending on the trip:
  captain-called depths of **100–200 ft** (`an_uw-5pjfw`) and, elsewhere,
  as deep as **250–300 ft** (`shZCjX2-fkI`) and **240–360 ft**
  (`eehDVb6_GoI`) are all documented; [knife jigging](knife-jigging.md#reach-for-this-when)
  logs a further **250–350 ft** data point from a different source. ⚠
  Flagged gap — no corpus source: whether the spread tracks day vs. night,
  a different mark, or a different trip.
- **Windy conditions** — step up in jig weight so the lure still reaches the
  bite zone quickly; see [Jig weight and rig](#jig-weight-and-rig) below.
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-internal):
  [rockfish & lingcod](../species/rockfish-lingcod.md#situations--techniques)
  routes its trophy-lingcod row ("want one trophy lingcod rather than
  numbers," heavy jig class on a level-wind with 30 lb braid) to this note,
  and names small flat-falls as the numbers answer on shallow rock — a
  program nothing in this list covers: every trigger here is large bluefin
  marked deep on rail-rod tackle, and rockfish appear below only as an
  *incidental* catch of a flat-fall "fished too deep in shallow water."
- **Night, especially the sunset/sunrise window.** Live bait flylined is
  generally the best overall bluefin option, but flat-fall fished at night —
  bite window right after sunset or right before sunrise — has, for
  stretches, been described as the only presentation drawing bites at all
  (named example: late August–September 2019) (`tpmOYXYQwhU`), consistent
  with the same sunset/sunrise night timing behind the mono-leader choice
  below (`FXWOIB0TPfE`).

## Mechanics

- **Tie the jig directly to the end of the line** with a
  [San Diego jam knot](../rigging/san-diego-jam-knot.md) (`an_uw-5pjfw`).
- **Free-spool the reel** and let the jig sink; most bites come on this
  initial fall (`an_uw-5pjfw`).
- **Count to about 30 or 40** as the lure sinks — a rough, reel-agnostic way
  to judge when the jig nears the bite zone, rather than marking the line or
  watching a line counter (`an_uw-5pjfw`).
- **Wind the jig back up about halfway, then let it fall again** — repeat
  this partial-crank/free-spool cycle instead of winding continuously to the
  surface between drops. Flat-fall lures are built to be bitten on the sink,
  so maximizing time spent falling keeps the lure working the zone where the
  bites happen (`an_uw-5pjfw`).
- **Don't hold one static guessed depth** — if nothing bites after a while,
  wind up partway and let it fall again rather than leaving the jig sitting.
  Holding a static depth without re-dropping is a stated common mistake,
  especially late in a session when anglers tire (`eehDVb6_GoI`).

## Jig weight and rig

- **Go heavier, not lighter, especially in wind.** A heavier jig reaches the
  bite zone faster; too light in wind and the jig never gets down to the
  fish quickly enough (`an_uw-5pjfw`, `fgTmUq78ofQ`, `shZCjX2-fkI`).
  - Worked examples: **Shimano 250 g** and **300 g** flat-falls
    (`an_uw-5pjfw`); a weight ladder of **350 g, 320 g, 250 g, and 120 g**,
    defaulting to the heavier **250–320 g** tier and dropping to **120 g**
    only when it's calm enough that the boat isn't drifting much
    (`fgTmUq78ofQ`); a **250 g vs. 150 g** comparison, where the 150 g
    doesn't sink fast enough to beat a wind-driven drift to the bite zone
    (`shZCjX2-fkI`); a **320 g** pre-rigged lure fished for fish sounding
    240–360 ft down, with a stated **250 g** floor at that depth
    (`eehDVb6_GoI`).
- **Calm-day/windy-day split, with a stated mechanism.** On glass-calm days
  in the fall big-fish window (roughly August–November), a lighter
  **100–130 g** flat-fall works; windy days (example: ~20 mph) call for a
  much heavier **400–500 g** knife jig instead of a light flat-fall
  (`qLKLlZdiflA`) — see [knife jigging](knife-jigging.md#depth-control--jig-calling)
  for that side of the same seasonal call. Mechanism: a flat-fall's broad
  profile and fluttering fall suit fish that are holding station and not
  moving through fast — they "want it to flow through them, not past them" —
  while a big, fast-sinking jig sent past a holding fish can get ignored
  (`qLKLlZdiflA`).
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-internal): [bonito](../species/bonito.md#situations--techniques)
  routes a picky-surface-bite row to this note at **80 g** on light casting
  class — lighter than every weight documented here, against the
  heavier-not-lighter doctrine above, and outside this note's own scope of
  large bluefin marked deep on rail-rod tackle.

## Leader and terminal rigging

Leader material is contested between mono and fluorocarbon; the choice turns
on bite conditions (night vs. bright daylight) and the specific rig, not one
fixed rule.

- **Mono** — cheaper, and sufficient because much of the best flat-fall
  fishing comes at night (right at sunset or right before sunrise), when a
  leader's underwater visibility matters less than on a bright-daylight
  bite: **150–200 lb mono, about 4 ft** (`FXWOIB0TPfE`). Other builds run
  **200 lb mono crimped to a large swivel** that ties to the main line
  (`an_uw-5pjfw`), and, at **about 30 in** ("wouldn't go much shorter than
  that"), connect to an **80 lb main line** with a San Diego jam knot at the
  swivel rather than tying the 200 lb leader directly — knotting straight
  through material that heavy "makes an awful big knot" (`fgTmUq78ofQ`). A
  fish-size-keyed version ties the leader to grade: **200–400 lb mono, about
  30 in** — past roughly **50–60 lb**, bluefin's teeth get bigger, and a
  lighter leader (example given: 60 lb) risks abrasion if a big fish fully
  engulfs the lure; heavier mono removes that risk (`shZCjX2-fkI`). An
  earlier, lighter data point runs **100 lb fluorocarbon** instead, reasoned
  as abrasion resistance (full parameters in
  [Searcher big/giant tuna rig ladder](../tackle/searcher-big-tuna-rig-ladder.md#200-g-shimano-flat-fall-tackle-tip--weight-color-leader-and-gear-speaker-not-named-on-camera-tackle-tip-thursday-vol-3-jlfzih15fec-uploaded-2018-07-12),
  `jLFZIh15Fec`).
- **Fluorocarbon** — Art (SearcherSportfishing, via Turner's Outdoorsman)
  favors it on a named pre-rigged lure for abrasion resistance: **200 lb
  fluorocarbon, 36–48 in**, crimped with chafing gear to a heavy swivel.
  Reasoning: if a big bluefin swallows the lure whole, the leader sits
  against its mouth/teeth, so heavier abrasion-resistant material (not the
  80–100 lb main line itself) takes the wear (`eehDVb6_GoI`).
- **Crimp size must match the leader diameter.** An incorrectly sized crimp
  is a stated failure point that can give way fighting a 200 lb fish
  (`eehDVb6_GoI`); see [crimping](../rigging/crimping.md).

### Hooks

- **Configuration** — dual hooks off the tail, off the nose, or both; a
  chandelier rig (two hooks up top, two below); or a single big J hook
  below. No configuration is preferred over the others (`shZCjX2-fkI`).
- **Check for undersized factory hooks and upsize.** Factory hooks on some
  flat-fall models run small; ask the boat's crew, and swap for bigger hooks
  to improve landing rate on bluefin (`shZCjX2-fkI`, `fgTmUq78ofQ`).
- **Swap treble hooks for a single hook** on a flat-fall/knife-jig lure
  fished for tuna — a treble runs smaller than a comparable single hook
  (`shZCjX2-fkI`). On a single-hook lure specifically, too small a hook
  risks pulling free if it doesn't seat cleanly in the curve of a tuna's
  soft-mouthed jaw — the general reason a single, larger hook is preferred
  (`eehDVb6_GoI`). Upgrade specifics (including named Tady- and Lead
  Masters-brand replacements) are in
  [assist hooks](../rigging/assist-hooks.md#stock-flat-fall-assist-hooks-wear-out--upgrade-and-size-to-the-grade-of-fish-art-taylormike-searchersportfishing-via-turners-outdoorsman)
  (`eehDVb6_GoI`, `fgTmUq78ofQ`).
- Current-generation flat-falls are also called sufficient as shipped, with
  manufacturers described as having "keyed in" on the swap-it-out feedback
  and improved stock hooks/leader accordingly (`tpmOYXYQwhU`) — a
  differently-dated, differently-leaning data point from the
  wear-out-and-upgrade doctrine above; the two describe different failure
  modes (general sufficiency vs. wear over time) rather than a clean
  contradiction. ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: whether hook durability
  differs by jig model or generation. ⚠ Fact-check (unverifiable): the cited
  transcript (`tpmOYXYQwhU`) contains no discussion of flat-fall hook/leader
  sufficiency or of manufacturers being "keyed in" on swap-it-out feedback at
  all — its only manufacturer-quality content is a general aside on reel and
  lure casting ability improving over time.
- **Incidental catches** on this lure class: yellowfin tuna, yellowtail
  sitting deep on kelp paddies, and — fished too deep in shallow water —
  rockfish (`shZCjX2-fkI`).

## Tracking depth on the drop

- **Mark the line or run colored/metered braid** so a captain-called depth
  (example: 180 ft) is known rather than guessed — anything that raises the
  odds of hooking up is worth using (`lf3S28nh-kk`).
- **A plain-spectra substitute:** measure line out at home in 100 ft or
  200 ft increments and mark it with a Sharpie, then read the marks passing
  through the guides on the drop the same way colored line is read
  (`lf3S28nh-kk`).
- **Line-out is not true depth once drifting.** The jig trails out and away
  from a straight vertical drop as the boat moves, so reaching a 180 ft mark
  does not mean the jig is actually 180 ft down — actual depth runs
  shallower than the footage paid out. Read a marked or colored line as a
  line-out count, not a depth gauge (`lf3S28nh-kk`); see
  [line scope](../conditions/current-diagnostics.md#line-scope--reading-and-correcting-drift-off-vertical)
  — the same effect the wind-halfway/re-drop cadence above is already
  managing.

pairing, a 300 g glow flat-fall example, and session leader guidance — is in

## Gear class

Fished on [rail-rod class](../tackle/gear-classes.md#rail-rod-class--what-makes-a-rod-a-rail-rod-mechanism)
tackle — heavy action, graphite, roughly 7 ft, with extra-long hypalon
rubber on the foregrip so the rod can be braced against the rail rather than
fished entirely in hand — paired with a narrow two-speed reel, not a
single-speed. Reel examples: a **Penn Fathom 60, two-speed** (`an_uw-5pjfw`);
an **Avid HX Raptor-class two-speed**, spooled with 80 lb test
(`fgTmUq78ofQ`). ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): at this point in the
transcript the reel is named only as "a triple speed avid reel with 80 pound
test"; "HX Raptor" is said later in the same video, in an unrelated segment
about reel picks, not tied to this leader/lure example. Rod example: a
Seeker OSP rail rod, captioned "3 to 1" —
asr-uncertain, plausibly the same Seeker OSP 1x3 rail-rod product already
logged in [gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md#rail-rod-class--what-makes-a-rod-a-rail-rod-mechanism),
not confirmed by model number (`an_uw-5pjfw`).

## Common failures

- **Holding one static guessed depth instead of re-dropping** — especially
  late in a session when anglers tire (`eehDVb6_GoI`).
- **Reading line-out as true depth once drifting** — the jig trails behind
  vertical, so a marked-line count overstates actual depth (`lf3S28nh-kk`).
- **Undersized factory hooks and small treble hooks that don't seat** in a
  tuna's soft jaw — check with the crew and upsize, or swap to a single
  hook (`shZCjX2-fkI`, `eehDVb6_GoI`).
- **Leader material mismatched to conditions or fish size** — see
  [Leader and terminal rigging](#leader-and-terminal-rigging) above for the
  mono/fluoro decision and the abrasion mechanism.
- **Crimp size not matched to leader diameter** — a stated common mistake
  that can fail under a big fish's load (`eehDVb6_GoI`).
