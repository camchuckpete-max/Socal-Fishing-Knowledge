# Flat-Fall Jigging

A **free-spool-and-count** presentation for large bluefin tuna marked deep:
drop the jig on slack line, let it fall to the bite zone, and work it in
partial up-and-down cycles rather than a continuous rip — flat-fall lures are
built to be eaten **on the sink** (Capt. Art Taylor, SearcherSportfishing,
"Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 125 (Flat Fall & set-up)," `an_uw-5pjfw`,
2020-11-12; `searcher-sportfishing` registered channel, first citation of
this specific retrieve mechanic — medium confidence). Distinct from
[yo-yo iron](yo-yo-iron.md) (a full-speed continuous crank on a heavy iron)
and [knife/speed jigging](knife-jigging.md) (a fast, continuous rip to the
top) — flat-fall is worked passively, without an active jigging stroke. Lure
specs for the class live in [knife jigs](../lures/knife-jigs.md#the-class--models)
alongside the other wing-fall/butterfly profiles. Execution only; where/when
to reach for it is the reverse map in **Linked from** below.

## Reach for this when

- Large [bluefin tuna](../species/bluefin-tuna.md) are marked deep and the
  captain calls the fish's depth over the PA — the same called-depth workflow
  as [knife jigging](knife-jigging.md#reach-for-this-when), but worked with a
  flat-fall's fall-triggered cadence instead of a continuous rip.
- **Bite zone:** typically **100–200 ft down**, per the captain's read of the
  fish-finder in the wheelhouse. Shallower than the **250–350 ft** flat-fall
  bite depth Capt. Dave Hansen gives elsewhere in this KB (see [knife
  jigging](knife-jigging.md#reach-for-this-when)) — kept side by side as a
  different data point, not reconciled, since neither source states whether
  the gap is night vs. day, a different mark, or a different trip.
- **Windy conditions:** step up in jig weight so the lure still reaches the
  bite zone quickly — see [Jig weight and rig](#jig-weight-and-rig) below.

## Mechanics

- **Tie the jig directly to the end of the line** with a [San Diego jam
  knot](../rigging/san-diego-jam-knot.md).
- **Free-spool the reel** and let the jig sink; **most bites come on this
  initial fall.**
- **Count to about 30 or 40** as the lure sinks — a rough, reel-agnostic way
  to judge when the jig is nearing the bite zone, rather than marking the
  line or watching a line counter.
- **Wind the jig back up about halfway, then let it fall again** — repeat
  this partial-crank/free-spool cycle rather than winding continuously back
  to the surface between drops. Stated reasoning: flat-fall lures are built
  to be bitten **on the sink**, so maximizing time spent falling — instead of
  winding all the way up between drops — keeps the lure working the zone
  where the bites happen.

## Jig weight and rig

- **Go heavier, not lighter, especially in breeze.** A heavier jig reaches
  the bite zone faster; too light in wind and the jig never gets down to the
  fish quickly enough. Worked examples shown on camera: a **Shimano 250 g**
  flat-fall and a **300 g** flat-fall (brand name — low confidence,
  sponsored-claim caveat; `searcher-sportfishing` is a registered channel but
  product/model endorsements stay low per its registry caveat).
- **Leader:** **200 lb monofilament**, **crimped** (matched to the correct
  crimp size for that mono diameter) to a **large swivel**, which ties
  directly to the end of the main line. A different leader material from the
  **200 lb fluorocarbon** crimped rig given in an earlier tip on this same
  channel — see [Searcher big/giant tuna rig
  ladder](../tackle/searcher-big-tuna-rig-ladder.md#flat-fall-leader-rig--sk-250-g-vs-nomad-320-g-two-speed-outfit-speaker-not-named-on-camera-tackle-tip-thursday-vol-54-flat-falls-zkb13fnt6p0-uploaded-2019-06-27)
  — kept side by side, not reconciled, since the two videos give different
  leader materials for the same style of rig.
- **Rod/reel example (product/model — low confidence, sponsored-claim
  caveat):** a **Penn Fathom 60, two-speed** reel paired with a **Seeker
  OSP** rail rod (captioned "3 to 1" — asr-uncertain, plausibly the same
  **Seeker OSP 1x3** rail-rod product already logged in [gear
  classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md#rail-rod-class--what-makes-a-rod-a-rail-rod-mechanism),
  not confirmed by model number). The rail mechanism itself — **heavy
  action, graphite, 7 ft**, with extra-long hypalon rubber on the foregrip so
  the rod can be worked from the rail — reads at this note's medium
  confidence; see [rail-rod class](../tackle/gear-classes.md#rail-rod-class--what-makes-a-rod-a-rail-rod-mechanism)
  for the mechanism.
