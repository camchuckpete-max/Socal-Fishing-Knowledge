# Flyline

Execution for flylining live bait: a hooked bait fished **with no weight**,
left to swim on its own out and away from the boat — the most natural
presentation in the live-bait arsenal. The whole game is a lively bait, a
hook sized to it, and the right hook position for the situation. Bait keeping
and selection depth live in [fishing live bait](../bait/fishing-live-bait.md).

## Reach for this when

- Live **bait is in the water** and gamefish are on the chum or the bait line —
  [tuna](../species/yellowfin-tuna.md), [bluefin](../species/bluefin-tuna.md),
  [yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md),
  [white seabass](../species/white-seabass.md), or
  [dorado](../species/dorado.md) working a paddy or the boat.
- **[Baja] Casting live bait tight to visible structure** — a mangrove edge,
  a cove mouth, a specific pocket — where a weighted rig would snag and only
  a natural, unweighted drift reaches the strike zone (see Baja mangrove
  structure-casting, below).

## Hook position by situation

- **Nose hook** — through the clear spot right in front of the eyes. The
  all-purpose flyline position: the bait swims naturally, stays up, and can
  also be slow-trolled. Most reactive, most lively.
- **Caution on a tensioned nose-hooked bait:** any tension on the line (e.g.
  rolling the reel to keep it swimming) drags the bait's head back toward the
  boat instead of letting it swim away — the opposite of what a finicky bite
  wants (`48ZFXnCTTQE`). Shoulder- and butt-hook mechanics that avoid this are
  in [fishing live bait](../bait/fishing-live-bait.md).
- **Shoulder / dorsal hook** (mackerel especially) — keeps the line **up out
  of the water** and holds the bait **high, skating across the top** for a
  reaction bite. On sardine, the shoulder ("wishbone") hook point lets an
  unweighted bait be dropped straight over the side to dive to fish sitting
  30–40 ft under the boat; a true nape hook on a long cast to skittish,
  boiling bluefin folds the hook down on the retrieve instead, cutting
  resistance and hiding the point from a fish looking up (`aD0Iim9C15o`).
  Full retrieve technique in [fishing live bait](../bait/fishing-live-bait.md).
- **Belly hook** — just above the anal fin. Sends the bait **down and away**
  from the boat, deeper in the column. Cast shoulder and belly hooks
  **underhand, not overhand**: unlike the cartilage at the nose, both
  placements sit in thinner tissue, and a hard overhand swing tears the bait
  off the hook (`SAltQjih0ms`) — see [underhand
  casting](underhand-casting.md#soft-hook-placements-need-the-gentler-swing).
- **Butt hook** — near the vent. Also **down and away**, and it defeats **sea
  lions** at the islands that pick off nose-hooked baits while the bait still
  flylines and swims well. **Zero-weight only:** the instant any weight goes
  on the line, switch to the nose hook — a butt-hooked bait dragged by a
  sinker swims backward, which doesn't look natural (`w5_x6kkN-xE`). Full
  mackerel-specific write-up in [fishing live
  bait](../bait/fishing-live-bait.md).

## Bait, hook, and leader selection

- **Match the hook to the bait, not the fish.** A bait dragging an oversized
  hook won't swim right and won't get bit — size the hook to the sardine,
  mackerel, or anchovy in hand. A **1/0** live-bait hook is a common
  all-around size; step up to **2/0–3/0** on big donkey baits, step down on
  small bait. See [hooks](../tackle/hooks.md).
- **Bait choice:** sardine (the SoCal staple), mackerel (heartier, sometimes
  preferred), or anchovy — nose-hook location is the same clear spot on all
  three. Stated species pairings: mackerel for [striped
  marlin](../species/striped-marlin.md); sardine for tuna,
  [barracuda](../species/barracuda.md), [bonito](../species/bonito.md), and
  [yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md); anchovy for [calico
  bass](../species/calico-bass.md) (`gKrYKvqHUjk`).
- **Bait selection is the deciding factor.** Cull the bloody, scaly, lethargic
  baits into the chum bucket and fish the lively ones — hardest to catch in
  the tank. If a bait isn't pulling line off the reel within about **60
  seconds**, wind it in, flick it off, and grab a fresh one.
- **Circle hooks:** hold the hook **high on the arch and press straight down**
  into the bait — holding it from the bottom leaves no gap and mangles the
  bait.
- **Wire gauge by bait:** flylining sportboat sardine for bluefin, reach for a
  **4/0 thin-wire circle hook**, straight-tied — no swivel, no bead — with a
  [San Diego jam](../rigging/san-diego-jam-knot.md). Thin wire specifically
  because a sardine "has a hard time swimming" a standard/heavier-gauge hook,
  unlike a heartier mackerel; fluorocarbon leader throughout, since bluefin
  bite through anything lighter (`ftEvyfwjZFU`).

## Match line weight to the bait, not just the target fish

Carry a dedicated setup sized to the bait actually being fished, and swap
setups when the bait changes rather than flylining every bait on the same
line because it's rigged and ready: **40 lb** line to fly line a mackerel,
dropping to **15–20 lb** when the tank switches to (or only has) anchovy — a
small anchovy can't swim 40 lb line through the water nearly as well as it
swims 15–20 lb (`gKrYKvqHUjk`).

**Distance beats weight for getting bit.** The goal is to get the flylined
bait out beyond every other bait on the boat, into its own zone. **No weight,
hook tied directly to the line,** is the standard: a split shot or sliding
weight added to help the cast is a workaround for not knowing how to cast,
not a technique upgrade — learn to cast the bait itself instead
(`gKrYKvqHUjk`). The reel mechanics that enable this distance — dialing a
conventional's cast-control brake down as skill improves, and the
spinning-vs-conventional tradeoff for anglers still learning to cast — are
gear-level, in [rod & reel selection](../tackle/rod-and-reel-selection.md).

## Line sizing, bite guard, and depth control for finicky bites

- **Why thinner line gets bit:** in warm water, live bait doesn't have time to
  "cure" and toughen up, so a bait fished on line that's too thick doesn't
  swim convincingly and won't draw a finicky bite. Go **thinner, not
  thicker** — the opposite of the instinct to size up for a big tuna. Working
  range for a tough bite: **20–25 lb, no more than 30** (`48ZFXnCTTQE`).
- **Why lighter line breaks leaders:** braid has no stretch and fluorocarbon
  very little, so the shock of a bite or hookset lands entirely on the
  mono-to-fluoro connection — that parts light leaders, not the fluoro being
  "too light" for the fish (`48ZFXnCTTQE`).
- **The fix — a mono top shot + short heavy bite guard:** run **25 lb**
  monofilament main/top-shot line to a **50 lb fluorocarbon** section about
  **2–3 ft** long, joined with a [Seaguar knot](../rigging/seaguar-knot.md).
  The 25 lb mono is what's actually pulling through the water (so the bait
  swims like it's on 25 lb), while the short 50 lb fluoro rides inside the
  fish's mouth, along the gill plates and teeth, taking the abrasion a
  straight 25 lb leader can't survive (`48ZFXnCTTQE`). Stepping the
  bite-guard fluoro up — **30 lb main → 40 lb leader**, **25 lb main → 40 lb
  leader** — against 30+ lb yellowfin and bluefin measurably improved landing
  rate on the same trip. **25 lb mono, properly drag-set, handles far more
  pressure than most anglers assume** — test it before assuming a heavier
  build is needed.
- **Bait-not-swimming applies directly to bluefin:** when they won't bite,
  sometimes the bait simply isn't swimming right on heavier line — drop to
  **20–25 lb** against fish that can grade **40–60 lb**, set the drag
  correctly, and don't overpull; a well-finessed fish on light line can still
  be led in and gaffed at the boat. His hook picks for this scenario — an
  **Owner Mutu Hybrid, size 2**, and a small **J hook** over a circle hook,
  for baiting-speed reasons — are logged with the rest of the style/product
  doctrine in [hooks](../tackle/hooks.md#style--how-the-hook-sets). A
  properly lively bait gets away from the boat fast; if it doesn't, a long
  soak still works (`icpm7gADxvU`).
- **Mono sinks a flylined bait deeper than braid, not the other way around.**
  Braid is woven/textured, and a textured surface drags more in the water
  than smooth monofilament, so mono is the slicker, easier-swimming material
  for a weak bait. Braid also **floats**; mono does not — on a long soak with
  braid + a short top shot, the line sits on top of the water for most of its
  length and the bait rides shallow, only as deep as the short top shot lets
  it, while straight mono sinks and pulls the bait down close to the boat
  (~25 ft back in one worked example). At **200 ft back**, braid with a
  **10 ft** fluoro top shot holds the bait around **10–12 ft** down, while
  straight mono at the same 200 ft puts it **50–60 ft** down — a large
  practical difference for bluefin that won't come to the surface. When the
  bite is wide open this doesn't matter; when it's finicky, rig at least one
  rod with **50+ yards of mono** for offshore bait-flylining (`48ZFXnCTTQE`).

## Fishing it

- **No weight** — let the bait swim. **Cast well away from the boat**; fish
  are finickier close to the hull, and a bait sent out and away gets bit far
  more often. For the swing mechanics of casting an unweighted bait — and how
  hook position decides overhand vs. underhand — see [live-bait pendulum
  cast](live-bait-pendulum-cast.md).
- On butt- or belly-hooked baits, **keep tension** the moment it lands: a
  little pull makes the bait swim away like a propeller; give it slack and it
  turns and swims right back under the boat.
- **Casting a butt-hooked bait onto a visible school or foamer:** cast to the
  **edge** of the school rather than into the middle, then jerk the mackerel's
  tail as it lands — a butt-hooked bait swims away from a tail jerk, sending
  it toward the fish instead of sitting still on the surface (`w5_x6kkN-xE`).
- **Weighted exceptions to the no-weight default**, each independently
  reasoned rather than one rule:
  - Current running against the wind can flatten and sweep an unweighted
    bait flat; a light **1/2 oz** weight keeps it swinging naturally instead
    (`Rb5I2ljAqeE`).
  - A better grade of fish sitting deeper than the day before can call for
    **1/2–3/4 oz** of rubber-core or sliding sinker to get a flylined
    sardine down (`Ow3an9lSVh4`).
  - With **no visible sign** — no birds, no surface action — the read is
    that fish are sitting lower in the column; add a small sliding egg
    sinker to get the bait down "just a little bit further" (`qh3hL2Dt3HY`).
- **Baja Sea of Cortez open water (East Cape):** flylined squid, fished
  dead/slow-drifted with no weight, works as a chunk-style presentation close
  to shore, with fish holding **30–60 ft** down (`mL4Ph7t0WcQ`). Running
  multiple rods at different line-test classes at once — e.g. **30, 40, and
  25 lb** — and leaning the spread toward whichever gets bit is a fast way to
  find the day's preference, since the answer changes day to day
  (`84XPJAeH0Rw`). Continuous squid chum alongside a hook-baited squid on a
  smaller hook in the spread can hold actively feeding tuna on the boat, with
  fish sometimes taking the bait an estimated **50 ft** down (`GptrotE0x5M`);
  a circle hook is required for this fishing under Mexican sportfishing
  regulation (jurisdiction: Baja California Sur / Mexico; as of 2019-02 —
  **verify current CONAPESCA/DOF circle-hook rules before fishing**).

## Regional rigs

### SoCal offshore mackerel foamer rig

Outside San Clemente Island in to the beach, for bluefin on a private boat: a
**thick-wire Mustad circle hook** (sizing by bait, see [hooks](../tackle/hooks.md))
tied straight to about **3–4 ft of 80 lb fluorocarbon** with a San Diego jam
knot — no weight, no swivel, no beads. Bait is **mackerel**, nose-hooked,
thrown directly into the foamer (`RbqOKkINSCM`). Keep the leader short — a
long leader puts the knot up past the tip guide, where it hangs up on the
cast. Straighten fluorocarbon by warming it between the fingers before
tying, since it comes off the spool twisted and won't seat a clean knot
otherwise. This is a heavier, simpler big-bait rig for a boat-side foamer
bite on mackerel — a different situation (a fast, committed bite) from the
thinner-line bite-guard rig above, which is built for a slow, picky one.

### Baja mangrove structure-casting (Mag Bay)

Lopez Mateos, Magdalena Bay, Baja California Sur — inshore mangrove channels
and flats, not the SoCal offshore chum-line application above.

- **Anchor lay sets up the whole game:** read which way the tide runs through
  the lagoon before the anchor goes down, and predict how the boat will swing
  on the rode so the casting side lays right along the target mangrove edge,
  not just near it — the boat keeps swinging as the anchor digs in, so judge
  the lay only once it's actually holding (`FxgLol_IHa0`).
- **Tide phase governs when the bite turns on:** the **ebb from peak high
  toward low** is the best window — it flushes baitfish out of the mangrove
  roots past the ambush points. Near-**peak high** tide is a secondary,
  decent window. A **flood tide** moving from low toward high is the poor
  window — not enough water yet in the root zone for fish to stage and feed.
  Read the tide chart and time arrival to tide direction, not to being on the
  water first; direction is visible on the water as drifting debris
  (`dlxA22FVNGc`).
- **Stealth hook and leader for skinny, gin-clear water:** casting to water
  sometimes under **12 in** deep, a **size 4 thin-wire bronze hook** on
  fluorocarbon leader keeps the line and hook invisible; **25 lb**
  fluorocarbon is the stated re-tie spec, with spare hooks and leader carried
  because snagging trees and branches is routine. Separately: anything
  **lighter than 35 lb** has "zero chance" of getting a fish or gear back out
  of the bushes once it's snagged (`dlxA22FVNGc`).
- **Reel style is flexible:** a level-wind conventional works for punching
  casts into tight pockets; a spinning rod/reel casts these zones just as
  well for anglers who can't manage a conventional's backlash (`dlxA22FVNGc`).
- **The rig, stripped to the minimum:** a thin-wire hook tied straight to
  fluorocarbon leader, straight to braided main line — zero weight, no
  swivel. Stated sizing: **60 lb braid** to **35 lb fluorocarbon**
  (`-5kooyIyavs`). **Why no weight at all:** the water often runs a foot deep
  or less, so a sinker isn't just unneeded, it hangs up directly in the roots
  — flylining is the only presentation that works in this structure
  (`dlxA22FVNGc`).
- **Bait placement, not soak time, gets bit.** The target is a narrow strike
  zone right on the edge of the mangrove branches/roots — a specific cove,
  pocket, or the edge of a named tree. A bait that lands short, wide, or
  outside that zone won't get bit no matter how long it sits; if the cast
  misses the zone, wind it straight back in and recast rather than let it
  soak. On a fast-outgoing tide, even a well-placed bait is only in the zone
  for roughly a second before the current sweeps it out, reinforcing constant
  recast over passive soak (`-5kooyIyavs`).
- If a livebait stops swimming well on the retrieve, replace it before the
  next cast. Bait is sourced en route by cast/throw net at known spots on the
  way to the mangrove zone.
- **Species taken on this method at this spot in one session:** snook (the
  stated target/table-fish favorite), corvina, pargo, triggerfish, baby
  halibut, and a juvenile broomtail grouper — illustrating the technique's
  versatility across species holding the same structure zone, rather than one
  species-specific pattern (`-5kooyIyavs`).

### Anchovy bycatch — when to switch off flyline

Anchovy raises fish better than sardine or mackerel as chum, but the same
everything-eats-them quality is a liability once one's on a hook: a flylined
anchovy draws bycatch (e.g. mackerel) as readily as the target species (e.g.
yellowtail), forcing a cull through unwanted fish to find one keeper. The
stated fix is to drop the anchovy flyline for [surface iron](surface-iron.md)
or a [stickbait](../lures/tuna-poppers-and-stickbaits.md) when the bait tank
is anchovy rather than sardine (`8TO-tuYjwWk`). Many anglers also struggle
casting/hooking anchovy on the fly line at all; collar/gill-hooking rather
than plain flylining is one stated fix, said to swim the bait stronger — full
rigging in [fishing live
bait](../bait/fishing-live-bait.md#anchovy-nose-weighted-vs-gill-hook-by-rig).

## Gear class

A **soft/softer rod class** — a moderate, forgiving tip that loads on the
cast and keeps a light bait swimming naturally, and cushions the strike on
light leader. See [gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md). **Graphite fails
here:** a stiff graphite tip is too heavy to load up on the cast of a live
bait, so the whip-crack release either stuns the bait on landing (it won't
swim) or throws it off the hook; a slow-taper **glass or composite** rod
loads gently through the cast so the bait lands lively — glass casts best,
composite is the easier all-rounder for fighting a big fish once hooked
(`48ZFXnCTTQE`). Reel/rod specifics are in [rod & reel
selection](../tackle/rod-and-reel-selection.md).

Worked builds:

- **Yellowtail flyline:** an 8–8'6" rod, lighter action, rated 20–40 to
  20–50 lb, for extra casting distance; a free-spooling lever-drag reel by
  preference (a star-drag reel, e.g. a Shimano Torium 16, also works)
  carrying **50 lb braid** backing to a **30 lb monofilament top shot**, to
  **20–30 lb fluorocarbon** leader — 25 lb gets bit a little better, 30 lb
  gives extra leverage to turn a fish from rocks or kelp. Hook by bait size:
  **2/0** for jumbo bait/small mackerel, **1/0** for small sardines/anchovies
  (`A70kK2niu2Q`). An updated build about a year later standardizes on a
  **Shimano Torium 16** paired with a **Black Diamond 8'6" heavy rod**
  (20–50 lb, mid ≈30–35 lb), braid backing to **80–100 yd of 30 lb**
  monofilament top shot, then **4–6 ft** of **25–30 lb** fluorocarbon leader
  (matched within 5 lb of the main line) to a **1/0–2/0** live-bait hook. The
  leader runs longer than the ~3 ft actually needed after landing a fish
  because light (25 lb) leader gets cut and re-tied after every fish to clear
  nicks — the extra length is buffer against needing a full leader swap
  mid-trip (`LTCFjqtSI8g`).
- **Bluefin flyline, two-rod light/heavy system,** covering a **25–80 lb**
  line-test range across a trip. Light stick: an 8 ft Black Diamond rod
  (8'6" also works) rated 20–50 lb, paired with a Shimano Torium 16 (or
  Torium 20), spooled with a **30 lb** monofilament top shot — **50+ yards,
  more if possible** — to a short fluorocarbon leader matched to the top
  shot, for **20–50 lb grade** fish. Heavy stick: a shorter 7 ft Black
  Diamond rod rated 30–80 lb, paired with a Shimano Trinidad 20A, running
  **40 lb (sometimes 50 lb)** fly line directly, for bigger fish. Mono, not
  straight braid-to-fluoro, is preferred specifically for flylining because
  it comes off the reel more easily and doesn't stick to itself the way
  braid does, helping the bait swim naturally; straight braid-to-fluoro
  maximizes line capacity and is a fine alternative, just less efficient for
  this specific application (`jQW2HLkMsmY`). Hook sizing (Owner Mutu circle
  hooks, by grade) is in [hooks](../tackle/hooks.md).

Trip-level tackle data points corroborating these builds — line, hook, and
leader combinations logged by grade, boat, and location — are in the
[evidence file](evidence/flyline.md).

## Common failures

- **Hook too big for the bait** — it can't swim, so it doesn't get bit.
- Leaving a **dead or lethargic bait** out past the 60-second window.
- **Slack** on a butt/belly-hooked bait, letting it swim back under the boat.
- Wrong hook position for the conditions — nose-hooking into a pack of **sea
  lions** that then eat every bait.
- **Anchovy in the tank, targeting a specific species** — flylining draws
  bycatch as readily as the target fish; see anchovy bycatch, above.
