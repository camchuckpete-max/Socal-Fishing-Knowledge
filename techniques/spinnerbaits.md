# Spinnerbaits (Bay Bass)

A freshwater-bass-style spinnerbait crossed over onto SoCal harbor/bay
structure for [spotted bay bass](../species/spotted-bay-bass.md) and the
mixed bag that shares the grass with them. Distinct from the finesse
[bay bass plastics](../lures/bay-bass-plastics.md) program and from
[inshore crankbaits](inshore-crankbaits.md) — a spinnerbait's blade creates
**lift** on the retrieve rather than a crankbait's dig or a plastic's fall,
which is the reason it's reached for specifically over eelgrass. Source is a
single Time On The Water seminar panel (unregistered channel — medium
confidence).

## Reach for this when

- **Shore-pounding or skiff-fishing shallow eelgrass flats in a harbor**
  (Newport Harbor named on camera) — a long cast covers water fast and the
  blade keeps the bait riding up out of the grass instead of fouling in it.
- **Windy conditions** — the bait's weight and profile cast well into wind,
  where a lighter finesse plastic struggles.
- **Want a visual, dramatic fight** — one guide panelist named it his
  confidence bait specifically because the take and fight are dramatic, "it
  reminds me of fishing a certain [surface] iron" for the takedown.
- One panelist (a working SoCal/San Diego Bay guide) runs it as one of only
  **two** baits he fishes in the harbor day to day — a crankbait and a
  spinnerbait — citing it as his go-to confidence bait "whether in any bait
  fishing bay, Newport, San Diego Bay."

## The bait

- **War Baits HD spinner bait, 1 oz** (Lane) — named on camera as the
  specific model/weight fished for the Newport Harbor grass plan. The 1 oz
  size reads heavy for a harbor that's often only **10–12 ft** deep in the
  grass zones being fished, but the weight is deliberate (see execution
  below), not an oversight.
- **A second panelist (the guide) names his confidence spinnerbait as a
  "1 oz HP Warhead"** — likely the same product as Lane's War Baits HD
  above, but the model name as auto-captioned differs enough ("HP Warhead"
  vs. "HD," "Warhead" vs. "War Baits") that it is **not confidently resolved
  to the same model here** — flagged `asr-uncertain` rather than merged.
  Kept side by side rather than assumed identical.
- Blades are mounted so they sit **on top of** the bait rather than trailing
  behind it — the stated reason the retrieve lifts the bait rather than just
  spinning behind it.

## Execution

- **Long cast, let it hit bottom.** Don't start the retrieve on splashdown —
  let the bait sink all the way to bottom first.
- **Rip it out of the grass on the pickup.** The first turns of the retrieve
  are a deliberate rip to clear any grass fouled on the blades/hooks and to
  confirm the bait is turning and working properly before settling into the
  retrieve proper.
- **Wind it as those blades lift the bait off bottom** — the stated
  mechanism: as line comes tight and the bait starts moving, the blades
  create lift that brings the bait up and out of the grass on its own,
  rather than needing to be worked with the rod tip the way a crank does.
- **Slow the line down and let it pause/flutter, then repeat.** Once the
  bait is up and swimming, ease off, let it flutter back down toward the
  bottom, then resume the retrieve — a stop-and-flutter cadence rather than a
  steady grind.
- **Pop it periodically to trigger a strike.** A separate panelist
  corroborates the same bait class: popping the rod every now and then makes
  the blades flash/vibrate in place rather than just spin on a steady
  retrieve, and reports getting bit specifically right after a pop — cited
  as a recurring pattern, not a one-off.

## Reach for docks instead — decision and reason (Newport Harbor, Lane)

Asked what he'd fish shore-pounding Newport Harbor "tomorrow," a panelist
named on camera as **Lane** gave a two-bait Newport plan built around
**checking the tide first**, then reading water clarity to pick grass vs.
structure:

- **Grass, when the water has some color** — a downsized **PK3 swimbait on a
  neck-breaker head** (see [soft-plastic swimbaits](../lures/soft-plastic-swimbaits.md)),
  fished in the **"spotty" color** — deliberately imitating a spotted bay
  bass's own coloring, "feeding a spotty its own kind" — paired with the War
  Baits 1 oz HD spinnerbait above. He calls these two baits sufficient for a
  shore-pounding session in the grass: "I don't have a third."
- **Docks instead of grass, when the water runs clear — decision and reason
  stated on camera:** if he arrives and finds the water clear rather than
  colored, he switches straight to the docks rather than continuing to fish
  the grass, even though the fish are still living in the grass. His stated
  reasoning: in clear water the fish feel less comfortable coming out of the
  grass to feed because they're exposed to predators (birds, seals) with
  nothing to hide their approach; the docks/pilings give the same fish cover
  to feed from without that exposure. Under the docks he switches
  presentation to a **flipped slow-pitch jig or a crankbait worked right on
  the pilings** rather than the spinnerbait/moving-bait program above.
- **Read for which water you'll get:** clearer water is more likely later in
  the cooler months; a recent rain event (rain on the Wednesday and Thursday
  immediately before this hypothetical trip, in his example) dirties the
  water back up and tips the read back toward the grass plan.
