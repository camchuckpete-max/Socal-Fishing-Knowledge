# Swimbaits

Execution for soft-plastic swimbaits and slugs on kelp and reef bass. The core
skill set: **rig it weedless so it runs clean through structure, wind it fast
enough to track, and set on the pause — not the grab.** Where the fish hold and
which structure to work is the router
([calico bass](../species/calico-bass.md),
[barred sand bass](../species/sand-bass.md)); this note owns the rigging, the
retrieve, and the boat work.

## Reach for this when

- **Kelp or reef bass** and you need a bait that comes through cover clean —
  [calico](../species/calico-bass.md) up in the stringers, [sand
  bass](../species/sand-bass.md) over structure and hard bottom.
- You are **covering water** to locate biting fish along a bed edge rather than
  picking apart one spot — read the current and structure with
  [current structure](../conditions/current-structure.md).
- **Anchored in the surf or a bay mouth, working current:** cast a weedless
  slug/plastic and let the surf or current sweep it in and back out on a
  taut line off the anchor — draws bites from a mixed bag (calico, sand bass,
  halibut, small sea bass) in surf grass and junk that would foul a
  non-weedless rig (`RUuPNpK0x-s`).

## Bait vs. plastic

Plastics can out-fish live bait on calico when the fish are actively feeding:
"there's a place for live bait fishing, and that's on the party boat"
(`ZoIzpyyS9xw`) — the claim is that a plastic beats a bait angler working the
same water, not that bait doesn't work at all. The stated trick is fooling a
predator into biting something that's not alive or natural to it
(`ZoIzpyyS9xw`). The mechanical reason to leave the hook bare rather than
"tip" a plastic with cut squid or sardine: **a piece of bait pinned onto a
swimbait hook kills the bait's action** (`ZoIzpyyS9xw`).
Running bait-free also avoids the squid smell that draws flies and the sardine
scales that end up on everything anglers touch (`ZoIzpyyS9xw`).

Live bait still earns a spot for beginners: for a father-son or
grandfather-grandson trip with young kids (roughly 6–9 years old), anchor on
the leading edge of a kelp bed, fish spinning rods, and soak anchovies to keep
the action steady (`ZoIzpyyS9xw`).

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): one session logged cut squid
out-fishing the bare-hook approach at a specific spot on a specific day — a
single contrasting data point, not a competing doctrine; full account in
[evidence](evidence/swimbaits.md#bait-vs-plastic).

## Weedless rigging

- A **7-inch weedless / MC slug** on a **keel-weighted hook** — Gamakatsu
  **7/0, 3/8 oz** or a Trokar keel-weighted hook. Weights come **half oz, 3/4 oz,
  or nano**; the heavier **3/4 oz** is preferred because it **keeps the bait in
  the water while you wind fast** (`YIABTTYXeqc`, `n6PTy8g3pb0`).
- Replace the stock hook spring with a stronger one — an **Owner large spring**
  or **Trokar/Eagle Claw XL spring**. Bury the spring **all the way to the bait
  so the plastic butts up against the hook** — that keeps the bait tracking and
  makes it durable enough to land roughly **10 times as many fish on a single
  bait** (`YIABTTYXeqc`, `n6PTy8g3pb0`).
- Line: heavy leader against kelp and rock. **50 to 65 lb braid** on a
  **300-size reel** with **60 lb fluorocarbon leader** — the leader gets
  braided up and chafed just from throwing it in the kelp, so keep it stout
  (`YIABTTYXeqc`, `n6PTy8g3pb0`).
- A second workable combination runs the same idea with mono instead of
  fluoro: **65 lb braid to 40 lb mono**, cinched down tight for kelp-bed
  calico — markedly heavier than a freshwater largemouth setup (10 lb braid
  to 5 lb fluoro), which frames how much heavier the SoCal kelp/structure game
  runs (`9xNhdu2aBqE`).
- A heavier long-range-boat alternate: **7 ft 6 in to 8 ft 6 in rod** with a
  **level-wind (conventional) reel**, spectra or monofilament, throwing a
  **3/4 oz to 1½ oz leadhead** with a 4–8 in plastic — a heavier weight
  ceiling than the 3/8–3/4 oz keel-weighted range above, likely a casting-
  distance choice off a bigger boat rather than a contradiction (`r9bF3VtzDUg`).
- A 3/8 oz keel-weighted hook run clean through heavy kelp with no fouling
  corroborates the spec above; full account in
  [evidence](evidence/swimbaits.md#weedless-rigging) (`_r_qKX_7080`).

## Line, leader, and reel speed

- **Braid: 65 lb or 80 lb**, picked by lure type; one boat-side rig ran **65 lb
  braid to a 50 lb leader** (`P6Slg6RQiXw`).
- **Top shot: 50 to 60 lb mono or fluorocarbon**, either works — a length was
  given but heard indistinctly in the ASR as "3 to 4T" (possibly 3–4 ft; not
  independently confirmed) (`P6Slg6RQiXw`).
- **On heavier line (80 lb) step the leader to mono specifically for the
  stretch** — the same mono-for-stretch logic used elsewhere in this KB for
  heavy sinker-rig top shots (see [rod & reel
  selection](../tackle/rod-and-reel-selection.md)) (`P6Slg6RQiXw`).
- **Reel: high-speed, at least 6.2:1.** Mechanism — calico **come up, eat the
  bait, and come right back toward the boat**; a slow reel can't take up the
  resulting slack fast enough, and a fish that gets to change angle and dive
  back down toward the rocks/kelp before you're tight to it is more likely to
  get cut off. Gear ratio is about **how much line the reel recovers per turn
  of the handle**, not just a "fast reel / slow reel" label (`P6Slg6RQiXw`).

**Reel speed is a genuine doctrine conflict, not just a range choice.**
[Rod & reel selection — inshore
baitcast](../tackle/rod-and-reel-selection.md#inshore-baitcast--three-sizes-academy-ep15-onh1k2mop7q)
states Landesfeind's general inshore-baitcast doctrine to avoid 8:1+ ratios
because a SoCal gamefish doesn't swim at the angler. Florentino's
calico-specific claim above says the opposite — that calico **do** swim at
the boat after eating, so a reel of at least 6.2:1 is needed to take up slack
fast enough to stay tight before the fish dives back for cover. The ratio
ranges don't numerically clash (6.2:1 still sits under Landesfeind's 8:1
ceiling), but the underlying behavioral claim — does the fish swim at you or
not — is not reconciled between the two notes.

## The retrieve and the hookset

- **Fast, steady wind** to keep the bait tracking by default; speed varies with
  whether you fish **with, against, or perpendicular to the current**. On the
  surface-slug presentation the bait should **skitter along the top,
  occasionally popping out — but in the water more than out of it**, on a
  **one-two-three, pause** cadence; on the pause the bait **glides off to one
  side** like a freshwater glide bait.
- **Set on the pause, never on the grab.** The fish comes up from behind and
  eats it; **watch it eat, pause to let it turn away from you, then set.**
  Swing the instant you feel the bite and **nine times out of ten you pull the
  bait out of the fish's mouth.**
- **A missed strike from a smaller fish can trigger a bigger follow-up eat:**
  a smaller bass grabbed just the tail and came off, the strike itself pulled
  the bait forward, and a larger fish trailing behind saw it move and ate it
  whole on the next beat — a reason not to yank the bait away on the first
  bump (`9xNhdu2aBqE`).
- **Retrieve speed is bite-dependent, not fixed.** A slow roll or crawl along
  the bottom with stop-and-fall pauses can out-produce a fast wind, and
  cadence can be deliberately hunted (fast, slow, fast, slow) to find what
  triggers a strike — the pattern repeats across multiple independently
  logged sessions; full accounts in
  [evidence](evidence/swimbaits.md#the-retrieve-and-the-hookset)
  (`L3tkGVu516A`, `iQLyBzhOSi8`, `IATPg9110CE`, `PKf7G3uL4io`, `P6Slg6RQiXw`).
- **Rod: a graphite-composite blank with a soft glass tip** (a 7-foot
  live-bait blank, or a 7- to 8-foot graphite rod; a 9-footer for bomb casts
  at San Clemente Island). The soft tip **bends and buys the split second**
  to drop the rod tip before the set — a stiff XH rod just pulls the bait
  free. Length is a distance-vs-leverage trade-off: **8 to 8½ ft** casts
  farther into structure but tires an angler fished all day, while a **7 ft**
  rod gives more leverage pulling fish out of rock/kelp (`IATPg9110CE`).

## Depth and where you throw it

- **Weedless / slug up high:** short, targeted casts to **individual kelp
  stringers**, starting at the **leading edge** of the bed. The slug draws
  fish in from a distance and pulls them off structure, so bomb casts work;
  the weedless itself is a **short, pinpoint cast** to the stringers you can
  see.
- **Lead-head swimbait deeper:** sink the same class of bait **70 to 80 feet
  down for calicos** over structure — fish holding up and feeding will rise
  to meet it. For California halibut, a distinct count-and-pause retrieve on
  the same lead-head/swimbait combination is documented separately in
  [leadhead swimbait retrieve](leadhead-swimbait-retrieve.md) (`OURS05qEGcA`).
- **Pitch-and-sink at the kelp base:** pitch a **1 oz black/gold jig** (a
  leadhead the source calls a "hook up bait," brand not confirmed) right at
  the base of the kelp line and **let it sink straight to the bottom**; the
  bite typically comes on that sink. Run **25 lb fluoro** leader for this
  pitch presentation — lighter than the 60 lb fluoro logged above, a second
  workable combination rather than a contradiction (`KTsXdQXAnkU`).

## Weightless slug — the "reverse Sammy" retrieve

Rig the weedless slug essentially **weightless** — only a small **3/8 to
1/2 oz** belly weight, just enough to keep it upright, not to get it down —
so it fishes almost entirely in the **top 2 ft** of the water column,
year-round, including in cold water (`RUuPNpK0x-s`).

- **Retrieve:** short rod-tip twitches on a deliberate cadence make the slug
  dart and dance side to side on the surface — a saltwater version of a
  freshwater walking-the-dog topwater ("a big reverse Sammy"/Zara Spook
  action), a different retrieve style from the skitter-and-1-2-3-pause
  presentation above. Getting the cadence right is a learned skill, not a
  fixed count (`RUuPNpK0x-s`).
- **Why it works:** the erratic side-to-side action creates the illusion of a
  wounded baitfish thrown up against the kelp canopy and struggling to get
  away — the same visual trigger as a live sardine getting mobbed and thrown
  up onto the canopy while fly-lining.
- **Tackle:** **50 or 60 lb fluorocarbon leader to 50 lb braid** — heavy,
  because the bait is fished weedless right in the junk where the biggest
  fish live.
- **Year-round, not just warm-water doctrine:** one cold-water outing
  produced six calico bass and ten other bites in **55–56°F** water — colder
  than the same source's own stated typical target temperature of **64°F**.

## Working a kelp bed from the beach side

A distinct casting angle for a kelp bed that sits close to a beach: fish
**tight to the island**, cast **over the top of the kelp toward the beach**,
then swim the bait from the beach side back **over the canopy**, lifting the
rod tip to walk it across the top. Bass are visible sitting in the kelp as
the bait crosses, and the bite comes **the instant the bait drops off the
outer edge and starts to sink** — the same leading-edge take zone documented
elsewhere in this note, approached from the beach side of the bed instead of
from open water (`ZoIzpyyS9xw`).

Line: **50 lb** (down from 65 lb, after finding 50 lb still casts well and
doesn't hang up as easily), fishing a weedless bait based on a "warhead" head
with the wire weed guard removed by hand — the eye geometry still runs clean
without it (`ZoIzpyyS9xw`).

## Beach bounce in ripping current — the "stupid tube"

A soft-plastic tube bait fished **tight to the beach** with the **current
ripping**, no kelp involved — a distinct beach presentation, not a kelp one.
**Weight: 1.5 oz** — enough to hold bottom contact while working the rig in
**free spool**. **Retrieve: bounce it off the bottom**; each bounce, the
current sweeps the bait **5 to 10 ft further** down-current before the next
bounce, rather than winding it back. Produced "calicos every cast" one
session (`9a-Zy_D6c3w`).

⚠ Fact-check (single-source): no leader, hook, or exact beach location given,
and no structure (kelp, sand, rock) named beyond "tight to the beach"
(`9a-Zy_D6c3w`) — record as a data point rather than generalized doctrine.

## Kelp canopy — punching gaps under the canopy

Where fish hold **up underneath a dense floating canopy** rather than at the
edge, flip a heavy leadhead on a short, accurate cast into gaps in the kelp
itself — a technique transfer from freshwater bass flipping/punching. Fish
work the dropped bait from underneath the canopy before eating it
(`P6Slg6RQiXw`).

⚠ Fact-check (single-source): documented from one session; full account in
[evidence](evidence/swimbaits.md#kelp-canopy--punching-gaps-under-the-canopy).

## Fighting fish out of the kelp

Once bit, **set the hook and then wind — never pump the rod.** A continuous,
steady grind on the reel keeps constant pressure on the fish; pumping (lift,
drop, reel down) gives slack on every drop that a hooked calico uses to dive
back into the kelp. This applies broadly to calico on any gear, not just this
jig presentation — the same grind-don't-pump doctrine as
[chunking](chunking.md) (`KTsXdQXAnkU`).

⚠ Fact-check (unverifiable): kelp sliding all the way down a weedless hook's
shank can pop the hook free of the fish's mouth boatside. A described counter
— slack the line and drop the tip fully so the fish's head falls back
underwater, let it shake its head, then resume winding — is unconfirmed
whether it actually saved the fish it was tried on (`L3tkGVu516A`).

## Boat work — covering water

- **Whoever is at the wheel positions the boat** to keep the caster on the
  productive edge; the natural drift is usually in toward the beach, so swing
  the bow out to feed the angler the **outside leading-edge stringers**
  (`YIABTTYXeqc`, `n6PTy8g3pb0`).
- **Cover water:** fish a bed **20 to 25 casts** if you're getting bites, only
  **10 or 12 casts** if you're not, then move. Locating biting fish is a
  numbers game across many beds; slow down and pick apart only where you get
  bites or see followers (`YIABTTYXeqc`, `n6PTy8g3pb0`).
- Color matters little — a **green-top / gold-bottom** ("hot koch") is a
  confidence pattern that works cloudy or sunny, warm or cold. Fish what you
  believe in (`YIABTTYXeqc`, `n6PTy8g3pb0`).
- **Running quiet matters more over hard rock bottom, which echoes, than over
  sand**, where you get away with more: drift a spot on the wind instead of
  running the trolling motor the whole way in, and shut off electronics, the
  bait pump, and the livewell pump, lifting the trolling motor coming into
  the spot; full account in
  [evidence](evidence/swimbaits.md#boat-work--covering-water) (`PKf7G3uL4io`).

## Covering more water than the bait anglers

Framed for a drifting boat working a sonar mark or a squid-nest area with
[white seabass](../species/white-seabass.md) as the target, but the
boat-position geometry generalizes to any drift-boat program where bait
anglers are stacked on one rail fishing [dropper loop](dropper-loop.md).

- **The bait rail's effective fishing area is a narrow swath.** Standard
  doctrine puts bait anglers on the **wind-in-face side of the boat**; ten
  anglers stacked on that rail fish dropper loops that together cover a
  swath roughly **30–40 ft wide by the boat's length** (a 65 ft boat, worked
  example) (`X-itrm5QkcM`).
- **The remaining anglers cast flukes to the flanks instead of crowding the
  bait rail** — a 15-angler boat splits 10 bait / 5 fluke, with the fluke
  casters working **two casting arcs** off the sides. As the boat drifts
  toward a flanking angler and the cast distance shortens, that angler also
  stays clear of the bait rail's lines (`X-itrm5QkcM`).
- **The reason to fish this way is total water covered, not confidence in the
  lure over the bait:** the flanking flukes cover **more total water** than
  the ten bait anglers — statistically more likely to draw a bite if the fish
  are willing to eat the lure, true in most cases (`X-itrm5QkcM`).
- **Why both presentations can produce on the same drift:** for white seabass
  specifically, the fish "swim through" rather than holding still — if they
  pass through the bait rail's narrow swath the dropper loops get bit, if
  they pass through the flanks the flukes find them. Complementary coverage
  of the same drift, not one method out-fishing the other (`X-itrm5QkcM`;
  see [white seabass](../species/white-seabass.md#situations--techniques)).
- **A near-identical worked example roughly nine months earlier** — very
  likely the same underlying account rather than independent corroboration —
  adds catch counts: on the charters that established the pattern, 10 bait
  anglers put roughly **10 fish** on the boat (a limit day) while 5 fluke
  anglers on the same drifts caught **15 fish** — roughly **three times the
  per-angler effectiveness** (15/5 vs. 10/10) (`hjZ3hgq0Bzg`).
- **Halibut-specific extension of the same geometry:** the boat drifting
  toward the flanking fluke-caster's side presents the fluke to the fish
  **before** the bait rail's rigs reach it. An inactive halibut that will
  only eat an anchovy on a dropper loop still gets caught by the bait anglers
  eventually, but a fish willing to chase a lure gets covered first by
  whoever is first in front of it — the stated reason to prefer the flank
  position specifically against a fish that will chase (`hjZ3hgq0Bzg`).

## Scent

- **When to reach for scent:** scent earns its keep specifically on
  **bottom-oriented creature-style baits** when the bite is tough — the
  mechanism is that scent makes a fish **hold on to the bait a split second
  longer**, buying the time to get a hookset. It's **less necessary on
  reaction baits** — surface/weedless presentations and swimbaits worked
  with full-force strikes don't need the extra hold time. A confidence tool:
  "if it gives you confidence, why not use it, it couldn't hurt" (`ZoIzpyyS9xw`).
- **Named local scent brands:** Ron Mueller / Edge Products hot sauce, and
  Tommy Gomes' Uni Butter (San Diego) — two that don't smell "god awful" on
  the bait and don't draw wasps/flying insects the way some other scents do
  (`ZoIzpyyS9xw`).
- **Application:** squeeze scent directly onto the bait, or drop the bait
  into a sealed baggie or container with scent already in it and reseal
  between casts — mixing Uni Butter with hot sauce into a custom batch is one
  way to do this (`ZoIzpyyS9xw`).
- **Deck hazard:** hot sauce scent spilled or dripped on a fiberglass deck or
  rail is **very slick** — a safety/mess concern, not just a cleanliness one
  (`ZoIzpyyS9xw`).
- **Dirty hands are a scent detractor:** gas, oil, and dirty boat-line
  residue on an angler's hands, transferred onto a bait, **shuts the fish off
  fast**. Wash hands with a bar of soap or a squirt bottle before starting to
  fish, every time (`ZoIzpyyS9xw`).
- **A stated change of practice corroborates the confidence-tool framing
  above:** after previously not prioritizing scent, scenting baits as a
  standard part of the game plan is credited (without a known mechanism —
  "I can't tell you what's in it, works, I can't tell you why it works")
  with a personal-best calico bass; a garlic-scented spray is favored for
  soft plastics specifically (`lP6cg4eEU6s`).

## Multi-bait rigs

**Double rig:** run **two swimbaits off a three-way swivel** to pick up the
**followers** that trail a hooked bass — the second bait dangling alongside
the fought fish gets eaten and lifts the catch rate. Main line → three-way
swivel (San Diego jam knot); off one arm, a **shorter leader (~12 in)** to the
**smaller bait up ahead** (e.g. a 5-inch); off the other, a **longer leader
(~24 in)** to the **bigger main lure** (e.g. a 7-inch) running behind — the
12 in / 24 in split keeps the two baits separated. Heavier gear — **40 lb
Seaguar Blue Label leader on a Tranx 300 with 65 lb PowerPro and an 8-ft heavy
rod** — fished **up high in the kelp** in warm summer water when fish are
chasing bait (`9br4Z4sfcNI`). A hooked 3–4 lb calico that drew a follower
which ate a second angler's pitched bait, only for the originally hooked
(bigger) fish to spit the hook, illustrates both the payoff and the risk of
leaving a hooked fish in the water longer to draw followers; full account in
[evidence](evidence/swimbaits.md#multi-bait-rigs) (`tJFSV3AcIdE`).

**Umbrella rig / A-rig — bay bass:** a step up from the two-bait rig above —
a wire "spider" harness (a Recon Baits-style A-rig) carrying several small
swim jigs at once. A five-jig anchovy-profile build for [spotted bay
bass](../species/spotted-bay-bass.md) in Mission Bay, San Diego: bend the
short arms outward for an even spread while the longer center arm(s) stay
centered, using a **parabolic (gradual) bend, not a hard/sharp one** that
point-loads and weakens the wire. **Five hooks** (one per jig) is fine in
saltwater; a personal **three-hook cap** applies instead in freshwater — no
jurisdiction or as-of date is given for that freshwater cap, so treat it as a
stated personal operating rule rather than verified regulation text, and
verify current CDFW freshwater/saltwater gear rules before relying on it.
**Tie on with a Palomar** over the whole rig rather than a uni knot — a uni
knot may slip under a hard pull, and a Palomar won't — see
[essential knots](../rigging/essential-knots.md#parameters--judgment)
(`G6YRT4HNxr8`). A second Mission Bay build runs a heavier **1/4 oz VMC** jig
head on the bottom arm for a keel effect with lighter swim-bait heads on the
middle/top arms, small spinner blades added for extra flash in stained water
(skipped in clearer water), **60–80 lb braid to a 40 lb fluorocarbon top
shot** (stepping to 20 lb fluorocarbon on the dropper), worked on a straight
grind just above the eelgrass canopy, varying a straight vs. an angled tug to
change the fall angle and strike trigger, and also fished pitched to
docks/pilings on a long cast; full account in
[evidence](evidence/swimbaits.md#multi-bait-rigs) (`6-mi3Qxn37c`).

rule, knot, and the second build's retrieve/fall-angle detail) is deep
per-species execution sitting in a general technique note — queued for a
spotted-bay-bass × swimbait sub-article, `sources/relocation-queue.md`.

## Targeting a boiler bite

For open-water/less-kelp "boiler" calico — fish surfacing/busting on bait
rather than holding tight to structure — see the router row in [calico
bass](../species/calico-bass.md#situations--techniques).

- **Target selection over persuasion:** cast to a visibly committed,
  aggressive fish rather than working a piece of water trying to convince a
  neutral one to bite (`lP6cg4eEU6s`).
- **Read the water-clarity edge:** look for **less kelp, more open water**
  and **crystal-clear water**, over the more typical stained/seafoam-green
  water nearby — the clarity line is where to commit the cast, since the
  bait "gets lost" and fish spook in the cloudier water beside it
  (`lP6cg4eEU6s`).
- **Bait:** a Lucky Craft 119 (a minnow-profile hard swimbait; the model
  number is not independently confirmed beyond the on-camera name) paired
  with a **5–9 in soft-plastic trailer**. Profile order: **baitfish/anchovy
  first**, then **crab/crustacean-style**, then a **shiny/flash** change-up,
  plus scent (see [scent](#scent) above) (`lP6cg4eEU6s`).
- **Seasonal aggression:** in **July and August**, warmer water raises the
  fish's metabolism, so they eat more, bigger, and more aggressively — size
  baits up and fish faster during that stretch (`lP6cg4eEU6s`).

⚠ misplaced-content: the boiler-bite program above (target-selection rule,
clarity-edge read, lure/trailer/profile order, seasonal size-up rule) is deep
per-species execution sitting in a general technique note — queued for a
calico-bass × swimbait sub-article, `sources/relocation-queue.md`.

## Gear class

**Bass casting / swimbait class** — 7–9 ft graphite or graphite-composite
rod, low-profile 200–300 reel, 50–65 lb braid, stepping to 65–80 lb braid /
40 lb fluoro for the double-rig and A-rig builds ([gear
classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md)). Kelp and rock structure call for
heavier leader than open-water bass fishing regardless of fish size — even a
7-inch weedless bait chafes and braids up against structure on anything
lighter, so the 25–65 lb fluoro/mono step-ups documented throughout this note
are a structure decision, not a fish-size one. Baits and models: [soft-plastic
swimbaits](../lures/soft-plastic-swimbaits.md).

## Common failures

- **Swinging on the grab** instead of pausing — the single biggest cause of
  pulled baits, on both weedless and slug.
- **Too stiff a rod (XH)** — no tip flex, pulls baits out of mouths; want the
  rod to bend a bit once hooked.
- **Leader too light in kelp/rock** — it chafes and braids up; run it heavy.
- **Loose spring / bait not butted to the hook** — the bait slides down, stops
  tracking, and tears off; bury the spring to the plastic.
- **Camping a dead bed** — if the bites aren't there in a dozen casts, move.
