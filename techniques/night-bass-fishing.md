# Night Bass Fishing

Fishing calico bass, sand bass, and spotted bay bass after dark on the
premise that "nighttime's the right time" — bigger, more confident trophy
fish move shallow to feed once light drops, on the same walls, kelp, and
harbor structure fished by day. This note owns night-specific **color
selection, lure profile, structure choice (lit vs. dark water), and the
heavier-leader philosophy** that goes with fishing after dark for a trophy
fish rather than volume. Species-level moon/timing doctrine (the
full-moon-suppresses-the-bite pattern) lives in
[calico bass](../species/calico-bass.md#where--when) and
[sand bass](../species/sand-bass.md#where--when); general kelp/reef
execution is in [swimbaits](swimbaits.md) and
[inshore crankbaits](inshore-crankbaits.md). Source is a single Time On The
Water seminar panel (unregistered channel — medium confidence throughout;
individual speakers named where the transcript itself identifies them).

## Reach for this when

- Fishing local coastal walls or kelp at night specifically for a trophy
  calico or sand bass rather than a high-count day.
- Fishing a harbor/bay at night, where artificial shoreline lighting
  concentrates both bait and bass in the shallows.
- Running a crankbait on riprap or the rock sections of a wall at night
  (as distinct from open kelp, which one panelist avoids working with a
  crank after dark).

## Color: silhouette by default, translucent on a bright-light exception

- **Default night color is about silhouette, not exact color-matching.**
  One panelist frames it as a loose cousin of fly fishing's "match the
  hatch," but at night the fish are reading shape/contrast against the
  surface more than precise coloring — a good silhouette matters more than
  the exact shade.
- **Creature/craw palette, aimed at trophy fish specifically:** browns,
  oranges, some greens and reds, imitating spider crabs and lobster — framed
  on camera as "high protein, big fish bait," not a general forage match.
  Named combos: **black and blue craw, black and red craw, black and
  chartreuse,** and **purple.**
- **Straight black / "motor oil" black** is a second staple night
  silhouette, fished alongside the creature-craw colors above — one
  panelist describes roughly half his night box as straight black jigs.
- **A moon/ambient-light exception, decision and reason stated on
  camera:** a second panelist, who fishes a 5–7 in baitfish profile at
  night rather than craw baits, still favors a **dark presentation (black,
  or purple with flake)** as his own default — but on nights with more
  moon/ambient light he switches instead to a bait with a **clear,
  see-through body and reflective flake (silver or blue)**, reasoning that
  on those brighter nights the fish get more visual and a translucent
  profile reads better than a solid dark silhouette. He frames dark
  silhouette and clear/flake as opposite ends of one spectrum, picked by how
  much light is actually on the water that night — not a fixed rule.
- **Daytime iron colors, same session, for contrast:** brown, orange, and
  mint — consistent with, not a contradiction of, the mint/brown-orange
  color data already logged in
  [surface iron — colour](surface-iron-color.md).

## Booty spin — a night search bait built to be seen top to bottom

A **Kicker "booty spin"** swimbait (a paddle-tail-style swimbait with a
small blade in place of a plain tail) rigged on a **1 oz head**, run in a
**black back / clear bottom / red-cheek** pattern meant to read as an
anchovy silhouette. Fished on the 1 oz head it covers the **whole water
column, top to bottom** in one bait — named as a strong pick when bait fish
are stacked up against a wall at night. See
[soft-plastic swimbaits](../lures/soft-plastic-swimbaits.md) for the class
this bait sits in.

## Structure: fish the lit water in a harbor at night

- **Decision and reason, stated on camera:** artificial light near the
  water — dock/parking-lot lighting, warehouse lights, the big spreader
  lights on the oil-terminal islands at the Port of Long Beach — pulls bait
  and bass up into the shallows at night, because the fish feel comfortable
  exposing themselves to feed where they can see and be seen, in a way they
  won't in the dark. Reaction baits (crankbait/hard bait) work better in
  these lit zones specifically because the fish can see a moving bait there,
  versus blind-working unlit stretches of the same harbor.
- **Pre-scout the lit zones before the trip.** One panelist described
  spending time on a mapping app (Google Maps, named on camera) marking
  where the light posts sit along an inner harbor ahead of a trip, then
  running a deliberate pattern that hits each marked lit stretch on the
  water specifically: a few casts, move to the next light if nothing bites,
  and flip a jig to fill the gaps in between stops — rather than blind-
  working the harbor start to finish.
- **Cover water; don't grind one spot.** For night crank work generally,
  the stated priority is covering ground: two or three casts per zone, then
  push forward (trolling-motor pace) to the next zone rather than working
  one stretch exhaustively.

## Crankbait choice for kelp at night

One panelist doesn't fish much open kelp with a crankbait after dark, but
will run a crank on **riprap or the rock parts of a wall inside the kelp**
at night. For that specific application: switch to a **deeper-diving
square-bill ("coffin-style" bill)** rather than a longer-lipped diver — the
coffin bill comes through kelp cleaner than a long lip when fished right up
tight and parallel to the edge. Worked in roughly **8–10 ft** of water, on a
rod set up with a bit more **parabolic (softer through the blank)** bend
than his daytime crank rod. See [inshore crankbaits](inshore-crankbaits.md)
for the general crank program this sits alongside.

## Heavy leader for trophy night fishing — the reason, not just the number

The panel frames fishing heavy as a shared, group-wide philosophy for
targeting trophy fish ("speaking for all of us... we're mainly chasing
those trophy fish"), but the specific line-class numbers below all come from
one panelist (Matt):

- **80 lb braid / 50–60 lb fluorocarbon leader** (Matt, referenced by name
  later in the same panel — "like Matt was saying about heavy line").
- **65 lb braid / 50 lb fluorocarbon leader**, also Matt, on the same night
  program.
- **The stated reason is abrasion, not stealth.** Bass "really don't care
  about line, color" — the heavy leader isn't there to avoid spooking fish.
  It's there because a big fish hooked around rocks, kelp, or boilers at
  night will bury itself in the structure, and the leader has to survive
  that; even **50 lb** is called light for a calico that can reach 10 lb
  fished around structure. On lighter line (15–20 lb) you might land a big
  one if you're lucky, but pulling hard enough by hand to horse a fish out
  of the rocks will part it.
- **The goal is one trophy fish, not volume** — this crew frames itself as
  "not really chasing... a lot of fish. We're mainly chasing those trophy
  fish," which is the stated rationale for fishing heavy even at the cost of
  fewer bites.

## Gear class

The same **heavier bass class** tackle documented in
[calico bass — gear summary](../species/calico-bass.md#gear-summary-class-terms) —
nothing about the rod/reel class itself is night-specific; what changes
after dark is leader weight (step up) and lure choice (silhouette/craw
colors, the booty spin, and the coffin-bill crank above), plus targeting
lit structure inside a harbor.
