# Night Bass Fishing

Night bass fishing targets calico bass, sand bass, and spotted bay bass after
dark on the premise that "nighttime's the right time" (`XyKF34C5iB4`) —
bigger, more confident trophy fish move shallow to feed once light drops, on
the same walls, kelp, and harbor structure fished by day. This note owns
night-specific **color selection, lure profile, structure choice (lit vs.
dark water), and the heavier-leader philosophy** that goes with fishing after
dark for a trophy fish rather than volume. Species-level moon/timing doctrine
(the full-moon-suppresses-the-bite pattern) lives in
[calico bass](../species/calico-bass.md#where--when) and
[sand bass](../species/sand-bass.md#where--when); general kelp/reef execution
is in [swimbaits](swimbaits.md) and
[inshore crankbaits](inshore-crankbaits.md).

## Reach for this when

- Fishing local coastal walls or kelp at night specifically for a trophy
  calico or sand bass rather than a high-count day.
- Fishing a harbor/bay at night, where artificial shoreline lighting
  concentrates both bait and bass in the shallows.
- Running a crankbait on riprap or the rock sections of a wall at night, as
  distinct from open kelp, which this program skips for night crank work.

## Color: silhouette by default, translucent on brighter nights

- **Night color is about silhouette, not exact color-matching.** The fish
  read shape and contrast against the surface more than precise coloring, so
  a clean silhouette matters more than the exact shade (`XyKF34C5iB4`).
- **Creature/craw palette, aimed at trophy fish:** browns, oranges, some
  greens and reds, imitating spider crabs and lobster. Named combos: **black
  and blue craw, black and red craw, black and chartreuse,** and **purple**
  (`XyKF34C5iB4`).
- **Straight black / "motor oil" black** is a second staple night
  silhouette, fished alongside the creature-craw colors above — roughly half
  of one night box is straight black jigs (`XyKF34C5iB4`).
- **On brighter nights, switch to translucent.** A baitfish-profile program
  (a 5–7 in bait rather than craw baits) still defaults to a **dark
  presentation (black, or purple with flake)** — but on nights with more
  moon/ambient light it switches instead to a bait with a **clear,
  see-through body and reflective flake (silver or blue)**: more ambient
  light makes the fish more visual, and a translucent profile reads better
  than a solid dark silhouette on those nights. Dark silhouette and
  clear/flake sit at opposite ends of one spectrum, picked by how much light
  is actually on the water that night — not a fixed rule (`XyKF34C5iB4`).
- **Daytime iron colors, same session, for contrast:** brown, orange, and
  mint — consistent with the mint/brown-orange color data already logged in
  [surface iron — colour](surface-iron-color.md) (`XyKF34C5iB4`).

## Vibration over flash after dark

- **A subtle, tight-tail vibration beats a big paddle-tail thump.** Fishing
  spotted bay bass after dark, run a bait that's "almost like it's not even
  kicking, just barely" rather than one with a big thump: at night fish key
  more on **lateral-line feel** than sight, so a quiet, consistent vibration
  reads better than visual flash (`lP6cg4eEU6s`).
- **Flash earns its keep in daylight instead.** Blade/flash baits produce
  more as the water warms and more bait shows up in the bay — daytime,
  warm-season fishing, rather than a year-round or night-specific pick. This
  is a season/light qualifier on the silhouette-by-default color doctrine
  above (`lP6cg4eEU6s`).

## Booty spin — a night search bait built to be seen top to bottom

A **Kicker "booty spin"** swimbait (a paddle-tail-style swimbait with a small
blade in place of a plain tail), rigged on a **1 oz head**, run in a
**black-back / clear-bottom / red-cheek** pattern meant to read as an
anchovy silhouette. Fished on the 1 oz head it covers the **whole water
column, top to bottom**, in one bait — a strong pick when bait fish are
stacked up against a wall at night (`XyKF34C5iB4`). See
[soft-plastic swimbaits](../lures/soft-plastic-swimbaits.md) for the class
this bait sits in.

## Structure: fish the lit water in a harbor at night

- Artificial light near the water — dock/parking-lot lighting, warehouse
  lights, the big spreader lights on the oil-terminal islands at the Port of
  Long Beach — pulls bait and bass up into the shallows at night: the fish
  feel comfortable exposing themselves to feed where they can see and be
  seen, in a way they won't in the dark. Reaction baits (crankbait/hard
  bait) work better in these lit zones specifically because the fish can see
  a moving bait there, versus blind-working unlit stretches of the same
  harbor (`XyKF34C5iB4`).
- **Pre-scout the lit zones before the trip.** Mark where the light posts sit
  along an inner harbor on a mapping app (Google Maps) ahead of time, then
  run a deliberate pattern that hits each marked lit stretch on the water: a
  few casts, move to the next light if nothing bites, and flip a jig to fill
  the gaps in between stops — rather than blind-working the harbor start to
  finish (`XyKF34C5iB4`).
- **Cover water; don't grind one spot.** For night crank work generally,
  make two or three casts per zone, then push forward (trolling-motor pace)
  to the next zone rather than working one stretch exhaustively
  (`XyKF34C5iB4`).

## Crankbait choice for kelp at night

Night crank work skips open kelp; on riprap or the rock parts of a wall
inside the kelp, switch to a **deeper-diving square-bill ("coffin-style"
bill)** rather than a longer-lipped diver — the coffin bill comes through
kelp cleaner than a long lip when fished right up tight and parallel to the
edge. Work it in roughly **8–10 ft** of water, on a rod with more
**parabolic (softer through the blank)** bend than a daytime crank rod
(`XyKF34C5iB4`). See [inshore crankbaits](inshore-crankbaits.md) for the
general crank program this sits alongside.

## Heavy leader for trophy night fishing

Fishing heavy is a shared philosophy for targeting trophy fish rather than
volume (`XyKF34C5iB4`):

- **80 lb braid / 50–60 lb fluorocarbon leader.**
- **65 lb braid / 50 lb fluorocarbon leader**, on the same night program.
- **The reason is abrasion, not stealth.** Bass "really don't care about
  line, color" — the heavy leader isn't there to avoid spooking fish. A big
  fish hooked around rocks, kelp, or boilers at night buries itself in the
  structure, and the leader has to survive that; even **50 lb** is light for
  a calico that can reach 10 lb fished around structure. On lighter line
  (15–20 lb) a big fish might get landed by luck, but pulling hard enough by
  hand to horse it out of the rocks parts the line (`XyKF34C5iB4`).
- **The goal is one trophy fish, not volume** — fishing heavy costs bites,
  and that cost is the accepted trade for targeting trophy fish over numbers
  (`XyKF34C5iB4`).

## Gear class

The same **heavy bass-casting class** tackle documented in
[calico bass — gear summary](../species/calico-bass.md#gear-summary-class-terms) —
nothing about the rod/reel class itself is night-specific; what changes
after dark is leader weight (step up, see above) and lure choice
(silhouette/craw colors, the booty spin, and the coffin-bill crank above),
plus targeting lit structure inside a harbor.

## Common failures

- Blind-working a harbor stretch start to finish instead of running the lit
  zones — bass expose themselves to feed under artificial light at night but
  stay tucked away in the unlit water nearby, so hitting the lit stretches
  specifically produces more than working the whole shoreline evenly
  (`XyKF34C5iB4`).
- Running a big-thump, flashy paddle-tail after dark — night bass key on
  lateral-line feel more than sight, so a loud thump reads worse than a
  subtle, barely-kicking presentation (`lP6cg4eEU6s`).
- Fishing a long-lip diving crank tight against kelp — it fouls; a
  deeper-diving coffin-style square-bill clears the same water cleanly
  (`XyKF34C5iB4`).
- Undergunning the leader around night structure — light line (15–20 lb)
  might land a fish by luck, but a big bass that reaches the rocks parts it;
  even 50 lb fluoro is light for a 10 lb-class calico around structure
  (`XyKF34C5iB4`).
