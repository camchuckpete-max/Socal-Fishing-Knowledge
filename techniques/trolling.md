# Trolling

General offshore trolling execution — how to run a spread, where each lure
lives in it, and how to pair a rod to a lure. This note owns the **universal
mechanics**; per-lure speeds, setbacks and top-shot specs live in each lure
note, and live-bait slow-trolling is a distinct technique with its own note:
[slow-trolling live bait](slow-trolling-bait.md). WHEN to pull which lure by
species and conditions is the species router:
[bluefin trolling](../species/bluefin-tuna-trolling.md).

## Reach for this when

- You are **covering water in transit** or searching a zone and want lures
  working while you look. See
  [bluefin trolling](../species/bluefin-tuna-trolling.md) for the species-level
  WHEN.
- You want lines out sweeping a bird/bait area (below).
- Fish aren't showing on the sounder or visually on the surface — a strike on
  a trolled lure marks fish that would otherwise go undetected. Troll less, or
  not at all, once schools are visible on the surface, or during a long drift
  (`8UAtGqEjDtU`). Standard heavy trolling outfit for this: **80 or 100 lb
  test** (`8UAtGqEjDtU`).
- You're working an area you don't know, it's between bites, or you have no
  sign and no recent report — trolling to locate is its own program, below
  under "Prospecting a zone."

## Running the spread — sweeps, not straight lines

- **Troll sweeping arcs around a bird/bait/surface sign** — don't straight-line
  away from it. Tuna move only low single-digit mph; at 12 knots a boat can be
  **4 miles away in 15 minutes**, leaving every fish it just marked. Zigzag big
  sweeps back and forth through the area instead (`HzE4FHHcvTk`).
- **Don't troll through the fleet's prop wash.** Lures work because they run
  **clean water** behind the boat; trolling through churned water defeats the
  point. Give other boats room and work your own clean lanes (`HzE4FHHcvTk`).
- **Sitting birds still mark the zone** — trolling past birds sitting on the
  water, and past hopscotching rafts, produces bites even when nothing is
  flying (`HzE4FHHcvTk`).
- **Working a located current break:** troll back and forth across it —
  inshore side, then offshore side — rather than a single straight pass; fish
  shift from one side of the break to the other with the bait, so stay locked
  onto the break instead of leaving it to search elsewhere (`dgauGbNxP84`).
  See [current diagnostics](../conditions/current-diagnostics.md#current-break-visual-id)
  for the visual ID of the break itself.

## Spread size and setback

- Number of lines is limited by rod holders and beam; a small boat without
  outriggers typically runs **2 to 3 lures**. Stagger the setbacks so lures
  track in clean water and don't tangle.
- The **furthest-back lure runs out of the elevated shotgun holder** (long,
  high). Deep-running divers go low and short in the gunnel holders; surface
  skirts and spreader bars go long out the elevated position.
- Per-lure running speed, setback distance and top-shot strength live in the
  lure notes — e.g. [Mad Mac](../lures/mad-mac.md),
  [spreader bar](../lures/spreader-bar.md),
  [cedar plug](../lures/cedar-plug.md),
  [DTX Minnow](../lures/dtx-minnow.md).

## Rod-tip elevation rule

Rod-tip height directly sets a lure's running depth: **every ~3 ft of tip
elevation costs ~1 ft of running depth** (cameron). Consequences for holder
placement:

- **Divers want the low gunnel holders** — every bit of running depth matters,
  so keep the tip down.
- **Spreader bars and surface skirts want the elevated shotgun** — they work
  at or on the surface, and the raised tip lifts them up and keeps the bar
  riding on top.

## Rod-to-lure pairing framework

Four factors decide which rod goes behind which lure (cameron):

1. **Tow / drag load** — high-load lures need **heavy rods** (e.g. full-size
   Mad Macs at [speed-troll](speed-trolling.md) speeds).
2. **Swimmers whose action dies on heavy line** — a stiff, heavy setup kills
   the action of a light-swimming lure; match it to lighter line/rod.
3. **Lures needing a softer rod to absorb the strike** — a forgiving tip
   cushions the hit and keeps the hooks in.
4. **Line-agnostic lures** — the rod just matches the target species.

Class terms for each: [gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md).

## Spreader bars vs conventional

- Spreader bars imitate a whole school and are deadly, but they take **beam
  and cockpit room** to deploy and clear. Conventional single-lure trolling is
  the low-footprint alternative.
- On a boat with limited cockpit room (e.g. a panga), spreader bars are
  preferred over conventional gear because there isn't room to deploy
  conventional-style spreads (cameron) — not a claim that bars beat single
  lures generally; on a bigger cockpit both are on the table.

## Prospecting a zone — trolling to locate (SoCal coast)

Trolling to locate is a search tool, not a presentation choice — this fills
the long-standing flagged stub in [yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md) for
trolling-to-locate, distinct from trolling as the presentation
(`HTowqnwAMeA`).

- **The job is prospecting, not catching.** Trolling is the private boater's
  fastest way to cover and read a zone in three situations: **working an area
  you don't know**, **between bites**, or **no sign and no recent report**
  (`HTowqnwAMeA`).
- **Carry variety across three axes — colour, style/profile, and dive depth.**
  On a given day fish key on one specific presentation, one specific depth, or
  one specific vibration, and the default sardine-pattern ~20 ft diving plug
  isn't always it; a single-lure trolling box is the failure mode
  (`HTowqnwAMeA`).
- **Colour by light — and it runs opposite to intuition.** Overcast or
  low-light mornings call for **high-visibility** colours, because they stand
  out. Bright, clear blue days call for **darker** colours — blues, purples,
  sometimes reds — for contrast against bright water. Stated as a contrast
  principle, not a match-the-hatch one; it inverts the common "bright day,
  bright lure" habit (`HTowqnwAMeA`).
- **Gear splits at the ~40 ft diving plug.** 20–30 ft divers run fine on a
  [flyline](flyline.md) outfit — no second setup needed. 40 ft diving plugs
  call for a real troll outfit: a **shorter 6–7 ft rod**, a heavier **40-class
  reel**, **80 lb braid** (`HTowqnwAMeA`).
- **Keep the top shot short — 15–20 ft of 50–60 lb.** Two reasons pull the
  same direction: braid's zero stretch is what gives the rod tip its
  sensitivity and its read on what the lure is doing down there, and too much
  mono absorbs that; and a fish hooked mid-column is already close to kelp and
  rock, so shorter, heavier mono lets you put the wood to it and lift it away
  from structure immediately. Heavier line, shorter mono — the opposite of the
  long-top-shot habit that suits surface work (`HTowqnwAMeA`).
- The live-bait slow-troll is a different technique with a different purpose:
  [slow-trolling live bait](slow-trolling-bait.md).

## Watching the spread — marlin trolling off Catalina

- A marlin stop can be run **short and tight**: outriggers roughly **four to
  five wakes** back, flatlines run close to the boat (`3qSY328fFYo`).
- **Troll "downhill," with the sun behind the boat** — it's much easier to
  watch the jigs because you can see them clearly behind the boat. The
  trade-off: a fish that comes up **behind** the jigs, close to the boat,
  shows a good silhouette against the water and is easy to spot; a fish that
  shows up further out, toward the sun/prop-wash glare, is much harder to see
  (`3qSY328fFYo`).
- **Watching the jigs and prop wash continuously is the top non-driving job.**
  A fish can hit a jig without popping the outrigger release clip or the
  rubber-band line release — it can whack a jig and not trip anything — and a
  missed strike can easily be mistaken for a piece of kelp popping a line.
  Missing that moment can cost several fish in a day (`3qSY328fFYo`).
- **Run the console sounder to display down to ~120 ft** while under way to
  spot bait on the meter, cross-checked against visual sign — skittering bait
  schools, a bait cloud metered directly under the boat before the first drop,
  identified on camera that day as mackerel (`3qSY328fFYo`).
- A marlin seen jumping alone isn't necessarily about to bite; what matters is
  whether a jumper **turns into a feeder working bait** — those fish are
  "usually all bite" (`3qSY328fFYo`).
- Rule of thumb for jig-raised marlin: expect to convert roughly **one hookup
  for every five or six** fish raised/interested (`3qSY328fFYo`).

## Backside Catalina — porpoise-associated yellowfin finesse trolling

- **Lure — a scaled-down, tungsten-headed King Buster-style trolling lure.**
  The stock King Buster "finesse trolling" lure has a plastic head that can
  pop out of the water at speed; re-heading it with a **tungsten 1/2 oz head**
  keeps it running true, rigged with a small **Yamashita stainless double
  hook**. The idea traces to a roughly 10–12-years-prior (circa mid-2010s)
  Dana Point program trolling cheap, fragile King Busters around porpoise pods
  for yellowfin (`ouBrIdO7d4k`).
- **Line/leader and speed:** **60 or 80 lb** straight to the lure, at
  **7–9 mph** — "not very fast" (`ouBrIdO7d4k`).
- **Approach — stay off and out in front of the pod; never cut through it.**
  Get **well out in front** of a moving dolphin/porpoise school rather than
  running through or alongside it. Pods under bow pressure split or bolt once
  they hear/see the boat, scattering the bait they were pushing — the same
  rule applies to a trolled [Mad Mac](../lures/mad-mac.md) for bluefin
  (`ouBrIdO7d4k`).
- **Where yellowfin sit relative to the pod:** on the edges — up in front, on
  the flanks — not necessarily riding directly under it. When dolphin are
  actively working and the baitball starts breaking up, yellowfin "foam out"
  ahead of or beside the pod, sometimes visible **~50 yd in front** of the
  school. This sits beside, not in conflict with, the separate doctrine in
  [yellowfin tuna](../species/yellowfin-tuna.md#finding-them) that yellowfin
  typically suspend **underneath** a traveling dolphin pod — that doctrine
  describes the resting/traveling pattern, this describes the pod's behavior
  once it is actively feeding and breaking up bait (`ouBrIdO7d4k`).
- **Result:** 60–70 lb yellowfin landed on the dolphin using this method,
  late September/early October (lobster opener), backside of Catalina
  (`ouBrIdO7d4k`).
- The same small trolled-feather approach is a Hawaii skipjack staple — a
  labeled contrast, not SoCal doctrine (`ouBrIdO7d4k`).

See [Mad Mac](../lures/mad-mac.md) for the bluefin speed-troll lure referenced
above, and [tuna feathers and skirts](../lures/tuna-feathers-and-skirts.md)
for this finesse-trolling lure's own spec entry.

## Regional lure kits and rigs

### Sea of Cortez trolling kit

Ahead of casting for cabrilla or yo-yoing, a Sea of Cortez trolling kit:
**Nomad-class, Rapala-class, and Yo-Zuri-class** diving hardbaits, plus
**mirror lures** — a staple among anglers who troll the Sea of Cortez.
Micro spreader bars for dorado are a distinct, smaller class from the
bluefin-scale bars covered elsewhere — see the
[Baja dorado micro spreader bar section](../lures/spreader-bar.md#baja-sea-of-cortez-micro-spreader-bars-for-dorado).
Feathers aren't commonly trolled there; when they are, they run small
(`M8hOYQ_6rSg`).

### Cabo San Lucas — surf-line slow-troll for sierra

A distinct, inshore counterpart to the offshore spreads above: small hoochies
slow-trolled in the surf line for sierra.

- **Lures:** small hoochies/feathers, sized down because sierra key on smaller
  bait. White, orange and pink all work; white performed well in the surf. A
  quarter-ounce slider sinker rigs into the head of each hoochie
  (`ll7r4A6atno`).
- **Wire leader is mandatory** — sierra have razor-blade teeth, the same
  bite-off logic as
  [barracuda](../species/barracuda.md#situations--techniques). Single-strand
  wire is preferred; braided wire is the fallback when single-strand isn't
  available locally. See [haywire twist](../rigging/haywire-twist.md) for
  single-strand termination (`ll7r4A6atno`).
- **Hook config:** a single or double hook rigged at the back of the skirt — a
  per-color rigger's choice, not a hard rule (`ll7r4A6atno`).
- **Mechanics:** drag the hoochies along the surf line at roughly **6 knots**;
  rubber bands looped on the stern cleats keep the hoochies down in the water
  instead of skipping on the surface (`ll7r4A6atno`).
- **Bite window:** early morning or right at sunset (`ll7r4A6atno`).
- **Water-color cue:** dirtier, lime-green surf water improves the bite —
  sierra have sharp eyesight and get bite-shy in clear, calm water but feed
  more readily once the water turns off-color. This is a region-specific
  mechanism (predator eyesight), distinct from the SoCal chlorophyll-edge/
  bait-holding mechanism in [water color](../conditions/water-color.md) — kept
  separate, not merged (`ll7r4A6atno`).

### Cabo San Lucas marlin — lure-size selection and afternoon-chop rigging

Shop guidance (Melton Tackle, ahead of a Cabo charter) — attributed shop
advice, not asserted SoCal/Baja doctrine:

- **Lure-size logic is tournament-scoring-driven.** Modern tournament scoring
  can make a 400 lb fish worth as much as a 600-pounder, so there's no scoring
  benefit to running oversized heads. A medium-size head works across the
  whole size range — 300 to 600+ lb — rigged with a single, big hook to carry
  a large fish (`XH-Hrfet6To`).
- **Afternoon-chop rigging.** Cabo's offshore water is characteristically flat
  in the morning, then builds choppy through the afternoon as heat off the
  land mass drives wind. Pick lures that run clean in calm morning water but
  still "hang in there" once the chop and wind build; no specific lure model
  is a chop-specific pick — treat it as a selection criterion
  (`XH-Hrfet6To`).
- **Named lures:** **Marlin Magic Lures** (Hawaii) — a scoop-nose, jetted head
  that pulls water through to create a large bubble trail, touted with
  multiple Bisbee's tournament wins (a promotional claim from the shop selling
  it). **Koi/Koya Lures** (name not confidently resolved from audio) — another
  Hawaiian handmade line, new to this shop's Cabo kit at the time; its "861"
  model is named for the weight of the first blue marlin caught on it. Older
  lures some Cabo boats still run were also named on camera; only **Seven
  Strand** resolves confidently from the captions (`XH-Hrfet6To`).
- **Rigging support:** the shop's rigger, named "Tracy" on camera, rigs both
  tournament and recreational gear for this Cabo program; the rep frames
  hook-setup quality as important enough to risk a "money fish" in
  tournament play. No rig diagram or step-by-step is given on camera — link
  the shop reference only, no rigging note created from this thin a
  description (`XH-Hrfet6To`).

A blue-marlin bite-but-lost data point from the same trip is in the

### Magdalena Bay — dead-bait rigging

A local marlin-lure-company owner/guide's dead-bait program at Magdalena Bay:
tie a dead bait's mouth closed before slow-trolling it, so it doesn't take on
water through an open mouth — this keeps it streamlined and swimming better
(`4t_Z75shK_E`). The same kit included a wahoo transit lure rigged with a
magnet holding the hook against the lure body so it "runs true" at speeds up
to **20 kt**, letting it stay in the water at transit speed between grounds so
a wahoo can be picked up incidentally without slowing the boat for a dedicated
spread — the lure-company owner describing his own product's performance on
his own trip, so treat as a promotional claim (`4t_Z75shK_E`).

## Drill-powered crank — resetting a long-set-back line

Running lures far back (see the [Mad Mac](../lures/mad-mac.md) setback note)
means a long, slow hand wind-in every time a line picks up kelp or a hook
pulls mid-fight. A drill-powered reel-crank attachment (branded on camera as
the "Makaira Rapid Crank") chucks into a cordless drill and spins the reel
handle directly, cutting the wind-in time way down — corroborated across
three separate SoCal/Baja crews (`xzIaUEDklrE`, `U1AgwmlY5bI`,
`elBPRrdkugU`).

- The tool is built around the **50-Wide reel** specifically, on the reasoning
  that "the most common reel is the 50 wide" (`U1AgwmlY5bI`).
- **Keep the drill's spare batteries charged** — a long wind-in by hand can
  otherwise take up to **10 minutes** (`U1AgwmlY5bI`).
- **Run the drill on its low speed setting, not high** — high speed gets "out
  of control" and puts extra tension on the reel itself (`elBPRrdkugU`).
- A trolled line can pick up kelp unnoticed for an extended stretch before
  anyone checks it — watch lines continuously, not just at hookup
  (`U1AgwmlY5bI`).

## Gear class

- [Trolling / spreader-bar class](../tackle/gear-classes.md) — heavy
  two-speed conventional, 150–200 lb, for high tow-load lures (full-size Mad
  Macs, spreader bars) and marlin/tuna programs.
- [Diver-troll / American-series class](../tackle/gear-classes.md) —
  single-speed lever-drag, 30–80 lb, for diver plugs and school-grade
  cedar/skirt work.
- The [jig-stick class](../tackle/gear-classes.md) doubles as a light troll
  outfit for 20–30 ft divers on the flyline setup; 40 ft divers need the
  heavier troll-class rod above (`HTowqnwAMeA`).

## Common failures

- **Straight-lining away** from sign you just marked (sweep instead).
- **Trolling through prop wash**, so no lure is in clean water.
- Putting a **diver in the elevated holder** (loses running depth) or a **bar
  in a low holder** (drags it under) — invert per the rod-tip rule.
- A **rod that fights the lure** — too heavy for a swimmer, too stiff to
  absorb a strike (pairing framework).
- A **trolled line picking up kelp unnoticed** for an extended stretch — watch
  lines continuously, not just at hookup (`U1AgwmlY5bI`).
