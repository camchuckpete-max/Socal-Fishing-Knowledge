# Trolling

General offshore trolling execution — how to run a spread, where each lure lives
in it, and how to pair a rod to a lure. This note owns the **universal
mechanics**; the WHEN-to-pull-what-by-conditions decisions are the router:
[bluefin trolling](../species/bluefin-trolling.md). Per-lure speeds, setbacks and
top-shot specs live in each lure note.

## Reach for this when

- You are **covering water in transit** or searching a zone, and want lures
  working while you look. See [bluefin trolling](../species/bluefin-trolling.md).
- You want lines out sweeping a bird/bait area (below).

## Running the spread — sweeps, not straight lines

- **Troll sweeping arcs around the bird, bait and surface sign** — do not
  straight-line away from it. Tuna move only low single-digit miles per hour; at
  12 knots you can be **4 miles away in 15 minutes**, leaving every fish you just
  saw. Zigzag big sweeps back and forth through the area instead (8/17/22).
- **Don't troll through the fleet's prop wash.** Lures work because they're
  running **clean water** behind the boat; trolling through churned water defeats
  the point. Give other boats room and work your own clean lanes (8/17/22).
- **Sitting birds still mark the zone** — trolling past birds sitting on the
  water, and past hopscotching rafts, produces bites when nothing is flying
  (8/17/22).
- **Observed** (Capt. Dave Hansen, Your Saltwater Guide, 2021-01-28, Cabo San
  Lucas, Baja — presenter not named on camera, inferred from channel
  self-references and the registry's Cabo-charter continuity): once a current
  break is located, work it by trolling **back and forth across it** (inshore
  side, then offshore side) instead of a single straight pass — fish shifted
  from one side of the break to the other with the bait, and the advice given
  on camera was to stay locked onto the break rather than leave it to search
  elsewhere. See
  [current diagnostics](../conditions/current-diagnostics.md#current-break-visual-id)
  for the visual ID of the break itself.

## Spread size and setback

- Number of lines is limited by rod holders and beam; a small boat without
  outriggers typically runs **2 to 3 lures**. Stagger the setbacks so lures track
  in clean water and don't tangle.
- The **furthest-back lure runs out of the elevated shotgun holder** (long,
  high). Deep-running divers go low and short in the gunnel holders; surface
  skirts and spreader bars go long out the elevated position.
- Per-lure running speed, setback distance and top-shot strength live in the lure
  notes — e.g. [Mad Mac](../lures/mad-mac.md),
  [spreader bar](../lures/spreader-bar.md),
  [cedar plug](../lures/cedar-plug.md),
  [DTX Minnow](../lures/dtx-minnow.md).

## Rod-tip elevation rule (universal doctrine, Cameron)

Rod-tip height directly sets a lure's running depth: **every ~3 ft of tip
elevation costs ~1 ft of running depth.** Consequences for holder placement:

- **Divers want the low gunnel holders** — you want every bit of running depth,
  so keep the tip down.
- **Spreader bars and surface skirts want the elevated shotgun** — they work at
  or on the surface, and the raised tip lifts them up and keeps the bar riding on
  top.

## Rod-to-lure pairing framework (universal doctrine, Cameron)

Four factors decide which rod goes behind which lure:

1. **Tow / drag load** — high-load lures need **heavy rods** (e.g. full-size
   Mad Macs at [speed-troll](speed-trolling.md) speeds).
2. **Swimmers whose action dies on heavy line** — a stiff, heavy setup kills the
   action of a light-swimming lure; match it to lighter line/rod.
3. **Lures needing a softer rod to absorb the strike** — a forgiving tip cushions
   the hit and keeps the hooks in.
4. **Line-agnostic lures** — where the lure doesn't care about line, so the rod
   just **matches the target species**.

Class terms for each: [gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md).

## Spreader bars vs conventional

- Spreader bars imitate a whole school and are deadly, but they take **beam and
  cockpit room** to deploy and clear. Conventional single-lure trolling is the
  low-footprint alternative.
- **Cameron runs bars over conventional because there isn't room on the panga**
  for conventional bar setups — a platform constraint of his boat (see his
  [boat](../profiles/cameron/boat.md)), not a rule that bars beat single lures.
  On a bigger cockpit both are on the table.

## Cabo San Lucas — surf-line slow-trolling for sierra

**Capt. Dave Hansen, Your Saltwater Guide (`ll7r4A6atno`, 2021-01-19);
dave-hansen registered voice, single mention of this rig — `medium`
confidence; region: Cabo San Lucas, Baja.** A distinct, inshore counterpart to
the offshore spreads above: small hoochies slow-trolled in the surf line for
**sierra** (not a dorado technique despite the video's title — a dorado was
an incidental catch on the same gear, logged in
[dorado](../species/dorado.md)).

- **Lures:** small hoochies/feathers — sized down because "the sierras are
  keyed in on the smaller bait." White, orange, and pink all named as working
  colors; white worked well in the surf on this trip. A small quarter-ounce
  slider sinker is rigged into the head of each hoochie.
- **Wire leader, mandatory:** sierra have "razor blade teeth," described as
  "like little baby wahoos" — a short piece of wire ahead of the hoochie is
  non-negotiable, the same bite-off logic as
  [barracuda](../species/barracuda.md#situations--techniques). **Single-strand
  wire preferred; braided wire is the fallback** when single-strand isn't
  available locally (the case on this trip) — see
  [haywire twist](../rigging/haywire-twist.md) for single-strand termination.
- **Hook config:** a single hook or a double hook rigged right at the back end
  of the skirt; on this trip the white hoochies ran a single hook, the
  orange/pink ones a double hook — a per-color choice made by the rigger, not
  a stated hard rule.
- **Trolling mechanics:** drag the hoochies along the surf line at roughly
  **6 knots**; rubber bands looped on the boat's stern cleats keep the
  hoochies down in the water (rather than skipping on the surface).
- **Bite window:** sierra bite best **early morning or right at sunset**.
- **Water-color cue:** dirtier, lime-green surf water improves the sierra
  bite — sierra have sharp eyesight and get bite-shy in clear, calm water but
  feed more readily once the water turns off-color. Region-specific to this
  Cabo surf-line pattern; not the SoCal chlorophyll-edge doctrine in
  [water color](../conditions/water-color.md), which runs on a different
  mechanism (bait-holding, not predator eyesight) — kept separate, not merged.

## Common failures

- **Straight-lining away** from sign you just marked (sweep instead).
- **Trolling through prop wash**, so no lure is in clean water.
- Putting a **diver in the elevated holder** (loses running depth) or a **bar in
  a low holder** (drags it under) — invert per the rod-tip rule.
- A **rod that fights the lure** — too heavy for a swimmer, too stiff to absorb a
  strike (pairing framework).
