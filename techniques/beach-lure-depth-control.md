# Beach Depth Control — Reading Bottom by Rod-Tip Height

A single lever for controlling how deep a lure runs while beach/surf casting,
without swapping lures: the **angle and height of the rod tip during the
retrieve**. Tackle Express (`QJd7A6oiV4U`, 2026-05-09, a 50-second clip;
unregistered channel, single mention — medium confidence). Stated to hold
**"across the board"** regardless of lure type — plastic on a lead head,
metal lure, or any minnow-style hardbait.

## Reach for this when

- Beach/surf casting any lure type and you need to hold it off, or push it
  deeper into, the water column without changing lures.
- The bottom changes shape or texture across the retrieve (a sandbar into a
  trough, or a patch of harder structure) and you need to adjust depth on
  the fly rather than swap lures mid-retrieve.
- You want to keep a lure from digging into, or hanging up on, a bottom
  feature you can feel or see coming — his named example is a **gravelly
  shell bar** that "gets hung up" and can cost you the lure.

## Mechanics

- **Rod tip down** (low, toward the water) → the lure runs at its
  **maximum depth**.
- **Rod tip at mid-height** → **moderates** the depth, holding the lure
  **a little higher** in the column than tip-down.
- **Rod tip all the way up** (high) → keeps the lure from running into the
  bottom at all — the shallowest of the three positions.
- Applies the same way across a **plastic lure on a lead head, a metal
  lure, and any minnow-style (hardbait) lure** — the mechanism is rod
  angle, not lure design.
- Framed as something read by feel and by eye on a given beach: different
  beaches have different bottom shapes and textures, and reading that lets
  you decide in the moment — his stated example: "I don't want my lure
  running into that gravelly shell bar."
- Stated payoff: controlling depth this way "will increase your catches."
