# Searcher Daytime Dart-Jig Outfit Ladder (Steve Carson, Penn/SearcherSportfishing, 2023-03-30)

Daytime dart-jig fishing is the daylight counterpart to nighttime giant-tuna
jig fishing: night jigs run roughly a **400 g jig on 250 lb leader** with big
hooks, because in the dark tuna can't inspect the gear closely, while daytime
tuna see the presentation clearly and demand a smaller, more realistic jig
(`pCd6QykcZ0w`). On the Searcher, daytime dart jigs catch school-grade tuna as
small as **15, 20, 25 lb**, occasionally up to **50 lb** — and a school of
20-pounders can also produce a **60, 70, 80 lb-plus** fish on the same small
jig, "elephants eat peanuts" (`pCd6QykcZ0w`). Catch isn't limited to
bluefin — **yellowfin, yellowtail**, and occasionally an
[opah](../species/opah.md) also take these jigs (`pCd6QykcZ0w`). This note (2023-03-30) is the earlier of two citations of this outfit; a
later, independently corroborating citation appears in [Searcher big/giant
tuna rig ladder — Dart/Colt Sniper finesse jig
rig](searcher-big-tuna-rig-ladder.md#dartcolt-sniper-finesse-jig-rig--daytime-tuna-steve-carson-pennsearchersportfishing-2024-06-13),
dated 2024-06-13. This note alone covers a second, heavier daytime tier not
in that later citation.

## Lure — dart jig, sized by application

The most popular daytime lure type is the **dart jig**, sold under many brand
names — see [iron jigs](../lures/iron-jigs.md) for the shared Dart/Coltsniper-
class documentation. Size steps up with target grade, bait size, and depth:

- **~50 g** — the smallest size carried; tuna over 50 lb have been caught on
  it (`pCd6QykcZ0w`).
- **~80 g** — the second-favorite size; most effective when tuna feed on
  small bait (`pCd6QykcZ0w`).
- **~100 g** — the general prospecting size absent a specific bait match —
  the **Williamson Gomame** (product/model pick, `low` confidence per the
  `searcher-sportfishing` sponsored-claim caveat, independent of this note's
  `medium` mechanism confidence). Its pronounced S-curve swim draws bites;
  heavy bite-mark wear on the jig corroborates this on camera
  (`pCd6QykcZ0w`).
- **~120 g and ~170 g** — the **Williamson Kentucky Jig** (same product-pick
  caveat), stepped up when fish run bigger even in daytime or sit deeper; no
  separate size/depth trigger is given beyond that (`pCd6QykcZ0w`). 170 g is
  a heavier ceiling for this daytime dart-jig class than the ~125 g maximum
  noted in [iron jigs — hook
  style](../lures/iron-jigs.md#hook-style--assist-vs-treble).

The jig has to be light enough to sink, since daytime tuna can hold at **100,
150, even 200 ft** down, per the boat's sounder reads (`pCd6QykcZ0w`) —
deeper than the 80/100/150 ft range from an earlier citation on the same
channel (`m2g97MxmAGI`) noted in [dart jig — daytime finesse
tuna](../techniques/dart-jig-tuna.md#sizing-color-and-depth).

## Light rig — standard daytime dart-jig outfit

- **Reel:** Penn Fathom II 30, two-speed (`pCd6QykcZ0w`).
- **Line:** reel filled three-quarters full with **65 lb braid**, topped with
  a **50 yd top shot of 40 or 50 lb test** monofilament (`pCd6QykcZ0w`).
- **Rod:** **8 ft**, rated **30–60 lb test** — a "Carnage Three" West Coast
  rod (brand/model `asr-uncertain`; the caption string does not resolve
  cleanly to a known West Coast rod builder). Fish the middle of the rod's
  rated line class, not the ends — the same rule as [Searcher big/giant tuna
  rig
  ladder](searcher-big-tuna-rig-ladder.md#general-rod-principle-across-all-three-rigs--fish-the-middle-of-the-rating)
  (`pCd6QykcZ0w`).
- **Leader — toothy 60–90 lb-plus tuna:** splice on a **4 ft length of 80 lb
  fluorocarbon** once fish start showing teeth at that size; a bigger tuna
  often swallows the jig all the way down, and the heavier leader resists
  being chewed through (`pCd6QykcZ0w`) — the same leader used in the later,
  corroborating [Searcher big/giant tuna rig
  ladder](searcher-big-tuna-rig-ladder.md#dartcolt-sniper-finesse-jig-rig--daytime-tuna-steve-carson-pennsearchersportfishing-2024-06-13)
  citation.

## Heavy rig — bigger or deeper daytime fish (100 lb-plus)

For daytime tuna running deeper or bigger — "catching 100-pounders during the
daytime, it does happen" (`pCd6QykcZ0w`):

- **Reel:** Penn Fathom 40 Narrow (`pCd6QykcZ0w`).
- **Rod:** **6.5–7 ft**, rated **30–80 lb test** (`pCd6QykcZ0w`).
- **Line:** reel filled three-quarters full with **80 lb braid**, topped with
  a **50 yd top shot of 60 lb monofilament** (`pCd6QykcZ0w`).
- **Bump to an 80 lb top shot once fish run over 100 lb** — the boat's
  captain ("Arbor Mike" per the auto-captions, `asr-uncertain` spelling)
  polices the swap on the rail (`pCd6QykcZ0w`).

## Hooks

Stock hardware (rings and hooks) shipped on these dart jigs is sized for
**5–20 lb** fish, undersized for Searcher-grade fish; the fix is to upgrade
to **at least a 5X-strong treble hook** (`pCd6QykcZ0w`) — a different
strength number than the 4X-strength Owner ST-66 pick documented for the same
stock-hooks-too-light problem in [iron jigs — hook
style](../lures/iron-jigs.md#hook-style--assist-vs-treble), not reconciled
between the two citations.

**Treble vs. single hook, by lure style:** for nighttime giant-tuna jigs (the
400 g class), big single hooks are preferred, sometimes a couple of them. For
the daytime dart-type jig, a heavy treble hook is preferred instead, after
"experimenting with every style of hook you can imagine" (`pCd6QykcZ0w`).
Trebles cost occasional pulled fish, but this lure style "gets a lot more
bites with a treble hook on it: can't exactly say why, but I can definitively
say that's what happens" (`pCd6QykcZ0w`). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source:
mechanism (no explanation is given for why a treble outperforms a single
hook on this specific lure style).
