# Searcher Daytime Dart-Jig Outfit Ladder (Steve Carson, Penn/SearcherSportfishing, 2023-03-30)

Steve Carson, aboard the **Searcher** (`pCd6QykcZ0w`, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol.
217 (Daytime Tuna Lures and Outfits)," uploaded 2023-03-30;
`searcher-sportfishing` registered channel — medium confidence for this
video's own parameters). An **earlier** (by about 15 months), independently
corroborating citation of the same dart-jig outfit already logged in
[Searcher big/giant tuna rig ladder — Dart/Colt Sniper finesse jig
rig](searcher-big-tuna-rig-ladder.md#dartcolt-sniper-finesse-jig-rig--daytime-tuna-steve-carson-pennsearchersportfishing-2024-06-13)
(`TU5quAG4atM`, 2024-06-13), kept as its own note per this repo's size
guideline rather than folded into that already-long ladder, and adding a
**second, heavier daytime tier** not covered in that entry.

Framed on camera as the daytime counterpart to nighttime giant-tuna jig
fishing — Carson's own contrast: nighttime jigs run roughly a **400 g jig on
250 lb leader** with big hooks, a rig he says he'll "cover in another video"
(not detailed here), because fish can't get a good look at the gear in the
dark; daytime demands a smaller, more realistic presentation since the fish
can see everything. Daytime dart-jig fishing on the Searcher catches
school-grade tuna as small as **15, 20, 25 lb**, occasionally up to **50 lb**,
but a school of 20-pounders can also produce a **60, 70, 80 lb-plus** fish on
the same small jig ("elephants eat peanuts"). Not always bluefin — also
**yellowfin, yellowtail**, and, per Carson ("knock on wood"), occasionally an
**opah**.

## Lure — dart jig, sized by application

The most popular daytime lure type is the **dart jig**, sold under many brand
names (see [iron jigs](../lures/iron-jigs.md) for the shared "Dart"/Coltsniper-
class documentation):

- **~50 g** — the smallest size Carson carries; he states he's personally
  caught tuna **over 50 lb** on it.
- **~80 g** — his stated second-favorite size; particularly effective when
  tuna are feeding on **small bait**.
- **~100 g** — his general prospecting size when not matching a specific bait
  size; this is the **Williamson Gomame**, his named favorite (a
  product/model pick — `low` confidence per the `searcher-sportfishing`
  registry's sponsored-claim caveat, though it repeats his named favorite
  already logged in [Searcher 40 lb all-around tuna
  outfit](searcher-40lb-all-around-tuna-outfit.md#also-fishes-jigs--a-dart-type-jig-aside)).
  Its selling point, per Carson: a light jig with a pronounced **S-curve
  swim** that draws bites — evidenced on camera by heavy bite-mark wear on
  his own jig.
- **~120 g and ~170 g** — the **Williamson Kentucky Jig** (product/model pick,
  `low` confidence, same caveat), stepped up to "when the fish are a little
  bit bigger even in the daytime or they're a little bit deeper" — Carson
  does not restate a number for this trigger (distinct from the 60–90 lb
  toothy-fish leader swap above). **A new upper-weight data point
  for this jig class:** extends the "roughly 50 g, 80 g, 100 g, maybe 125 g at
  the most" daytime sizing ceiling already logged in [iron jigs — Owner ST-66
  swap](../lures/iron-jigs.md#hook-style--assist-vs-treble) (from the later
  `TU5quAG4atM`, 2024-06-13) — kept side by side, not merged, since this
  earlier (2023-03-30) citation from the same speaker names a heavier 170 g
  jig fished in the same daytime dart-jig class.

**Depth capability drives the size choice:** the jig has to be light enough to
sink, because daytime tuna can hold at **100, 150, even 200 ft** down (Carson
citing Captain Mike's sounder reads) — a further, deeper data point beside
the **80/100/150 ft** depth citation already logged in [dart jig — daytime
finesse tuna](../techniques/dart-jig-tuna.md#sizing-color-and-depth--an-earlier-data-point)
from the same channel's earlier `m2g97MxmAGI` clip.

## Light rig — standard daytime dart-jig outfit

- **Reel:** **Penn Fathom II 30**, two-speed.
- **Line:** reel filled **three-quarters full** with **65 lb braid**, topped
  with a **50 yd top shot of 40 or 50 lb test** monofilament.
- **Rod:** **8 ft**, rated **30–60 lb test** — a **"Carnage Three" West Coast
  rod** (brand/model **asr-uncertain**; the caption string does not resolve
  cleanly to a known West Coast rod builder — not carried as a confirmed
  product name). Carson restates his **fish the middle of the rod's line
  rating** rule here — a further citation of the same rule already logged at
  `high` confidence (repeated doctrine, registered channel) in [Searcher
  big/giant tuna rig
  ladder](searcher-big-tuna-rig-ladder.md#general-rod-principle-across-all-three-rigs--fish-the-middle-of-the-rating).
- **Leader — toothy 60–90 lb-plus tuna:** splice on a **4 ft length of 80 lb
  fluorocarbon** to the standard rig above once fish start showing teeth at
  that size — stated reasoning: a bigger tuna often swallows the jig all the
  way down, and the heavier leader helps prevent it chewing through. A close
  match to, and an earlier (by about 15 months) independent citation of, the
  **3–4 ft of 80 lb fluorocarbon** dart-jig leader already logged in
  [Searcher big/giant tuna rig
  ladder](searcher-big-tuna-rig-ladder.md#dartcolt-sniper-finesse-jig-rig--daytime-tuna-steve-carson-pennsearchersportfishing-2024-06-13).

## Heavy rig — bigger or deeper daytime fish (100 lb-plus)

For daytime tuna running deeper or bigger — "catching 100-pounders during the
daytime, it does happen":

- **Reel:** **Penn Fathom 40 Narrow**.
- **Rod:** **6.5–7 ft**, rated **30–80 lb test**.
- **Line:** reel filled **three-quarters full** with **80 lb braid**, topped
  with a **50 yd top shot of 60 lb monofilament**.
- **Bump to an 80 lb top shot once fish run over 100 lb** — per Carson, the
  boat's captain ("Arbor Mike" per the auto-captions — asr-uncertain spelling
  of the nickname) polices this on the rail, and Carson describes stepping up
  from 60 to 80 lb test specifically once the grade crosses 100 lb.

## Hooks

Stock hardware (rings and hooks) shipped on these dart jigs is undersized for
Searcher-grade fish — sized for **5–20 lb** fish, not the bigger grade the
boat routinely hooks. Carson's fix: upgrade to **at least a 5X-strong treble
hook** — a further, independently-stated hook-strength data point beside the
**4X-strength Owner ST-66** pick already logged in [iron jigs — hook
style](../lures/iron-jigs.md#hook-style--assist-vs-treble), not reconciled
with it (a different strength rating, on the same underlying "stock hooks
aren't tuna-rated" problem).

**Treble vs. single hook, by lure style — stated reasoning:** for the
nighttime giant-tuna jigs (the 400 g class named in this video's opening
contrast), Carson prefers **big single hooks**, sometimes a couple of them.
For the daytime dart-type jig covered in this video, he prefers a **heavy
treble hook** instead, after having "experimented with every style of hook
you can imagine." He is explicit that trebles cost him occasional pulled
fish, but states — without a mechanism he can explain — that this specific
lure style "gets a lot more bites with a treble hook on it": *"can't exactly
say why, but I can definitively say that's what happens."*
