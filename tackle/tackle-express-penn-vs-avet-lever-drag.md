# Penn vs. Avet Lever Drag — Feature Comparison for 2-Speed Reels (Tackle Express)

A shop-counter comparison of two 2-speed lever-drag reels' physical lever and
drag-curve controls — Penn's own lever drag against an Avet Raptor Series
two-speed, identified on camera as a "JX Raptor" (`d0fxBYmAnIk`). A fuller,
earlier clip from the same channel covers the identical two reels in more
depth and supplies the model numbers and cost detail the short clip omits;
the lever-feel, strike-button, and drag-curve/preset points repeat
near-verbatim across both, read as the same underlying footage re-cut rather
than independent confirmation (`z5UoW9pntvE`; two companion clips track the
same source — see [star drag vs. lever drag](star-drag-vs-lever-drag.md) and
[two-speed reel technique](../techniques/two-speed-low-gear-fight.md)). The
reels: a **Penn Fathom two-speed 30** (distinct from Penn's star-drag Fathom)
against an **Avet Raptor (JX) two-speed** — Avet also sells a less-featured
**G2** version at the same size (`z5UoW9pntvE`). Per the confidence rubric,
`tackle-express` being an unregistered channel caps this note at medium.

## Bearings

- **Penn Fathom two-speed 30: 5 bearings. Avet Raptor (JX) two-speed: 8
  bearings.** Bearings support the gears, spool, and other moving parts —
  more bearings let those parts mesh together more precisely, so under load
  a well-supported gear train is less likely to twist, bind, or fail to
  engage cleanly with each gear (`z5UoW9pntvE`).

## Free spool vs. cast control — opposite trade-offs

- **Penn Fathom: strong free spool, no cast-control dial.** Casting it well
  depends on thumb control to avoid a backlash — a proficient angler gets a
  longer cast, but it "can cause problems" for someone new to thumbing a
  conventional reel (`z5UoW9pntvE`).
- **Avet Raptor: weaker free spool, but a cast-control dial.** The dial runs
  **1 through 5**, adding or easing drag on the spool during the cast via a
  magnet that moves closer to or farther from the spool — set at 2–3 it gives
  light help, set to 5 it gives "maximum" braking, useful for an angler not
  yet proficient at thumbing a cast (`z5UoW9pntvE`).

## Handle

- **Penn Fathom: a harder-feeling handle knob** with less grip than the
  Avet's, but no wear issues — it runs through the shop's line-spooling
  machine without denting or leaving indentations (`z5UoW9pntvE`).
- **Avet Raptor: a softer-touch handle knob**, preferred for grip with wet or
  slimy hands, but soft enough that careless line-spooling alignment can
  leave a wear mark on it. Its handle arm also runs a little longer than the
  Penn's — a difference that doesn't show up while actually fighting a fish
  (`z5UoW9pntvE`).
- Both handles run about the same overall size, with "a good amount of grip"
  on either (`z5UoW9pntvE`).

## Drag washer construction (Avet Raptor)

- The Raptor's drag washer is glued and notched onto the spool so it can't
  move under pressure — the stated mechanism behind the Raptor's higher drag
  capacity. Its side plate is also thicker than the G2's, to fit a thicker
  drag washer (`z5UoW9pntvE`).
- Stated payoff: "you can get a ton of drag in a very small reel" — spooled
  with braided line, filled or half-filled, that construction is stated to
  land a **150 lb tuna on a JX [Raptor two-speed]** reel (`z5UoW9pntvE`).

## Cost, manufacturing origin, and color/handedness options

- **Penn Fathom: made overseas, lower cost** — the pick for a
  budget-conscious angler who still wants a quality reel (`z5UoW9pntvE`).
- **Avet: costs more**, read as **USA-made** production driving the price gap
  (`z5UoW9pntvE`). ⚠ asr-uncertain: the caption renders the word as "usma" —
  the context and price contrast both point to a USA-vs-overseas distinction,
  but the word itself isn't clean in the transcript.
- **Avet offers more options:** multiple sizes and colors (blue, black,
  gunmetal, orange, pink) to match a rod or a whole reel collection. As of
  this clip (2022-06-30), Avet's colored models are right-hand retrieve
  only — left-hand retrieve isn't offered in any color; no later update
  appears in this corpus (`z5UoW9pntvE`).

## Lever feel and grip

- **Penn's lever runs smoother and sleeker**, with no knurled or roughened
  section to grab (`d0fxBYmAnIk`, `z5UoW9pntvE`).
- **Avet's Raptor lever adds a glide plate and a knurled end**, making it
  easier to slide back and forth and easier to hold with wet or
  slime-covered hands — the trade-off against Penn's smoother, ungrooved
  lever (`d0fxBYmAnIk`, `z5UoW9pntvE`).

## Strike-to-full-drag button

- **Both reels have a button that jumps the lever from strike (high) to full
  drag.** The Avet's button is small but not hard to reach — push it and the
  lever goes to full drag. Neither clip describes or names an equivalent
  control on the Penn (`d0fxBYmAnIk`, `z5UoW9pntvE`).

## Drag curve and preset

- **Both reels run the same kind of drag curve** — no numbers are given for
  the curve itself. Both have a preset button for presetting that curve; how
  to actually set the drag is deferred to a separate video not present in
  this corpus (`d0fxBYmAnIk`, `z5UoW9pntvE`).
