# Penn vs. Avet Lever Drag — Feature Comparison for 2-Speed Reels (Tackle Express)

lever and drag-curve controls — Penn's own lever drag (model not named on
camera) against an Avet **Raptor Series** two-speed (Tackle Express, unnamed

human-typed-metadata rule.

## Bearings

- **Penn Fathom two-speed 30: 5 bearings.** **Avet Raptor (JX) two-speed:
  8 bearings.** The stated reasoning: bearings support the gears, the spool,
  and other moving parts, so more bearings let those parts mesh together
  better — under load, a well-supported gear train is less likely to twist,
  bind, or fail to engage cleanly with each gear.

## Free spool vs. cast control — opposite trade-offs

- **Penn Fathom: very good free spool, no cast control.** Casting it well
  depends on thumb control to avoid a backlash — proficient anglers get a
  longer cast out of it, but it "can cause problems" (backlash) for someone
  new to thumbing a conventional reel.
- **Avet Raptor: not as good a free spool, but has a cast control dial.** The
  cast control is a **1-through-5 setting** that increases or decreases drag
  on the spool during the cast via a magnet that moves closer to or further
  from the spool. Set low (2–3) it gives a little help; set to 5 it gives
  "maximum" braking — useful for an angler who isn't yet proficient at
  thumbing a cast.

## Handle

- **Penn Fathom: a harder-feeling handle knob**, with less grip than the Avet
  but no wear issues — it can go through the shop's line-spooling machine
  without denting or leaving indentations.
- **Avet Raptor: a softer-touch handle knob**, preferred for grip (including
  with wet or slimy hands), but soft enough that careless line-spooling
  alignment can leave a wear mark on it. The Avet's handle arm is also
  described as a little longer than the Penn's — a difference the presenter
  says isn't noticeable while actually fighting a fish.
- Both handles are described as about the same size overall, with "a good
  amount of grip" on either.

## Drag washer construction (Avet Raptor specifically)

- The Raptor's drag washer is described as **glued and notched to fit onto
  the spool**, so it cannot move under pressure — stated as the mechanism
  behind the Raptor's increased drag capacity. The Raptor's side plate is
  also thicker than the G2's, to accommodate a thicker drag washer.
- Stated payoff: "you can get a ton of drag in a very small reel," enough
  (spooled with braided line, filled or half-filled) to land a **150 lb tuna
  on a JX [Raptor two-speed]** reel.

## Cost, manufacturing origin, and color/handedness options

- **Penn Fathom: made overseas, lower cost** — the presenter's pick for a
  budget-conscious angler who still wants a quality reel.
- **Avet: costs more** — captions render the reason as a "usma" product,
  read as **USA-made** given Avet's known US manufacturing (flagged
  `asr-uncertain` — the exact word isn't clean in the captions, but the
  context and price contrast both point to a USA-vs-overseas distinction).
- **Avet offers more options:** multiple sizes and multiple colors (blue,
  black, gunmetal, orange, pink named on camera) to match a rod or match a
  whole reel collection. **Left-hand retrieve is no longer available in any
  color** — Avet's colored models are stated to be right-hand only as of this
  video (2022-06-30); no updated status is given in this corpus.

## Lever feel and grip

- **Penn's lever drag runs smoother and sleeker** — it has no knurled or
  roughened section to grab.
- **Avet's Raptor Series lever adds a glide plate and a knurled end.** The
  knurled section is stated to make the lever easier to slide back and
  forth, and easier to hold onto with wet hands or fish slime on them — the
  stated trade-off against Penn's smoother, ungrooved lever above.

## Strike-to-full-drag button

- **Both reels have a button that jumps the lever from high (strike) into
  full drag.** On the Avet, the button is described as a little small, but
  not so small that it's hard to reach — push it and the lever goes to full
  drag. The clip doesn't describe or name an equivalent on the Penn side.

## Drag curve and preset

- **Both reels are said to run the same kind of drag curve.** No numbers are
  given for the curve itself.
- **Both have a preset button** for presetting that drag curve. How to
  actually set the drag is deferred to a separate video not present in this
  corpus.

## Scope and gaps
