## The setup

- **Application:** framed on camera as "more of my bass fishing, fishing
  shallower water" — the light rod in a four-rod quiver, sized for
  [calico](../species/calico-bass.md)/[sand](../species/sand-bass.md) bass and
  [halibut](../species/california-halibut.md), not a dedicated
  [yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md) outfit.
- **Rod:** rated **15–30 lb**.
- **Reel:** a **Shimano Tranx 400**. Captions render the stutter as "Tra- Tra-
  400"; resolved via cross-check rather than guessed: Tranx is the only
  Shimano casting-reel line with a "400" model, and this same channel's
  companion white seabass clip independently names the identical reel as the
  **"Shimano TRX 400"** (TRX being the Tranx's common shorthand among
  anglers) — see [white seabass rod, reel, and
  line](tackle-express-white-seabass-rod-reel-line.md) and [casting reel for
  sea bass and yellowtail](tackle-express-casting-reel-for-seabass-yellowtail.md),
  which separately names the Tranx line for this same species pairing.
- **Braid:** **50 lb**, spooled to roughly **300 yd**.
- **Leader:** **15 ft of fluorocarbon**, tied at **40 lb**.

## Stated reason for the leader upsize

**Doctrine (medium — Tackle Express, `SL4zZbzdXNA`, 2026-05-08):** even though
the rod itself is only rated to 30 lb, the leader is fished at **40 lb "just
for more abrasion resistance in case you hook that yellowtail" while bass
fishing** — the rod stays light for its primary bass/halibut application, and
only the leader is sized up as insurance, rather than swapping to a dedicated
yellowtail outfit. The point was prompted directly by an off-camera question
asking whether the setup would be "too small for the yellows that are there";
the answer given was **no**.
