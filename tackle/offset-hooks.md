# Offset Hooks — What "Offset" Means and Why Anglers Use It

Spun out of [hooks](hooks.md) — this note is a single, narrow point that note's
four-axis (style/size/wire-gauge/finish) framework doesn't cover: **offset is a
separate axis from style.** A hook is offset when its point is bent out of the
plane of the shank rather than running straight in line with it (an **inline**
hook keeps the point in the same plane as the shank). Offset shows up on **both
circle hooks and J hooks** — it isn't a circle-only trait (Billy, Fisherman's
Landing tackle shop, via SearcherSportfishing "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 204,"
`8MayoweWrAM`, 2022-11-10; unregistered channel/speaker — medium confidence).

## Why anglers like an offset hook

Two stated reasons:

1. **Easier to pin the bait on**, which means less time handling/holding the
   bait before it's back in the water — a livelier bait.
2. **Believed to seat the hook in the corner of the fish's mouth more
   easily** — framed as "a lot of guys think" this, not asserted as the
   source's own established mechanism.

## Regional practice claim

Most circle hooks fished in Southern California are offset; **J hooks come
both ways** (offset and inline).
