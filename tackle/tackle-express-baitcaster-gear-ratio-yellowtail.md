# Baitcaster Gear Ratio for Landing Big Yellowtail — 7:1 as the Middle Ground (Tackle Express)

A 7:1-ratio baitcaster is framed as the middle ground for big
[yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md): fast enough to fish
[surface iron](../techniques/surface-iron.md) casts and grind a
[yo-yo iron](../techniques/yo-yo-iron.md), with enough torque left over to
handle a big fish loading up the reel (`pOFsjDTqaxY`). The source is a
55-second tackle-counter clip; the reel's brand and model are never stated
beyond two internal designations, **HG** and **HL** (`pOFsjDTqaxY`).

## One reel for the cast and the grind

The pitch is a single baitcaster covering both jobs on the same trip —
"enough power to throw your surface irons, yo-yo jigs, hook that bigger fish,
and be able to get it to the boat" (`pOFsjDTqaxY`) — rather than carrying a
dedicated casting reel and a dedicated grinding reel.

## HG vs. HL — the handle-side split

**HL** and **HG** are the reel's only stated model designations. Asked
"which one is it, the HG or the HL?" the presenter answers HL, and gives the
reason as fishing baitcasters left-handed (`pOFsjDTqaxY`) — read as a right-
vs. left-hand retrieve split; the clip does not state that explicitly.

## 7:1 as a deliberate middle ground

**7:1 is framed as a middle ground, not a default pick:** "that middle
ground so you could fish things slower, or you could speed up your handle
and fish things a little quicker" (`pOFsjDTqaxY`) — one reel spanning both a
slower presentation and a faster retrieve instead of needing separate slow-
and fast-ratio reels.

**Mechanism — torque over speed on a big fish.** Contrasted directly against
an 8:1 reel: *"instead of going with 8:1 to where I'd be getting dogged
every now and then, having that little bit more torque is beneficial"*
(`pOFsjDTqaxY`). The lower 7:1 ratio trades a little cranking speed for more
torque, and that torque is what keeps a big yellowtail from stalling
("dogging") the reel once it's loaded up.
