# Jig-Rated Rods — Buy Toward the Top of the Rating, Not the Middle

A jig rod's printed gram-weight rating is a range to buy toward the top end
of, not the middle: pick a rod rated close to the jig weight you intend to
fish, at the top of its printed range. A second, separate check governs the
same purchase — the rod's power (blank stiffness) has to be soft enough to
load and unload that jig's weight, independent of whether the printed gram
range covers it at all. Both points come from a Tackle Express
buying-education interview with jigging specialist Cesar Chang
(`ZrcwcugaEe4`), also cut into two short standalone clips (`DTvXJrtytwg`,
`PbiZMk4Ft6w`).

## The rule

**Buy or select a rod rated close to your jig weight at the top end of its
range** — don't try to "cut in the middle" of the printed jig-weight rating
(`DTvXJrtytwg`).

Stated example: planning to fish a **200 g** jig in **3–400 ft** of water
calls for a rod rated close to 200 g **at the top** of its range, not a rod
whose range simply spans 200 g. A rod rated **100–300 g** does not fish a
200 g jig well — the numeric middle of that range — it actually fishes best
with **250–300 g** jigs, toward its own top end (`DTvXJrtytwg`).

## Rod power, not just gram rating

A rod's **power** — how stiff or soft the blank is — has to be soft enough
to load and unload with the jig's weight, separate from whether its printed
gram range covers that jig weight at all (`PbiZMk4Ft6w`).

Stated example: customers repeatedly ask Cesar Chang for a rod they name
"the 609" to fish a **150 g** jig. He turns them down — that rod's power is
too stiff for that jig weight: it won't bend enough to load and unload, so it
can't impart the action that makes a 150 g jig swim/work correctly. He
recommends dropping to roughly a **seven power**, or a **six power**, to get
the jig moving properly (`PbiZMk4Ft6w`).

**The relationship runs both ways:** a heavier jig calls for a heavier-power
rod — stated as the general direction, without a second paired
jig-weight/power number (`PbiZMk4Ft6w`).

This is a separate check from [the gram-range rule above](#the-rule): that
rule governs picking within a rod's printed jig-weight (gram) range; this one
governs the rod's power rating needing to be soft enough to load and unload a
given jig's weight. A rod's gram range can nominally span a jig's weight
while still being built on a power blank too stiff to work it — the exact
customer mistake described here. Neither "the 609" rod's brand/line nor a
fuller power-to-gram mapping beyond 150 g → seven or six power, heavier jig
→ heavier power is stated on camera; recorded as stated, not extrapolated
(`PbiZMk4Ft6w`).

## Contrast with the all-purpose line-rating rule

This is the **opposite selection heuristic** from [all-purpose rod — buy a
wide line rating, fish the middle of it](all-purpose-rod-line-rating.md),
which advises spooling and fishing the **middle** of a rod's printed **line**
(lb-test) rating. The two rules are not in conflict — they describe different
printed parameters on different rod classes (a general-purpose rod's line
rating in lb vs. a dedicated jig rod's jig-weight rating in grams,
`DTvXJrtytwg`) — but generalizing the fish-the-middle rule from one to the
other gets the jig-weight case backwards.

## Where this applies

Neither short clip names a technique, species, or region on camera, but the
fuller `ZrcwcugaEe4` interview they were cut from is framed around
**slow-pitch vs. speed-jigging** gear — both classes rate rods by max jig
weight in grams (see [slow-pitch jigging](../techniques/slow-pitch-jigging.md#gear-class)
and [knife/speed jigging](../techniques/knife-jigging.md#gear-class)),
not surface iron or trolling gear, which don't use a gram-weight rating.
Region/water isn't stated on camera; this is recorded as a general jig-rod
buying rule, not tied to one SoCal/Baja zone.
