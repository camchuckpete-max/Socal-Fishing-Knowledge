# Searcher 30 lb Large-Tuna Outfit (Steve Carson, Penn/SearcherSportfishing, 2023-03-02)

Steve Carson, aboard the **Searcher** (`riEkdu8PEds`, "Tackle Tip Thursday
Vol. 215 (Landing Large Tuna on 30# Outfit)," uploaded 2023-03-02;
`searcher-sportfishing` registered channel — medium confidence for this
video's own parameters, `high` where noted below for the doctrine that
repeats across his other citations). Framed as an optional, not essential,
rig — "one you probably don't have" — for anglers who already own the rest
of a tuna quiver and want an answer for a specific recurring bite state.

**Earlier, independently-filmed citation of the same "won't eat 40/50, will
eat 30" doctrine as [Rig 2 — 30 lb "big fish
finesse"](bluefin-rig-ladder-by-grade.md#rig-2--30-lb-big-fish-finesse) in
[bluefin rig ladder by grade](bluefin-rig-ladder-by-grade.md), same speaker,
predating that November 2023 San Diego Rod & Reel Club talk by about eight
months.** Both venues describe the identical scenario — 50–70 lb tuna
schooled around the boat, refusing 40 or 50 lb line — from `Steve Carson`, on
a `searcher-sportfishing`-registered venue here and an unregistered venue
there. The repeated, matching doctrine from the registered voice reaches
**high** confidence per the confidence rubric; product/model specifics stay
at this video's own medium (or low, for named products) as usual.

## The scenario (his stated pattern)

- **When — a recurring seasonal pattern, stated as holding for "the last five
  years in a row" as of this 2023-03-02 upload (i.e., since roughly 2018):**
  sometime around **late July–August**, tuna "get tired of everybody fishing
  for them" and get finicky; bait is also sometimes smaller than an angler
  would want. Schools of **40, 50, 60, 70 lb** tuna sit around the boat,
  visible, but won't eat **40 or 50 lb test or any heavier gear** — they will
  eat **30 lb test**.
- **Framed as an addition to an existing quiver, not a starter rig:** "if
  you've already got a lot of other tuna gear and you're looking for
  something... for when those tuna are acting like they just don't like
  you."
- **Ceiling, explicit:** built for the 50–70 lb-class fish described above —
  "not for 100-pounders... definitely not for 200-pounders."

## The outfit

- **Reel — a Penn Fathom II 15, two-speed.** His own favorite for this rig,
  stated reason: a **lighter spool**, which helps cast the bait further out
  from the boat when the fish aren't biting well close in.
  - **Named alternative, same clip — Penn Torque 15 Extra Narrow:** "a lot of
    people, including Art" (a fellow Searcher captain) and other captains
    prefer this reel instead. Same line capacity as the Fathom II 15, but a
    **bigger side plate** giving **more drag surface area and bigger gears**
    — traded off against a **heavier spool**. A second, independent citation
    of the **Torque 15 Extra Narrow** as a named step-up alternative reel,
    corroborating the same product already logged as a 40 lb-outfit upgrade
    option in [Searcher 40 lb all-around tuna
    outfit](searcher-40lb-all-around-tuna-outfit.md#the-outfit) (`m2g97MxmAGI`,
    2020-04-16, Capt. Art also named there) — this time compared directly
    against the Fathom II 15 on the lighter 30 lb rig rather than as an
    upgrade from a Fathom 25 Narrow.
- **Line — 350 yd of 40 lb test braid, topped with a 50 yd top shot of 30 lb
  test monofilament.** Attributed to **"the Searcher crew"** — "this is the
  way they like it... your mileage may vary." Stated reasoning for the mono
  top shot: (1) the stretch keeps the hook from pulling out of the fish's
  mouth, and (2) if the fish dives under the boat or tangles with another
  angler's line, monofilament is easier for the crew to handle/clear than
  braid would be — the same braid-vs-braid tangle reasoning already logged
  for a heavier rig in [Searcher big-tuna rig ladder — Rig, 80 lb (60 lb
  viable)](searcher-big-tuna-rig-ladder.md#rig--80-lb-60-lb-viable-the-all-around-light-tunajig-rig).
- **Rod — an 8 ft Penn Carnage III West Coast rod.** Reasoning for the 8 ft
  length: it lets the angler cast the bait a little further from the boat
  when the tuna aren't biting well close in. **Rating stated two ways on
  camera** — first as "10 to 40 pound," then restated moments later as
  "rated for... 15 to 40 pound test" — preserved as stated rather than
  reconciled (the second figure, 15–40 lb, matches this same 8 ft Carnage III
  rod's rating given independently in another SoCal source, `KajL5TsgBzU`, so
  it is more likely the accurate figure, but the "10 to 40" wording is left
  in place rather than silently dropped). **Stated rule, matching Steve
  Carson's own "fish the middle of the rating" doctrine elsewhere in this
  KB:** 30 lb test sits "right in the middle" of a 15–40 lb-rated rod — a
  further, earlier-dated citation of the same rule already logged in
  [Searcher big-tuna rig ladder — fish the middle of the
  rating](searcher-big-tuna-rig-ladder.md#general-rod-principle-across-all-three-rigs--fish-the-middle-of-the-rating).
- **Leader — about 4 ft of fluorocarbon**, joined to the 30 lb mono top shot
  with a **four-turn surgeon's knot** — a further, independent citation of
  Carson's own **4-turns-for-20–100 lb** rule already logged in [essential
  SoCal knots](../rigging/essential-knots.md#parameters--judgment). His
  stated rule for the leader length itself: long enough that the leader-to-
  mono knot doesn't pass through the rod tip on a cast — "everybody's
  casting style is a little bit different, so you're gonna have to figure
  out exactly how long that is for you"; four feet is his own personal
  number, not a fixed rule. Matches Captain Art's identical guide-clearing
  reasoning for the same knot, already logged in [Searcher 40 lb all-around
  tuna
  outfit](searcher-40lb-all-around-tuna-outfit.md#the-outfit).
- **Hook — an Owner Mutu circle hook.** Size **2** is called the most
  popular size for this rig; size **4** is "really good if you can find
  them," but he notes good luck finding that size in stock. Reasoning for
  fishing a hook this small on 50–70 lb fish: "it looks like a pretty small
  hook for a big tuna, but... it is a miracle, it will hold them." **Names
  the hook brand/model the November 2023 talk left unnamed:** [Rig 2's
  hook](hooks.md#a-finesse-rig-hook-ladder-by-grade-steve-carson-penn-san-diego-rod-and-reel-club-9jnis8hkzla-2023-11-14-unregistered-channelspeaker-single-mention--medium-confidence)
  is described there only as "a small Owner circle hook... not the 'Light'
  version and not the 'hybrid' version"; this earlier video confirms that
  hook is the **Owner Mutu**, at the same two sizes (2 and 4), with first/
  second preference simply reversed between the two citations — the same
  reversed-preference pattern already seen between this channel's two 40 lb
  starter-rig citations.

## Why the two-speed reel matters on this rig specifically

- **The core problem:** on 30 lb test against a 70 lb tuna, "first of all
  you're not going to stop him" — the fish runs hard in every direction at
  full speed. The angler's job is to **keep the line tight** throughout;
  slack line lets the hook fall right out of the fish's mouth. Once the fish
  gives up running and sounds, it typically "looks up at the boat and says
  I'm not going there" — i.e., stops short of the surface and circles.
- **Why a one-speed reel struggles here:** standard pump-and-reel (pump up,
  reel down) lets the fish point its nose down and kick on every reel-down,
  giving back a little line each cycle — true "even if you're a lot
  stronger than me."
- **The fix — shift the two-speed into low ("the Easy Button") and grind
  continuously,** rather than pumping. "You don't wind them straight in,
  that's not what happens" — the fish keeps circling, but **gains roughly
  1–2 ft of depth per circle** under the continuous low-gear grind, versus
  giving line back on every pump-and-reel cycle with a single-speed reel. A
  further, independent citation of the same continuous-grind mechanism
  already logged in full in [two-speed low-gear
  fight](../techniques/two-speed-low-gear-fight.md#the-fix--shift-to-low-then-grind-continuously).
