# Searcher 30 lb Large-Tuna Outfit

A 30 lb-class finesse rig for one specific bite state: 50–70 lb bluefin
schooled around the boat, visible, but refusing 40 lb test or anything
heavier. It's an addition to an existing tuna quiver, not a starter rig, and
it's capped at the grade it's built for — not 100-pounders, and explicitly
not 200-pounders (`riEkdu8PEds`). The same won't-eat-40/50-will-eat-30
doctrine is independently corroborated at [bluefin rig ladder by grade — Rig
2](bluefin-rig-ladder-by-grade.md#rig-2--30-lb-big-fish-finesse), reaching
high confidence for the recurring pattern itself even though the
product-specific picks below stay at this note's own medium confidence.

## The scenario

Sometime in **late July–August**, tuna "get tired of everybody fishing for
them" and turn finicky; bait is sometimes smaller than an angler would
prefer too. Schools of **40, 50, 60, 70 lb** tuna sit around the boat,
visible, but won't eat **40 or 50 lb test or anything heavier** — they will
eat **30 lb test** (`riEkdu8PEds`). The pattern had held for **five years
running** as of the 2023-03-02 upload — i.e., since roughly 2018
(`riEkdu8PEds`).

This rig is built as an addition to an existing quiver for anglers who
already carry the rest of a tuna spread and need an answer for the days
tuna go finicky like this — not as a first outfit.

## The outfit

- **Reel — Penn Fathom II 15, two-speed.** Preferred for this rig for its
  **lighter spool**, which helps cast the bait further from the boat when
  the fish aren't biting close in (`riEkdu8PEds`).
  - **Alternative: Penn Torque 15 Extra Narrow.** "A lot of people,
    including Art" (a fellow Searcher captain) and other captains prefer
    this reel instead (`riEkdu8PEds`). Same line capacity as the Fathom II
    15, but a **bigger side plate** giving more drag surface area and
    bigger gears, traded off against a **heavier spool** (`riEkdu8PEds`). A
    second, independent citation of the same product as a named step-up
    alternative reel, corroborating the same reel already logged as a
    40 lb-outfit upgrade option in [Searcher 40 lb all-around tuna
    outfit](searcher-40lb-all-around-tuna-outfit.md#the-outfit)
    (`m2g97MxmAGI`, Capt. Art also named there) — this time compared
    directly against the Fathom II 15 on the lighter 30 lb rig rather than
    as an upgrade from a Fathom 25 Narrow.
  - **Earlier citation, same rig, named "the 30 lb rig" (`QHY5kmU7OTU`):**
    still a finesse outfit, but "this is where things get fun" — the step
    up from the finesse rig once tuna grow past 50–70 lb but stay just as
    picky (`QHY5kmU7OTU`). **Penn Fathom two-speed, model 15, extra-narrow
    build** — confirms the
    Fathom (not just the Torque) also comes in an extra-narrow version. The
    trade-off matches above: taller/narrower, bigger side plate/gears/drag
    surface, heavier spool, despite identical line capacity to the regular
    15; the stated preference still leans toward the **regular 15** for its
    lighter spool, which casts this rig's smaller bait more easily. A
    **left-handed version of the extra-narrow two-speed exists** — no lefty
    option in the single-speed Fathom/Torque star-drag line
    (`QHY5kmU7OTU`).
- **Line — 350 yd of 40 lb test braid, topped with a 50 yd top shot of
  30 lb test monofilament.** Attributed to the Searcher crew's own
  preference — "this is the way they like it... your mileage may vary"
  (`riEkdu8PEds`). Two reasons for the mono top shot: (1) its stretch keeps
  the hook from pulling out of the fish's mouth; (2) if the fish dives
  under the boat or tangles with another angler's line, monofilament is
  easier for the crew to clear than braid — the same braid-tangle reasoning
  corroborated for a heavier rig in
  [Searcher big-tuna rig
  ladder](searcher-big-tuna-rig-ladder.md#rig--80-lb-60-lb-viable-the-all-around-light-tunajig-rig).
- **Rod — 8 ft Penn Carnage III West Coast.** The 8 ft length casts the
  bait further from the boat when tuna aren't biting close in
  (`riEkdu8PEds`). The rating is given two ways in the same clip — first
  **10–40 lb**, then restated moments later as **15–40 lb test**; both are
  preserved rather than reconciled, though the 15–40 lb figure
  independently matches this same rod's rating given elsewhere in this KB
  (`KajL5TsgBzU`), making it the more likely accurate figure. **Fish the
  middle of the rating:** 30 lb test sits "right in the middle" of a
  15–40 lb-rated rod — a further citation of the same rule already logged
  in [Searcher big-tuna rig
  ladder](searcher-big-tuna-rig-ladder.md#general-rod-principle-across-all-three-rigs--fish-the-middle-of-the-rating)
  (`riEkdu8PEds`).
- **Leader — about 4 ft of fluorocarbon**, joined to the 30 lb mono top
  shot with a **four-turn surgeon's knot** — a further citation of the
  4-turns-for-20–100 lb rule already logged in [essential SoCal
  knots](../rigging/essential-knots.md#parameters--judgment)
  (`riEkdu8PEds`). Leader length is sized so the leader-to-mono knot clears
  the rod tip on a cast; casting style varies angler to angler, so 4 ft is
  a personal number, not a fixed rule (`riEkdu8PEds`) — it matches Captain
  Art's identical guide-clearing reasoning for the same knot, logged in
  [Searcher 40 lb all-around tuna
  outfit](searcher-40lb-all-around-tuna-outfit.md#the-outfit).
  - **Earlier citation (`QHY5kmU7OTU`):** same **~4 ft of fluorocarbon**,
    tied with the same four-turn surgeon's knot.
- **Hook — Owner Mutu circle hook.** **Size 2** is the most popular size
  for this rig; **size 4** is "really good if you can find them," though
  it's often out of stock (`riEkdu8PEds`). A small hook still holds a big
  fish: "it looks like a pretty small hook for a big tuna, but... it is a
  miracle, it will hold them" (`riEkdu8PEds`). This citation names the
  brand/model that [Rig 2's
  hook](hooks.md#a-finesse-rig-hook-ladder-by-grade-steve-carson-penn-san-diego-rod-and-reel-club-9jnis8hkzla-2023-11-14-unregistered-channelspeaker-single-mention--medium-confidence)
  leaves unnamed — confirmed as the same Owner Mutu at the same two sizes
  (2 and 4), with first/second preference simply reversed between the two
  citations, the same reversed-preference pattern already seen between the
  two 40 lb starter-rig citations.
  - **Earlier citation (`QHY5kmU7OTU`):** **Owner Mutu circle hook,
    size 4** — matches this rig's size-4 secondary pick, no reversal this
    time. Past roughly 50 lb, bluefin/yellowfin "don't have teeth like a
    shark or a wahoo, but they have teeth," and a half-hour-plus fight lets
    them chew through the leader; the circle hook's corner-of-the-mouth set
    keeps the leader off the teeth through the fight. A 95 lb tuna has been
    landed on this exact rig and hook, "not that I recommend it"
    (`QHY5kmU7OTU`).

## Fighting the fish — two-speed low-gear grind

On 30 lb test against a 70 lb tuna, "first of all you're not going to stop
him" — the fish runs hard in every direction at full speed, and the
angler's job is to keep the line tight throughout; slack line lets the
hook fall out of the fish's mouth. Once the fish gives up running and
sounds, it typically stops short of the surface and circles — "looks up at
the boat and says I'm not going there" (`riEkdu8PEds`).

**Mechanism — why a one-speed reel struggles here:** standard pump-and-reel
(pump up, reel down) lets the fish point its nose down and kick on every
reel-down, giving back a little line each cycle — true "even if you're a
lot stronger than me" (`riEkdu8PEds`).

**The fix:** shift the two-speed into low ("the Easy Button") and grind
continuously rather than pumping. The fish keeps circling, but gains
roughly **1–2 ft of depth per circle** under the continuous low-gear
grind, versus giving line back on every pump-and-reel cycle with a
single-speed reel (`riEkdu8PEds`) — a further citation of the same
continuous-grind mechanism logged in full in [two-speed low-gear
fight](../techniques/two-speed-low-gear-fight.md#the-fix--shift-to-low-then-grind-continuously).

## Bait hooking for distant bluefin

When bluefin are breaking **100–200+ yd** off the boat and won't come
closer, hook the bait in the **nape of the neck** (behind the head) —
"shoulder hooking" — so the hook folds down as the bait swims away,
cutting resistance and letting the bait pull hard and carry more distance
under its own power. Let the bait swim out **100–150 yd**, then wind it
back in **slowly** — bites often come as the bait "slow-trolls" back
toward the boat (`QHY5kmU7OTU`). A closely-matching earlier citation of
the same shoulder-hook doctrine is logged in [fishing live
bait](../bait/fishing-live-bait.md#bridge-of-the-nose-the-sardine-wishbone-and-shoulder-hooking-for-distant-blue-fin-steve-carson-penn-fishing-tackle-searchersportfishing-tackle-tip-thursday-vol-226-hooking-your-bait-ad0iim9c15o-2023-08-24-searcher-sportfishing-is-a-registered-channelseries-but-this-specific-mechanism-is-a-single-mention--medium-confidence).

**Any sinker on this rig → nose-hook the bait** across the bridge of the
nose or sideways through it, regardless of sinker weight — "a small sinker
or a big sinker," with no specific weight figure given (`QHY5kmU7OTU`) —
matching the weighted-rig-mandates-nose-hook rule logged in the same
fishing-live-bait section.

## Wahoo variant

Rigged instead with **about 2 ft of light wire leader** in place of the
straight fluorocarbon leader above, this same 30 lb outfit doubles as a
live-bait wahoo rig on longer trips (`QHY5kmU7OTU`) — see
[wahoo](../species/wahoo.md) for the species-level doctrine this variant
feeds.
