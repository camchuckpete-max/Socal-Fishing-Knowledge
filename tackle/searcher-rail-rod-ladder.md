# Searcher Rail-Rod Ladder — Four Tiers by Line Rating (Steve Carson, Penn/SearcherSportfishing, 2023-12-07)

Steve Carson, aboard the **Searcher** (`sjOJiR6_HJ4`, "Tackle Tip Thursday
Vol. 229 (Rail Rods)," uploaded 2023-12-07; `searcher-sportfishing`
registered channel/series — medium confidence for this video's own
rig-specific parameters, `high` where noted below for doctrine that repeats
his other citations). Framed as a direct answer to anglers still bringing
**1980s/1990s-era 5 ft 2 in–6 ft stand-up trolling rods** aboard for
bluefin: "these are great in their day, put them away, sell them on eBay."
A companion, later-dated (by about five months) ladder to the [three-rig
ladder for big tuna, 80 lb up to 130
lb](searcher-big-tuna-rig-ladder.md#three-rig-ladder-for-big-tuna-80-lb-up-to-130-lb-steve-carson-pennsearcher-2023-07-13)
already logged in [Searcher big-tuna rig ladder](searcher-big-tuna-rig-ladder.md) —
this video adds a lighter livebait tier below that ladder's three rigs and
leads with the rod itself, tier by tier, rather than with the reel.

## What makes a rod a rail rod (his framing)

Same underlying mechanism already logged in [gear classes — rail-rod
class](gear-classes.md#rail-rod-class--what-makes-a-rod-a-rail-rod-mechanism)
and [fighting fish from the rail — big-tuna
variant](../techniques/fighting-fish-from-the-rail.md#big-tuna-variant-armpit-tuck-low-gear-and-body-weight-leverage-steve-carson-pennsearcher-2023-07-13):
a rail rod carries a **long, padded foregrip**, and on some models **double
reinforcement**. His stated rule for using it: **put the rubber (foregrip)
on the rail, never the blank** — putting the blank on the rail breaks the
rod. Mechanics as he states them here: tuck the rod butt under the **left
armpit**, lay the rod on the rail, keep the reel in **low gear**, and
**crank down, then pump up with body weight** — not the arms — and repeat.
He frames the rail as necessary for everyone except "NFL linemen" strong
enough to stand and fight 100–300 lb+ tuna unsupported: "the rest of us
normal humans have to use the rail." A crew member coaches anglers through
the fight in real time once hooked up. This armpit-tuck/low-gear/
body-weight mechanic matches, and doesn't add beyond, the fuller mechanics
already logged in [fighting fish from the rail — big-tuna
variant](../techniques/fighting-fish-from-the-rail.md#big-tuna-variant-armpit-tuck-low-gear-and-body-weight-leverage-steve-carson-pennsearcher-2023-07-13)
from his own 2023-07-13 video — not duplicated here beyond this summary.

## Rod length — 7 ft is the floor, longer only for bigger/stronger anglers

**Don't go shorter than 7 ft** — he calls out the old 5 ft 2 in rods by name
as the thing not to do, matching the same **5 ft 2 in** call-out already
logged in [Searcher big-tuna rig ladder — Rig
2](searcher-big-tuna-rig-ladder.md#rig-2--extra-heavy-rig-100-lb-penn-international-16visx),
where the stated reason is being able to move around the boat and around
other anglers. A bigger/stronger angler ("NFL lineman... or maybe just a
college football lineman") can instead choose a **longer** 7 ft 3 in or
7 ft 6 in rod. This is the opposite adjustment direction from, not a
contradiction of, [rod length — fitting the stick to the
angler](rod-length-for-angler-size.md): that note has a smaller-framed
angler sizing **down** about two inches from a stock rod for better reach;
this video has a bigger/stronger angler sizing **up** in length instead —
both keyed to angler build, kept side by side as two ends of the same
fitting logic rather than merged into one rule.

## The four-tier ladder

### Tier 1 — lightest rail rod: 7 ft 8 in, 40–100 lb, livebait

- **Rod:** **7 ft 8 in**, rated **40–100 lb test** — "the lightest that
  you'll usually see" as a rail rod.
- **Line — fish the middle of the rating:** **60 lb test** "perfectly,"
  with **50 or 80 lb** also fine; 60 sits closest to the middle of a
  40–100 rating.
- **Bait:** live sardine or live mackerel.
- **Rigs:** [flyline](../techniques/flyline.md), [drop
  shot](../techniques/drop-shot.md), or a sinker rig (see [sliding
  sinker](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md)) — "there's other videos that
  will describe those."
- **Action note:** this rod carries **a little more tip action** than the
  heavier tiers below, which he calls helpful specifically for live-bait
  fishing.
- **A close match, about five months earlier, same speaker:** [Rig 1 —
  heavy outfit, 80
  lb](searcher-big-tuna-rig-ladder.md#rig-1--heavy-outfit-80-lb)
  (2022-04-21) names the identical **7 ft 8 in, 40–100 lb** rod rating for
  an 80 lb-braid outfit graded to 175–200 lb bluefin — not stated as the
  same physical rod in this transcript, but the rating match is close
  enough to read as the same product, kept as corroborating rather than a
  confirmed identity.

### Tier 2 — "Carnage 3," 7 ft, 60–100 lb

- **Rod:** the **Penn Carnage 3**, **7 ft**, rated **60–100 lb test** — his
  own aside that the printed rating is hard to read on camera ("you'll have
  to trust me"). Product/model pick — **low confidence** per this channel's
  sponsored-claim rule; mechanism/parameters read at this note's own
  medium confidence.
- **Line — fish the middle:** **80 lb test** is "exactly" the middle of a
  60–100 rating, called "ideal."
- **Bait/lure:** large live baits, or medium-size jigs.
- **Grade:** tuna up to **roughly 225 lb**.
- **Foregrip:** extra reinforcement at this heavier tier specifically,
  because the angler will be rubbing it against the rail more.
- **Same product family, different rating, elsewhere on this channel:** an
  **8 ft Penn Carnage III West Coast rod, rated 15–40 lb** (or "10–40,"
  stated both ways on camera) is the rod on the [Searcher 30 lb large-tuna
  outfit](searcher-30lb-large-tuna-outfit.md#the-outfit), and an **8 ft
  "Carnage Three" West Coast rod, rated 30–60 lb**, is the rod on the
  [Searcher daytime dart-jig ladder's light
  rig](searcher-daytime-dart-jig-outfit-ladder.md#light-rig--standard-daytime-dart-jig-outfit).
  Read as three different line-class models within the same **Penn Carnage
  III** product line (30 lb, 40–100-ish, and this rig's 60–100 tier) rather
  than one rod cited three different ways — kept side by side, not
  reconciled.

### Tier 3 — "8130" model, 7 ft, 80–130 lb

- **Rod:** the **"8130" model**, still **7 ft**, rated **80–130 lb test** —
  "probably the most popular one if you're coming out on the Searcher...
  make sure if you don't have anything else, have this one."
- **Reel:** a **Penn International 16BX** (captioned this way — read as the
  same **Penn International 16VISX** already logged repeatedly elsewhere on
  this channel; asr-uncertain on the exact suffix).
- **Line — fish the middle:** **100 lb test**.
- **Grade:** tuna up to **roughly 300 lb**.
- **Corroborates, same speaker, about five months earlier:** [Rig — Penn
  International 16VISX, 100
  lb](searcher-big-tuna-rig-ladder.md#rig--penn-international-16visx-100-lb)
  (2023-07-13) — same **7 ft, 80–130 lb** rod rating, same reel family, and
  the same ~300 lb+ grade ceiling (including the same landed-313-lb personal
  catch referenced there). A further, independent citation of the same rig
  from the same speaker on the same channel within one calendar year.

### Tier 4 — 80–150 model, 100–130 lb top shots

- **Rod:** the **80–150 model** — length not restated in this segment (the
  two lighter tiers above are both given as 7 ft; this video doesn't
  confirm whether this heaviest tier holds the same length).
- **Reel:** a **Penn International 20**, "or even a 30."
- **Line:** **100 or 130 lb test** top shots.
- **Grade:** framed against **350–400 lb** tuna — "they're out there, trust
  me."
- **Corroborates, same speaker, about five months earlier:** [Rig — Penn
  International 20VISX, 100–130
  lb](searcher-big-tuna-rig-ladder.md#rig--penn-international-20visx-100130-lb)
  (2023-07-13) — same **80–150 lb** rod rating, same **20VISX (some anglers
  use 30VISX)** reel choice, same **100/130 lb** line, and the same
  "want more stopping power for fish past 300 lb" framing.

## General rod principle — fish the middle of the rating (repeated doctrine, `high`)

**Stated rule, restated here a third time from this same speaker/channel:**
throw out the high end and the low end of a rod's printed line rating and
fish as close to the **middle** as you can — "that's where you're going to
get the best performance," regardless of species or rod. Worked at every
tier above (60 lb on a 40–100 rod, 80 lb on a 60–100 rod, 100 lb on an
80–130 rod). Matches the identical rule already logged at `high` confidence
(repeated doctrine, registered channel) in [Searcher big-tuna rig ladder —
fish the middle of the
rating](searcher-big-tuna-rig-ladder.md#general-rod-principle-across-all-three-rigs--fish-the-middle-of-the-rating)
and again in [Searcher daytime dart-jig outfit
ladder](searcher-daytime-dart-jig-outfit-ladder.md#light-rig--standard-daytime-dart-jig-outfit)
— a further, independent citation.
