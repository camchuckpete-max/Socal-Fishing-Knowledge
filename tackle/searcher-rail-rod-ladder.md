# Searcher Rail-Rod Ladder — Four Tiers by Line Rating (Steve Carson, Penn/SearcherSportfishing, 2023-12-07)

A four-tier bluefin rail-rod ladder, organized by line rating rather than
reel model, aimed at anglers still bringing 1980s/1990s-era 5 ft 2 in–6 ft
stand-up trolling rods aboard: "these are great in their day, put them away,
sell them on eBay" (`sjOJiR6_HJ4`). It adds a lighter livebait tier below the
three rigs in [Searcher big-tuna rig
ladder](searcher-big-tuna-rig-ladder.md#three-rig-ladder-for-big-tuna-80-lb-up-to-130-lb-steve-carson-pennsearcher-2023-07-13)
(2023-07-13) and organizes by rod first, reel second.

## What makes a rod a rail rod — mechanism

A rail rod carries a long, padded foregrip and, on some models, double
reinforcement (`sjOJiR6_HJ4`) — the same mechanism already covered in [gear
classes — rail-rod
class](gear-classes.md#rail-rod-class--what-makes-a-rod-a-rail-rod-mechanism)
and [fighting fish from the rail — big-tuna
variant](../techniques/fighting-fish-from-the-rail.md#big-tuna-variant-armpit-tuck-low-gear-and-body-weight-leverage-steve-carson-pennsearcher-2023-07-13),
not repeated here beyond this summary.

Put the foregrip on the rail, never the blank — blank-on-rail breaks the rod
(`sjOJiR6_HJ4`). Fight mechanics: tuck the rod butt under the left armpit,
lay the rod on the rail, keep the reel in low gear, crank down, then pump up
with body weight rather than the arms, and repeat; a crew member coaches
anglers through the fight in real time (`sjOJiR6_HJ4`). The rail is necessary
for anyone fighting 100–300 lb+ tuna except anglers strong enough to stand
and fight unsupported — "the rest of us normal humans have to use the rail"
(`sjOJiR6_HJ4`).

## Rod length — 7 ft is the floor

**Don't go shorter than 7 ft.** The floor rules out the 5 ft 2 in–6 ft
stand-up trolling rods named above, matching the same 5 ft 2 in call-out in
[Searcher big-tuna rig ladder — Rig
2](searcher-big-tuna-rig-ladder.md#rig-2--extra-heavy-rig-100-lb-penn-international-16visx),
where the stated reason is moving around the boat and other anglers
(`sjOJiR6_HJ4`).

A bigger or stronger angler can size up instead of down, to 7 ft 3 in or
7 ft 6 in (`sjOJiR6_HJ4`). This complements, rather than conflicts with, [rod
length for angler size](rod-length-for-angler-size.md), where a
smaller-framed angler sizes down about two inches from a stock rod for
better reach — both apply the same build-to-rod fitting logic in opposite
directions.

## The four-tier ladder

### Tier 1 — lightest rail rod: 7 ft 8 in, 40–100 lb, livebait

- **Rod:** 7 ft 8 in, rated 40–100 lb test — the lightest rating typically
  seen on a rail rod (`sjOJiR6_HJ4`).
- **Line:** 60 lb test fishes the middle of the rating "perfectly"; 50 or
  80 lb also work (`sjOJiR6_HJ4`).
- **Bait:** live sardine or live mackerel (`sjOJiR6_HJ4`).
- **Rigs:** [flyline](../techniques/flyline.md), [drop
  shot](../techniques/drop-shot.md), or a sinker rig (see [sliding
  sinker](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md)) (`sjOJiR6_HJ4`).
- **Action:** carries more tip action than the heavier tiers below, useful
  specifically for livebait fishing (`sjOJiR6_HJ4`).

The 7 ft 8 in, 40–100 lb rating matches the rod in [Searcher big-tuna rig
ladder — Rig 1](searcher-big-tuna-rig-ladder.md#rig-1--heavy-outfit-80-lb),
filmed 2022-04-21, there paired with an 80 lb-braid outfit graded to
175–200 lb bluefin. Not confirmed as the same physical rod, but the rating
match reads as the same product line.

### Tier 2 — "Carnage 3," 7 ft, 60–100 lb

- **Rod:** Penn Carnage 3, 7 ft, rated 60–100 lb test (`sjOJiR6_HJ4`).
- **Line:** 80 lb test is exactly the middle of the 60–100 rating, called
  "ideal" (`sjOJiR6_HJ4`).
- **Bait/lure:** large live baits, or medium-size jigs (`sjOJiR6_HJ4`).
- **Grade:** tuna up to roughly 225 lb (`sjOJiR6_HJ4`).
- **Foregrip:** carries extra reinforcement at this tier, because the angler
  rubs it against the rail more than on the lighter Tier 1 rod
  (`sjOJiR6_HJ4`).

The same Penn Carnage III line appears at two other ratings elsewhere on
this channel: an 8 ft West Coast model rated 15–40 lb (stated on camera as
both "15–40" and "10–40") on the [Searcher 30 lb large-tuna
outfit](searcher-30lb-large-tuna-outfit.md#the-outfit), and an 8 ft West
Coast model rated 30–60 lb on the [Searcher daytime dart-jig ladder's light
rig](searcher-daytime-dart-jig-outfit-ladder.md#light-rig--standard-daytime-dart-jig-outfit)
— three distinct line-class models within one product family (roughly 30 lb,
40–100, and this tier's 60–100), not one rod cited three different ways.

### Tier 3 — "8130" model, 7 ft, 80–130 lb

- **Rod:** the "8130" model, 7 ft, rated 80–130 lb test — the most popular
  Searcher rail rod: "make sure if you don't have anything else, have this
  one" (`sjOJiR6_HJ4`).
- **Reel:** a Penn International 16BX (captioned this way; read as the Penn
  International 16VISX cited repeatedly elsewhere on this channel —
  asr-uncertain on the exact suffix) (`sjOJiR6_HJ4`).
- **Line:** 100 lb test, the middle of the rating (`sjOJiR6_HJ4`).
- **Grade:** tuna up to roughly 300 lb (`sjOJiR6_HJ4`).

This matches [Searcher big-tuna rig ladder — Penn International 16VISX,
100 lb](searcher-big-tuna-rig-ladder.md#rig--penn-international-16visx-100-lb),
filmed 2023-07-13, five months before this video's 2023-12-07 date: the same
7 ft, 80–130 lb rod rating, the same reel family, the same roughly 300 lb+
grade ceiling, and the same 313 lb personal-catch reference.

### Tier 4 — 80–150 model, 100–130 lb top shots

- **Rod:** the 80–150 model; length isn't restated for this tier (the two
  lighter tiers above are both given as 7 ft, but this video doesn't confirm
  whether the heaviest tier matches) (`sjOJiR6_HJ4`).
- **Reel:** a Penn International 20, "or even a 30" (`sjOJiR6_HJ4`).
- **Line:** 100 or 130 lb test top shots (`sjOJiR6_HJ4`).
- **Grade:** framed against 350–400 lb tuna — "they're out there, trust me"
  (`sjOJiR6_HJ4`).

This matches [Searcher big-tuna rig ladder — Penn International 20VISX,
100–130
lb](searcher-big-tuna-rig-ladder.md#rig--penn-international-20visx-100130-lb),
filmed 2023-07-13, five months before this video: the same 80–150 lb rod
rating, the same 20VISX reel choice (30VISX as an alternative), the same
100/130 lb line, and the same framing toward anglers who want more stopping
power for fish past 300 lb (`sjOJiR6_HJ4`).

## Fish the middle of the line rating

Throw out the high and low ends of a rod's printed line rating and fish as
close to the middle as possible — "that's where you're going to get the
best performance," regardless of species or rod (`sjOJiR6_HJ4`). Applied at
every tier above: 60 lb on a 40–100 rod, 80 lb on a 60–100 rod, 100 lb on an
80–130 rod. The same rule appears in [Searcher big-tuna rig ladder — fish the
middle of the
rating](searcher-big-tuna-rig-ladder.md#general-rod-principle-across-all-three-rigs--fish-the-middle-of-the-rating)
and in [Searcher daytime dart-jig outfit
ladder](searcher-daytime-dart-jig-outfit-ladder.md#light-rig--standard-daytime-dart-jig-outfit).
