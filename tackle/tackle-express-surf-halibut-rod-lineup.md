# Surf Halibut Rod Lineup — Light vs. Heavy, and Why PE1 Beats Heavy Braid (Shannon Gallagher, Tackle Express)

Tackle Express, "Halibut Gear & Techniques | Beaches & Harbor Tips with
Shannon Gallagher (Pt 4/4)," `fHMwcm1xRQk`, uploaded 2026-01-13. `tackle-express`
is an **unregistered** channel, single mention — **medium** confidence. Rod
picks and line spec for the hard-bait/leadhead side of surf halibut fishing
covered in [California halibut](../species/california-halibut.md) and
[inshore crankbaits](../techniques/inshore-crankbaits.md); the drop-shot side
of the same series has its own gear covered in
[drop shot](../techniques/drop-shot.md).

## Selection principle

Gallagher's stated framing for picking a surf rod at all: think about **what
size hook you're fishing on it**, because you have to be able to keep that
hook pinned in a fish to land it — rod choice follows hook/lure choice, not
the other way around.

## Light rod — small trebles and single-hook lures

- Roots the whole class in **Japanese shore-jig / Japanese sea bass
  rods** — 8 to 11 ft, a long split-grip handle, fished with a spinning reel.
- His demonstrated example: a **Major Craft Sienna 962M**, 9½ ft. Described
  as **moderate action** and **"really light"** — it **casts well** and
  **fishes both treble-hook lures and single-hook lures pretty well**, i.e.
  one rod covers the minnow-hardbait and the leadhead-swimbait halibut
  presentations without swapping sticks.
- **Reel: 2500 or 3000 size.**
- Stated reason he favors a **longer** rod in this class: it **casts
  farther** and **absorbs bounces from an angry fish**, which keeps hooks
  pinned better than a shorter, stiffer stick would.

## Line — PE1, not heavy braid, and why (the Lucky Craft anecdote)

- He fishes **PE1** on most of his light surf gear — for any hard bait, or a
  leadhead/neck-breaker on a **light-wire hook**. PE1 translates to roughly
  **10 lb braid** in US line labeling. He specifies an **8-carrier "smooth
  braid"** PE line style specifically (not a generic braid).
- **Stated reason: casts significantly better, especially upwind** — any
  casting-distance edge available, he takes it.
- **The cautionary anecdote:** when Lucky Craft hard baits (~$20 each) first
  came out, anglers were reluctant to risk losing one, so many of his friends
  jumped straight to **30–40 lb braid** on their surf reels for extra
  confidence against break-offs. Stated result: it **"casted terribly and
  fished even worse."** His conclusion carried forward into the PE1
  recommendation above: heavier line than the rod/lure combination calls for
  actively hurts casting distance and action, it doesn't just add margin.

## Heavier rod — weedless swimbaits and bigger plugs in kelp

- A second, **slightly heavier** surf rod in the same lineup, which he
  frames as **"more of what we consider a striper rod"** — named on camera as
  a **Major Craft Triple Cross LSJ**, 9 ft 6 in.
- **What it's for:** fishing a **neck breaker head with a weedless
  swimbait** when there's kelp around — halibut "don't necessarily want to
  get you in the kelp, but they will," so the extra backbone lets you **pull
  a little harder and still set the hook** through/near cover. Same rod
  reappears later in the series for sport-boat halibut (not covered in this
  transcript).
- He'd also fish it for a **chatterbait** if using one on a swimbait rod, and
  for **larger plugs carrying at least a size-4 treble** — again, the
  selection principle above: bigger hook, more rod needed to keep it pinned.
- Casts well despite the added power, per his framing ("this thing casts
  really well and it's got a little bit more power to it").
