# Searcher 40 lb All-Around Tuna Outfit

The 40 lb outfit is the single most important all-around combo for a
first-timer to bring aboard any San Diego tuna boat — not gear specific to
one boat (`fyJA3o2hVh0`). San Diego tuna run **20–200 lb**, averaging roughly
**30–60 lb** (`fyJA3o2hVh0`). This exact rig isn't built to land a
200-pounder: it runs a little heavy on the smallest fish it sees (**about
15 lb**), scales cleanly through **30–40–50–60 lb**, and stays the right tool
up through **70–80 lb** fish (`ueDBCY1mIPk`) — a beginner's first and only
outfit before they own anything else (`ueDBCY1mIPk`). A later citation, same
channel, restates it as **"the first combo you buy"** for someone stepping up
from trout, bass, or pier gear (`ueDBCY1mIPk`).

This rig closely matches, and is the earliest-dated citation in this KB of,
[Rig 3 — 40 lb (the starter rig)](bluefin-rig-ladder-by-grade.md#rig-3--40-lb-the-starter-rig),
the same speaker's later (2023-11-14) talk. The two notes cite each other as
corroboration rather than merging, per this repo's note-size guideline.

## The outfit

- **Reel:** a Penn Fathom 25 Narrow, two-speed (`fyJA3o2hVh0`) — later cited
  as the updated **Penn Fathom II, two-speed, 25 Narrow**, the same
  size/gearing pick (`ueDBCY1mIPk`). An earlier citation names it the
  **Fathom FTH-25** (captions render "fathom fth 25 and ld2 to speed" — read
  as Fathom FTH-25, two-speed; the "ld2" fragment is `asr-uncertain` but
  consistent with the two-speed pick above) (`m2g97MxmAGI`). The low-gear
  shift button is nicknamed **"the easy button"** — "push a little button in
  for low twist, a little [pull it out for] high" (`fyJA3o2hVh0`) — a second,
  independent citation of the same nickname for a two-speed's gear-shift
  button, this time naming the specific reel; see [two-speed low-gear
  fight](../techniques/two-speed-low-gear-fight.md#mechanism--what-shift-to-low-actually-changes).
  - **Budget-up alternative:** a **Penn Torque 15 Extra Narrow**, named by "a
    lot of anglers," Capt. Art among them, as a step-up choice on this same
    40 lb outfit "if your budget will allow" (`m2g97MxmAGI`).
- **Line:** the reel filled **about three-quarters** with **65 lb test
  braid** (roughly **350 yd** at that fill level), topped with **25–50 yd of
  40 lb test monofilament** (`fyJA3o2hVh0`). A later citation repeats the
  identical build — three-quarters fill, 65 lb braid, ~350 yd, under a 50 yd
  mono top shot (`ueDBCY1mIPk`).
- **Rod:** an **8 ft rod, rated 25–50 lb test** — the 40 lb mainline sits in
  the middle of that rating (`ueDBCY1mIPk`). Runs a little long by design, to
  prioritize casting distance: "everybody should learn how to cast"
  (`ueDBCY1mIPk`).
- **Leader connection:** a **four-turn surgeon's knot** — this rig's
  earliest-dated citation of the 4-turns-for-20–100 lb rule in [surgeon's
  knot, mono-to-fluoro leader
  connection](../rigging/surgeons-knot-mono-to-fluoro.md#parameters--judgment)
  (`fyJA3o2hVh0`, `ueDBCY1mIPk`).
  - **Knot-length rule (Capt. Art, Searcher):** tie the knot **as long as
    possible** — as far from the hook as the leader length allows — but not
    so long it passes into the rod tip on a cast (`fyJA3o2hVh0`). The same
    knot-clearing-the-guides mechanism is documented generically for
    mono-to-braid connections in [line & leader](line-and-leader.md#when-mono-top-shot).
- **Leader:** **3–5 ft of fluorocarbon**, tied to the 40 lb mono top shot
  with the surgeon's knot above (`fyJA3o2hVh0`); a later citation narrows it
  to **about 4 ft** (`ueDBCY1mIPk`).
- **Hook:** an **Owner Mutu circle hook, size 2**, sometimes **size 4**, for
  most of the fly lining on this rig (`fyJA3o2hVh0`) — small enough to fool a
  tuna expected to run about 30 lb,
  but strong enough to hold up to 100 lb if hooked; not built *for* 100 lb
  tuna, but has a "more than reasonable chance" of landing one (`fyJA3o2hVh0`).
  A later citation keeps size 2 primary but flips the availability framing:
  **size 2**, with **size 4 as the step-up "if you can find them"** — size 4s
  are harder to find (`ueDBCY1mIPk`). Added reasoning: tuna "don't like big
  hooks," so a small hook hooks up better, and a small circle hook still
  holds a big fish — tuna in the **60, 70, and 80 lb** range have been hooked
  and held on this same hook, "sometimes even bigger" (`ueDBCY1mIPk`).

## Also fishes jigs — a dart-type-jig aside

- Tuna sometimes **prefer a jig to live bait** (`fyJA3o2hVh0`). The
  **dart-type jig** — also called "cold snipers" on camera, read as **Colt
  Sniper**, consistent with this note's and [iron jigs](../lures/iron-jigs.md)'s
  existing Colt Sniper usage — is one of the most popular styles, sold under
  many brand names (`fyJA3o2hVh0`).
- Favorite named model: the **Williamson Gomame** (`fyJA3o2hVh0`,
  `QHY5kmU7OTU`) — a product pick that stays `low` confidence regardless of
  repetition, per the channel's sponsored-claim caveat. Sized **1¾, 2¾, and
  3½ oz**; the middle/smallest sizes are preferred because they cast easiest
  and sink deepest, fastest (`QHY5kmU7OTU`). Fished when a school is keyed on
  bait smaller than a standard sardine or mackerel — "they are locked onto
  biting a small bait, and that's all they're going to buy" (`QHY5kmU7OTU`).
  Stated etymology: "we learned in 2021 that gomame means small dried
  sardines" (`fyJA3o2hVh0`) — not independently verified in this corpus.
- **Sizing that fishes this 40 lb rig: roughly 80 g dart jigs**
  (`fyJA3o2hVh0`) — lighter than the **50–125 g** daytime Colt Sniper class
  logged for a dedicated, lighter finesse rig in [Searcher big/giant tuna rig
  ladder — Dart/Colt Sniper finesse jig
  rig](searcher-big-tuna-rig-ladder.md#dartcolt-sniper-finesse-jig-rig--daytime-tuna-steve-carson-pennsearchersportfishing-2024-06-13).
- **Hook retrofit:** swap the factory hook for an **Owner ST-66 treble**,
  heavier wire than stock — a further citation of the swap already logged in
  [iron jigs — bluefin hook swap](../lures/iron-jigs.md#hook-style--assist-vs-treble)
  (`QHY5kmU7OTU`). **Split ring:** rated split rings only — **Owner-brand,
  roughly 120 lb test**, "quite a bit heavier" than the line class needs, not
  generic unrated rings (`QHY5kmU7OTU`). **Sizing rule:** go **one size up**
  from the factory treble — e.g. a 1/0 factory treble steps up to a 2/0
  ST-66 (`QHY5kmU7OTU`).
- **Treble vs. single:** this style of jig draws **more bites on a treble
  than a single hook**, for a reason that isn't explained — tested
  repeatedly, confirmed by other anglers (`QHY5kmU7OTU`) ⚠ Flagged gap — no
  corpus source: mechanism. A further, earlier-dated citation of the same
  unexplained treble-draws-more-bites claim is logged in [iron jigs — a
  third, earlier citation](../lures/iron-jigs.md#hook-style--assist-vs-treble)
  (`pCd6QykcZ0w`). Past **100 lb** tuna, some jigs are still rigged
  **single-hook** instead — a single hook holds a big fish better and the
  treble is more prone to pulling out at that grade: "I'd rather get four
  bites [on the treble] and maybe land two, [than] get only one bite [on the
  single] and land it" (`QHY5kmU7OTU`). On this exact jig/rod/reel combo, an
  angler has hooked (not landed) a 200 lb tuna and fought it roughly 90
  minutes — evidence that this outfit gives a fighting chance well beyond
  its built-for grade (`QHY5kmU7OTU`; full account in the evidence file).
- **Bite mechanics — most bites come on the sink, not a skip-jig retrieve:**
  cast at a boiling/breaking school, then let the jig **fall to the depth
  the captain calls** (one example: 150 ft) rather than working it back fast
  on top (`QHY5kmU7OTU`) — a further, independent citation of the
  bites-come-on-the-sink mechanic in [dart jig —
  mechanics](../techniques/dart-jig-tuna.md#mechanics) (roughly 40% of bites
  on the sink there).
