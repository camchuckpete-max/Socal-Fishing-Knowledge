# Searcher Bluefin Jig Ladder by Daypart and Depth (Steve Carson, Penn/SearcherSportfishing, 2023-10-12)

Jig selection for bluefin runs on two axes, in order: how well the fish can
see the lure (daytime vs. dawn/dusk vs. full night), then depth
(`vVOkxHx58Eg`). The ladder runs from a 100 g daytime jig up to a 400 g
personal ceiling, with hook style — treble vs. single, and single-hook
placement — changing by weight tier rather than staying fixed. This is a
**heavier tier** than, and distinct from, the 50–170 g daytime dart-jig/
Colt-Sniper class in [dart jig — daytime finesse
tuna](../techniques/dart-jig-tuna.md) and [Searcher daytime dart-jig outfit
ladder](searcher-daytime-dart-jig-outfit-ladder.md) — this ladder's lightest
jig (100 g) sits at the top of that class's range and continues well past it.
General jig-weight/depth and assist-hook rigging doctrine for the whole class
is in [knife jigs](../lures/knife-jigs.md); this note holds one speaker's
full daytime-to-night progression and a hook-style rule that changes by
weight tier.

## The ladder

Jig weight and hook style step up together, lightest/most-visible-light to
heaviest/darkest (`vVOkxHx58Eg`):

| Jig weight | When / depth | Leader | Hook style |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **~100 g** | Midday, full daylight — the most-used daytime jig; good down to about **200 ft** | not stated for this tier | heavy-duty **20-size treble hook** (upgraded from stock) |
| **~170 g** | Dawn/dusk (limited light), or any time fish sit deeper than the 100 g jig's ~200 ft ceiling — as deep as **~250 ft** | variable — sometimes **130 lb fluorocarbon**, sometimes straight to the **50 or 60 lb mainline**, sometimes **80 lb fluorocarbon**; check with the crew day-of | still a treble hook — the largest jig paired with a treble; heavier jigs move to single hooks |
| **~280 g** | Good down to about **300 ft**, depending on current and wind; beyond that depth, or in wind, may not reach the fish | **200 lb fluorocarbon**, ~2.5–3 ft, to a **~400 lb test swivel** | heavy single hooks, one off the nose and one off the tail (captioned "70 5x"; read as **7/0, 5X-strong**; exact number `asr-uncertain`) |
| **~300 g** | Same depth range as the 280 g tier | **200 lb fluorocarbon**, ~3 ft | two single hooks, one front and one back — same nose/tail pairing as the 280 g tier |
| **Night deep-drop rig** ("Eddie bomb" — see below) | Night, fish down to **~400 ft** | heavy fluorocarbon, ~2.5–3 ft | one big treble off the bottom, **or** two assist hooks off the top (angler's choice) |
| **~400 g** | Personal heaviest jig fished in this ladder — good to about **500 ft** | not stated | **two hooks on the tail end only** (not nose+tail) |
| **~220 g** | Mid-depth **speed jigging / fast-pitch jigging** (mechanical cranking retrieve) | not stated | **two hooks off the nose end** — usually the best placement for this retrieve style |

Treat this ladder as a starting point, not a fixed lookup — check with the
crew, since exactly which jig and depth works can change every hour
(`vVOkxHx58Eg`).

## Daytime — ~100 g, the Williamson Gomame

The 100 g daytime jig is the **Williamson Gomame** — a named product pick
that stays `low` confidence regardless of repetition, per the channel's
sponsored-claim caveat (`vVOkxHx58Eg`) — the same named-favorite jig, weight,
and daytime-prospecting framing already logged in [iron jigs — dart-type
jigs, "cold snipers," and a named
favorite](../lures/iron-jigs.md), [Searcher 40 lb all-around tuna
outfit](searcher-40lb-all-around-tuna-outfit.md), and [Searcher daytime
dart-jig outfit ladder](searcher-daytime-dart-jig-outfit-ladder.md). Bluefin
— even big ones — eat comparatively little food and can see the lure well in
daylight, so daytime presentation calls for smaller, more careful gear than
the low-light and night tiers below (`vVOkxHx58Eg`).

Stock hooks on these jigs run undersized for Searcher-grade fish, which run
bigger than what these lure lines are built for "pretty much anywhere else in
the world" (`vVOkxHx58Eg`); swap to a heavy-duty **20-size treble** on the
100 g jig specifically. The same stock-hooks-too-light problem recurs (at a
different weight tier and hook strength — a 5X-strong treble on the
50–170 g dart-jig class) in [Searcher daytime dart-jig outfit ladder —
hooks](searcher-daytime-dart-jig-outfit-ladder.md#hooks).

## Dawn/dusk — ~170 g

As light drops (dawn, dusk), or fish sit deeper than the 100 g jig's ~200 ft
ceiling — as deep as ~250 ft in this window — step up to a 170 g jig
(`vVOkxHx58Eg`). Leader is situational, not fixed: sometimes 130 lb
fluorocarbon to guard against bite-offs, sometimes tied straight to 50 or
60 lb mainline, sometimes 80 lb fluorocarbon — check with the crew the day
you go out rather than pick a leader test in advance (`vVOkxHx58Eg`).

This is the largest jig paired with a treble hook; above this weight tier,
hooks move to singles.

**Safety rule:** never add extra hooks to a lure already rigged with a
treble — a real safety issue for the angler and the crew (`vVOkxHx58Eg`).
Treble-hook lures stay single-treble; the nose/tail double-single-hook rigs
below are a different lure class, not a trebled lure with hooks added.

## ~280 g and ~300 g — nose-and-tail single hooks

Stepping up to roughly 280 g (good down to about 300 ft, depending on
current and wind — beyond that depth, or in wind, this size may not reach
the fish) and 300 g (same depth range), the pick is heavy single hooks, one
off the nose and one off the tail (`vVOkxHx58Eg`). Leader: 200 lb
fluorocarbon, roughly 2.5–3 ft; the 280 g rig specifically adds a ~400 lb
test swivel, which needs only about 2.5–3 ft of leader ahead of it.

Bluefin frequently bite the jig as it sinks rather than striking cleanly at
one end, so the fish may not get a good aim on it and can bite at either
end — the nose-and-tail hook pairing covers that ambiguity (`vVOkxHx58Eg`).
Trade-off: a hook on each end also lets a hooked fish snag the *other* hook
and come in sideways, which takes more pulling force to land.

The 280 g jig is captioned "Williamson kensaki" in this transcript
(`vVOkxHx58Eg`); the model name is not independently confirmed elsewhere in
this corpus and is carried as `asr-uncertain` rather than asserted as a real
Williamson model. Like the Gomame above, a named product pick from this
channel stays `low` confidence per the channel's sponsored-claim caveat,
independent of the name uncertainty.

The 300 g jigs often run glow zebra-stripe patterns, though glow's actual
contribution here is explicitly doubted rather than confirmed
(`vVOkxHx58Eg`) — an attributed doubt, not settled doctrine. This sits
beside, not merged into, the broader fleet-wide glow-vs-doesn't-matter split
in [knife jigs —
color](../lures/knife-jigs.md#color--mostly-angler-confidence-with-one-real-mechanism),
where multiple registered/attributed voices take both sides.

## Night deep-drop rig — the "Eddie bomb"

For fish down to ~400 ft at night, the pick is a weighted jig/sinker hybrid
captioned "Eddie bomb" in this transcript — the name is not independently
confirmed elsewhere in this corpus and is flagged `asr-uncertain`; carried
here by function rather than as a verified product name. As with the other
named picks in this note, treat any product/model identification here as
`low` confidence per the channel's sponsored-claim caveat, independent of the
name uncertainty. Shaped and sized like a 12–16 oz torpedo/bank-style sinker
(`vVOkxHx58Eg`).

**Buying caution:** some sinkers sold in tackle stores as jig bodies have
only a small loop of wire molded into the front and back — a big tuna pulls
the wire straight out. Buy one wired all the way through the body
(`vVOkxHx58Eg`).

**Rigging:** one big treble hook off the bottom, or two assist hooks off the
top end — angler's choice. Leader either way: roughly 2.5–3 ft of heavy
fluorocarbon, heavy enough that the fish don't react to it, since a night
bite reacts to profile, not leader visibility (`vVOkxHx58Eg`).

**Mechanism:** the rig sinks straight down, so it reaches a called depth
quickly — useful when tuna are marked as deep as 400 ft, even at night, when
bluefin can still see the lure despite the dark. Its smaller profile also
matches the smaller bait size tuna key on at night (`vVOkxHx58Eg`). Searcher
captain Mike is named as a fan of this rig (`vVOkxHx58Eg`).

This depth range and captain's-call framing matches, without adding a name
to it, the 300–400 g "when the captain says I'm reading big ones down at
400 feet, you better have a jig that can get down there" doctrine in
[Searcher big-tuna rig ladder —
Rig 2](searcher-big-tuna-rig-ladder.md#rig-2--extra-heavy-rig-100-lb-penn-international-16visx).

## ~400 g — personal ceiling, tail-only double hook

400 g is the heaviest jig fished personally in this ladder — beyond that,
"I'm going to be resting" (`vVOkxHx58Eg`), though 500 g jigs "work really
well" for anglers willing to fish them. The 400 g jig reaches roughly
500 ft; no depth ceiling is given for 500 g jigs beyond noting they work at
the added depth.

Hook style flips here: two hooks on the tail end only, not nose-and-tail.
This deep and this big, fish tend to suck the jig all the way in rather than
strike cleanly at one end, so a nose-and-tail double-hook setup risks
hooking a big, deep fish in two places at once, which makes it much harder
to pull in (`vVOkxHx58Eg`). Flagged as a personal call, not a fixed rule — it
may not hold across other slow-pitch/speed-jigging styles.

## Speed/fast-pitch jigging — nose-paired hooks

Speed jigging (also called fast-pitch jigging) uses a mechanical, cranking
retrieve rather than a drop-and-wait presentation. For mid-depth speed
jigging, a 220 g jig is sized right, rigged with two hooks off the nose
end — usually the best placement for a cranking retrieve (`vVOkxHx58Eg`). A
third, situational hook-placement rule alongside the nose-and-tail rig
(280–300 g) and the tail-only rig (400 g) above: placement here is driven by
retrieve style, not by depth or bite behavior. Mechanics of the
speed-jigging retrieve are in [knife / speed
jigging](../techniques/knife-jigging.md); general hook-count/placement
conflicts across sources are tracked in [knife jigs — assist-hook
rigging](../lures/knife-jigs.md#the-class--models).
