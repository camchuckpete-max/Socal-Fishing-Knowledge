# Tackle Express — Cedros Four-Rod Quiver (the Gear Vault)

The four-rod quiver an angler brought and used fully on a Cedros Island, Baja
Pacific (`baja-pacific-north`) trip ("everything I brought, I used")
(`_C7TJoGT0nw`), fished for a lighter-grade, 19–33 lb Cedros yellowtail bite
(see [Cedros Island](../locations/cedros-island.md#what-you-are-fishing-for)).
Setups 1 and 3 are covered in two shorter clips cut from this same
interview, indexed as standalone notes; this note covers setups 2 and 4, not
otherwise captured elsewhere in this KB, and indexes all four together as the
trip's quiver.

## Setup 2 — medium Colt Sniper / structure rod

A **Phoenix rod, 9 ft, rated 20–50 lb**, paired with an **Okuma Andros**
reel — the narrow version, sized for a smaller hand ("small hands, so just
one thumb kind of covers the whole spool") (`_C7TJoGT0nw`) — a lever-drag
two-speed, not a casting reel. Spooled with **65 lb** line and a **60 lb
fluorocarbon** leader run **about 5 ft** (`_C7TJoGT0nw`).

Application: casting **Colt Snipers** (see [iron jigs — Cedros Colt Sniper
versatility](../lures/iron-jigs.md)) and some **Z-Man** soft plastics (see
[soft-plastic swimbaits — Cedros Z-Man Diesel
MinnowZ](../lures/soft-plastic-swimbaits.md)); a couple of **yo-yo jigs**
were also dropped on it with no issue (`_C7TJoGT0nw`).

The two-speed lets the angler drop a Colt Sniper through the bottom, hook a
bigger fish, shift into low gear to pull it clear of the rocks, then shift
back to high gear to keep fighting (`_C7TJoGT0nw`) — a shift-down-to-clear-
structure, shift-back-up-to-fight sequence distinct from, but consistent
with, the general two-speed-for-leverage doctrine in [two-speed low-gear
fight](../techniques/two-speed-low-gear-fight.md).

## Setup 4 — the favorite: a low-profile baitcaster

A **United Composite rod, 9 ft, model "9E"**, paired with a **Daiwa Lexa
500** reel (on-screen caption reads "Alexa 500"; resolved via cross-check —
detail in the evidence file). Specified as the **HL** version (left-handed
retrieve; the angler fishes all his baitcasters left-handed) at a **7:1**
gear ratio (`_C7TJoGT0nw`) — a deliberate middle ground: slower than an 8:1
to avoid getting "dogged" by a hard-pulling fish, while still fast enough to
speed up the retrieve when wanted, with more torque from the lower ratio
(`_C7TJoGT0nw`). Spooled with **80 lb Max Quattro** braid, size 65, roughly
**350 yd**, with **60 lb fluorocarbon** leader tied on before the trip
(`_C7TJoGT0nw`).

It was a recent purchase, made right before this trip, that outperformed
expectations on camera (`_C7TJoGT0nw`). It became the favorite of the four
setups because it casts far enough for surface irons and yo-yo jigs, landed
the trip's biggest fish, carries a
**level wind** ("you just have to turn the handle, you don't have to worry
about your line management") (`_C7TJoGT0nw`), and has an **external braking
system and spool tensioner** adjustable in the field — e.g. dialing down the
brake when switching from a light surface iron to a heavier **60 g Colt
Sniper** that started backlashing on the heavier lure (`_C7TJoGT0nw`). That
field-adjustable brake is the stated advantage over the trip's conventional
reels (setup 3's Torium/Trinidad): "you can't do that with a Torium or any of
those conventional reels" (`_C7TJoGT0nw`) — the brake/tensioner adjustment is
specific to the low-profile baitcaster design. It also produced no
backlashes across the trip ("even professionals will still backlash... one
out of 10 times. This reel here, no backlashes, nothing") and the longest
casts of anyone on the boat (`_C7TJoGT0nw`).

The same purchase is described again in a standalone short cut from this
interview (`y9YlqMQh3BI`), repeating the rod/reel pairing and confirming it
as a recent purchase that "did awesome" on the trip — the same footage
re-cut, not independent confirmation, so this stays at medium confidence.
