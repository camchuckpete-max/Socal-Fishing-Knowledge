## Outfit 1 — flat-fall / knife-jig rail-rod outfit

- **Rod:** a **Seeker rail rod**, captioned "OSP one pot one by three sixty"
  — read as the **Seeker OSP 1x3, rated 60–100 lb test** — with extended
  hypalon foregrip above the reel so that portion of the blank rests on the
  rail.
- **Reel:** a **Penn Fathom 60, two-speed.**
- **Line:** **100 lb spectra**, with two stated options for the working end:
  - **100 ft of monofilament top shot (80 or 100 lb)** — reasoning: mono
    gives stretch/give against a big bluefin's headshakes.
  - **Tie the flat-fall/knife jig directly to the end of the spectra, no top
    shot** — works, but spectra has no stretch, so "you're going to feel
    every tailbeat of that fish," a more punishing fight than fishing a mono
    top shot. Matches, and doesn't add beyond, the same mono-vs-straight-
    spectra tradeoff already logged in [flat-fall jigging — mono over
    fluorocarbon for the
    leader](../techniques/flat-fall-jigging.md#mono-over-fluorocarbon-for-the-leader-and-a-single-hook-swap-size-searchersportfishing-tackle-talk-live-fxgwoib0tpfe-2020-04-08)
    from a different Tackle Talk Live episode on the same channel, about a
    year earlier.
- **Make sure it's a two-speed reel** — stated as the one non-negotiable
  spec for this outfit.
- **Lure example:** a **300 g, glow-in-the-dark Shimano flat-fall**,
  pre-rigged from Fisherman's Landing on what looks like **200 lb leader**
  (asr/visual estimate, not stated as a measured spec) — the boat can also
  rig flat-falls for anglers on board. **Go heavier in wind/fast drift** —
  the stated reasoning is the same as this KB's existing flat-fall doctrine:
  a light jig in a fast drift never gets down to the fish, so match jig
  weight to how hard the boat is moving, not a fixed number. See [flat-fall
  jigging — jig weight and rig](../techniques/flat-fall-jigging.md#jig-weight-and-rig)
  for the fuller weight ladder already logged from other Searcher episodes.
- **Hooks:** not routinely swapped on this particular pre-rigged Shimano
  lure ("it's got the proper hooks"), but **check hook size on non-Shimano
  flat-falls specifically** — some ship with hooks a little too small, worth
  verifying before fishing.

### Tracking depth on a flat-fall drop — marked line as an alternative to colored depth line

Asked directly whether colored depth-marked line is worth it for flat-fall
fishing:

- **Colored line's advantage, stated plainly:** anything that increases your
  odds of hooking up is worth using — knowing you're fishing **at 180 ft**
  because the captain called that depth over the PA is more useful than
  guessing.
- **You don't need the commercial colored product to get the same benefit:**
  measure line out at home (by the 100 ft or 200 ft) and mark it with a
  **Sharpie** on plain white spectra — then count sharpie marks passing
  through the guides on the drop to read approximate line-out, the same way
  colored line is read.
- **The caveat that matters more than the marking method itself:** line-out
  footage is **not** the same as true depth once the boat is drifting — the
  jig trails out and away from a straight vertical drop as the boat moves,
  so "when you get to your count of 180 ft, that's not where you're at
  depth-wise." Read marked/colored line as a rough line-out count, not a
  depth gauge, and expect actual jig depth to run shallower than the
  footage marked out.

## Yellowtail on a kelp-paddy stop — three methods, one stop

When the boat is running the offshore zone and comes across a kelp paddy,
the stated plan is to stop and work it for yellowtail with up to three
rigged options ready:

- **Yo-yo iron:** a **Salas 6X (regular or Junior)** — **blue and white**
  named as "the universal color for fishing yellowtail... for decades now,"
  with **scrambled egg** as the second/backup color. **40–60 lb test**, a
  small two-speed reel with **80 lb braid**, cast out and let it sink.
  Consistent with, not new beyond, the blue-and-white/scrambled-egg
  traditional-color doctrine already extensively logged in [yo-yo
  iron](../techniques/yo-yo-iron.md).
  - **Stated reason to keep a yo-yo iron rigged even when fish are showing on
    top:** anglers fly-lining baits on **25–40 lb test** sometimes hook
    yellowtail while the fish visible on the surface run smaller — the
    inference given on camera is that **the better-grade fish can be sitting
    a little deeper** than the ones showing on top, so having the "right
    yo-yo lure in your arsenal" covers the bigger fish the surface bite
    alone would miss.
- **Surface iron:** for fish up and boiling, a **Tady 4/0** in **mint** —
  named as "really really popular and really really effective," **30 or
  40 lb test**, retrieved **slow.** A **Tady 45** in the same mint color, or
  in sardine color, is named as the other rigged option for this program.
  **Corroborates, from a further registered-channel source, the mint
  confidence-color reports already logged in [surface iron —
  colour](../techniques/surface-iron-color.md#data-points-kept-side-by-side)** —
  kept side by side there as a further data point, not folded into the
  "colour is not the variable" doctrine that note leads with.
- **Flyline:** a **25–40 lb outfit**, fluorocarbon leader, a **2/0 J hook**,
  flylining a live sardine while drifting on the paddy.

## Casting Colt Sniper into spooled/feeding bluefin

Distinct from the boiling-on-top yellowtail program above — casting a Colt
Sniper (captioned "cold sniper" throughout) into a school of bluefin
actively feeding/spooling on the surface:

- **Sizes named:** **120 g** (both a pre-rigged-with-fluorocarbon example and
  an unrigged one shown on camera, called "a little on the bigger side" for
  casting into feeding schools), plus **60 g, 80 g,** and "maybe 110 g" as
  smaller options that also work.
- **Line/rod:** roughly **50–60 lb mono** on the cast/running line, an
  **8.5–9 ft rod**, worked from up on the bow/rail as the boat pulls up on
  the school.
- **Mechanics:** cast to try to land it in the middle of what the fish are
  feeding on, then start a **quick retrieve.**
- **Leader:** a short, roughly **3 ft piece of fluorocarbon** recommended,
  matched to the mono running line — stated reason: fish may grab the lure
  up toward the front rather than only at the back where the hooks sit, so
  a little fluoro up top helps rather than only protecting right at the
  hook.
- **They also get bit on the sink** — not just the retrieve. Stated
  alongside the observation that anglers dropping flat-falls straight down
  get bit "on the sink" too, i.e. the same fall-triggered bite this KB's
  flat-fall doctrine already documents. Two workable presentations named as
  "both pretty effective": **skip it across the surface**, or give it a
  **five-second sink** after the cast before starting the retrieve.
- **Hook swap — single outperforms treble, a stated preference (new data
  point, not previously logged in this KB for Colt Sniper):** when swapping
  hooks on a Colt Sniper, the stated practice is to pull the factory treble
  and replace it with a **single hook** rather than another treble — "seems
  to be a little more effective." Framed on camera as new information even
  to one of the co-presenters in the moment, i.e. not universally known crew
  doctrine going into this session.
- **Split ring:** the factory split ring is generally fine to keep even when
  upgrading to a bigger/heavier hook; most of the time only the hook gets
  swapped, and the split ring is upgraded too only if it looks too small
  for the new hook. Spare hooks and split rings are carried aboard for
  anglers who don't have their own.
- **Hook gauge for bigger fish:** the factory treble on a Colt Sniper can run
  a little thin-gauge — stated as a specific problem from the prior
  (2020) season's bluefin, where fish were getting bit on Colt Snipers on
  the sink but the thin factory hook was a weak point; upgrading hook gauge
  is recommended when fishing around larger fish.

## Leaders by lure/technique — this session's summary

Asked directly about splicing a fluorocarbon leader onto lures generally:

- **Flat-fall / knife jig:** typically just **mono**, not heavy
  fluorocarbon — fluoro "certainly applies" if an angler wants to invest in
  it, but it's **not necessary**; stated explicitly as not a line-class or
  visibility issue, since most of this boat's flat-fall fishing happens at
  night. Matches, and doesn't add beyond, the existing mono-over-fluoro
  flat-fall doctrine in [flat-fall jigging](../techniques/flat-fall-jigging.md#mono-over-fluorocarbon-for-the-leader-and-a-single-hook-swap-size-searchersportfishing-tackle-talk-live-fxgwoib0tpfe-2020-04-08).
- **Colt Sniper:** a fluorocarbon leader is called advantageous — see the
  ~3 ft spec above.
- **Yo-yo iron:** **no leader necessary** — tie the jig straight to the end
  of the top shot.
- **Surface iron:** **no leader necessary** — tie the lure straight to the
  end of the mono.
- **Straight braid to a jig/iron, personal preference against it:** one
  presenter states he personally never ties straight spectra-to-lure with no
  top shot at all, because he wants at least a little stretch; when other
  anglers do tie the leader (or jig) directly to spectra, "that's fine," but
  he prefers a mono top shot ahead of the connection either way.

## Knots used this session

- **Surgeon's knot — mono-to-fluorocarbon leader connection.** Overlap the
  two lines with plenty of tag to work with, form a circle, pass both tag
  ends through the circle **three times**, cinch wet, trim tags to about
  **1/4 in.** Matches the 3-turn surgeon's-knot family already logged in
  [essential knots](../rigging/essential-knots.md#parameters--judgment).
  **Leader-length reasoning, a parameter not previously logged in that
  note:** keep the finished fluorocarbon leader to roughly **2.5–3 ft**
  (a little longer on a longer rod) — the stated reason is that the bulky
  surgeon's-knot connection shouldn't have to pass through the rod's **top
  guide** on the cast, since the tip guide runs smaller than the rest of the
  guide train; too long a leader forces that bulky knot through the tip
  guide on every cast. A longer leader is also useful as a buffer against
  needing to re-tie a fresh fluorocarbon section every time the line gets
  chafed down near the hook.
- **San Diego jam knot — for tying hooks, jigs, and lures onto the leader.**
  Through the eye, back to form a loop held with the fingers, wrap the tag
  around both lines **six or seven times**, keeping it cinched tight
  throughout the wrap, pass back through the loop nearest the hook and then
  the finger loop, add tension rings if available, and pull tight, leaving
  a small tag in case the knot
  needs to cinch down further under load. Called "probably the strongest
  knot out there" and recommended for **every** lure/hook connection this
  session covers — yo-yo iron, surface iron, Colt Sniper, and flat-fall/knife
  jig alike, plus larger hooks generally. **Earliest-dated Searcher-channel
  demonstration of this specific 6–7-wrap count found in this KB so far**
  (this video predates the previously-earliest 7-wrap citation, Captain
  Armando's `YeV--HarEYQ`, 2021-10-21, by about seven months) — likely the
  same Captain Armando, since he is one of the two named guest captains on
  this session, though the knot-tying portion of this transcript is not
  individually attributed to him by name on camera. See [San Diego jam
  knot](../rigging/san-diego-jam-knot.md#parameters--judgment) for the full
  wrap-count range already logged from other episodes.
