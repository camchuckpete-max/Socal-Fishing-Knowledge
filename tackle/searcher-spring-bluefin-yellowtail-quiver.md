# Searcher Spring Bluefin/Yellowtail Quiver — 2.5- and 3-Day Trips (Team Searcher, Tackle Talk Live, 2021-03-26)

Tackle for *Searcher*'s 2.5-day limited-load trips (May–June) and 3-day
Memorial Day–weekend trips, starting 2021-04-30, targeting bluefin tuna (the
stock still around from a strong April–May 2020 season) and yellowtail on
kelp paddies (`lf3S28nh-kk`). Four rigged outfits — kite, flat-fall/knife-jig,
casting/flyline/yo-yo, and live-bait/surface — cover the trip, stepped up to a
heavier lineup for a 3-day July run. A companion, differently-scoped citation
to this KB's other Searcher outfit-quiver notes: this one is built around a
specific spring bluefin/yellowtail trip window rather than a general 1.5–4 day
minimum ([three-outfit quiver](searcher-three-outfit-minimum-quiver.md)) or a
Guadalupe Island season ([four-outfit quiver](searcher-four-outfit-guadalupe-quiver.md)).

  1x3 rail rod** (`lf3S28nh-kk`) — captioned "QSL 3x1," read against the
  Seeker OSP 1x3 already logged elsewhere in this KB (e.g. [Searcher rail-rod

  and some captioned "Phantom 60s" — aren't confidently resolved to a
  specific rod or reel product line (`lf3S28nh-kk`) ⚠ asr-uncertain.

  for these trips (`lf3S28nh-kk`).
- Bringing a personal kite outfit: use the biggest rod-and-reel combo
  available. The boat's own is spooled with **130 lb braid**; a personal
  outfit's top shot should run **100 lb minimum**, on a big, heavy rail rod
  (`lf3S28nh-kk`).
- Kite leaders are sold aboard or brought by the angler; no leader-
  construction parameters are given in this session — see [flying fish
  harness](../rigging/flying-fish-harness.md) for the harness build. Bait for
  the kite program is flying fish carried aboard (`lf3S28nh-kk`).
- One-on-one kite instruction happens on the water rather than in this
  session (`lf3S28nh-kk`).

See [kite fishing](../techniques/kite-fishing.md) for execution and the other

seminar) — this session's Accurate twin-drag/Seeker rail-rod pairing and
130 lb braid/100 lb-minimum top shot spec (`lf3S28nh-kk`) is a further,
independent data point.

## Outfit 1 — flat-fall / knife-jig rail-rod outfit

- **Rod:** a **Seeker OSP 1x3 rail rod, rated 60–100 lb test** (`lf3S28nh-kk`)
  — captioned "OSP one pot one by three sixty" ⚠ asr-uncertain — with
  extended hypalon foregrip above the reel so that portion of the blank rests
  on the rail.
- **Reel:** a **Penn Fathom 60, two-speed.** Fishing a two-speed reel is the
  one non-negotiable spec for this outfit (`lf3S28nh-kk`).
- **Line:** **100 lb spectra**, with two options for the working end
  (`lf3S28nh-kk`):
  - **100 ft of monofilament top shot (80 or 100 lb)** — mono's stretch/give
    absorbs a big bluefin's headshakes.
  - **Tie the flat-fall/knife jig directly to the spectra, no top shot** —
    works, but spectra has no stretch, so the angler feels every tailbeat, a
    more punishing fight than a mono top shot. Matches the mono-vs-straight-
    spectra tradeoff in [flat-fall jigging — leader and terminal
    rigging](../techniques/flat-fall-jigging.md#leader-and-terminal-rigging),
    logged there from a different Tackle Talk Live episode on the same
    channel about a year earlier.
- **Lure example:** a **300 g, glow-in-the-dark Shimano flat-fall**,
  pre-rigged from Fisherman's Landing on roughly **200 lb leader** (a visual
  estimate, not a measured spec) — the boat also rigs flat-falls for anglers
  aboard (`lf3S28nh-kk`). **Go heavier in wind/fast drift**: a light jig in a
  fast drift never gets down to the fish, so jig weight is matched to how
  hard the boat is moving, not a fixed number — see [flat-fall jigging — jig
  weight and rig](../techniques/flat-fall-jigging.md#jig-weight-and-rig) for
  the fuller weight ladder logged from other Searcher episodes
  (`lf3S28nh-kk`).
- **Hooks:** not routinely swapped on this pre-rigged Shimano lure ("it's got
  the proper hooks"), but check hook size on non-Shimano flat-falls
  specifically — some ship with hooks a little too small (`lf3S28nh-kk`).

### Tracking a flat-fall's drop depth

Asked whether colored depth-marked line is worth it: knowing the jig is at a
captain-called depth (example: 180 ft) beats guessing, and a plain-spectra
substitute gets the same benefit — measure line out at home in 100 or 200 ft
increments, mark it with a Sharpie, and read the marks through the guides on
the drop the way colored line is read. The caveat: line-out footage isn't
true depth once the boat is drifting, since the jig trails out and away from
a straight vertical drop as it moves — a 180 ft mark does not mean the jig is
actually 180 ft down (`lf3S28nh-kk`). Full mechanism and the line-scope
correction are in [flat-fall jigging — tracking depth on the
drop](../techniques/flat-fall-jigging.md#tracking-depth-on-the-drop).

## Same quiver, extended to a 3-day trip (July)

Asked whether the same setup covers a 3-day July trip (`lf3S28nh-kk`):

- **Flat-fall:** a rail rod, **80 lb test**, two-speed (`lf3S28nh-kk`).
- **Sinker rig or yo-yo iron:** **50–60 lb.**
- **Live bait, two separate setups** rather than one:

## Yellowtail on a kelp-paddy stop — three methods, one stop

When the boat runs the offshore zone and comes across a kelp paddy, the
stated plan is to stop and work it for yellowtail with up to three rigged
options ready (`lf3S28nh-kk`):

- **Yo-yo iron:** a **Salas 6X (regular or Junior)** — **blue and white**
  named as "the universal color for fishing yellowtail... for decades now"
  (`lf3S28nh-kk`), with **scrambled egg** as the second/backup color;
  **40–60 lb test**, a small two-speed reel with **80 lb braid**, cast out
  and let it sink.
  Consistent with the blue-and-white/scrambled-egg traditional-color doctrine
  already logged in [yo-yo iron](../techniques/yo-yo-iron.md). Anglers
  flylining baits on **25–40 lb test** sometimes hook yellowtail while the
  fish showing on the surface run smaller; the better-grade fish can sit a
  little deeper than the ones on top, so a rigged yo-yo covers the bigger
  fish the surface bite alone misses.
- **Surface iron:** for fish up and boiling, a **Tady 4/0** in **mint** —
  "really really popular and really really effective," **30 or 40 lb test**,
  retrieved **slow.** A **Tady 45** in the same mint color, or in sardine
  color, is the other rigged option. This corroborates the mint
  confidence-color reports already logged in [surface iron —
  colour](../techniques/surface-iron-color.md), kept there as a further data
  point rather than folded into that note's "colour is not the variable"
  doctrine.
- **Flyline:** a **25–40 lb outfit**, fluorocarbon leader, a **2/0 J hook**,
  flylining a live sardine while drifting on the paddy.

## Casting Colt Sniper into spooled/feeding bluefin

Distinct from the boiling-on-top yellowtail program above — casting a Colt
Sniper (captioned "cold sniper" throughout) into a school of bluefin actively
feeding/spooling on the surface (`lf3S28nh-kk`):

- **Sizes:** **120 g** is on the bigger side for casting into feeding
  schools; **60 g, 80 g,** and roughly **110 g** also work as smaller
  options (`lf3S28nh-kk`).
- **Line/rod:** roughly **50–60 lb mono** on the cast/running line, an
  **8.5–9 ft rod**, worked from the bow/rail as the boat pulls up on the
  school.
- **Mechanics:** cast into the middle of the feeding fish, then start a quick
  retrieve; a short, roughly **3 ft** fluorocarbon leader matched to the mono
  running line is recommended, since fish sometimes grab the lure up toward
  the front rather than only at the hook end.
- Bites also come **on the sink**, not just the retrieve — the same
  fall-triggered bite this KB's flat-fall doctrine documents. Two workable
  presentations: skip it across the surface, or give it a **five-second
  sink** before starting the retrieve.
- **Hook swap:** the stated practice is to pull the factory treble and
  replace it with a **single hook** rather than another treble — "seems to
  be a little more effective," framed on camera as new information even to
  one of the co-presenters. The factory split ring is usually fine to keep;
  it's upgraded too only when it looks too small for the new hook. Spare
  hooks and split rings are carried aboard for anglers without their own.
- **Hook gauge for bigger fish:** the factory treble can run thin-gauge — a
  specific problem in the 2020 season, when fish got bit on Colt Snipers on
  the sink but the thin factory hook was a weak point; upgrade hook gauge
  when fishing around larger fish.

Full mechanism detail (bite behavior, hook-swap rationale) is in [foamer
casting — Colt Sniper](../techniques/foamer-casting.md#colt-sniper--sizing-sink-bite-and-hook-swap).

## Leaders by lure/technique — this session's summary

Asked directly about splicing a fluorocarbon leader onto lures generally
(`lf3S28nh-kk`):

- **Flat-fall / knife jig:** typically just **mono**, not heavy fluorocarbon
  — fluoro "certainly applies" for an angler who wants to invest in it
  (`lf3S28nh-kk`), but it's not necessary; not a line-class or visibility
  issue, since most of this boat's flat-fall fishing happens at night.
  Matches the mono-over-fluoro flat-fall doctrine in [flat-fall jigging —
  leader and terminal
  rigging](../techniques/flat-fall-jigging.md#leader-and-terminal-rigging).
- **Colt Sniper:** a fluorocarbon leader is advantageous — see the ~3 ft
  spec above.
- **Yo-yo iron:** no leader necessary — tie the jig straight to the top
  shot.
- **Surface iron:** no leader necessary — tie the lure straight to the mono.
- **Straight braid to a jig/iron:** one presenter personally never ties
  straight spectra-to-lure with no top shot at all, wanting at least a
  little stretch; other anglers tying the leader (or jig) directly to
  spectra is "fine," but he prefers a mono top shot ahead of the connection
  either way.

## Knots used this session

- **Surgeon's knot — mono-to-fluorocarbon leader connection.** Overlap the
  two lines with plenty of tag, form a circle, pass both tag ends through
  the circle **three times**, cinch wet, trim tags to about **1/4 in**
  (`lf3S28nh-kk`) — matches the 3-turn surgeon's-knot family in [essential
  knots — parameters](../rigging/essential-knots.md#parameters). Keep the
  finished fluorocarbon leader to roughly **2.5–3 ft** (a little longer on a
  longer rod): the bulky surgeon's-knot connection shouldn't have to pass
  through the rod's smaller top guide on the cast, and a longer leader
  buffers against re-tying a fresh fluorocarbon section every time the line
  chafes near the hook — full parameter detail in [surgeon's knot — mono to
  fluoro](../rigging/surgeons-knot-mono-to-fluoro.md#parameters)
  (`lf3S28nh-kk`).
- **San Diego jam knot — for hooks, jigs, and lures.** Through the eye, back
  to form a loop held with the fingers, wrap the tag around both lines **six
  or seven times**, keeping it cinched tight throughout, pass back through
  the loop nearest the hook and then the finger loop, add tension rings if
  available, and pull tight, leaving a small tag in case the knot needs to
  cinch down further under load (`lf3S28nh-kk`). Called "probably the
  strongest knot out there" and recommended for every lure/hook connection
  this session covers — yo-yo iron, surface iron, Colt Sniper, and
  flat-fall/knife jig alike, plus larger hooks generally. This is the
  earliest-dated Searcher-channel demonstration of this 6–7-wrap count found
  in this KB so far, predating the previously-earliest 7-wrap citation
  (`YeV--HarEYQ`, 2021-10-21) by about seven months — see [San Diego jam knot
  — parameters](../rigging/san-diego-jam-knot.md#parameters) for the full
  wrap-count range logged from other episodes.
