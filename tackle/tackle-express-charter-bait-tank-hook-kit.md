# Charter Bait-Tank Hook & Leader Kit — Whatever's in the Tank (Tackle Express)

Tackle Express (`3xx_vES0kdo`, "What Hooks To Bring On A SoCal Charter (Bait
Tank Guide)," uploaded 2026-07-22; unregistered channel, single mention —
medium confidence). Framing: on a SoCal charter you don't find out what's in
the bait tank — sardine, anchovy, squid, or mackerel — until you're already on
the boat, so the fix is carrying an assortment sized to cover whichever bait
shows up, across island yellowtail/calico, kelp-paddy dorado/yellowtail,
offshore bluefin/yellowfin, and bottom fishing. This is a parameter checklist,
not a selection framework — the underlying circle-vs-J and bait-sets-the-size
doctrine lives in [hooks](hooks.md) and isn't repeated here.

## Anchovy — small fly-liner hooks

- **Owner, size 4**, stepping up to **size 2** for larger anchovies.
- Also names a **size 4** hook the captions render **"OSHAY must add"** as "a
  great anchovy hook" for flylining — **asr-uncertain**: the model name isn't
  confidently resolved and isn't carried forward as a brand claim; recorded
  only as a second, distinct size-4 anchovy hook alongside the plain Owner
  pick above.

## Sardine — ringed fly-liner

- **Size 2 ring fly-liner**, hooked in the **shoulder** or the **butt** to
  swim the bait away from the boat and draw bites — the same
  away-from-the-boat mechanism already logged in
  [fishing live bait](../bait/fishing-live-bait.md) for shoulder/butt hook
  placement, not repeated here. Good for a kelp paddy (dorado, yellowtail) or
  up on the islands for yellowtail, calico ("calos"), and whatever else is
  around. No brand is stated in this instance; a same-channel prior citation
  in [hooks — Owner Ringed Flyliner](hooks.md#owner-ringed-flyliner--downsizing-as-the-coronado-islands-bite-improved-searchersportfishing-tackle-tip-thursday-vol-23-f9xjgmevvag-2018-11-20-unregistered-channel--medium-confidence)
  names that product at the same size-2 mark — kept as a separate data point
  rather than merged, since this source doesn't itself name the brand.

## Tuna (bluefin, yellowfin) — flyline

- A **ringed Mutu-style circle hook** ("ring mu" in the captions), **size 4**
  or **size 2**, fished "the last few seasons" as his go-to for flylining
  bluefin/yellowfin on the surface — consistent with, not new beyond, the
  extensive Owner Mutu circle-hook doctrine already logged in
  [hooks — size](hooks.md#size--bait-first-fish-second).
- Paired with **75 lb Blue Label fluorocarbon** — noticeably heavier than the
  25 lb flyline leader given as the general standard below; preserved as
  stated rather than reconciled to one number. ("Blue Label" is Seaguar's
  product line; this channel names it in full — "Seaguar Blue Label" — in a
  separate video, see
  [Night Bluefin TG-Jig Rig — the "Jerry Rig" leader](tackle-express-night-bluefin-tg-jig-rig.md#leader--the-jerry-rig).)

## Squid — dropper loop and slider

- **Owner**, a hook the captions render **"oy twist"** — **asr-uncertain**,
  exact model not resolved — sized **5** and **7** to cover small and big
  squid respectively.
- **Weight for dropper loop:** a **10 oz or 12 oz torpedo sinker**, a couple
  in the box, to get squid down on a [dropper loop](../techniques/dropper-loop.md)
  or a slider rig.
- **Sliders: 1/8 oz to 1/2 oz**, sized to current, for flylining squid as well
  as sardine and mackerel — gets the bait deeper and off the surface,
  particularly when birds are picking at baits fished up top. See
  [sliding sinker](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md) for the general technique
  this weight range serves.

## Sea bass — squid vs. fin-bait backup (9MzNNwHiBXs)

A separate, short Tackle Express clip (`9MzNNwHiBXs`, "Hook Mistakes That Cost
You Fish! Squid vs. Finbait Tips," uploaded 2025-06-12; unregistered channel,
single mention — medium confidence). Framing: [white seabass](../species/white-seabass.md)
(the source's own term is "sea bass") eat squid, but also eat **fin bait —
sardines as well as mackerel**. On a charter boat, squid isn't always
available — if **weather** plays an issue, or squid **simply aren't around**,
the next resort is fin sardines and mackerel, so carrying proper **J hooks**
sized for fin bait is essential, not an afterthought.

- **Owner Flyliner hooks**, sizes the captions render as **"a 10 and 2"** —
  read here as **1/0 and 2**, though not confidently resolved
  (**asr-uncertain**) — work well for **sardines and mackerel between 5 and
  about 6–7 in**.
- **Bait-sets-the-size still applies:** bait bigger or smaller than that range
  calls for a different hook size — the same doctrine already logged in
  [hooks — size](hooks.md#size--bait-first-fish-second), not repeated here.
- Also fine on a [dropper loop](../techniques/dropper-loop.md) — the source
  cuts off mid-sentence ("these will work just fine on the dropper loop
  as—") but the point stands: the same hooks serve both a flylined and a
  dropper-loop presentation.

## Bottom fishing (rockfish, whitefish, sheephead)

- **Size 1 and size 3/0 "gorilla lights"** — a small J hook carried for
  bottom/ground fishing to "fill the sacks." Corroborates the **Owner Gorilla
  Light** product name already logged in
  [hooks — a finesse-rig hook ladder by grade](hooks.md#a-finesse-rig-hook-ladder-by-grade-steve-carson-penn-san-diego-rod-and-reel-club-9jnis8hkzla-2023-11-14-unregistered-channelspeaker-single-mention--medium-confidence)
  (there sized 2 and 4 for a 15 lb finesse bluefin rig); this source gives a
  different application (bottom fishing) and different sizes (1, 3/0) — kept
  as a separate data point, not merged into one fixed size.

## Leader

- All the fly-liner hooks above are fished on **fluorocarbon**.
- **25 lb Blue Label** is named as the standard flyline leader — "hands down
  has been the key" — whether fishing tuna or up on the islands.
- Also carry **heavier fluoro, 40 and 50 lb**, for when fish are eating the
  heavier line: the stated reasoning is to **feed line to a fish eating
  heavy**, improving hookup and landing odds over forcing everything onto the
  lighter 25 lb standard.
