# Searcher Lever-Drag Reel Sizing — Penn Fathom 30 vs. 60

Two Penn Fathom two-speed lever-drag sizes anchor the Searcher's own boat
loaner-gear ladder: a Fathom 30 for general duty and a Fathom 60 paired with
Seeker rail rods for bigger fish. The reel models are sponsor picks and stay
low confidence; the general size-to-application pairing logic is the
transferable part. Split into its own note rather than folded into [rod &
reel selection](rod-and-reel-selection.md) (already over this repo's size
guideline) or [gear classes](gear-classes.md#rail-rod-class--what-makes-a-rod-a-rail-rod-mechanism)
— linked from both. A separate, unrelated bluefin-outfit line-capacity
worked example for the 30-size/50-wide reel class closes out the note.

## Penn Fathom 30 — general loaner duty

The Fathom 30 is the smaller of the Searcher's two loaner-gear picks — the
reasonably priced, smaller two-speed reel of the pair (captions render this
"the reasonable priced smaller two feet reel"; read as "two-speed reel"
since the segment compares two-speed reels throughout, flagged
`asr-uncertain`) (`ddataaVWoDc`). Spool it with 65 lb spectra/braid backing
to a 20–40 lb monofilament top shot (captions give an ambiguous range —
"anywhere from 25 to 20 to 40 pound monofilament" — read as a 20–40 lb
window; the extra "25" is unresolved, flagged `asr-uncertain`)
(`ddataaVWoDc`). It's cited for line capacity and drag system; no specific
technique or species is named for this size beyond general boat
loaner-gear duty.

## Penn Fathom 60 — rail-rod duty for bigger fish

The Fathom 60 pairs with Seeker rail rods aboard the Searcher for bigger
fish (`ddataaVWoDc`). Line: 80 lb braid to up to 100 lb monofilament; a
separate on-camera figure — "probably 60 to 200 pound" — has an unresolved
referent (line strength vs. target-fish grade) and is left as stated,
flagged `asr-uncertain`. It's used for larger tuna and, separately, for a
[dropper loop](../techniques/dropper-loop.md) rig fishing yellowtail on the
bottom (`ddataaVWoDc`); the place named on camera for that application is
captioned "the loop the island" and is left unresolved rather than
inferred.

This pairing corroborates the Fathom 60/rail-rod pairing in [gear classes —
rail-rod class](gear-classes.md#rail-rod-class--what-makes-a-rod-a-rail-rod-mechanism)
— a different application (flat-fall bluefin there, general rail/troll duty
plus bottom dropper-loop yellowtail here). An earlier Searcher-channel clip
separately recommends a Fathom 60 for anglers on an ultra-limited-load trip
in fall 2019, cited for line capacity and landing big tuna at Guadalupe
Island (`gqEjWrPpa48`); it plausibly names the same place this note's own
"the loop the island" caption garbles, though that is not direct
confirmation.

## Durability, both sizes

Both sizes are cited as durable and run hard as loaner gear, "put to the
test" by heavy use and weather exposure over a season (`ddataaVWoDc`). A
second, "both"-paired use case in the same line is unresolved from garbled
captions and not asserted, flagged `asr-uncertain`.

## Capacity worked examples, 30/50-wide class

Two worked bluefin-outfit line-capacity figures for reels in the
30-size/50-wide class, from an audience Q&A, kept as attributed examples
rather than a fixed rule (`eehDVb6_GoI`):

- A 30-size or 50-wide reel: at least 400 yd of braid, with the rest of the
  spool filled with a monofilament top shot (`eehDVb6_GoI`).
- A 50-wide spooled with 135 lb braid specifically: roughly 750 yd to fill
  the reel (`eehDVb6_GoI`) — a single-material capacity figure (no top
  shot) for a different spooling choice on the same reel class, not
  reconciled into the figure above.
