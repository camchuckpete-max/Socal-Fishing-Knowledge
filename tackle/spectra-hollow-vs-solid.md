# Hollow vs Solid Spectra — Construction, Rigging, and When Each Earns Its Place

Hollow-core and solid (low-carrier) spectra differ on two independent axes:
construction and rigging (below), and surface texture in kelp (further
below). Below roughly 135 lb, spectra is made solid only — hollow starts at
135 lb and up (`jQzOdmP0zoQ`). Above that floor, the choice turns on
maintenance style — splice hollow at leisure vs. tie a fast knot on solid at
the rail — and how much a skinnier diameter's bait action matters for the
presentation; no source calls one categorically better (`46wHgdTJWIM`), and
two sources lean opposite ways on it, detailed below. This note is the
rigging/construction detail behind [line & leader](line-and-leader.md)'s
braid-construction summary.

## Line-class pairing and the hollow manufacturing floor

Solid spectra is most commonly run in the lighter line classes — 50, 60, 80
lb, sometimes heavier; hollow is more commonly run in larger classes, up to
200 lb. This is a common pairing, not a hard rule (`46wHgdTJWIM`).

Hollow braid is manufactured starting at **135 lb and up**; anything rated
below 135 lb is solid (`jQzOdmP0zoQ`) — stated as a manufacturing fact rather
than the softer common-pairing framing above, but it is this speaker's stated
breakpoint, not cross-confirmed elsewhere in this KB.

## Hollow-core — rigging and spliceability

- **Small connections:** the hollow core lets running line thread down inside
  it, keeping a connection at the terminal end compact. See [hollow-core
  splice & serving](../rigging/hollow-splice-and-serving.md) for the
  splicing/serving procedure this enables.
- **Spliceable — recovery use:** two hollow spectra lines can always be
  spliced together. If a reel spooled with hollow spectra tangles on deck, or
  develops a problem partway down the spool, cut the line at that point,
  splice in fresh hollow spectra, and refill the reel instead of stripping and
  re-spooling the whole thing.
- **Spliceable — proactive maintenance, not just recovery:** stripping the top
  100 yards of hollow spectra off the reel every couple of years and
  re-splicing fresh line removes accumulated twist and casts better
  (`46wHgdTJWIM`). This is a distinct use case from the break-off/tangle
  recovery splice logged in [hollow-core splice &
  serving](../rigging/hollow-splice-and-serving.md#when-to-use-it).

## Solid spectra — diameter and bait action

- **Skinnier for the same rating** — more line fits on the reel at a given
  class, the same diameter/capacity mechanism logged for braid generally in
  [line & leader — actual diameter over
  label](line-and-leader.md#actual-diameter-over-label--three-different-20-lb-numbers-james-kikkawa-pcs-show-2024-jigging-101-panel-et2tuskzcss-uploaded-2024-03-16-unregistered-channelpanel--medium-confidence).
- **Less drag in the water:** solid spectra presents less resistance than
  hollow, so a weighted bait swims more naturally instead of catching air
  behind it — some anglers judge this worth trading away hollow's rigging
  convenience for, when it's the difference between getting a bite and not
  (`46wHgdTJWIM`).
- **Disadvantage:** solid spectra is not spliceable the way hollow is.

## Hollow lean — rigging convenience vs. solid's bait action

This source's own preference runs to hollow, for the rigging and
spliceability advantages above — but concedes that when a lighter, skinnier
solid-spectra setup gets a bait swimming enough better to be the difference
between a bite and no bite, that outweighs the rigging convenience
(`46wHgdTJWIM`). Read as situational, not a fixed recommendation.

## A second, competing lean — solid for capacity and knot speed over splice time

This position runs opposite the hollow lean above — framed on camera as
preference, not a rule (`jQzOdmP0zoQ`):

- **Capacity:** solid's skinnier diameter for a given rating fits more line on
  the reel — the same mechanism as above.
- **Tying beats serving:** attaching a top shot inside hollow braid means
  serving it (see [hollow-core splice &
  serving](../rigging/hollow-splice-and-serving.md)), which takes long enough
  that a lot of anglers do that serving work during evening downtime on a
  trip (e.g. after dinner) rather than at the rail. Tying a knot to attach the
  top shot is faster than serving (`jQzOdmP0zoQ`).
- **Exception — a longer trip with big gear:** on a longer multi-day trip (a
  **10-day trip** in the source's example) that calls for big gear, this
  source crimps the top-shot connection instead of tying a knot — see
  [crimping](../rigging/crimping.md#when-to-use) for the mechanical how-to and
  a parallel crimp-beats-knot reasoning logged there for leader classes.

## Smooth vs. rough — a separate axis: kelp-cutting texture

This is a different property than hollow-vs-solid construction, though the
two often travel together:

- **Hollow-core** braid (e.g. 16-strand types) is smooth — it almost feels
  like fluorocarbon — and lets a fluoro leader be inserted and cinched for a
  knotless connection (see [wind-on leader](../rigging/wind-on-leader.md) and
  the hollow-splice work above). It also holds up as a strong everyday line
  in a Seaguar Threadlock/TactX comparison (`YrvQZojc1q0`) — but that video is
  a sponsored brand-vs-brand comparison; treat the brand-superiority claim
  itself as low confidence and take the smooth-vs-rough distinction as the
  durable part.
- **Solid / low-carrier** (e.g. 4-strand) braid has a rough, "pebble" tooth.
  That tooth saws through kelp stringers with a seesaw motion when a
  [calico](../species/calico-bass.md), [yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md)
  or [white seabass](../species/white-seabass.md) buries the line — a smooth
  braid just slides against a stringer and won't cut it.
- Fish a rough, high-tooth braid near kelp — the "rubber sound" it makes
  through the guides on [surface iron](../techniques/surface-iron.md) and
  tuna poppers is a sign the tooth is doing its job — and a smooth, round,
  easy-handling braid for open-water squid-grounds fishing where nothing
  abrades, since it doesn't tear up a thumb on the cast (`aXF0bxAFtU0`). Do
  not put hollow braid on an inshore baitcaster (`ONH1K2MOp7Q`).
