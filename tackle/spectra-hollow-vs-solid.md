# Hollow vs Solid Spectra — Construction, Rigging, and When Each Earns Its Place

Spun out of [line & leader](line-and-leader.md) — this note is the detail
behind that note's "Braid construction" summary. Hollow-core and solid
(low-carrier) spectra differ on two independent axes: how they're built and
rigged (this note), and how their surface texture behaves in kelp (also this
note, second half). Neither source frames one as simply better than the
other.

## No universal answer, stated directly

**(SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 102," `46wHgdTJWIM`,
2020-06-04; unregistered channel/speaker — medium confidence):** "there's
definitely not a right or wrong or black or white, this one's better and this
one's worse" between hollow and solid spectra — differences that call for one
over the other in a given situation, not a hierarchy.

## Typical line-class pairing — a tendency, not a rule

Same source: solid spectra is most commonly run in the **lighter line
classes — 50, 60, 80 lb, sometimes heavier**; hollow is more commonly run in
**larger classes, up to 200 lb**. Stated on camera as "not a rule" — just the
common pairing, not a constraint.

## Hollow-core — rigging and spliceability

- **Small connections:** because the core is hollow, running line can be
  threaded down inside it, letting a connection at the terminal end stay
  compact. See [hollow-core splice & serving](../rigging/hollow-splice-and-serving.md)
  for the actual splicing/serving procedure this enables.
- **Spliceable — recovery use:** two hollow spectra lines can always be
  spliced together. If a reel spooled with hollow spectra tangles on deck, or
  develops a problem partway down the spool, cut the line at that point,
  splice in fresh hollow spectra, and refill the reel — instead of stripping
  and re-spooling the whole thing.
- **Spliceable — proactive maintenance, not just recovery:** the source
  describes doing this periodically as upkeep rather than only after a
  problem — "sometimes like every couple of years I'll just take the top
  hundred yards of hollow spectra off of my reel and re-splice a new," giving
  a clean line with no twists that "casts a little better." This is a
  distinct use case from the break-off/tangle recovery splice already logged
  in [hollow-core splice & serving](../rigging/hollow-splice-and-serving.md#when-to-use-it).

## Solid spectra — diameter and bait action

- **Skinnier for the same rating** — more line fits on the reel at a given
  class, corroborating the diameter/capacity mechanism already logged for
  braid generally in [line & leader — actual diameter over label](line-and-leader.md#actual-diameter-over-label--three-different-20-lb-numbers-james-kikkawa-pcs-show-2024-jigging-101-panel-et2tuskzcss-uploaded-2024-03-16-unregistered-channelpanel--medium-confidence).
- **Less drag in the water:** some anglers feel a bait swims better on solid
  spectra than hollow because solid presents less resistance — it isn't
  "catching as much air," so the weight and bait move more naturally. Framed
  in source as a feel some anglers report, not asserted as a settled
  mechanism.
- **Disadvantage:** solid spectra is not spliceable the way hollow is.

## Practical takeaway (46wHgdTJWIM)

The source's own lean is toward hollow, for the rigging and spliceability
advantages above — but concedes that if a lighter, skinnier solid-spectra
setup gets a bait swimming enough better to be "the difference between
getting a bite or not getting a bite," that outweighs the rigging
convenience. Read as situational, not a fixed recommendation either way.

## Smooth vs rough — the kelp-cutting axis (separate from hollow/solid)

This is a different property than hollow-vs-solid construction, though the
two often travel together:

- **Hollow-core** braid (e.g. 16-strand types) is smooth — it almost feels
  like fluoro — and lets you **insert a fluoro leader and cinch it for a
  knotless connection** (see [wind-on leader](../rigging/wind-on-leader.md)
  and the hollow-splice work above). It is also strong as an everyday line
  (TactX/Threadlock comparison, `YrvQZojc1q0`). *Brand superiority claims in
  that video are sponsored — treat them as low confidence; the
  smooth-vs-rough distinction is the durable part.*
- **Solid / low-carrier (e.g. 4-strand)** braid has a rough, "pebble" tooth.
  That tooth is a feature in the kelp: it **saws through stringers** with a
  seesaw motion when a [calico](../species/calico-bass.md),
  [yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md) or
  [white seabass](../species/white-seabass.md) buries you, where a smooth
  braid just slides and won't cut. Landesfeind fishes a **rough, high-tooth
  braid near kelp** and likes the "rubber sound" in the guides on
  [surface iron](../techniques/surface-iron.md) and tuna poppers; a
  **smooth, round, easy-handling braid** for open-water squid-grounds fishing
  where nothing abrades — it doesn't tear up your thumb on the cast. Do
  **not** put hollow braid on an inshore baitcaster.
