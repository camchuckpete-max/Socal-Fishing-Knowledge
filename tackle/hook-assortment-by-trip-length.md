# Hook Assortment by Trip Length — Overnight Through 5–6 Day

A minimal hook box, sized by species and bait rather than by a long shopping
list — one boat's answer to "what do I actually need to pack" (Carl,
Fisherman's Landing / SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday" Vol. 190
(Must-Have Hooks), `fg2v1kxoTMA`, 2022-07-21; `searcher-sportfishing`
registered channel — medium confidence, single mention of this specific
trip-length framing). This is a checklist note; the underlying style/size
selection logic (circle vs. J, bait-sets-the-size) lives in
[hooks](hooks.md) and isn't repeated here.

Framed on camera as the pack to have aboard for anything from an
**overnight trip through a 3–4 day trip** — and most of the same
hooks carry over onto **5–6 day trips** (named on camera as **Guadalupe** or,
asr-uncertain, rendered "Leos rocks" in the captions — read as **Alijos
Rocks** given the 5–6 day, school-size-tuna framing matches this corpus's
other Alijos Rocks trip material, e.g.
[trolling — Alijos Rocks, Baja](../techniques/trolling.md#alijos-rocks-baja--bomb-lure-trolling-for-wahoo)
and [regions](../locations/regions.md); not confidently resolved beyond that
inference) for **school-size tuna**.

Carl states he'll demonstrate using **Owner Mutu-family hooks** "just to keep
everything simple," and is explicit that brand is a free choice —
"you can change out whatever your favorite brand of hooks are going to be."
Treat the specific model/size numbers below as one demonstrated reference
set, not a claim that a given brand is required.

## Yellowtail — kelp paddy / offshore, J hook

- **J hook**, sized **No. 2 up to 2/0**.
- The specific hook named on camera as the most common style/serial number
  for this is rendered **"94150"** in the captions (asr-uncertain on the
  brand: the sentence is about Owner Mutu hooks, but 94150 is not an Owner
  catalog number — it matches the **Mustad 94150**, already logged in this
  KB as the classic No. 2 anchovy/sardine J-hook baseline, see
  [Mustad 94150 / 94151](hooks.md#mustad-94150--94151--the-historical-anchovysardine-j-hook-baseline-searchersportfishing-tackle-tip-thursday-vol-100-test-of-time-scqnyveqfhy-2020-05-21-searcher-sportfishing-registered-channel--productmodel-pick-low-confidence-per-the-channels-registry-sponsored-claim-caveat)
  — a second, independently dated SearcherSportfishing citation (2020-05-21
  and 2022-07-21) for the same model number on the same species, kept as a
  separate data point rather than merged since the brand attribution here
  isn't fully resolved).
- Good for **school-size yellowtail, school-size yellowfin, and school-size
  bluefin up to about 20–30 lb**.

## Bigger bluefin — step up to circle

- Same species set, bigger fish: **circle hook** — consistent with, not new
  beyond, this KB's circle-for-big-bluefin doctrine (see
  [circle vs. J — the core decision](hooks.md#circle-vs-j--the-core-decision-swrsycmt4tw-m2q22sppkem)).
- **Bait size drives hook size**, matching the bait-sets-the-hook-size rule
  in [hooks — size](hooks.md#size--bait-first-fish-second). Most of the time
  the hook is matched to the bait; a **stealth rig** — a bigger bait fished
  on a smaller hook — is used sometimes, but stated as the exception, not the
  default.
- **Most common circle hook size in recent years, as of this 2022-07-21
  upload:** **No. 1 or No. 2 Owner Mutu circle hook**, or **1/0**.

## Sinker rig — bigger bait

- On a tuna [sinker rig](../rigging/rubber-band-deep-rig.md), step up to a
  **3/0 or 4/0 circle hook** when fishing bigger bait — a larger sardine or
  mackerel. This runs bigger than the 1/0–2/0 sinker-rig circle-hook range
  already logged in that note; kept there as a further attributed data point
  rather than merged into one fixed number.
