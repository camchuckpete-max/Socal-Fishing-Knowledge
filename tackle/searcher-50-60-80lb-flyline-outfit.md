# Searcher 50/60/80 lb Live-Bait Flyline Outfit — "Don't Leave the Dock Without It" (Steve Carson, Penn/SearcherSportfishing, 2019-09-12)

Steve Carson, aboard the **Searcher** (`k4U3ETqmlEc`, "Tackle Tip Thursday
Vol.65 (Must-have Outfit)," uploaded 2019-09-12 — a 72-second segment).
`searcher-sportfishing` is a **registered** channel/series and Carson a
recurring named voice on it, but this is this rig's only citation so far in
this KB (single mention) — mechanism/parameters below read **medium**
confidence per the rubric; the named reel is a product pick and stays **low**
confidence per this channel's sponsored-claim caveat (Carson is Penn's rep).
Framed on camera as filmed with the **midsummer tuna bite** on ("abiding all
over the place" in the caption — read as "biting all over the place," a
plausible ASR mishearing rather than a distinct claim); no location is named
on camera, so none is inferred here. Presented as a "don't leave the dock
without it" outfit — Carson's recurring framing for a must-have rig on this
channel.

## The outfit

- **Reel:** captioned **"Penn Fathom fourteen arrow"** — **asr-uncertain**.
  Read here as plausibly **Penn Fathom 40 Narrow**: this note's own line
  class (80 lb braid backing) and rod rating (40–100 lb) match almost exactly
  the **Penn Fathom 40 Narrow** rig Carson names for an 80 lb outfit rated
  the same way, three years later, in [Searcher big-tuna rig ladder — Rig 1
  (heavy outfit, 80
  lb)](searcher-big-tuna-rig-ladder.md#rig-1--heavy-outfit-80-lb)
  (`VxHYxXmPoWQ`, 2022-04-21) — "fourteen arrow" scans phonetically close to
  "40 [oh-]narrow." Not confirmed by this transcript alone, so kept as a
  plausible resolution rather than a confirmed model; product/model pick is
  **low confidence** regardless, per the sponsored-claim rule.
- **Line:** **80 lb test braid**, filling the spool "almost all the way up,"
  leaving room for a top shot.
- **Top shot:** captioned **"casting length pop shot"** — **asr-uncertain**,
  read here as a **top shot** (a plosive-confusion ASR error; "top shot" is
  the term this channel uses elsewhere for the same casting-length mono/fluoro
  section, e.g. the 60/80 lb top shots logged in [Searcher big-tuna rig
  ladder](searcher-big-tuna-rig-ladder.md)) rather than a distinct "pop shot"
  rig. Sized at **50, 60, or 80 lb test** — Carson's stated rule: pick the
  test based on what the fish are biting that particular day, since "some
  days are biting better, some days are not biting as well."
- **Hook:** **1/0 to 4/0 circle hook** (captioned "a 102 a four oh circle
  hook" — asr-uncertain on the low end, read as 1/0 per the same aught-sizing
  caption pattern logged elsewhere in this KB), sized to the bait: bait size
  and how lively the bait is decide where in that range to go.
- **Rod:** **7, 7.5, or 8 ft**, rated **40–80 lb or 40–100 lb** (both ratings
  given on camera, kept side by side rather than reconciled) — sized "so you
  can handle those bigger grade tuna."

## Grade

Under **50 lb** tuna, this outfit is "a little bit of overkill." Its purpose
is bigger fish: tuna running **75, 100, 125, maybe even 150 lb** — Carson's
stated close: "this is your workhorse outfit when those bigger tuna [are]
around the boat."

**Corroborating resolution of the "fourteen arrow" reel caption (Captain Arch,
SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 158 (Essential Outfits),"
`zuAuk-Kfa1Y`, 2021-10-07; `searcher-sportfishing` registered channel —
product/model pick, low confidence):** a separate Tackle Tip Thursday episode
cleanly captions a **Penn Fathom 40** two-speed reel (no ASR garbling this
time) as one of a four-outfit rental quiver's larger-tuna fly-lining rig,
spooled with 40 lb test rather than this note's 80 lb — a lighter step on the
same reel family, not a duplicate of this specific outfit. It is nonetheless a
further, independent data point supporting the plausible **Penn Fathom 40
Narrow** reading proposed above from this note's own garbled "fourteen arrow"
caption, since it confirms "Fathom 40" is a real, in-use reel name on this
channel rather than an artifact of the ASR error alone. Full outfit in
[Searcher four-outfit Guadalupe
quiver](searcher-four-outfit-guadalupe-quiver.md#outfit-2--penn-fathom-40-or-30-two-speed-40-lb-test--larger-tuna-fly-lining).

## Earlier, independent citation — "Tackle Talk Live," Steve Carson w/ Penn (2021-01-28)

Steve Carson, Penn, guest on SearcherSportfishing's "Tackle Talk Live: 5
Outfits: Don't Leave the Dock Without Them!" (`QHY5kmU7OTU`, uploaded
2021-01-29, filmed 2021-01-28; `searcher-sportfishing` registered channel —
medium confidence for this video's own parameters), predating this note's
own dated video by roughly seven months and naming the rig his **"50/60/80 lb
rig"** — the fourth of his five "don't leave the dock without" outfits, this
one covering **tuna roughly 75–175 lb**.

- **Reel — Penn Fathom 40 Narrow, two-speed** — resolves this note's own
  ASR-uncertain "fourteen arrow" caption with a clean, unambiguous naming of
  the same reel, a further, independent corroboration alongside the
  `zuAuk-Kfa1Y` data point already logged above.
- **Rod:** here **6.5 ft**, though he states the same rig could run as long as
  8 ft depending on preference — a shorter figure than, but overlapping the
  low end of, this note's own 7/7.5/8 ft range.
- **Line:** **500 yd of 80 lb test braid**, leaving room for a **25–50 yd top
  shot** sized to "whatever the captain tells you you better be using that
  day" — 50, 60, or 80 lb test, chosen day-to-day. Matches this note's own
  80 lb braid and 50/60/80 lb top-shot range closely.
- **Grade:** tuna roughly **75–175 lb** — narrower on the low end than this
  note's own 75–150 lb figure, and adding an upper bound this note's own
  citations don't state.
- **Sinker-rig application, with a named condition window:** rigged with the
  **4–12 oz sinker rig** (rubber-band style) to present a live sardine
  **120–300 ft down** for tuna in this grade — a further, independent
  citation of the sinker-bait doctrine already logged in this note's own
  "fourteen arrow" resolution above, this time naming the depth and sinker
  range. **Stated condition window, attributed to the Searcher crew's own
  on-the-water observation (2020 season):** the sinker rig tended to produce
  best **around sunset, just after sunset, and right before sunrise**, on a
  school of fish holding under a stopped or drifting boat — daytime flat-fall
  jigs sometimes drew nothing from the same school while the sinker rig
  connected, at which point the rest of the boat would switch over.
- **Also the yo-yo iron/yellowtail rig, and the wahoo bait rig:** the same
  outfit doubles as the **yo-yo iron** rig for yellowtail (mid-sized fall-type
  jigs also work on it), and — on longer trips into wahoo grounds — as the
  **live-bait wahoo bomb/raider-jig** rig. Framed as a genuine multi-purpose
  outfit: on a kelp-paddy stop it can go straight down on a yo-yo lure for
  yellowtail, then switch to sinker rig or fly line later the same day if
  tuna show up.
- **Leader/mono test — no fixed number, matched to the day's bite and bait
  size:** stated explicitly that there's no single right leader test for the
  sinker rig at this grade — it depends on how big the tuna are, how well
  they're biting, and how big the bait is, so the exact fluoro/mono class is
  a judgment call rather than a fixed figure.
