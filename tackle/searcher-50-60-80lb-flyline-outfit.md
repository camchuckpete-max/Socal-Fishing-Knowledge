# Searcher 50/60/80 lb Live-Bait Flyline Outfit

The 50/60/80 lb outfit is the Searcher's workhorse
[flylining](../techniques/flyline.md) rig for tuna once they outgrow lighter
gear — a little bit of overkill under 50 lb, but the right tool for fish
running 75, 100, 125, and up to 150–175 lb (`k4U3ETqmlEc`, `QHY5kmU7OTU`). The
name comes from its interchangeable top-shot test — 50, 60, or 80 lb, picked
to match the day's bite — run over an 80 lb braid backing on a 40–80/100 lb
rod. Beyond fly-lining bait, the same outfit doubles as a deep sinker rig, a
yo-yo iron rig for yellowtail, and a live-bait wahoo rig on longer trips
(`QHY5kmU7OTU`).

## The outfit

- **Reel — Penn Fathom 40 Narrow, two-speed.** Captioned "fourteen arrow"
  (`k4U3ETqmlEc`) — asr-uncertain, read as Fathom 40 Narrow: it matches this
  outfit's own 80 lb braid backing and 40–100 lb rod rating, and matches the
  later [Rig 1 — heavy outfit, 80
  lb](searcher-big-tuna-rig-ladder.md#rig-1--heavy-outfit-80-lb) build from
  the same speaker (`VxHYxXmPoWQ`, 2022-04-21); "fourteen arrow" scans
  phonetically close to "40 [oh-]narrow." Two independent citations
  corroborate the reading: a cleanly-captioned **Penn Fathom 40**, two-speed,
  on a lighter 40 lb-test build in the same channel's four-outfit rental
  quiver (`zuAuk-Kfa1Y`; full outfit in [Searcher four-outfit Guadalupe
  quiver — Outfit
  2](searcher-four-outfit-guadalupe-quiver.md#outfit-2--penn-fathom-40-or-30-two-speed-40-lb-test--larger-tuna-fly-lining)),
  and an unambiguous **Penn Fathom 40 Narrow, two-speed** naming from an
  earlier, independent citation of this same 50/60/80 lb rig
  (`QHY5kmU7OTU`). The model/product pick stays low confidence regardless of
  corroboration count, per this channel's sponsored-claim rule.
- **Line — 80 lb test braid**, filling the spool almost all the way, leaving
  room for a top shot (`k4U3ETqmlEc`). An independent citation of the same
  rig specifies **500 yd of 80 lb test braid** with a **25–50 yd top shot**
  (`QHY5kmU7OTU`).
- **Top shot — 50, 60, or 80 lb test**, picked to match what the fish are
  biting that day: "some days are biting better, some days are not biting as
  well" (`k4U3ETqmlEc`). Captioned "casting length pop shot" — asr-uncertain,
  read as top shot, the term this channel uses elsewhere for the same
  casting-length mono/fluoro section (e.g. the 60/80 lb top shots in
  [Searcher big-tuna rig ladder](searcher-big-tuna-rig-ladder.md)). A second
  citation matches the same 50/60/80 lb range, chosen day to day per the
  captain's call (`QHY5kmU7OTU`).
- **Hook — 1/0 to 4/0 circle hook**, sized to the bait: bait size and how
  lively the bait is decide where in the range to go (`k4U3ETqmlEc`).
  Captioned "a 102 a four oh circle hook" — asr-uncertain on the low end,
  read as 1/0 per the aught-sizing caption pattern used elsewhere in this KB.
- **Rod — 7, 7.5, or 8 ft, rated 40–80 lb or 40–100 lb** (both ratings given
  on camera), sized "so you can handle those bigger grade tuna"
  (`k4U3ETqmlEc`). A second citation runs a
  shorter **6.5 ft** rod for the same rig, though the same speaker states it
  could run as long as 8 ft depending on preference (`QHY5kmU7OTU`) —
  overlapping the low end of the range above.

## Grade

Under 50 lb tuna, this outfit is "a little bit of overkill" (`k4U3ETqmlEc`);
its purpose is bigger fish — tuna running 75, 100, 125, maybe even 150 lb,
"the workhorse outfit when those bigger tuna [are] around the boat"
(`k4U3ETqmlEc`). An earlier, independent citation of the same rig — the
fourth of five "don't leave the dock without" outfits, there named the
"50/60/80 lb rig" — narrows the low end and adds an upper bound: tuna roughly
75–175 lb (`QHY5kmU7OTU`).

## Sinker-rig application (120–300 ft)

- Rigged with a **4–12 oz sinker rig** (rubber-band style) to present a live
  sardine **120–300 ft down** for tuna at this grade (`QHY5kmU7OTU`). See
  [rubber-band deep rig](../rigging/rubber-band-deep-rig.md) for the rig
  build and [sliding sinker](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md) for the general
  technique.
- **Best window** — the Searcher crew's own 2020-season pattern: the sinker
  rig tended to produce best around sunset, just after sunset, and right
  before sunrise, on a school holding under a stopped or drifting boat;
  daytime flat-fall jigs sometimes drew nothing from the same school while
  the sinker rig connected, at which point the rest of the boat switched
  over (`QHY5kmU7OTU`; full account in the evidence file).
- **Leader/mono test has no fixed number** — it's matched to the day's bite
  and bait size (how big the tuna are, how well they're biting, how big the
  bait is), a judgment call rather than a fixed figure (`QHY5kmU7OTU`).

## Also fishes yo-yo iron and wahoo bait

The same outfit doubles as the [yo-yo iron](../techniques/yo-yo-iron.md) rig
for [yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md) (mid-sized fall-type jigs also
work on it), and — on longer trips into wahoo grounds — as the live-bait
[wahoo bomb & raider jig](../techniques/wahoo-bomb-casting.md) rig
(`QHY5kmU7OTU`). On a kelp-paddy stop it goes straight down on a yo-yo lure
for yellowtail, then switches to the sinker rig or fly line later the same
day if tuna show up.
