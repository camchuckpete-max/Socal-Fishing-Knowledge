# Evidence — Hooks

Trip reports and per-source provenance for [hooks](../hooks.md): channel

## Circle vs. J — the core decision

- `LsFMBCa9DOQ` (StokedOnFishing, "Three Days of Epic Bluefin Fishing on the
  Ranger 85," mid-August 2024, Cortez/Tanner Bank; unregistered channel,
  medium confidence): asked circle vs. J for a finicky bluefin bite fished on
  No. 4/No. 2 hooks, a crew member stated a personal preference for J
  hooks — faster to pin a bait than a circle, less handling time, livelier
  bait, more bites. Gave a bite-shyness mechanism for hook/line size: "if
  they see that hook they will not bite it, if they see that line they also
  won't bite it"; working line ran 25–30 lb, sometimes dropped to 20 lb. Sits
  against the circle-hook-for-bluefin doctrine documented elsewhere in this
  note (Ray Sharifi's corner-of-the-mouth reasoning; Capt. Dave Hansen's
  teeth-saw-through-the-leader mechanism; the Constitution captain's
  circle-hooks-always-for-bluefin briefing) — one crew's stated preference on
  one trip, not reconciled with that doctrine.
- `mL4Ph7t0WcQ` (StokedOnFishing, 2016-12-16, Lighthouse Point beach, East
  Cape, Baja California Sur): surf-casting bait (~30 ft cast, held stationary
  and thumbed on the spool) for roosterfish on a circle hook, anglers were
  coached to let the fish take it and run rather than swing — "give him a 10
  count, at least 10 seconds" — before winding tight, matching the 10-count
  circle-hook timing in the main note. Once hooked, the instruction was to
  just wind rather than set. A tuna caught the same session on flylined
  squid was likewise handled as a circle-hook fish (no active hookset
  described).
- `mG8ZZLFGlT8` (StokedOnFishing, 2016-12-22 upload, Lighthouse Point beach,
  East Cape, Baja California Sur, "Stoked On Hotel Buena Vista Beach Resort,
  Part 3"): the payoff of the same Lighthouse Point roosterfish chase teased
  in the prior episode (no bites that day) — back on the beach on live bait,
  the coaching was again to "just take your time" rather than rush the
  hookset; once fish were located the bite window was fast, a hookup "within
  seconds" to at most "a couple minutes." A young angler (Jayden) landed his
  first roosterfish. Corroborates, without adding new parameters, the
  10-count patience coaching in the `mL4Ph7t0WcQ` entry above.
- `y0LVT59inEA` ("Searcher Sportfishing Hook Selection," SearcherSportfishing,
  2016-06-14; registered channel, high confidence — the specific 40 lb
  threshold now repeated by this speaker, Captain Art, across two dated
  videos): predates the `YntRJAN88fs` citation by about four years and gives
  the identical 40 lb circle-hook cutoff and mechanism, using the term
  "gorilla live bait hooks" for standard J hooks. Line-class J-hook sizing
  (captions render "40 or a 5o hook," read as 4/0 or 5/0 given the jump up
  from a No. 1/2 J hook) and stock-assortment sizes (captions render "1 to
  60," read as 1 through 6/0) are asr-uncertain. Framed on camera as a
  sponsor plug ("we're fortunate to be sponsored by Owner Hooks," sold on the
  Searcher or at Fisherman's Landing Tackle), so the brand recommendation
  itself stays low confidence per the registry sponsored-claim caveat even
  though the 40 lb threshold and line-class sizing rate normally as
  mechanism/parameter claims. Samples shown on camera: Gorilla J hook in 1/0
  and 4/0; circle hook in 2/0 and 4/0 (captioned "MW"/"M," plausibly Owner
  Mutu, not confidently resolved).
- `YntRJAN88fs` ("Tackle Talk Live (from the boat 3/24)," SearcherSportfishing,
  2020-03-25; registered channel, medium confidence, single mention of this
  specific number): states the 40 lb circle-hook cutoff as a bare number
  where the mechanism-based doctrine (Ray Sharifi, Capt. Dave Hansen) says
  only "big bluefin." Recalls the 2019 season (dated from "last year" on the
  2020-03-25 upload), poor enough the crew ran hooks as small as 1/0 and 2/0
  "just to get a bite."
- `uWGGeDOprsE` ("Tackle Tips for Alijos Rocks and The Ridge,"
  SearcherSportfishing, 2009-08-25; speaker not identified on camera;
  registered channel/series, single mention, medium confidence).
- `RfiC8sfIWTk` (Ray Sharifi, Dirty Hookers, 2023-05-04; `ray-sharifi`
  registered, SoCal-capped — medium confidence): the corner-of-mouth/offset
  bluefin claim and the mackerel bait-hook sizing (8/0, 6/0, 4/0) both trace
  here. Corroborated by Capt. Dave Hansen's teeth/bite-off mechanism,
  `RbqOKkINSCM` below.
- `RbqOKkINSCM` (Capt. Dave Hansen, Your Saltwater Guide, 2021-05-25;
  `dave-hansen` registered voice — high confidence, repeated doctrine): states
  he normally prefers J hooks ("if you've been to my seminars before... I
  like to use J-hooks") but switches to circle specifically for big bluefin
  because bluefin have a lot of teeth (unlike yellowfin) and will saw through
  the leader. Same source gives the mackerel-bait hook sizing (8/0, 6/0,
  4/0), fished on a private boat.
- `zVIfArUrpDI` (Dave Hansen, Your Saltwater Guide, 2021-06-15; `dave-hansen`
  registered voice, single mention of this specific rationale — medium
  confidence): on a rockfish dropper-loop gangion he runs circle hooks
  specifically because most of his clients can't feel the bite; frames it as
  "goes against everything I stand for," implying a personal J-hook
  preference elsewhere set aside for client skill level.
- `48ZFXnCTTQE` (Cesar, Eric's Tackle, via Dirty Hookers, 2023-11-01; medium
  confidence — registered voice, single mention; not yet repeated doctrine):
  source for both the smallest-hook-sizes J-beats-circle claim (10-count
  circle / 3-count J timing) and the ringed-vs-non-ringed preference
  (non-ringed + loop knot: less hardware weight on a stressed live bait, no
  ring-on-hook clank).
- `UfuiWFVvz2E` (StokedOnFishing, video uploaded 2026-08-11, Cortez Bank,
  SoCal; Constitution charter; unregistered channel — medium confidence): a
  pre-trip tackle briefing repeating circle-hooks-always-for-bluefin,
  J-hooks-recommended-for-yellowtail as trip-wide guidance.
- `yGXSrUauo2w` (Steve Carson, Penn/SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Tip
  Thursday Vol. 231 (Sliding Sinker for Yellowtail)," 2024-04-25; registered
  channel, repeated doctrine — high confidence for the style choice itself,
  the named reel/rod in the full build staying low per the sponsored-claim
  caveat): for yellowtail up to ~22 lb, a J hook is better than a circle hook
  in his own live-bait rigs (circle otherwise the default for his tuna
  rigs). Sized 2/0, stepping to 4/0 for a large bait.
- `R48YdVFfEOI` (Billy, Fisherman's Landing Tackle Shop, via
  SearcherSportfishing "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 134 (Rod Handling),"
  2021-01-21; registered channel — medium confidence, single mention):
  the don't-swing/three-count/45°-J-hook beginner framing. Transcript
  confirms: "for you new guys just starting out you give it a solid... three
  count... if you're using a circle hook it's just going to come tight in
  the corner of his mouth, if you're using a j hook I like to raise the rod
  at a 45 degree angle."

## Downsize the hook, not the line

- `FXWOIB0TPfE` ("Tackle Talk Live from the Boat (4/7/20)," SearcherSportfishing,
  2020-04-08; registered channel — medium confidence, single mention of this
  specific number): the primary source for the rule. Transcript confirms:

  year I mean we are using number ones and number twos just to get a bite."

  carry an assortment of both circle and J hooks rather than one fixed size.
- `shZCjX2-fkI` ("Tackle Talk Live: Bluefin Tuna," Captain Armando,
  SearcherSportfishing, 2021-01-14; registered channel, now repeated a third
  time by this channel, high confidence): "our recommendation would be just
  go down and hook size, don't go down in line class." Tanner Bank data point
  referenced on camera as "last year," resolved from the 2021-01-14 taping
  date to the 2020 season.

## Factory sharpness — iron trebles vs. wahoo hooks

- `49joKHD7Umc` (SearcherSportfishing, Tackle Talk Live, 2020-06-03;
  registered channel, single mention — medium confidence): surface/yo-yo
  iron treble hooks generally don't need sharpening (soldered rings, sharp
  out of the box); the crew does sharpen wahoo bomb hooks. No mechanism given
  for the difference in this source.
- `4uNPLknRAQg` (Searcher Sportfishing, "Tackle Talk Live Recap (Wahoo
  5.5.20)," 2020-05-06; registered channel/series — this "sharpen wahoo
  hooks" practice now repeated across two same-channel sources about a month
  apart): broadens the practice to casting lures, bombs, and live-bait hooks
  all sharpened out of the package before wahoo fishing, and supplies the
  mechanism `49joKHD7Umc` doesn't: a wahoo's very bony jaw often won't take
  an unsharpened hook on the hookset.
