# White Seabass Rod, Reel, and Line — Star-Drag vs. Baitcaster, and Why Mono Matters (Tackle Express)

channel, single mention — medium confidence). Rod/reel/line parameters for a
dedicated white seabass kit, paired with the rig content in
[white seabass](../species/white-seabass.md), [dropper
loop](../techniques/dropper-loop.md), and [leadhead

## Reel: two classes, split by presentation

- **Conventional star-drag reels** (a two-speed isn't needed — white seabass
  don't demand it) are the pick for the **dropper loop**: drop straight to
  bottom, wind up a turn or two. They'll fish everything else on this list too
  (jigs, sliders, lead-head-and-squid, artificials) and their larger spool
  gives better line capacity for a long mono top shot over braid.
- **Low-profile baitcasters** (his example: a Shimano TRX 400) — castable via
  a spool tensioner and internal brakes — are the pick for **casting**:
  artificials (flukes, hookup baits), a sliding egg sinker, or a lead-head and
  squid. Usable for the dropper loop in a pinch but not ideal. Trade-off:
  noticeably less line capacity than the conventional reels.
- **His own boat setup:** run both — a baitcaster rigged with a fluke or
  hookup bait alongside a conventional star-drag rigged with a jig or dropper
  loop sent to the bottom.

## Line: mono for the stretch, because of soft mouths

- **White seabass have soft mouths** — fishing straight braid on these reels
  carries a real risk of pulling the hook out. **Run a lot of monofilament**
  for the stretch: **no less than 50–100 yards, up to half or a full spool.**
- **Drag:** run it a little lighter than usual for the same soft-mouth reason,
  **except** when fishing tight to nasty kelp or rock where a heavy drag is
  required to keep the fish out of structure.
- **Baitcaster line-capacity trade-off:** the low-profile reels don't hold
  much mono outright. Running braid instead to preserve capacity is fine, but
  **pair it with a soft/moderate-action rod** to make up for the stretch mono
  would otherwise provide — a stiff rod on straight braid with a soft-mouthed
  fish is the failure mode this pairing avoids.
