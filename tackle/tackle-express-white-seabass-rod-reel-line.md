# White Seabass Rod, Reel, and Line — Star-Drag vs. Baitcaster, and Why Mono Matters (Tackle Express)

A dedicated white seabass kit splits by presentation: a conventional
star-drag reel for the dropper loop and other dropped baits, a low-profile
baitcaster for casting artificials, and monofilament line to protect a
soft-mouthed fish (`Eo_bA8IWvyU`). It supplies model-level detail for the rig
content in [white seabass](../species/white-seabass.md), [dropper
loop](../techniques/dropper-loop.md), and [leadhead
mods](../rigging/leadhead-mods.md).

## Reel: two classes, split by presentation

- **Conventional star-drag reels** — a two-speed isn't needed for white
  seabass — are the pick for the **dropper loop**: drop straight to bottom,
  wind up a turn or two. They also fish everything else on this list (jigs,
  sliders, lead-head-and-squid, artificials), and their larger spool gives
  better line capacity for a long mono top shot over braid (`Eo_bA8IWvyU`).
- **Low-profile baitcasters** — castable via a spool tensioner and internal
  brakes, e.g. a **Shimano TRX 400** — are the pick for **casting**:
  artificials (flukes, hookup baits), a sliding egg sinker, or a lead-head and
  squid. Usable for the dropper loop in a pinch but not ideal; trade-off is
  noticeably less line capacity than the conventional reels (`Eo_bA8IWvyU`).
- **Two-rod boat setup:** run a baitcaster rigged with a fluke or hookup bait
  alongside a conventional star-drag rigged with a jig or dropper loop sent to
  the bottom (`Eo_bA8IWvyU`).

## Line: mono for the stretch, because of soft mouths

- White seabass have soft mouths, so straight braid on these reels carries a
  real risk of pulling the hook out. Run a lot of monofilament for the
  stretch: no less than 50–100 yards, up to half or a full spool
  (`Eo_bA8IWvyU`).
- Run drag a little lighter than usual for the same soft-mouth reason, except
  when fishing tight to nasty kelp or rock, where a heavy drag is required to
  keep the fish out of structure (`Eo_bA8IWvyU`).
- Baitcaster line-capacity trade-off: the low-profile reels don't hold much
  mono outright. Running braid instead to preserve capacity is fine, paired
  with a soft/moderate-action rod to make up for the stretch mono would
  otherwise provide — a stiff rod on straight braid with a soft-mouthed fish
  is the failure mode this pairing avoids (`Eo_bA8IWvyU`).
