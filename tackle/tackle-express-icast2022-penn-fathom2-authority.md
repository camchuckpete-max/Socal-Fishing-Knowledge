# Penn Fathom II and Penn Authority — ICAST 2022 Launch Parameters (Tackle Express)

A Tackle Express booth walk of the 2022 ICAST trade show (`75lUj-uaArQ`)
captures two Penn product-launch segments with SoCal-relevant saltwater
parameters: the Fathom II two-speed lever-drag relaunch and the new Authority
spinning reel. The on-camera speaker for both is a Penn representative filmed
at Penn's own booth announcing products that launched at the show that same
week, which is sponsored/promotional trade-show content, not neutral
third-party instruction — per the confidence rubric ("low — sponsored or
promotional claim, regardless of channel") this note is capped at **low**
confidence in full. Most of the video's other product coverage (freshwater-
bass and general-market spinning/casting tackle) carries no SoCal/Baja
saltwater decision logic and is skipped per the curation bar; its Accurate
Tern 2 segment is re-cut footage already extracted in [Accurate Tern 2 —
Cast-Control Brake and Redesigned Twin
Drag](tackle-express-accurate-tern-2-reel.md) and is not re-extracted here.

## Penn Fathom II lever drag — what changed from generation 1

- **Cosmetic only:** same diecast-aluminum body material as generation 1;
  paint color changed from black to silver (`75lUj-uaArQ`).
- **Shift system:** generation 1 used a twist-on/twist-off dial; generation 2
  uses an "international-style" shift, aimed at being more intuitive for less
  experienced anglers — advanced anglers "never had a problem" with the old
  system (`75lUj-uaArQ`).
- **Preset drag dial — reworked for accidental-bump resistance.** Generation 1
  had a 4-stop drag arc; a fish running and jumping mid-fight could bump the
  dial past a stop and badly mis-set the drag. Generation 2 uses a continuous
  drag arc "like international... for 50 years," and the dial must be
  **pulled out firmly to turn**, then locks back in place when released, so it
  cannot be changed by brushing it during a fight (`75lUj-uaArQ`).
- **Unchanged (kept because it worked):** all-stainless-steel gear train and
  Dura-Drag drag material — the same drag material used in Penn International
  reels (`75lUj-uaArQ`).
- **Handles:** two-speed versions ship with a power handle; one-speed versions
  ship with a football/knob handle; the two handle styles are fully
  interchangeable if an angler wants the other style (`75lUj-uaArQ`).
- **Sizing convention** (stated as true of "any of the modern Penns," not
  Fathom-II-specific): the size number is the yards of that pound-test
  monofilament the reel holds — an 80 holds 300 yd of 80 lb mono, a 10 holds
  300 yd of 10 lb mono, etc. (`75lUj-uaArQ`).
- **New size — the 80,** roughly a hair bigger than the old Fathom 60
  (`75lUj-uaArQ`).
- **The 60 is redefined.** Generation 1's 60 was a wide version of the 40
  (same gears, same side plate as the 40, just a wider spool). Generation 2's
  60 becomes a **narrow version of the 80** instead — a single-speed unit was
  on hand at the show; the two-speed 60 was confirmed coming but not yet in
  stock to show (`75lUj-uaArQ`).
- **Left-hand availability capped at the 40 size** so far — no left-hand 60 or
  80 yet. The 25 Narrow and 30 sizes were already offered left-hand and that
  carries over to generation 2 (`75lUj-uaArQ`).

### The 40 Narrow's gear-ratio retune — a SoCal-specific change

The 40 Narrow's two gear ratios both changed for generation 2, a change
flagged as specific to San Diego/SoCal anglers and not intended for other
regions' fisheries; the rest of the generation-2 lineup kept the same gear
ratios and drag settings as generation 1 (`75lUj-uaArQ`).

- **High gear:** generation 1 was **5.1:1**; generation 2 raises it to
  **6.0:1** — **50 in of line per handle turn**, aimed at wahoo fishing
  (`75lUj-uaArQ`).
- **Low gear:** generation 1 was **2.5:1**; generation 2 lowers it to
  **1.9:1** — **16 in of line per handle turn**, aimed at grinding on big
  bluefin (`75lUj-uaArQ`).
- **Availability** (promotional detail, kept for the record): stated to ship
  to stores late September 2022 (`75lUj-uaArQ`).

## Penn Authority spinning reel — new for 2022

A new Penn saltwater spinning reel line, pitched on camera as nicer than the
Penn Slammer. It won "Best New Saltwater Reel of the Year" at the ICAST 2022
awards the night before this interview — a promotional detail, kept for the
record and not treated as fishery doctrine (`75lUj-uaArQ`).

- **Sizes:** run from 2500 up to 10,500; not every intermediate size is named
  on camera (`75lUj-uaArQ`).
- **Recommended SoCal size — the 6500:** suited to light surface iron, stick
  baits, and poppers, and also works for straight bait fished off a spinning
  reel. Stated capable of landing a 50–60 lb fish mixed into a school of
  ~20 lb bluefin (`75lUj-uaArQ`).
- **Durability rating — IPX8 vs. the Slammer's IPX6.** IPX is the same
  commercial waterproofing scale used for cameras and cell phones. IPX6
  (Slammer) tolerates boat spray or a wave breaking over the reel
  occasionally (e.g. surf fishing); IPX8 (Authority) means the reel can be
  **fully submerged up to 3 ft deep for up to 30 minutes** and survive — e.g.
  a reel in a rod holder that falls into the water while the angler naps. The
  reel **cannot be cranked while submerged**; the rating covers survival, not
  underwater function (`75lUj-uaArQ`).
- **Internal gearing — heavy, not lightweight, by design.** Heavy-duty
  CNC-machined gears, described as extra heavy, oversized, and big-toothed.
  Stated trade-off: "these are not lightweight reels... I have reels that
  weigh less than that gear" (`75lUj-uaArQ`).
- **Pinion-gear support — a stated fight-under-heavy-drag difference.** The
  Authority supports the pinion gear with a ball bearing on **both** ends;
  the Slammer supports it with a ball bearing on one end and a bushing on the
  other. At average drag settings the difference is unlikely to be felt, but
  becomes noticeable as drag is cranked up hard — e.g. a fish thought to be a
  20 lb tuna that turns out to be an 80 lb fish (`75lUj-uaArQ`).
- **Slow oscillation — stated casting-distance benefit for stick baits.** The
  spool lays braid down more evenly than a standard (faster) oscillation,
  benefiting plastic or wooden stick baits that don't cast well through the
  air: roughly **5–10% more casting distance** (hedged on camera as "hard to
  put a number on it"), framed as adding up over a multi-day trip such as an
  8-day long-range trip (`75lUj-uaArQ`).
- **Capability ceiling — explicitly NOT a SoCal long-range claim** (labeled
  contrast, not merged into SoCal doctrine). Stated capable of catching a
  200 lb tuna, but not on a long-range boat: the 200–300 lb yellowfin
  examples given are from **small, fast boats running down schools of big
  yellowfin in Panama and Costa Rica** — a different fishery and boat format
  from the SoCal/Baja long-range fleet this KB otherwise documents. Kept as
  an explicit out-of-region contrast, per this KB's region-separation rule,
  not as a claim about what the Authority does on a SoCal long-range trip
  (`75lUj-uaArQ`).
- **Availability** (promotional detail): stated to ship to stores late
  September 2022 (`75lUj-uaArQ`).
