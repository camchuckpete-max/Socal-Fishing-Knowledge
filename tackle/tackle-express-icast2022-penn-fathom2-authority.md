## Penn Fathom II lever drag — what changed from generation 1

- **Cosmetic only:** same diecast-aluminum body material as generation 1;
  paint color changed from black to silver.
- **Shift system:** generation 1 used a twist-on/twist-off dial; generation 2
  uses an "international-style" shift, Steve Carson's stated reason being
  it's more intuitive for less experienced anglers (advanced anglers "never
  had a problem" with the old system).
- **Preset drag dial — reworked for accidental-bump resistance.** Generation 1
  had a 4-stop drag arc; a fish running and jumping mid-fight could cause an
  angler to bump the dial past a stop and badly mis-set the drag. Generation 2
  uses a continuous drag arc "like international... for 50 years," and the
  dial itself must be **pulled out firmly to turn**, then locks back in place
  when released — stated design intent is you cannot change the preset by
  accidentally brushing it during a fight. Carson's own shorthand: "preset,
  pull to turn."
- **Unchanged (kept because it worked):** all-stainless-steel gear train and
  Dura-Drag drag material — the same drag material used in Penn International
  reels.
- **Handles:** two-speed versions ship with a power handle; one-speed versions
  ship with a football/knob handle; the two handle styles are fully
  interchangeable if an angler wants the other style.
- **Sizing convention (stated as true of "any of the modern Penns," not
  Fathom-II-specific):** the size number is the yards of that pound-test
  monofilament the reel holds — an 80 holds 300 yd of 80 lb mono, a 10 holds
  300 yd of 10 lb mono, etc.
- **New size — the 80.** Roughly a hair bigger than the old Fathom 60.
- **The 60 is redefined.** Generation 1's 60 was a wide version of the 40
  (same gears, same side plate as the 40, just a wider spool). Generation 2's
  60 becomes a **narrow version of the 80** instead — a single-speed unit was
  on hand at the show; the two-speed 60 was confirmed coming but not yet in
  stock to show.
- **Left-hand availability capped at the 40 size** so far — no left-hand 60 or
  80 yet. The 25 Narrow and 30 sizes were already offered left-hand and that
  carries over to generation 2.

### The 40 Narrow's gear-ratio retune — "a San Diego special"

Steve Carson calls this change out specifically for San Diego/SoCal anglers,
distinct from the rest of the generation-2 lineup (which kept the same gear
ratios and drag settings as generation 1): the **40 Narrow's** two gear
ratios were both changed.

- **High gear:** generation 1 was **5.1:1**; generation 2 raises it to
  **6.0:1** — a stated **50 in of line per handle turn**. Stated reason: "for
  all you wahoo anglers."
- **Low gear:** generation 1 was **2.5:1**; generation 2 lowers it to
  **1.9:1** — a stated **16 in of line per handle turn**. Stated reason: "for
  you big bluefin grinders."
- Carson, on the retune: "I don't really care if the guys in Florida ever use
  this reel again, because in San Diego it's now our favorite reel."
- **Availability (promotional detail, kept for the record):** stated to ship
  to stores late September 2022.

## Penn Authority spinning reel — new for 2022

A new saltwater spinning reel line Carson introduces by calling it "nicer
than the [Penn] Slammer." **Won "Best New Saltwater Reel of the Year" at the
ICAST 2022 awards** the night before this interview — a promotional detail,
kept for the record and not treated as fishery doctrine.

- **Sizes:** run from 2500 up to 10,500; not every intermediate size is named
  on camera.
- **Recommended SoCal size — the 6500.** Carson's own pick, reasoned as "I
  fish in the same place your guys do": good for light surface iron, stick
  baits, and poppers, and also works for straight bait fished off a spinning
  reel — called "an absolutely great all-around reel." Stated capability: if
  a school of ~20 lb bluefin happens to have a 50–60 lb fish mixed in, the
  6500 can still land it.
- **Durability rating — IPX8 vs. the Slammer's IPX6.** IPX is the same
  commercial waterproofing scale used for cameras and cell phones. Stated
  practical difference: IPX6 (Slammer) tolerates boat spray or a wave
  breaking over the reel occasionally (e.g. surf fishing); IPX8 (Authority)
  means the reel can be **fully submerged up to 3 ft deep for up to 30
  minutes** and survive — worked example given on camera: a reel sitting in a
  rod holder that falls into the water while the angler is napping. Explicit
  caveat: **you cannot crank the reel while submerged** — the rating covers
  survival, not underwater function.
- **Internal gearing — heavy, not lightweight, by design.** "Heavy duty CNC
  machine gears," described as extra heavy, oversized, big teeth. Stated
  trade-off, on camera: "these are not lightweight reels... I have reels that
  weigh less than that gear."
- **Pinion-gear support — a stated fight-under-heavy-drag difference.** The
  Authority supports the pinion gear with a ball bearing on **both** ends;
  the Slammer supports it with a ball bearing on one end and a bushing on the
  other. Stated effect: at "average" drag settings the difference likely
  wouldn't be felt, but it becomes noticeable as the drag is cranked up hard
  — his example: a fish an angler thought was a 20 lb tuna turning out to be
  an 80 lb fish.
- **Slow oscillation — stated casting-distance benefit for stick baits.** The
  spool lays braid down more evenly than a standard (faster) oscillation.
  Stated benefit specifically for plastic or wooden stick baits that "don't
  fly that well through the air": roughly **5–10% more casting distance**
  (his own hedge: "hard to put a number on it"). Framed as adding up over a
  multi-day trip — his example, "an 8-day long-range trip."
- **Capability ceiling — explicitly NOT a SoCal long-range claim (labeled
  contrast, not merged into SoCal doctrine).** Carson says the Authority has
  "plenty of capability to catch a 200 lb tuna," then immediately qualifies:
  "not on a long range boat... we're talking about a different format." The
  200–300 lb yellowfin examples he gives are from **small, fast boats running
  down schools of big yellowfin in Panama and Costa Rica** — a different
  fishery and boat format from the SoCal/Baja long-range fleet this KB
  otherwise documents. Kept here as an explicit out-of-region contrast, per
  this KB's region-separation rule, not as a claim about what the Authority
  does on a SoCal long-range trip.
- **Availability (promotional detail):** stated to ship to stores late
  September 2022.
