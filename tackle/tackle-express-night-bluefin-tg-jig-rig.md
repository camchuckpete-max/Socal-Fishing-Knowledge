# Tungsten (TG) Jig Rig for Bluefin — Night, Day, and Surface (Tackle Express)

Tackle Express fishes tungsten (TG) knife jigs on a reel/line/leader ladder
built to run all day: a heavy leader for graded night bluefin, a lighter
re-rig for daytime speed-jigging, and the same jig cast at surface foamers
when fish show. Read as **TG (tungsten) jig** throughout — one clip's
captions render the class both "PG jigs" and "TG jigs," matching the
documented **Daiwa TG** jig (`3-0kaaHqA7A`); see [knife jigs — night lure
sizing](../lures/knife-jigs.md#night-lure-sizing-matched-to-outfit-size-and-profile-shape-gavin-pacific-queen).
Most of the parameters below are cited across several Tackle Express uploads
from the same June–August 2025 shoot, some of which recut the same footage
rather than independently confirming it; the full source-cluster breakdown is
in the evidence file.

## Reel and line

- **Reel:** a **Shimano Talica 20**, **Talica 25**, or one of the newer
  **Daiwa Saltiga 55 or 60** two-speeds (`3-0kaaHqA7A`).
- **Line:** **100–150 lb braid**, spooled to the top with a **metered
  braid** — named product: **Power Pro Depth Hunter Offshore**, metered
  every **100 ft** (`3-0kaaHqA7A`). Metered line lets the angler match the
  jig's depth to the captain's called depth on the sounder rather than
  guessing, which the source calls the key to a bite on the tungsten jig.
  This sits within, not beyond, the 100 ft interval already logged as one
  option among several (25/50/60/100 ft) in
  [knife jigging — depth control & jig calling](../techniques/knife-jigging.md#depth-control--jig-calling);
  Power Pro Depth Hunter Offshore is a new named product for that same
  100 ft-interval doctrine.

## Leader — the "Jarry rig"

The build's own title spells it **"Jarry,"** not "Jerry" (`BsWb5BQ1Tdc`) —
human-typed title metadata overriding the ASR "Jerry" spelling carried by
three earlier same-shop clips. On camera the build is credited to **James
and Harry**; "Jarry" reads as a portmanteau of the two names, though the
source doesn't spell that out.

- A named house leader build, **200 lb** class: a **200 lb Seaguar Blue
  Label fluorocarbon** bite leader, a **welded** ring/hook connection (the
  transcript's "welded 90 hook" — exact hook designation not resolved,
  asr-uncertain), and a **500 lb swivel** (`3-0kaaHqA7A`, `CsKEtrcLgiQ`,
  `dBAk9NScxSc`).
- **Construction detail and stated reasoning:** the ring at the hook is
  deliberately a **solid ring, not a split ring** — split rings have been
  seen to **bind**. The hook itself rides on a swivel, with a **second
  swivel at the top of the chafing leader**; with both in place, "there is
  no way this connection is going to bind." A **350 lb Mustad Saltism split
  ring** is also named as a build component — captions cut across the
  sentence naming its position, so its exact place in the hook-to-leader
  chain isn't confirmed (asr-uncertain) (`BsWb5BQ1Tdc`).
- Tied straight off the 100–150 lb braid for the whole night session
  (`BsWb5BQ1Tdc`).
- **Night vs. day leader swap, stated reasoning (`dBAk9NScxSc`):** the heavy
  leader is fished unseen in the dark, letting the angler bring a hooked fish
  right onto the boat without finesse; it comes off at daybreak — daytime
  fish "have light and it's easier for them to see" it — for a lighter day
  rig (below). The source also states the lighter rig "allow[s]" speed
  jigging during the day, but gives no mechanism for why a lighter rig
  itself enables that retrieve (⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: mechanism
  — only the visibility reason for dropping the night rig is stated).
- A named leader-to-jig connection school alongside the crimp, direct-tie,
  split-ring, and swivel-plus-split-ring schools already logged side by side
  in [knife jigging — leader-to-jig
  connection](../techniques/knife-jigging.md#gear-class-detail) — this
  shop's own named build, not reconciled with the others.
- **A third, earlier citation, three years before the clips above
  (`DuNkl8F45NE`, 2022):** the same build, the same James-and-Harry
  attribution, the same solid-ring (not split-ring) reasoning almost word
  for word, and the same 350 lb Mustad Saltism split ring — but this earlier
  video gives the bite leader as a **200–300 lb range, stepped up to
  400 lb for heavy night fishing**, rather than the single fixed 200 lb
  Seaguar Blue Label spec above. Kept side by side as the same shop's build
  described three years apart, not reconciled into one number. Full detail,
  including the hook upgrades and the reel/rod ladder this earlier video
  pairs with the rig, is in [trophy bluefin jig
  outfit](tackle-express-trophy-bluefin-jig-outfit.md).

## Depth range and drop timing

- Dropped on marks the captain calls anywhere from as shallow as **100 ft**
  to as deep as **500 ft** (`3-0kaaHqA7A`) — the same **100–500 ft-class
  night-fish spread** already logged (as ~100 ft to 500–600 ft) in
  [bluefin rig ladder by grade — nighttime ladder (Gavin, Pacific Queen)](bluefin-rig-ladder-by-grade.md#nighttime-rig-ladder-by-aggressiveness-and-grade-gavin-pacific-queen).
- **Wait for the boat to fully stop before dropping:** on the captain's
  "drop them" call, let the boat come to a **complete end of its slide**
  before sending the jig down (`3-0kaaHqA7A`) — a timing cue distinct from,
  and additional to, the bow-vs-stern casting-position doctrine already
  logged in [knife jigging — mechanics](../techniques/knife-jigging.md#mechanics).
- Watch the line's depth marks on the drop; when the jig reaches the
  captain's called mark, put the reel in gear and start turning
  (`3-0kaaHqA7A`).

## Retrieve and redrop

- **Bite timing:** roughly half the time the jig gets picked up on the fall;
  most TG-jig bites come on a **speed burn back to the boat**, typically on
  the **first drop** (`3-0kaaHqA7A`).
- **If no bite on the first drop:** wind up roughly **10–15–20 cranks**,
  drop back down, and repeat (`3-0kaaHqA7A`) — a distinct numeric cadence
  from, not a correction of, the "wind 10 full turns (~30–40 ft),
  flutter-drop 3–4 sweeps" rockfish-style working pattern already logged in
  [knife jigging — depth control & jig calling](../techniques/knife-jigging.md#depth-control--jig-calling).

## Day, surface, size, and durability

The same TG jig gets re-rigged rather than swapped out as the day goes on
(`A7rveRBkL-g`):

- **Night (deep, graded/heavy bluefin):** the 200 lb "Jarry rig" bite leader
  above.
- **Daytime:** a **standard assist hook** fished on **80 lb or 100 lb**
  [line/leader class not otherwise specified] (`A7rveRBkL-g`) — consistent
  with the ~100 lb straight-tied assist hook cited above for the same
  daytime swap (`dBAk9NScxSc`).
- **Casting at surface fish:** the same jig on **popper and surface
  setups**, or on a dedicated **speed-jig setup** (`A7rveRBkL-g`).
- **Best window for the biggest fish:** dusk into full dark — described as
  hooking into something "you cannot turn the handle on" (`A7rveRBkL-g`).
- **Surface/foamer outfit, named gear (`CsKEtrcLgiQ`):** for casting at fish
  up on the surface in the morning, the "Jarry rig" bite leader comes off and
  the jig goes on a **9 ft, 50–100 lb-class popping rod**, typically spooled
  with **80 lb braid**, with a **single assist hook** direct-tied to **80–100
  lb fluorocarbon** — a more specific rod/braid pairing than the bare "80 lb
  or 100 lb" day-fishing range above, not a correction of it.
- **Why cast a tungsten jig at a foamer specifically:** being denser and
  smaller for the same weight, it casts farther and lands with a splash on
  the far side of (or into) the foamer, then swims back through it on a
  straight retrieve like a stickbait (`CsKEtrcLgiQ`, `dBAk9NScxSc`). Stated
  result: "a lot of short bites as well as a lot of hookups coming through
  those foamers, which you can't say for a lot of lures" — framed as a real
  edge over other lure classes for foamer work, not just a casting-distance
  convenience (`CsKEtrcLgiQ`).

**Size for weight and cast distance:** tungsten is **74% denser than lead**,
so a tungsten jig runs noticeably smaller than a lead jig of the same
weight — a **250 g tungsten jig runs the same physical size as a 120 g lead
jig** (`A7rveRBkL-g`), captioned "cold snipe"/"current sniper," which most
likely names the **Colt Sniper** metal casting jig (already documented up to
120 g in [tuna poppers and stickbaits — Colt Sniper](../lures/tuna-poppers-and-stickbaits.md#colt-sniper-stickbait--jerkbait-yellowtail--a-different-product-from-the-colt-sniper-casting-jig-above)),
matching that note's established "cold sniper" ASR-corruption pattern — name
not confirmed beyond the pattern match, asr-uncertain. Being smaller for the
same weight, the jig **casts noticeably farther** and can be fished on a
**heavier leader** at the same physical size — the stated reason to reach
for it on big bluefin specifically, where both casting distance and leader
strength matter (`A7rveRBkL-g`). A **100 g tungsten** jig is likewise named
as castable farther than an equivalent-size lead jig, by the same logic
(`A7rveRBkL-g`). A second data point compares a **250 g tungsten jig**
against named lead knife-jig models at the same 250 g — a **250 g "SK"
jig**, a **200 g Rip Roller**, and "a streaker" (all already documented as
named lead knife-jig models in [knife jigs](../lures/knife-jigs.md)) — a
different comparison than the 250 g ≈ 120 g figure above, kept side by side
as the same narrator's two framings of the same size-for-weight point, not
reconciled into one number (`CsKEtrcLgiQ`). Smaller size also gets the jig
down to the fish faster and presents a more realistic profile of what
bluefin are actually eating — tied directly to a trip where the fish were
"gorging on microbait day and night" (see the origin story in the evidence
file); framed as the trigger for reaching for a tungsten jig over a
same-weight lead jig when bait is small, not a claim that it's always the
better choice (`CsKEtrcLgiQ`).

**Durability:** tungsten doesn't bend or turn fragile under repeated
big-fish use and holds its paint better than lead (`A7rveRBkL-g`); it also
won't scuff on a bluefin's sharp teeth the way lead does (`CsKEtrcLgiQ`).
Stated reasoning: as long as the leader is heavy enough, an angler can "keep
sending it down there on those big bluefin" without babying the jig
(`A7rveRBkL-g`) — the source frames this as good for a "lifetime" of use,
explicitly conditioned on fishing it with **heavy line** and **not getting
it into tangles**, not an unconditional lifetime claim (`CsKEtrcLgiQ`).

**Price:** tungsten jigs in this class run **$40 to $90** (`CsKEtrcLgiQ`) —
context for why the source frames one as worth having in the box "just in
case," not a recommendation to buy a specific model.

**Additional named brands:** alongside the already-documented **Daiwa TG**
jig, the shop also stocks **Major Craft Jig Parra** tungsten jigs, with
**JF Jigging** tungsten jigs described as "on their way" — not yet in stock
as of the 2025-06-18 upload (`CsKEtrcLgiQ`).

**Rockfish — an attributed open item, not a documented practice:** the
narrator says he's "probably going to use" the tungsten jig for rockfish
"this year" — which resolves against the 2025-06-18 upload date to the
**2025** season — reasoning only that it "gets down there so fast."
Stated as a plan, not a result he'd already observed; captured here as an
attributed open item, not fishery doctrine (`CsKEtrcLgiQ`). See [rockfish
and lingcod](../species/rockfish-lingcod.md) if this is corroborated by a
future source.
