# Bluefin Rig Ladder by Grade

A line-class ladder matched to expected bluefin grade, spanning several
independently sourced rig sets — from a 15 lb finesse rig through PE-rated
jigging classes to 100 lb night outfits. The wide-open "chewing the paint off
the boat" bite happens less than 10% of the time; most days need a finesse
rig just to draw a bite at all, not a rig strong enough to land what's
already eating (`9JnIS8HkZlA`). This is a companion to the application-based
offshore quiver in [rod & reel selection](rod-and-reel-selection.md) and the
class lexicon in [gear classes](gear-classes.md); rig numbering below follows
each source, not a single house-standard ladder.

## Rig 1 — 15 lb finesse (the rig needed most)

Needed a couple of periods a year — typically late August into early
September, but really whenever the bite is tough rather than wide open
(`9JnIS8HkZlA`).

- **Reel:** single-speed star-drag, Penn Fathom/Torque 15-size class
  (10-fathom capacity).
- **Line:** braid, filled to leave roughly a 50 yd top shot.
- **Top shot:** 20 lb mono/fluoro test — lighter than the 25 lb commonly
  quoted publicly by captains; there's little practical difference between
  20 and 25 lb, but 20 draws noticeably more bites (`9JnIS8HkZlA`).
- **Leader:** fluorocarbon, sized so the knot never passes through the tip
  guide/roller on a cast — roughly 4 ft, though the right length varies with
  rod length (8 ft vs. 9 ft) and casting style (`9JnIS8HkZlA`). Cross-check
  against the leader-length table in [line & leader](line-and-leader.md).
- **Hook:** J hook, Owner Gorilla Light, size 2 (size 4 substitutes if
  unavailable) — a light hook that lets the bait swim (`9JnIS8HkZlA`; the
  product name is corroborated by `ptoIvB2MspE`). See [hooks](hooks.md) for
  the circle-vs-J framework this sits inside.
- **Knot:** a 4-turn surgeon's knot joins the monofilament to the
  fluorocarbon — see [surgeon's knot, mono-to-fluoro leader
  connection](../rigging/surgeons-knot-mono-to-fluoro.md) for the turn-count
  detail and how it compares to this KB's other mono-to-fluoro knot (Seaguar
  knot).
- **Ceiling:** do not fish this rig on fish over about 40 lb (`9JnIS8HkZlA`).

A closely-matching earlier citation of this same rig — reel filled 3/4 with
braid to a ~50 yd, 20 lb-test top shot, 4–5 ft of 20 lb fluorocarbon leader,
Owner Gorilla Light hook (there sized 4, not 2 — a discrepancy against this
rig's hook size) — is logged separately in [Searcher finesse live-bait
outfit — 20 lb and 15 lb](searcher-finesse-live-bait-outfit.md), which also
documents a lighter 15 lb top-shot step-down and an attributed 44 lb bluefin
catch on that step-down.

A second, independent citation corroborates and adds backing/rod/situational
detail (`dg1sbr6GuB8`), framed for school-size tuna, roughly 15–40 lb,
swimming in a big school but not biting well:

- **Reel:** Fathom 15, star drag — no two-speed needed at this grade
  (matches the reel above).
- **Rod:** rated 12–30 lb.
- **Line:** 300 yd of 30 lb test braid backing, topped with a 50 yd top shot
  of 20 or 25 lb test — a little longer than other top shots in this ladder.
  The stated reason: more stretch, because the hook is small; without
  stretch in the system, a hooked fish pulls the small hook right out of its
  mouth (`dg1sbr6GuB8`).
- **Leader:** 4–5 ft of fluorocarbon, tied to the monofilament top shot with
  a four-turn surgeon's knot — matches the knot and turn count above; a
  third citation of the same rule is logged in [surgeon's knot,
  mono-to-fluoro leader
  connection](../rigging/surgeons-knot-mono-to-fluoro.md#parameters--judgment).
- **Hook:** Owner Gorilla Light, size 2 — matches the hook above.
- **Ceiling:** don't fish this outfit when big tuna are around — "you're not
  going to catch a 50-pound tuna with this, you're not going to catch
  100-pound tuna with this," and they'll still eat it (`dg1sbr6GuB8`). See
  [evidence](evidence/bluefin-rig-ladder-by-grade.md) for an on-the-water
  instance of hooking, and not landing, large tuna on this exact rig.
- **A second application:** the same finesse rig doubles as a live-bait
  albacore rig, conditional on albacore returning in numbers — "it could
  happen this year or it may never happen" (`dg1sbr6GuB8`).

## Rig 2 — 30 lb, big fish finesse

A couple of times a year, 50–70 lb bluefin sit around the boat and won't eat
a 40 or 50 lb outfit — "they're 50, they're 60, they're 70 lb, they won't
bite on 40, they won't bite on 50... now you got to catch them on 30"
(`9JnIS8HkZlA`). An independent, earlier citation of the same doctrine
(`riEkdu8PEds`) describes the identical scenario — late July/August, 40–70 lb
tuna around the boat that won't eat 40 or 50 lb line but will eat 30 lb — as
a pattern that had held for the five years prior to its 2023-03-02 upload.
The full rig (reel/line/rod/leader/hook), the reel trade-off between a Penn
Fathom II 15 and a Penn Torque 15 Extra Narrow, and the fighting mechanic are
in [Searcher 30 lb large-tuna outfit](searcher-30lb-large-tuna-outfit.md) —
that earlier citation also names the hook this rig's "small Owner circle
hook" leaves unnamed: an Owner Mutu, same 2/4 sizing.

- **Reel:** two-speed, Penn Fathom 15-size — roughly 350 yd of braid plus a
  50 yd top shot (`9JnIS8HkZlA`).
- **Hook:** a small Owner circle hook — explicitly not the "Light" version
  and not the "hybrid" version (exact model asr-uncertain beyond those two
  exclusions), size 4 preferred, size 2 the largest to go, because the fish
  are still finicky even at this grade (`9JnIS8HkZlA`).
- **Why circle, at this grade specifically:** a big bluefin's teeth chew
  through a leader "in 5 minutes, less than it takes to land [one]"
  (`9JnIS8HkZlA`) — the circle hook's corner-of-the-mouth hookset keeps the
  leader off the teeth. Matches the circle-hook-for-bluefin mechanism in
  [hooks](hooks.md#circle-vs-j--the-core-decision-swrsycmt4tw-m2q22sppkem).
- **Fighting a 50–70 lb fish that circles below the boat instead of coming
  up:** the standard pump-up/reel-down technique lets the fish kick its tail
  and re-descend a little on every pump, which can protract the fight to an
  hour or two on a 65 lb fish — inviting sharks, gear tangles with other
  anglers, and other ways for it to go wrong. A continuous low-gear grind,
  not pump-and-reel, is the fix — full mechanism and the after-the-fight
  reminder in [two-speed low-gear fight](../techniques/two-speed-low-gear-fight.md)
  (`9JnIS8HkZlA`).

## Rig 3 — 40 lb (the starter rig)

The default recommendation for anyone new to bluefin fishing, including
family members on their first trip (`9JnIS8HkZlA`).

- **Reel:** two-speed, a ~25-size narrow-spool class reel (exact model
  syntax asr-uncertain beyond the size/narrow-spool description); 10-fathom
  capacity.
- **Line:** 50 lb braid, filled to leave room for 50 yd of 40 lb
  monofilament top shot. Rule of thumb: run the braid one class heavier than
  the top shot — 50 lb braid to a 40 lb top shot is the worked example, for
  lack of a true in-between braid weight to step just one size up
  (`9JnIS8HkZlA`).
- **Leader:** roughly 4 ft of fluorocarbon, same 40 lb test as the top shot;
  4-turn surgeon's knot, as Rig 1.
- **Hook:** Owner Mutu circle hook, size 4, sometimes 2 — same
  circle-hook-for-teeth reasoning as Rig 2, and consistent with the
  Owner-Mutu-by-grade doctrine in [hooks](hooks.md).
- **Ceiling / landing rate:** handles tuna up to about 100 lb, with a high
  landing rate once the drag is dialed in (`9JnIS8HkZlA`).
- **Two-speed reminder:** the same low-gear grind as Rig 2 applies once a
  fish is down deep and circling — stay in high gear for small/medium fish,
  drop to low only for a genuinely big fish, and shift back to high after
  landing the fish. The most common mistake is skipping that last step:
  anglers celebrating a catch often keep fishing in low gear without
  noticing (`9JnIS8HkZlA`). See [two-speed low-gear
  fight](../techniques/two-speed-low-gear-fight.md).

A closely-matching earlier data point for this same rig — a Penn Fathom 25
Narrow, two-speed, 65 lb braid to a 40 lb mono top shot, Owner Mutu circle
hook size 2 (sometimes 4), joined to fluorocarbon leader with the same
four-turn surgeon's knot — is logged separately in [Searcher 40 lb
all-around tuna outfit](searcher-40lb-all-around-tuna-outfit.md).

## Rig 4 — 50 lb ("50 PB"): live bait for grade, and the trolling-lure rig

- **When (live bait):** for a grade between Rig 3 and a 60 lb outfit —
  roughly 125 lb-class fish too big to fight comfortably on 30 lb but that
  won't commit to eating on 60 lb line. Not every angler needs this rig, but
  the in-between class is becoming more common (`9JnIS8HkZlA`).
- **Same outfit, second job — trolling a lure:** this 50 lb-class outfit
  also carries a favorite trolled tuna lure. Full lure/hook/leader detail (a
  Williamson-brand lure in the "current/cold sniper" class, the
  small-dried-sardine bait-insert detail, hook and treble-hook doctrine) is
  in [tuna feathers and
  skirts](../lures/tuna-feathers-and-skirts.md#a-trolled-currentcold-sniper-class-lure-steve-carson-penn)
  rather than duplicated here, since it's lure implementation, not a rod/reel
  spec.
- **Leader (troll application):** tie the lure direct to the 50 lb top shot,
  or — the preferred build when a 125 lb-class grade is expected — run a
  short piece of 100 lb fluorocarbon ahead of the lure so the mainline
  itself is protected from the fish's teeth; a circle hook isn't an option
  on this lure style, so bite-through risk sits on whatever line is closest
  to the hook (`9JnIS8HkZlA`).

## Jigging rig ladder — PE-rated rod classes

A separate, jigging-specific line-class ladder, distinct from the
live-bait/troll rigs above (`Et2TUskzCSs`). PE ratings are tied to the jig
weight each class **activates** — loads and works the blank — and to the
fighting-drag range run on bluefin at that class, rather than to a bare
line-test number.

- **PE 6–10:** activates 250–500 g jigs. Fighting drag runs ~25 lb as a
  comfortable starting point; some anglers push to 40 lb, and a few claim
  45 lb (`Et2TUskzCSs`).
- **PE 4–8:** activates 150–350 g jigs. Fighting drag ~20–30 lb, sometimes
  35 lb, with 30 lb the realistic ceiling (`Et2TUskzCSs`).
- **PE 3–6** (the lightest class): activates 100–250 g jigs. Fighting drag
  ~15 lb, max around 25 lb (`Et2TUskzCSs`).

A second data point, one year later, adds rod lengths and West-Coast-line-
class equivalents to the same PE tiers (`gimFbgN5Jjk`), framing rod choice
around three things a sportboat bluefin rod has to satisfy at once —
handling the drag pressure needed to land the fish, landing it efficiently
enough not to tie up the rail, and still activating the right jig-weight
range — rather than picking by target-fish size alone. Two picks, if
narrowing a sportboat quiver to just two rods, both 5.5–6 ft:

- **PE 4–8** ("80 lb stick"): activates 150–300 g, sometimes up to 350 g,
  jigs; handles roughly 20–30 lb of drag. The single rod to bring if only
  bringing one.
- **PE 6–10** ("100 lb stick"): activates jigs as light as 200 g, typically
  250–500 g; handles 25–40 lb of drag. The bigger-fish and night pick.

Both ranges sit inside, not beyond, the PE 4–8 / PE 6–10 windows already
logged above — corroboration with rod-length detail added, not a new number
(`gimFbgN5Jjk`). A rod's printed jig-weight rating is a starting point that
needs on-the-water testing to dial in a working compromise. Even inside the
overlap range, a jig activates better on the lighter-rated of two rods: a
250 g jig, which both a PE 4–8 and a PE 6–10 can technically move, does
noticeably better on the lighter PE 4–8 — pick the lightest rod that still
activates the jig being fished, not the heaviest one that can
(`gimFbgN5Jjk`). Guide construction on these blanks (spiral/acid-wrap vs.
traditional, and the matching narrow-reel choice) is its own decision — see
[spiral-wrap guides and narrow reels for jigging rods](jigging-rod-guide-wrap.md).

**Spiral-wrapped ("acid-wrap") guides**, standard on these blanks
(`gimFbgN5Jjk`): the guide train spirals to face progressively downward,
transitioning the rod toward a spinning-rod guide layout by the tip section.
Two benefits over a conventional guide train: less side-to-side twisting
play under heavy load, and geometry that stops the line from grabbing the
rod tip during the jigging motion — the same tip-wrap failure mode logged as
the reason for spiral/acid wraps in [knife
jigging](../techniques/knife-jigging.md#gear-class-detail). The trade-off:
the transition zone is unforgiving of a poorly timed cadence — slack line
that jumps up and wraps a guide right as a big bluefin comes tight on 25 lb
of drag can take guides off the blank with it.

Cross-reference: [knife / speed jigging](../techniques/knife-jigging.md) and
[slow-pitch jigging](../techniques/slow-pitch-jigging.md) own the mechanics
these classes are fished with; [gear classes](gear-classes.md) is the
class-term lexicon this ladder feeds.

## Daytime rig ladder by grade

A third, daytime-specific ladder (`G1YIlakiRqg`), read alongside — not
merged with — the Rig 1–4 ladder above, since the rig numbering and line
classes don't line up one-to-one. This source's boat range runs 5 lb up to
200+ lb bluefin/yellowfin in the same season, so the ladder works bottom-up,
lightest to heaviest, rather than naming one do-it-all rig.

Each rung below is a line class in the
[gear-classes](gear-classes.md#line-classes) sense — the class term is what
a [profile](../profiles/README.md) resolves against; the named models are
one source's instances of the class, not the class itself.

- **25 lb all-purpose flyline setup** (25 lb [line
  class](gear-classes.md#line-classes)). An 8–8.5 ft rod rated 15–30 lb or
  15–40 lb (a Calstar L-series 700/800/850/900, specifically the 850L) on a
  two-speed Fathom 15 reel. 30 lb braid to 15–25 ft of fluorocarbon — long
  enough that the fish doesn't see the Spectra, short enough that a
  flylined sardine doesn't have to pull a lot of thicker line through the
  water (`G1YIlakiRqg`). Two-speed is run even at this light class, because
  25 lb-class SoCal bluefin trips regularly hook 40–60, even 80 lb fish on
  25 lb test (`G1YIlakiRqg`; corroborating data point in [two-speed low-gear
  fight](../techniques/two-speed-low-gear-fight.md)). Fish to 80 lb have
  been landed on this exact setup.
- **Same style, bumped one size:** a Fathom 15 Narrow — similar line
  capacity to the Fathom 15 above but bigger gears/drag, more suited to
  bigger fish — with 40 lb braid to 15–25 ft, sometimes 30–40 lb,
  fluorocarbon, on a Seeker T80 rod (roughly 8'6", rated 20–50 lb)
  (`G1YIlakiRqg`).
- **40 lb setup** (40 lb [line class](gear-classes.md#line-classes)). For
  30–60 lb fish, more aggressive fish, or bigger bait: a Fathom 25 reel with
  40 lb Spectra on a Calstar 6480 rod, 15–25 ft fluorocarbon leader — same
  leader-length logic as above (`G1YIlakiRqg`).
- **50–60 lb jig setup** (50–60 lb [line
  class](gear-classes.md#line-classes)). For Colt Sniper-style (or any
  style) jig fishing, springtime 15–80 lb-class fish: a Talica 12 reel,
  50 lb Spectra, 7–8 ft rod rated 30–60 lb or 40–80 lb. Leader runs longer
  for jig fishing specifically — 30–40 ft of fluorocarbon: long enough to
  stay invisible underwater and draw more bites, short enough that the lure
  doesn't have to pull excess thick line off the reel or sink slower under
  the extra fluoro's drag (`G1YIlakiRqg`).
- **Sinker-rig setup.** For fish marked deep: a 7–8 ft rod rated 40–80 lb or
  50–100 lb — "your standard 80 or 100 lb rod" — spooled with 80 lb Spectra,
  Power Pro Max Quattro in 80 or 100 lb preferred for its thin diameter and
  strength, which helps the rig stay vertical in a fast drift and reach
  depth faster (`G1YIlakiRqg`). Mark unmarked Spectra every 100 or 50 ft
  with a Sharpie (a Rapala line counter helps measure it off the back of the
  boat), or use pre-marked multicolor braid that changes color every 100 ft;
  the same marking habit applies to inshore yellowtail jigs at the islands,
  for always knowing exactly where the lure/bait is (`G1YIlakiRqg`).
  Izorline's multicolored Spectra runs six colors, each 100 ft long
  (`dkY7wJ4UM1c`) — resolves an earlier asr-uncertain brand flag on this
  line. Worked example: a captain calls "drop it 300 feet," the angler
  counts three color changes going by, then stops, and is 300 ft down — the
  same 100-ft-per-color counting mechanism as manual marking, pre-marked at
  the factory instead.
- **Knife-jig setup — a PE 4–8 rod as the one-rod answer.** The pick if
  buying only one rod to cover everything: a rod rated PE 4–8 (PE × 10 ≈
  lb-test, so 40–80 lb), fishing jigs from about 150 g up (no stated upper
  bound on the rod itself). Reel options: a Jigging Master PE7, or — the
  budget pick — a Fathom 40, filled with 80 or 100 lb Power Pro Max Quattro,
  100 lb mono or fluoro leader 30–40 ft, working 150–250 g jigs
  (`G1YIlakiRqg`).

## Nighttime rig ladder by aggressiveness and grade

Same seminar (`G1YIlakiRqg`). Night bluefin fishing used to call for one
massive rod/reel/lure, and sometimes still does — but current practice calls
for at least two, ideally three, nighttime outfits to cover how aggressive
the fish are, their size, and ocean conditions (windy vs. flat, fast vs.
slow drift), because night fish can show anywhere from ~100 ft down to
500–600 ft (`G1YIlakiRqg`). Lure weight, profile shape, and hook detail for
both outfits below are in [knife jigs](../lures/knife-jigs.md); casting
position, rod-holding, and the missed-bite redrop technique are in [knife
jigging](../techniques/knife-jigging.md).

- **Heavy go-to outfit — the default whenever the fish will let it be
  fished.** A Shimano Talica 25 (over 300 lb landed on this exact setup),
  spooled nearly to the top with 100 lb multicolor Spectra, color-marked
  every 100 ft with a Sharpie mark at the halfway point of each 100 ft
  section for an effective 50 ft resolution (some brands mark every
  30 ft/10 m instead) (`G1YIlakiRqg`). A wind-on leader, 130–200 lb, roughly
  25 ft — see [wind-on
  leader](../rigging/wind-on-leader.md#why-a-wind-on-over-a-separate-crimped-fight-leader-gavin-pacific-queen)
  for the reasoning (no separate crimped fight leader/swivel, fewer failure
  points, far less tangling on a 30-plus-angler sportboat rail); the lure
  ties or crimps directly to the wind-on's terminal end.
- **Lighter outfit for fish under ~60 lb.** Same build as the daytime
  50–60 lb jig setup above (Talica 12 class), fished at night instead:
  50–60 lb Spectra nearly to the top, 30–40 ft of roughly 60 lb fluorocarbon
  leader, on a smaller reel/spool overall — a program built specifically for
  the 15–60 lb grade, which in 2023 was showing up higher in the water
  column than the bigger fish. A smaller outfit lets a light lure sink to
  the fish's depth roughly twice as fast as the same lure would on the heavy
  Talica 25 outfit (`G1YIlakiRqg`).
- **Rig selection is grade-driven, and grade dictates line class more than
  it dictates target species:** smaller fish land faster on the heavy
  outfit too, but the lighter build gets bit when the heavy one won't
  (`G1YIlakiRqg`).
- **Fish the heaviest tackle that will still draw bites — a stop-and-go
  framing.** Much night fishing is short-stop/stop-and-go: the captain
  spots a school, stops the boat, and the fish may only hold around the
  boat for 1–2 minutes before moving off. Landing a fish quickly on heavy
  gear keeps the whole boat fishing through that short window; a fish that
  outgrades a 50 lb outfit can take up to an hour to land on it, tying up
  one angler and costing the other 30-plus anglers aboard their shot at the
  same school before it leaves (`G1YIlakiRqg`).

## Drag — weakest-link setting, and a scope-shift trap

- **Setting method:** starting from the reel's free-spool position (drag
  lever backed all the way off), roll the drag lever forward by feel —
  pulling the line by hand — until it's close to right, then confirm with a
  drag scale (`9JnIS8HkZlA`).
- **Weakest-link rule:** identify the setup's weakest link — top shot,
  braid, or a knot not fully trusted (have a crewmate re-tie it if so) —
  then set the strike drag at no more than one-third of that link's pound
  test; a quarter (25%) is the more usual target, since the lever can always
  be advanced toward full for more drag once the fish's initial hard run is
  over (`9JnIS8HkZlA`). Corroborates the quarter-to-a-third rule already
  logged in [drag setting](drag-setting.md).
- **A scope-shift trap — spool diameter changes effective drag (mechanism,
  also logged in [drag setting](drag-setting.md)):** dropping a jig 400 ft
  down can already leave a spool a quarter to a third empty; if the hooked
  fish then runs another 100–200 yd, the spool can end up two-thirds empty
  with the drag lever never touched. A smaller-diameter spool multiplies the
  same lever setting into much higher actual drag, so a drag correctly set
  at hookup can be dangerously tight by the time a deep-hooked fish has
  taken a long run without the angler changing anything. Re-check (or back
  off) the drag after a long run on a rig that started deep or heavily
  spooled down, rather than trusting the initial setting through the whole
  fight (`9JnIS8HkZlA`).

## Two-speed threshold, and a half-to-full-day line-class ladder

A separate line-class ladder framed around yellowtail first, tuna second
(`UP_3fBu7VPA`), kept side by side with the ladder above rather than merged
in — the rig numbering and line classes don't line up one-to-one between the
two.

- **The two-speed threshold is genuinely unsettled, not a fixed number.**
  One answer is 30 lb line class; younger, stronger anglers push for 40 lb,
  and a few go as far as 50 lb before calling a two-speed a must-have
  (`UP_3fBu7VPA`).
- **Target species matters more than line class for this decision.** A
  two-speed isn't required to fish 40 lb — modern lightweight two-speed
  reels (Penn Fathom-class) fish light line and flyline well regardless —
  but whether a two-speed is worth having turns on the target: seabass and
  yellowtail don't reward fishing one, while 50–80 lb bluefin on 30–40 lb
  line benefit heavily from dropping into low gear and gaining line an inch
  at a time, because a single-speed reel's gear ratio is too high to crank
  effectively against sustained resistance (`UP_3fBu7VPA`). A 25 lb-class
  two-speed (e.g. a Fathom 25 / Saltist 35 class) is an especially
  versatile, budget-friendly all-rounder for a newer or budget-conscious
  angler: it flylines well and lands fish up to roughly 80–100 lb on
  65–80 lb braid.
- **A single-speed data point, for scope:** 60–70 lb bluefin were landed on
  25 lb single-speed gear in 2023 — not the ideal situation, but doable
  (`UP_3fBu7VPA`); kept beside, not a correction to, the
  two-speed-benefits-big-fish doctrine above.
- **Half-day vs. overnight gear range:** one or two rods covers a half-day
  trip; an overnight trip — especially with tuna around — calls for the
  full range, up to and including a 100 lb rig "just in case," since recent
  seasons' nighttime fishing and bigger average grade make it worth having
  even if it goes unused (`UP_3fBu7VPA`). See the parallel "bring a range"
  doctrine in [rod & reel
  selection](rod-and-reel-selection.md#sportboat-bring-a-range-day-and-night).
- **Loaner/rental top-shot convention:** for gear that goes out to guests of
  widely varying skill, a 25–50 yard mono top shot ahead of a fluorocarbon
  leader is the stated figure; a tighter version of the same convention runs
  3/4 spool of braid to a 25–30 yard top shot (`UP_3fBu7VPA`). Keeping some
  mono in the system rather than running braid straight to a bare fluoro
  leader serves two purposes: a guest who doesn't know how to tie a leader
  can still tie a hook straight onto plain mono, and a short fluorocarbon
  leader shrinks fast under repeated cut-and-retie after each fish, so the
  mono top shot buffers that wear rather than the leader itself
  (`UP_3fBu7VPA`).
- **Leader-length floor — don't let retying shrink it below ~5–6 ft:**
  repeated cut-and-retie cycles boating fish can whittle a leader down to as
  short as 1–2 ft, at which length bites drop off; 5–6 ft is the stated
  minimum to rebuild back up to, rather than fishing it out at 1–2 ft
  (`UP_3fBu7VPA`).
