# Searcher Yellowtail Live-Bait Sliding-Sinker Rig — 30 lb Class (Steve Carson, Penn/SearcherSportfishing, 2024-04-25)

Steve Carson, dockside in San Diego alongside the **Searcher** (`yGXSrUauo2w`,
"Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 231 (Sliding Sinker for Yellowtail)," uploaded
2024-04-25 — a 2:44 pre-season segment). `searcher-sportfishing` is a
**registered** channel/series and Carson a recurring named voice on it, but
this specific rig build is this note's only citation so far in this KB
(single mention) — the mechanism/parameter content below reads **medium**
confidence per the rubric; the named reel model is a product pick and stays
**low** confidence regardless, per this channel's sponsored-claim caveat
(Carson is Penn's rep). Framed on camera as "one of the most popular rigs for
catching yellowtail" with live bait, and explicitly **the standard bread-and-
butter rig for both local and long-range yellowtail fishing out of San
Diego** — contrasted on camera with blue/yellowfin tuna, wahoo, and dorado as
the trip's other, longer-range targets.

## Applies to

**Yellowtail in the 12–22 lb range** — Carson's own stated ceiling for this
build ("if the yil are not too big... maybe in the 12 to 22 pound range").
No heavier-grade version is given on camera.

## The base outfit — same 30 lb rig family as the tuna build

- **Reel:** Penn Fathom 15, **two-speed**.
- **Rod:** **8 ft**, rated **15–40 lb**.
- **Line:** **30 lb** top shot (mono).

Carson frames this rod/reel/line combination as **already covered in an
earlier Searcher video** ("we've done videos on before... look them up on the
Searcher Channel") rather than new material — that earlier video isn't yet in
this KB's corpus, so the outfit spec here is carried as stated rather than
independently sourced. It closely matches — same 8 ft, 15–40 lb-rated rod,
same 30 lb top shot, same reel family (a **Fathom II 15** there vs. a plain
**Fathom 15** here; generation not specified on camera in this shorter video,
kept side by side rather than merged) — the
[Searcher 30 lb large-tuna outfit](searcher-30lb-large-tuna-outfit.md),
Carson's own dedicated 30 lb rig for 50–70 lb tuna. **The two builds diverge
at the hook and terminal end**, not the rod/reel/line: the tuna outfit runs a
circle hook straight to the leader, while this yellowtail build swaps in a J
hook and a sliding sinker — see both sections below.

## Hook — J over circle, sized to the bait

- For yellowtail up to about 22 lb, **a J hook is better than a circle hook**
  — stated on camera as the one thing that differs from Carson's tuna rig
  above. **A circle hook will still work**, just not his preference.
- **Size:** roughly **2/0**, stepping up to **4/0** if the bait is large.
  (Captions render these as "20" and "40" — read here as the aught sizes 2/0
  and 4/0, not bare numbers, since a size-20/size-40 hook doesn't exist in
  this fishery and the reading lines up with the 2/0 yellowtail J-hook figure
  already logged in [hooks](hooks.md#picking-by-whats-biting-mid-trip-gevnj2y1ep8-2023-09-02).)
- Corroborates, rather than introduces, the existing **"J hooks recommended
  for yellowtail"** doctrine already logged in
  [hooks — circle vs. J, the core decision](hooks.md#circle-vs-j--the-core-decision-swrsycmt4tw-m2q22sppkem) —
  see that note for the full cross-source doctrine and the reasoning (reaction
  eaters, fish that run at the boat).

## Sliding sinker — straight to the hook, no stopper

- **Egg sinker, sliding directly down to the hook** — "slides unfettered
  right down to the hook." Carson is explicit that **no bead, stopper, or
  other hardware is needed**: "you don't need any stoppers or anything else,
  none of that is necessary."
- **This is a leaner build than the Carolina-rig format** documented as this
  KB's general sliding-sinker pattern in
  [techniques/sliding-sinker.md](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md#building-the-rig)
  (egg sinker → bead → swivel → leader → hook) — no swivel or separate leader
  section is shown or described on camera here, kept side by side as a
  simpler, single-piece variant rather than reconciled into that format.
- **Weight:** **2, 3, or 4 oz**, chosen for the day's conditions — step up for
  more wind or when the fish are sitting deeper. **Above 4 oz, Carson says a
  different rig works better** (not detailed in this video — a forward
  reference to unaired content, not a corpus source). (asr-uncertain: the
  caption also renders a fragment — "this little one is just a one" —
  immediately before the 2/3/4 oz figures, while Carson appears to be holding
  up multiple sinkers on camera; this could indicate a lighter ~1 oz option
  was also shown, but the caption is too garbled to confidently add it as a
  fourth weight, so only the clearly stated 2–4 oz range is carried here.)
- **Adjust weight through the day** as the fish "change their mind" — go up
  or down in weight mid-session rather than treating the day's starting
  weight as fixed.

## Why — stated reasons

- **Gets the bait away from the boat.**
- **Gets the bait under bonita (or similar) working the surface, and in
  front of the yellowtail more quickly** than an unweighted presentation
  would — Carson's own stated reason for reaching for this rig over a
  flylined bait specifically when surface bycatch is intercepting baits
  before they reach the target fish.

## Weight contrast with other yellowtail sliding-sinker data points

Carson's 2–4 oz working range is **notably heavier** than the ~1/4–1/2 oz
egg-sinker weights logged as single-trip Observed data points for island
yellowtail (Cedros, San Benito, Catalina, San Clemente Island) in
[techniques/sliding-sinker.md](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md#cedros-yellowtail-egg-sinker-weight-stokedonfishing) —
kept side by side, not reconciled: this is a San Diego local/long-range
dockside briefing from a registered channel/voice versus several unregistered,
single-trip island observations, and Carson doesn't specify a location beyond
"aboard the Searcher."
