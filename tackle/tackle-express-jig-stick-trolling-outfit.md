## The dual-purpose setup

An **8-ft jig stick** (Calstar blank) paired with a **20-size reel** — named
on camera both as **Torium** and **Trinidad's**, with the reel in this
specific clip captioned "Calstar Trinidad 20" (read as the Calstar rod
paired with a Trinidad reel, not a single product name) — spooled with
**65 lb braid**, giving **close to 400 yd** of capacity. The stated reason
for that much capacity: so you don't have to worry about getting spooled.

The speaker runs this same outfit as **both** his jig stick and his trolling
outfit, giving the reason on camera only as "it was easy" — no further
mechanism given for why one rod covers both jobs. **"That 20-size reel is
kind of the size I like to fish"** for this dual-purpose role — stated as a
personal preference, not elaborated against other sizes.

This is the same **8–10 ft / 25–60 lb surface-iron / jig-stick class**
already logged in [gear classes](gear-classes.md#rod--reel-classes) — this
clip adds a second application (trolling) to that class alongside surface
iron, plus the specific reel models (Torium, Trinidad) and line
capacity/class figures above.

## Clicker sequence — troll, then convert to fight mode

A reel-clicker cadence for running this outfit out trolling and converting
straight to fighting a fish on the bite, given step by step on camera:

1. **Clicker on, drag backed off a couple of clicks** — this is the trolling
   setting: clicker audibly signals a strike, drag light enough not to load
   up the rod while it's just dragging a lure.
2. **On a bite: advance the drag two to three clicks, and turn the clicker
   off** — that converts the outfit from "trolling" to "fighting the fish
   you're now on."

No further detail (drag numbers, line class this was tested against beyond
the 65 lb braid above, or what lure/species this was used for) is given in
this short clip.
