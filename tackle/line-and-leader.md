# Line & Leader — Braid, Mono, Fluoro by Application

Three materials, three jobs. Braid is the **main line** (capacity, no stretch,
sensitivity, sink); mono and fluoro are the **top shot / leader** spliced or
knotted on top of it. The spool from reel to hook, in SoCal, is almost always
**braid backing → mono or fluoro top shot → (short heavier fluoro bite leader on
big fish)**. This note covers *which material, what weight, and why*, by
application. Rod/reel matching lives in
[rod & reel selection](rod-and-reel-selection.md); the class shorthand is in
[gear classes](gear-classes.md).

## The three materials — what each one actually does

| Material | Behavior in water | Where it earns its place |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Braid (spectra)** | No stretch, tiny diameter for its strength, neutrally buoyant, high sensitivity | Main line / backing — capacity and feel |
| **Monofilament** | Stretches, has memory (coils), **suspends** (near-neutral), absorbs water | Top shot for stretch/forgiveness; internal line on spreader-bar setups |
| **Fluorocarbon** | **Sinks**, extremely abrasion-resistant, UV-resistant, doesn't absorb water, near-**invisible** underwater | Leaders and top shots — stealth + abrasion at the hook |

Mono suspends and fluoro sinks because they are different chemical compounds,
not the same line in different colors (`GoVI7CtN6L8`). Braid and mono are both
fairly visible; fluoro is the invisible one. A retailer's product-specific
counterpoint on mono color/visibility is a promotional claim rather than
independent testing, logged in
[Izorline XXX — spool sizes, colors](tackle-express-izorline-xxx-mono.md).

## When braid — and how heavy

Braid is the main line on essentially every SoCal setup. Choosing the class
(`aXF0bxAFtU0`):

- **Downsize, don't upsize.** 65 lb braid is roughly the diameter of 20 lb mono,
  so the reflex to "fish 65 for everything" is a mistake. Lighter braid loads a
  smaller reel, casts better, and — because it presents **less water drag** —
  scopes straighter when you drop deep. At 700 ft on an electric reel, a spool
  of 50 lb braid scopes down far more than 30 lb braid because of the extra
  resistance to current/drift (`aXF0bxAFtU0`). Fish the lightest braid the job
  allows: mostly **30 or 50 lb**, up to **65** on a jig stick, **65–80** for
  casting at 100 lb-class bluefin. **Below 30 lb** braid gets too thin for a
  baitcaster (fine on a spinning reel).
- **A steeper diameter-equivalence example, and the stealth reason for a
  leader stated plainly.** Braid is a woven, threaded material — strong and
  rigid. One worked example: an **8 lb braided line has a diameter equivalent
  to 1 lb-test** monofilament/fluorocarbon (`jeJAsY_M0oc`) — a steeper
  thinness ratio than the 65 lb-braid ≈ 20 lb-mono comparison just above; both
  are independent sources' own numbers, not one reconciled ratio. Fishing
  straight braid to the lure lets the fish see the line and costs bites — the
  same stealth/visibility reasoning behind this note's fluoro-leader doctrine
  elsewhere in this section (`jeJAsY_M0oc`).
- **Braid over mono as the main line for deep bottom fishing.** For a rockfish
  quick gangion fished at **400–600 ft**, spool with braid, not mono — mono
  "has a tendency to want to float," so it won't get a bait straight down to
  the fish fast enough, and the small target spots typical of rockfish
  grounds punish a slow, scoped drop (`zVIfArUrpDI`). Fill the reel **all the
  way to the rim** so there's no capacity shortfall on the drop or the fight
  (`zVIfArUrpDI`). Line-class and rig detail in
  [rockfish deep-dropping](../techniques/rockfish-deep-dropping.md#line-class-and-spooling-dave-hansen).
- **Thinner braid sinks faster.** On tuna gear, an 80 lb braid sinks faster
  than 100 lb for the same reason it scopes less — less diameter, less drag
  (cameron; see [gear classes](gear-classes.md) line-class notes). Reach for
  the thinner class when you need a bait or jig down quickly.
- **A straight-pull "you won't break it" claim, tied to jig weight.** Two shop
  staff report never having broken 80 lb braid on a rod and reel by pulling on
  it — there isn't enough drag pressure available on a reel to part it
  (`Rtwz0oEfrKw`). This is consistent with, not a correction of, the
  diameter-over-label doctrine below: braid's real-world failure mode is
  abrasion, not straight-pull breaking. For jig fishing: **65 lb braid for
  jigs 200 g and under** (worked example: **120–150 g**), letting the jig
  sink to depth without needing heavy drag to hold it — the same
  thinner-braid-sinks-faster logic above (`Rtwz0oEfrKw`). 65 lb holds fine in
  a straight pull "if you're not rubbing against the side of the boat" — the
  risk at that class is hull abrasion, not breaking strength (`Rtwz0oEfrKw`).
  - **The same interview session, uploaded a day later, extends the ladder.**
    For **350 g jigs or heavier**, the stated pick is **80 lb braid**
    (`mgUjxUoGkZU`). Named contrast: some anglers fish 100 or 130 lb at that
    jig weight, which is called unnecessary — a rod-and-reel's drag
    realistically never loads hard enough to break 80 lb (`mgUjxUoGkZU`).
    Together with the ≤200 g → 65 lb pick above, this gives a two-point
    ladder — **≤200 g → 65 lb; 350 g+ → 80 lb** — with the 200–350 g gap
    unaddressed in either clip.

### Actual diameter over label — three different "20 lb" numbers (James Kikkawa, PCS Show 2024 "Jigging 101" panel, `Et2TUskzCSs`, uploaded 2024-03-16; unregistered channel/panel — medium confidence)

For jigging specifically, where keeping the line vertical in the water is the
whole game (see the downsize/scope logic above), don't trust the number on
the spool — there are three different, non-equivalent ways a line gets called
"20 lb," and only one of them tells you what you actually want to know
(`Et2TUskzCSs`):

- **PE rating** (PE1, PE2, PE3...) is a diameter-class standard, roughly
  equivalent to 10 lb / 20 lb / 30 lb steps — a sizing convention, not a
  breaking-strength claim.
- **IGFA-class line** is rated to break **at or near its stated number** — a
  12 lb IGFA-class line breaks around 12 lb, by design, for record-keeping
  purposes.
- **What a manufacturer prints on the spool** is often neither — a line sold
  as "20 lb" braid tested in a **straight pull typically broke in the 36–41
  lb range** across multiple brands tested (`Et2TUskzCSs`). Braid's
  real-world failure mode is **abrasion, not straight-pull breaking**
  anyway — if the braid is in rocks or structure, the fight is already lost
  regardless of the printed number, so straight-pull strength is the wrong
  thing to worry about; **diameter for scope/current resistance** is the
  right thing.
- **Verifying actual diameter** is a testing-methodology point, not a
  SoCal-specific claim: because printed diameters are unreliable across
  brands, an Australian line-testing lab measures actual line diameter under
  a **microscopic camera** rather than trusting labels — the only real way to
  compare competing braid brands (`Et2TUskzCSs`).
- **Why this matters for jigging specifically:** picture the length of line
  in the water as a long strip of fabric — a **50 lb braid is roughly 2.5×
  the diameter of 20 lb**, presenting proportionally more surface area to
  current and boat drift, so it **scopes out** (pulls off-vertical) far more
  than the thinner line for the same conditions (`Et2TUskzCSs`). This is the
  mechanism behind this note's existing thinner-braid-scopes-less doctrine
  above, restated with the diameter-over-label framing. **Reel-fill
  implication:** keep the reel spooled as full as possible for consistent
  inches-per-crank; **effective drag climbs the deeper into the spool you
  go** — a drag set correctly at the surface reads tighter once you're
  fishing deep into the spool, so the deeper you're fishing, the lighter your
  strike-drag setting should be (`Et2TUskzCSs`). This corroborates, from a
  second source, the spool-diameter drag trap already logged in
  [drag setting](drag-setting.md) and in
  [slow-pitch jigging](../techniques/slow-pitch-jigging.md#fighting--landing-light-tackle).

### "Line-class rod rating" is obsolete on modern braid gear (`ZoIzpyyS9xw`)

Cross-panel doctrine from a Fred Hall Show calico bass panel: with modern
high-strength braid, the printed line-class number on a rod ("8–16,"
"20–30") has stopped meaning anything useful.

- One signature-rod build had the manufacturer print **"unlimited"** rather
  than a line-class number: *"that line rating on the rods [is] pretty much a
  thing of the past"* (`ZoIzpyyS9xw`). What actually matters is **how heavy a
  bait/head the rod tip can load and cast**, not a breaking-strength
  suggestion — a rod tied straight to 65 lb braid with the drag locked down
  completely held without breaking the blank; "you can't break a rod unless
  you high-stick it" (`ZoIzpyyS9xw`).
- The same point from the line side: a **7 ft rod rated 12–20 lb mono** is
  spooled with **50–65 lb braid** — the printed mono rating and the braid
  actually on the reel are two different numbers, because braid has been
  around for roughly **14–15 years** and became popular in the **last eight
  years** as of this 2013 talk (i.e., roughly since 2005), and printed rod
  ratings "have yet to catch up to what we're doing" (`ZoIzpyyS9xw`).
- **Practical takeaway:** size the rod to the lure weight you're casting (see
  [gear classes](gear-classes.md)), not to a line-class number printed on the
  blank — the number no longer constrains what braid you actually run.

### Why heavier braid than the fish requires — spool mechanics, not fish strength

Even where 30 lb straight through could land the fish, big-bait swimbait
fishing (8–10 in swimbaits) calls for **50–60 lb+ braid** for a mechanical
reason that has nothing to do with breaking strength (`ZoIzpyyS9xw`):

- **Thin braid digs into itself on the spool** under the load of casting and
  fighting a fish with a big bait — a 30 lb-diameter braid packed tight will
  cut down into the layers beneath it, and the next cast the buried loop
  catches, killing the cast or parting the line. Bumping the spool to 50–60
  lb (bigger diameter, same or lower actual breaking-strength headroom
  needed) keeps the wraps from digging in (`ZoIzpyyS9xw`).
- **A related, more dangerous failure mode:** after fighting and landing a
  fish on zero-stretch spectra, failing to **peel line back off the reel to
  where the hook was originally set** before the next cast lets the fight's
  tension **cut a groove down through the spool**. The next cast runs out
  fine until it hits that groove, where the line "just stops" — bad enough
  that the stated advice was to throw the reel away rather than try to clear
  the backlash (`ZoIzpyyS9xw`). Fix: **strip line back out to where the hook
  was set** (not just re-spool loosely) before the next cast on any fish that
  pulled hard against a locked or heavy drag.

### Trophy calico bass tackle (n6yun, Fred Hall panel, 2013-03-09)

Targeting trophy (7–10 lb+) calico bass, three panelists gave different
line/leader builds rather than one shared number (`ZoIzpyyS9xw`); the numbers
vary, but the panel's own minimum-line discussion below states the real
variable is where the fish gets hooked relative to structure, not line class.
Set against the bait-size doctrine in
[calico bass](../species/calico-bass.md#doctrine--conflicts).

- **Setup 1:** a rod rated 15–30 lb monofilament, spooled with 65–80 lb
  PowerPro, tied straight to the swimbait (no leader) for a 5.5 in Big
  Hammer; for **weedless** baits, 80 lb braid to a 60 lb leader. Reel: a
  Shimano Calcutta D400, on an "extra heavy" rod (`ZoIzpyyS9xw`).
- **Setup 2:** runs lighter than the others — **40 lb braid** straight-tied
  on inshore/small-reel setups, stepping to **50 or 65 lb** on heavier
  setups. Prefers a rod with a **softer tip** to make up for braid's lack of
  stretch, giving the fish a beat longer to take the bait before the hookset
  (`ZoIzpyyS9xw`).
- **Setup 3:** minimum **50 lb braid**, up to **80 lb**, with **short
  top-shot leaders of 50, 60, or 80 lb fluorocarbon** matched to the braid
  class (`ZoIzpyyS9xw`).
- **Minimum line for a casual/family angler, and the real variable behind
  landing odds:** answering an audience question about a reasonable minimum
  (fishing 20–30 lb with kids aboard), **30 lb is fine** as a floor, but the
  odds of **losing** a big fish on 30 lb climb on an outside kelp edge (e.g.,
  Palos Verdes) versus fishing inside the kelp, because of abrasion. Line
  class is not the dominant variable, though: the panelist's own biggest
  local calico bass came on **20 lb Spider Wire Ultracast** to a short 20 lb
  leader, straight-tied, no wire. **What actually decides it is where the
  fish gets hooked relative to structure** — a fish hooked in open water over
  soft bottom (e.g., eelgrass, 15 ft) gives a real fight; a fish hooked in
  3 ft of water over surging rock only has to cover the short distance it
  takes to fully load the rod before it reaches structure and cuts off,
  regardless of line strength (`ZoIzpyyS9xw`).
- **Leader-check discipline after a big fish** (⚠ asr-uncertain: speaker
  surname uncertain in the source captions): calico bass teeth can abrade
  even 40 lb leader down to almost nothing in the last **6–8 in above the
  hook** — described as looking like it had been cut with a knife. Rule: run
  your fingers over the leader and check for nicks after landing any decent
  fish (3–5 lb+), and re-tie if there's any doubt, regardless of whether
  fishing 40 or 60 lb (`ZoIzpyyS9xw`). One angler starts a session with a
  **4 ft leader** on the swimbait and trims/re-ties it down over roughly two
  hours of fishing as it gets nicked (`ZoIzpyyS9xw`).

### New braid needs a break-in period before judging it

- Braid ships from the factory with a **wax coating**, or on some spools
  **excess dye** — neither is the line's settled, broken-in state
  (`hMLwRt6Sxn8`).
- **Fish it for roughly an hour** before deciding whether a new spool of
  braid is any good, rather than forming an opinion straight off the spool
  (`hMLwRt6Sxn8`).
- A leader overtop of braid protects against braid's **low abrasion
  resistance**, even though braid itself is exceptionally strong — the same
  leader reasoning already logged above and in
  [line ↔ leader matching](#line--leader-matching--the-actual-combo-logic)
  (`hMLwRt6Sxn8`).

### Braid wear from cast volume — carry a spare spool

Cast count, not fish landed, is what wears the braid running through the
guides. On a high-volume cast-and-burn program — **2–3 casts per minute,
500–700 casts a day**, the rate a dedicated Sea of Cortez
[cabrilla](../species/cabrilla.md) day runs at — the braid is worn by the end
of one day's fishing, so a **spare 300 yd one-shot spool of braid plus extra
top shot** goes in the bag for a multi-day trip (`EmZO8QiOfik`). The
arithmetic is a property of cast volume, not of the target species: any
program throwing at that rate hits the same wear, and the fix is a spare
one-shot rather than a heavier class.

### Braid construction — hollow vs solid, smooth vs rough

Hollow-core and solid (`low-carrier`) spectra differ in rigging/splicing
options, typical line-class pairing, diameter/bait-swim feel, and kelp-cutting
tooth — detail spun out to
[hollow vs solid spectra](spectra-hollow-vs-solid.md), including the
splice-as-maintenance practice (periodically re-splicing the top ~100 yd of a
hollow-core reel) and the smooth-vs-rough kelp-cutting axis (rough braid near
kelp, smooth braid on open-water squid grounds).

## When mono top shot

Mono's stretch and its habit of staying on the spool are the point:

- **Casting insurance on a jig stick / flyline rig.** Without confidence
  casting straight braid, run a mono top shot **long enough that the
  braid-to-mono knot never clears the spool on a full cast** (`aXF0bxAFtU0`).
  A knot dragged through the guides — say a 25 yd top shot on a 25 yd-plus
  cast — hangs up, backlashes, and braid's zero stretch then **snaps the
  leader and loses the jig, even at 40 lb**.
- **A short floating top shot for topwater plugs — walking baits and
  poppers.** Splice about **1.5 ft of 12 lb mono** to the braid, then tie the
  mono straight to the topwater lure (`CdgSBcN6PDo`). Two reasons, both
  specific to a hard bait worked on the surface: (1) mono floats, so that
  last stretch of running line right at the lure floats too, instead of the
  braid's sinking tendency dragging on the bait and interfering with its
  action; (2) stretching the short mono section under tension straightens
  out any coil memory in it (braid carries little to no memory to begin
  with), leaving a direct, low-slack connection — with sharp hooks, the
  moment a fish eats and turns away the weight is already felt and the fish
  is already hooked; just reel down and sweep (`CdgSBcN6PDo`). **Explicit
  exception: not for a frog** — fish a topwater frog on straight braid
  instead, no mono splice (`CdgSBcN6PDo`).
- **The same mechanism, stated for the leader-to-top-shot knot specifically.**
  Tie that knot **as long as possible, but not so long it passes into the rod
  tip on a cast** — a standing rule ("if you forget, [they'll] remind you")
  (`fyJA3o2hVh0`). Full rig in
  [Searcher 40 lb all-around tuna outfit](searcher-40lb-all-around-tuna-outfit.md).
- **Stretch / shock for big tuna.** On a first-timer's
  [bluefin](../species/bluefin-tuna.md) setup: at least **400 yards** of line,
  ideally over **80 lb backing**, with a **50 or 60 lb mono top shot** for the
  little bit of forgiveness when the fish eats and you come tight, then a
  short fluoro leader (`EyB98RuKfeQ`).
- **Internal line on spreader-bar / heavy troll setups** — mono's stretch
  belongs inside those rigs; see
  [rod & reel selection](rod-and-reel-selection.md) and the trolling lure
  notes.
- **A rail-rod stretch preference, framed as physical-fatigue insurance.** On
  a rail rod in a breeze (15 knots, boat rolling), a stated personal
  preference for **100–150 ft of mono** ahead of the fluoro leader
  specifically for the stretch — framed as "easier on the body" over a
  fight, not just hookset forgiveness (`YntRJAN88fs`). A second, independent
  voice for the physical-fatigue argument, reasoned from mono-vs-fluoro
  stretch rather than braid-vs-mono, is logged in the
  [evidence file](evidence/line-and-leader.md).
  **Fewer connections vs. more stretch — a stated tradeoff, not a rule:**
  running a mono top shot ahead of the fluoro leader means **three**
  connections (braid-to-mono, mono-to-fluoro, fluoro-to-hook) instead of the
  **two** needed going straight from braid to a full-spool fluoro top shot
  (braid-to-fluoro, fluoro-to-hook). The three-connection route is one stated
  preference, for the extra give, at the cost of one more knot; some anglers
  prefer fewer connections and skip the mono layer instead — not resolved
  either way (`YntRJAN88fs`).
  **The mechanism behind the mono-to-fluoro size-match rule — drag setting,
  not just bite avoidance:** the bare "match the rated size across a
  mono-to-fluoro join" rule logged below (Izorline) has a reason. The drag
  gets tuned to the **mono top shot's** class (worked example: **40 lb
  mono**), so stepping the fluoro leader down lighter to draw more bites
  (worked example: to **25 lb fluoro**) may get the bite, but the lighter
  fluoro then can't hold up to a drag that's still set for 40 lb mono — the
  fish gets bit, then lost. If a lighter fluoro is worth it for more bites,
  the drag has to come down to match the fluoro, not stay set for the
  heavier mono above it (`YntRJAN88fs`).

The "you need fluoro so light doesn't travel down the line to the hook"
argument is a **myth for short top shots**: as long as the mono is below the
surface and the braid sticks out of the water, light can't transmit through
braid into the mono anyway (`aXF0bxAFtU0`). Mono top shots are legitimate.

## When fluoro — leader and line

Fluoro does two different jobs; keep them separate:

- **Fluoro leader** (bulk spools of true leader material) is the stealth +
  abrasion section at the business end. It is a **must** on finicky SoCal
  pelagics — "you won't get bit without it" on bluefin (`EyB98RuKfeQ`). Even
  reaction bites want it for abrasion, just shorter and heavier.
- **Fluoro as main line** (100% fluoro line, cheaper in bulk) fills a reel for
  **small hard baits** — Lucky Craft 100-pointer / deep divers, jerkbaits. It
  sinks so the bait runs deeper, is spongier than braid (fewer pulled hooks on
  small trebles), and pitched on a ball head it **sinks straight down** instead
  of the floating-line resistance dragging the bait off target (`aXF0bxAFtU0`).
  It jumps off the spool until broken in (a couple trips, or use
  conditioner). **Keep light fluoro out of a hot garage** — heat breaks it
  down; store 8–10 lb line indoors.
- **Buy Japan-made fluorocarbon — a stated shop rule of thumb, no mechanism
  given on camera.** Preference for fluorocarbon leader material manufactured
  in Japan over other origins (`gn2yquuU6eM`). Two named examples, both made
  by Sunline: **Aftco Psycho** and **Sunline FC Sniper**. A brand-preference
  data point, not a tested claim; see also the spooling-mistake caution from
  the same source in
  [spooling line — tension and line-twist avoidance](spooling-line-tension-and-twist.md#leader-material-isnt-spool-line-tackle-express-gn2yquuu6em-are-you-spooling-your-line-wrong-watch-this-fishing-fix-2026-06-15-unregistered-channel-single-mention--medium-confidence).

### Length and weight, by application

- **Live bait, finicky / daytime:** longer, lighter fluoro. On a 30 lb setup a
  longer leader (roughly rod-length, ~7–8 ft) of 30 lb; on light flyline gear
  4–5 arm lengths of 25–30 lb (`xPFm_ZV2PZU`). Thin-diameter, low-memory
  fluoro cinches best on the tiny hooks a small
  [sardine](../bait/fishing-live-bait.md) can carry.
- **Channel Islands multi-species trip, halibut floor / general range.**
  Fluorocarbon is called not essential but beneficial specifically for
  halibut and yellowtail on a Channel Islands trip — described on camera as
  "a little bit more line shy" (`PnAnAS6QoG8`). Typically as light as **20 lb
  for halibut**; **25–40 lb** fluorocarbon otherwise covers a Channel Islands
  trip.
- **Reaction bite — poppers / stickbaits / jigs:** shorter (~4–6 ft) and heavier
  fluoro. Stealth matters less; abrasion and strength matter more — step to 60,
  80, even 100 lb (`0EpILTF0yvE`, `xPFm_ZV2PZU`).
- **Big bluefin, kite / flat-fall / sinker rig:** heavy fluoro leader, 80–150 lb,
  loop-to-loop off a [wind-on leader](../rigging/wind-on-leader.md), often crimped
  to a snap swivel; a short **very heavy** [bite leader](../rigging/bite-leaders.md)
  (≈130–200 lb) is the critical last link. Kite flyer rigs run heavy (up to
  300 lb) because the line is out of the water — strength over invisibility.
- **Long-range tuna backing capacity — a branded product tip.** For filling a
  **Talica 16-class reel** for big tuna out of Fisherman's Landing: **80 lb
  Power Pro Maxcuatro braid** — marketed as roughly **25% thinner than
  standard Power Pro at the same strength** — leaving more capacity for large
  tuna (the caption's exact size phrase is garbled; read as roughly
  100 lb-class fish, not confirmed), topped with a **42 lb Seaguar
  fluorocarbon top shot** (`1wFoa11jPxQ`). ⚠ asr-uncertain: the connecting
  knot is named on camera but not reliably legible in the auto-captions
  ("easy to tie... and super strong"); not entered into
  [essential knots](../rigging/essential-knots.md) without a verified name.
  Low-confidence, branded-promotion source — corroborates this note's
  thinner-braid-more-capacity mechanism above, but names specific retail
  products rather than giving independent testing or reasoning.
- **A second citation of the same product, adding a 100 ft metering
  interval.** The same "25% thinner" Maxcuatro marketing claim, also marketed
  as **color-metered every 100 ft**, run on Shimano Ocea Jigger/Grappler
  Type J and Trevala jigging outfits for a night bluefin bite to the 100-lb
  mark, plus on a Shimano Ledge Runner reel for bait fished behind the boat
  between jig stops (`Lj2NCznK7Zg`). Full detail in
  [Maxcuatro metered braid — Shogun trip](tackle-express-shogun-maxcuatro-braid.md).
- **A third citation, a direct head-to-head against PowerPro's other named
  metered braid.** The same "25% thinner" claim, here framed against **Power
  Pro Depth Hunter Offshore** specifically, plus line-class availability
  (Depth Hunter Offshore: 65, 80, 100, 130 lb; Max Quattro: 80, 100 lb) and a

  [Maxcuatro vs. Depth Hunter Offshore](tackle-express-maxcuatro-vs-depth-hunter-offshore.md).
- **Surface-iron backing/top-shot combo.** On a mid-sized star-drag reel for
  surface iron: **65 lb spectra as backing**, topped with **~100 yd of 40 lb
  monofilament** — stepping the top shot to **50 lb** for bluefin
  (`3g82igEL8yk`). A braid-backing/mono-top-shot combo, distinct from the
  straight-braid mainline in the application table in
  [rod & reel selection](rod-and-reel-selection.md); see that note for the
  rest of this build.
- **A second surface-iron rigging data point — hollow spectra plus a 100 ft
  mono top shot before a fluoro leader.** For jig fishing, either straight
  mono or a spectra-backed setup is fine; when running spectra, **hollow
  spectra is stated as better for bigger fish** than solid (`49joKHD7Umc`).
  Roughly **100 ft of monofilament** ahead of a fluorocarbon leader gives
  "enough stretch." Main-line class: **40 lb**, with 30 lb also fished by
  preference. The leader-to-hook connection for this build is a **20 lb
  fluorocarbon leader** tied on with an improved clinch knot (see
  [improved clinch knot](../rigging/improved-clinch-knot.md)) — notably
  lighter than the 40–50 lb top-shot classes logged elsewhere in this
  section; not reconciled with them (`49joKHD7Umc`).
- **Inshore bass swimbait/crank, action-preserving leader.** Staying in the
  **20–25 lb** fluoro leader range specifically preserves bait action —
  heavier leader (30 lb) starts to stiffen enough to noticeably deaden a
  swimbait's action (`lP6cg4eEU6s`). Paired with **40 lb braid** as the main
  line, for casting performance and spool capacity. Lighter than the heavier
  50–65+ lb kelp/structure leader classes logged in
  [swimbaits — weedless rigging](../techniques/swimbaits.md#weedless-rigging-erik-landesfeind)
  and [night bass fishing](../techniques/night-bass-fishing.md#heavy-leader-for-trophy-night-fishing--the-reason-not-just-the-number) —
  a lighter, action-preserving choice for open-water/less-structure work
  where break-off risk is lower, rather than a heavy abrasion-first one.
- **Tackle-shop minimum leader buy, same lead-head fluke class.** One spool
  of fluorocarbon, roughly **30 lb**, covers the
  [Sudden Impact / Basstrix lead-head fluke class](../lures/soft-plastic-swimbaits.md);
  buying more, carry **25, 30, and 40 lb** spools (`Oi5n-uVpY9g`). Heavier
  than the 20–25 lb action-preserving pick above — a general tackle-shop
  minimum-buy answer covering bigger fish (halibut, sea bass) rather than a
  stated action-preservation choice.

## Line ↔ leader matching — the actual combo logic

The right combination is decided by **what abrades you** and **what you need to be
able to break off** (`aXF0bxAFtU0`):

- **Fishing structure you can tangle in** (kelp, eel grass, anchor lines, rip-rap)
  → **leader HEAVIER than the braid.** All the abrasion happens at the last foot
  by the hook, not along the braid. Rules of thumb: 30 lb braid + hard bait →
  40 lb leader; weedless [swimbait](../techniques/swimbaits.md) on 50 lb braid →
  60 lb leader (`aXF0bxAFtU0`).
- **Fishing where you snag bottom and must break off** a lead head or sinker
  ([rockfish](../species/rockfish-lingcod.md), sandbass in 100 ft) → **leader
  SIGNIFICANTLY LIGHTER than the braid.** You want the short leader to part near
  the hook, not the braid — a braid break can cost you half a spool (`aXF0bxAFtU0`).
  **Material on that leader, standard rockfish double dropper loop:** 30 or
  40 lb mono, not fluoro, is the default — cheap and effective since
  rockfish aren't leader-shy; step up to fluoro only for a tough bite. See
  [rockfish leader line](tackle-express-rockfish-leader-line.md).
- **Leader size climbs with target, not calendar:** on offshore top shots, 15–40 lb
  fluoro covers paddy fish and small-to-mid tuna; 80 lb and up is the workhorse
  for bigger bluefin (`xPFm_ZV2PZU`).
- **Regional floor — Cedros Island, Baja (resident yellowtail):** because a
  home-guard 40+ lb yellowtail can turn up anywhere around the island (even a
  30-pounder puts you in the rocks), a Cedros trip's **line-class floor is
  40 lb**; **50 lb** covers most everything fished that trip (short bait
  leaders and surface iron), with **80 lb** carried as a just-in-case class
  for black sea bass and bigger white seabass (`WE643Fue1_A`). See
  [yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md).
- **A second Cedros data point — 80–100 lb fielded as standard, not
  backup.** A separate Cedros trip ran **80–100 lb line class** outfits as
  the standing gear — not an 80 lb just-in-case class — for the resident
  home-guard yellowtail, fished over shallow rock structure (**28–30 ft**)
  (`QSmE3mdEL28`). A heavier bracket than the 40 lb floor / 50 lb
  most-everything breakdown above from a different trip — read as two
  operators' independent line-class choices for the same resident-fish risk
  (getting spooled into shallow rock), not a correction of each other.
- **Mono over fluoro for surface iron, one attributed preference:** one
  Cedros trip runs straight monofilament rather than fluorocarbon
  specifically for surface iron fishing — fluoro for everything else on the
  trip — with no reason stated in source (`WE643Fue1_A`). A standalone data
  point on material choice for [surface iron](../techniques/surface-iron.md),
  not reconciled with the general top-shot guidance above.
- **Wire leader over rock structure — Gonzaga Bay, Sea of Cortez (Baja):** for
  cabrilla and barred pargo/snapper fished tight to rocky structure, one boat
  rigs a **wire leader on 65 lb spectra mainline** — described as "real
  similar to Calico fishing," i.e. the same close-structure casting/dropping
  approach as [calico bass](../species/calico-bass.md), stepped up to wire
  specifically for the rocks and toothy fish this structure holds
  (`_Wb4z4ammoM`). Stated reason for the heavier build: these reef fish run
  **bigger than Cal bass**, reportedly to **15+ lb** (`_Wb4z4ammoM`). No wire
  gauge or leader length given in source.
  - **Depth/bait/drag, same trip, a separate bait-dropping technique:**
    grouper specifically were also fished by dropping baits rather than
    casting, starting around **80–90 ft of water**, on a wire leader with the
    **biggest baits available**, drag run button-down/locked rather than
    eased — the crew fished expecting to get broken off at that setting
    (`A6s-A1NARuA`). No line class given for this specific drop in source.
    See also the parallel (mono/fluoro, not wire) leader build for this same
    species/region in
    [sliding-sinker — Baja grouper leader](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md#baja-sea-of-cortez-grouper-leader-cesar) —
    the two sources specify different leader materials for the same fishery.
- **Line-class ladder by expected grade, Baja long-range "lower banks":** on
  an 11-day long-range trip roughly **608 nautical miles** south of San
  Diego, with sonar marks read as big fish (reportedly up to **200 lb-plus**)
  and a nearby fleet boat already hooked up, the pre-bite brief set gear for
  the next morning's bite: **fish 100 lb test as the floor** — "don't go in
  the water with anything less than a hundred pounds"; only step down to
  **80 lb** after first assessing the grade, and **never start the morning at
  80 lb**; **60 lb is ruled out entirely** for this grade of fish
  (`pk2blktDQ9Y`). A long-range/giant-grade data point, distinct from the
  40 lb-floor / 50 lb-most-everything Cedros yellowtail brackets and the
  80–150 lb big-bluefin bracket logged above — a different fishery and grade.
- **Three-leader system by structure, San Diego rock/pier and kelp calico:**
  one angler carries three rigged setups rather than one universal leader
  (`NXvqLUZ6qp8`):
  - **Rock/pier structure** (example given: pilings in San Diego): straight
    fluorocarbon, no braid — stated reason is pure abrasion, rock "tears up"
    line "like taking a shirt and putting it on the blacktop and rubbing
    it." Roughly **15 lb fluorocarbon** for this structure, sized to the
    small calico there (pound to pound-and-a-half typical, occasionally
    bigger) (`NXvqLUZ6qp8`).
  - **Kelp / "boiling rock"** (breaking water over structure): **50–65 lb
    braid** to a fluorocarbon leader, most often **20 lb**. Leader-length
    rule of thumb: **roughly the length of the rod**, so the leader knot
    stays outside the tip guide and can be handled by hand at the boat
    rather than having to crank the knot through the guides (`NXvqLUZ6qp8`).
  - A **third, all-braid setup** (55–65 lb, no leader) carried for a
    different feel — not detailed further in source.

  A SoCal-specific structure-driven data point, consistent with and adding
  concrete numbers to the general kelp/rock leader-heavier-than-braid rule
  above.

## Connections (parameters — steps are in the rigging notes)

- **Leader running through the guides:** the **FG knot** is the smoothest
  braid-to-leader join; details and the Albright alternative in
  [FG & Albright](../rigging/fg-and-albright.md).
- **Leader kept outside the guides:** a **double uni** is fast and reliable — the
  trick is **9 turns on the braid side, 5 on the mono/fluoro side** (5-and-5 slips,
  9-and-9 is bulky). A Tony Pena knot points its tag toward the reel so it only
  catches coming in. See [essential knots](../rigging/essential-knots.md).
- If you like a longer leader through the guides, use a **minimum guide size of 8**
  — many bass rods have size-6 guides that aren't leader-friendly.
- **Match the rated size across a mono-to-fluoro join.** When tying
  monofilament to fluorocarbon, use the **same rated size on both sides of
  the knot** — "don't change that, otherwise it'll lose your fish"
  (`ew7Lru8wmQs`). No knot name, specific size, or mechanism (diameter vs.
  breaking strength) given in source; a standalone parameter alongside the
  double-uni and FG-knot guidance above. The drag-setting mechanism for this
  same rule — matching the fluoro leader to the mono top shot's class because
  the drag is tuned for the mono, not the fluoro — is logged above under
  [When mono top shot](#when-mono-top-shot).
- **A distinct, earlier rule — match the leader to the running line, or step
  ONE size above, not necessarily the same size.** For a 7-day Alijos
  Rocks/"the Ridge" trip's terminal tackle: "you either want to match the
  line class that you have or be one step above" — worked example given:
  **40 lb monofilament** running line, **50 lb fluorocarbon** leader
  (`uWGGeDOprsE`). Not the same claim as the same-rated-size-on-both-sides
  rule directly above (that rule is about the mono-to-fluoro knot holding;
  this one is about choosing the leader's own class relative to the main
  line, and explicitly allows going heavier, not just matching). Full
  trip-tackle context in
  [Searcher five-outfit quiver — 7-day trip to Alijos Rocks and "The Ridge"](searcher-alijos-rocks-ridge-7-day-quiver.md#terminal-tackle).
