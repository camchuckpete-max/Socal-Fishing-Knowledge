# Trophy (Triple-Digit) Bluefin Jig Outfit — Jig Selection, Rigging, Reel/Rod Ladder, and Fight Timing (Tackle Express)

Tackle Express builds a jig-selection, rigging, and reel/rod package
specifically for triple-digit (100 lb+) bluefin (`DuNkl8F45NE`). Because the
biting jig changes day to day, the program leans on carrying a wide jig
spread plus hook, leader, and quick-change-rig upgrades rather than
committing to one setup. A shorter 2025 Tackle Express clip (`Q4sW6JRQzAY`)
restates the same doctrine in miniature — jig weight range, rigging upgrade,
and reel/line class — folded into the sections below rather than logged as
independent confirmation, since it recaps the same shop's earlier footage
rather than a new trip. This 2022 citation predates, and partly corroborates,
the "Jarry rig" leader build documented later in [Tungsten (TG) jig rig for
bluefin](tackle-express-night-bluefin-tg-jig-rig.md).

## Jig selection is condition-dependent — carry a spread, not one jig

Jigging big bluefin is condition-dependent: a jig that gets bit one day is
not a guaranteed bite the next, with no way to predict in advance which jig
that will be, so the fix is carrying a range rather than committing to one
(`DuNkl8F45NE`).

- Carry jigs spanning **~200 g flat-fall** jigs (a broad-profile,
  slow-falling jig through the water column) up through **320 g, 400 g, and
  500 g knife jigs** for punching down faster; pick by how fast the jig
  needs to reach the fish's actual depth (`DuNkl8F45NE`).
- A same-session swap tracked this logic in practice: starting on a 250 g
  "SK" jig, stepping down to a 200 g "SL" knife jig when fish showed
  shallower and faster-moving, then stepping back up toward a 300 g West
  Coast Jiggerz or Mustad Rip Roller once the captain called fish at
  300–400 g grade, 400 ft deep — full detail in the [evidence
  file](evidence/tackle-express-trophy-bluefin-jig-outfit.md) (`DuNkl8F45NE`).
- This range sits beside, not merged into, two other same-problem
  citations: the [Searcher bluefin jig ladder by daypart and
  depth](searcher-bluefin-jig-ladder-by-daypart-and-depth.md) (100 g–400 g,
  organized by light level rather than a live depth call) and the
  **150–500 g** by-depth-and-current range in [knife
  jigs](../lures/knife-jigs.md) — three sources' own weight/depth framings,
  not reconciled to one.
- **2025 recap widens the named range** (`Q4sW6JRQzAY`, 2025): the shorter,
  later Tackle Express clip names the box as spanning **160 g flat-fall up
  to a 560 g knife jig** — a wider band than this 2022 citation's named
  ~200–500 g — on the same carry-a-spread framing. This shop's own doctrine
  now spans three overlapping but distinct weight/depth citations (this
  note's ~200–500 g, the daypart/depth ladder's 100 g–400 g, and this
  160–560 g 2025 recap), not reconciled into one fixed range.
- The quick jig-swap above is only practical because of the quick-change
  leader rig below — swapping a fully rigged jig for another takes only a
  pair of split-ring pliers.

## Hook and leader upgrades — every jig in the box gets rebuilt before it's fished

Every jig gets pulled off the shop shelf and re-rigged before it's fished —
none are fished on stock hooks or leader (`DuNkl8F45NE`):

- **Bottom hook:** a "big, nice, upgraded" Mustad J hook (captions render
  this "mustang j hook," read as Mustad per this channel's established
  Mustad branding elsewhere in this corpus; asr-uncertain), or, depending on
  jig style, a hook the transcript captions only as a "travel hook" — exact
  model/brand not resolved (asr-uncertain) (`DuNkl8F45NE`).
- **Top assist hook** (a separate hook from the bottom hook): a "Must-have
  J" upgraded assist hook (named product not independently confirmed;
  asr-uncertain). It counterweights the jig as a fighting fish throws it
  around, and is meant to dig into the fish's mouth in a way that impedes
  breathing and drives it toward the boat — stated as not always working
  that reliably, which is the stated reason the leader is upgraded as
  heavily as it is below (`DuNkl8F45NE`).
- **Leader:** **200–300 lb** as the standard bite-leader class, stepped up
  to as heavy as **400 lb** for heavy night fishing. Reasoning: a swallowed
  small jig (the video's own example — a 160 g flat-fall) can end up down
  the fish's gullet and against its teeth for the length of the fight, and
  bluefin "do have teeth" — the longer the fight runs, the more chance the
  fish chews through the line. The heavy leader is fighting-chance
  insurance against that, not a stealth trade-off (`DuNkl8F45NE`).
- This 200–400 lb range sits beside, not reconciled with, the fixed 200 lb
  Seaguar Blue Label "Jarry rig" spec in [Tungsten (TG) jig rig for
  bluefin — leader](tackle-express-night-bluefin-tg-jig-rig.md#leader--the-jarry-rig) —
  same shop, same underlying build (see next section), but this earlier
  citation gives a range rather than one fixed test.
- **2025 recap** (`Q4sW6JRQzAY`, 2025): stock jigs "come stock off the shelf
  ready to catch local island fish and smaller grade bluefin. These are not
  100 lb fish ready" — the same rebuild-every-jig reasoning already carried
  above. The re-rig is named as **200–300 lb leader** (inside, not
  exceeding, the 200–400 lb range above) paired with "heavy duty hooks,
  whether it be a big treble, a big offshore hook, or something like a
  Mustad Tuna J hook" — naming a treble hook as a third bottom-hook option
  alongside the Mustad J hook and unnamed "travel hook" above, not
  reconciled to one fixed hook.

## The quick-change rig — an earlier citation of the Jerry/Jarry rig

This 2022 citation independently corroborates, three years before the 2025
clips already logged, the same shop-built quick-change leader system
documented as the **"Jarry rig"** in [Tungsten (TG) jig rig for bluefin —
the "Jarry rig"](tackle-express-night-bluefin-tg-jig-rig.md#leader--the-jarry-rig):

- Same on-camera builder attribution — "here at Tackle Express, James and
  Harry put together the [Jerry/Jarry] rig" (this transcript's own ASR
  spells it "jerry rig," matching the ASR-only spelling in the two 2025
  clips before the `BsWb5BQ1Tdc` title correction to "Jarry") (`DuNkl8F45NE`).
- Same stated reason to run a solid ring rather than a split ring at the
  hook — "we've heard and seen split rings bind." The hook rides on a
  swivel, with a second swivel at the top of the chafing leader — "there is
  no way this guy is going to bind" (`DuNkl8F45NE`).
- Names the same 350 lb Mustad Saltism split ring as a build component
  (captions render the brand "mustache saltism," corrected here the same
  way the later note corrects it; asr-uncertain) (`DuNkl8F45NE`).
- **Stated payoff:** the rig lets an angler swap a fully rigged jig for
  another with just a pair of split-ring pliers — off the spent jig, onto
  the next one, back on the line — fast enough to matter because these fish
  "come under the boat and they're gone just as fast as they came under."
  It also lets the jig be unclipped and stowed at night or during long runs
  so it isn't banging around the boat or scratching reels, leaving a bare
  hook ready to re-rig fast when fish show (`DuNkl8F45NE`).
- Not reconciled beyond this: the 2025 citations give one fixed leader test
  (200 lb) and a welded ring/hook connection; this 2022 citation gives a
  leader range (200–400 lb) and doesn't specify weld vs. crimp at the ring —
  the same shop's build described three years apart, not one settled spec.

## Reel and line ladder

Stated goal: a reel putting out **30 lb+ of drag** with **400–800 yd** of
line capacity (`DuNkl8F45NE`).

- **Named reels:** Shimano **Talica 20**; an item captioned "HXWs" — not a
  Shimano model, and almost certainly the same Avet HXW named again later
  in the clip ("your talikas... your internationals... your avid hxws,"
  grouping it with Talica/International as one two-speed class — see the
  Avet HXW bullet below); **Speedmaster 25**; **Penn International 30**;
  **International 50** (`DuNkl8F45NE`).
- **Talica 20:** spooled with **80 lb or 100 lb Power Pro Max Quattro** — a
  thinner braid for its test, chosen to hit the reel's line-capacity target
  on a compact body (`DuNkl8F45NE`).
- A second, single-speed reel class, named for its retrieve speed —
  captioned "the osha [bleeped] 4000" (not independently resolved;
  asr-uncertain) — picks up "a lot of inches per crank," holds a lot of
  line, and suits the speed-jigging retrieve specifically. Being star-drag
  and single-speed, it can mean a harder, more effort-intensive fight than a
  two-speed, depending on the grade of fish hooked (`DuNkl8F45NE`).
- **Avet HXW** (captioned "avid hxw," read as Avet's HXW line;
  asr-uncertain): one of the smallest, least expensive reels the speaker
  has personally seen land some of the biggest fish in this class — spooled
  with **100 lb Power Pro Max Quattro**, holding just under **700 yd**
  (`DuNkl8F45NE`).
- The two-speed feature is a requirement, not an option, on this program's
  Talica/International-class reels for this grade of fish — see the
  fight-timing section below for why.
- A separate, later Tackle Express clip states a distinct reason to run
  thin braid (`XtPUnC5jQnM`, 2026-02-09; unregistered channel, single
  mention — medium confidence; no on-camera name given): it recounts
  anglers being surprised the narrator landed a fish — the video's title
  states **180 lb**, a number the 32-second clip's audio never restates,
  carried from the human-typed title only — on an **80 lb** outfit. He also
  recalls landing a **140 lb** fish a year earlier (2025, resolved against
  the 2026-02-09 upload date; species not named on camera in this clip,
  placed here on context since this is the same channel's established
  triple-digit-bluefin jigging subject elsewhere on this note — flagged
  rather than asserted as confirmed) in daytime on **65 lb** braid with a
  **100 lb top shot**. Stated reasoning: thinner braid lets a smaller,
  lighter jig get down to the strike zone, and working it back up
  afterward doesn't wear the angler out — a distinct reason from the
  reel-capacity reasoning above for the Talica 20's 80/100 lb pairing, not
  reconciled with it (`XtPUnC5jQnM`).
- **2025 recap**, same reel class: reels restated as **size 20 Shimano
  Talica** (captioned "talas") up through an **International 50 wide**,
  spooled with **80 lb up to 250 lb spectra** — the first spectra test
  range on this note, sitting alongside, not reconciled with, the Talica
  20's 80/100 lb Power Pro Max Quattro pairing above (`Q4sW6JRQzAY`, 2025).

## Rod ladder — rated to the top shot, not just the line class

Rods are rated to the top shot actually fished, not just to the mainline
braid test (`DuNkl8F45NE`).

- **Rating classes:** double XH up through **4XH**, spanning printed ranges
  of roughly **40–100 lb, 50–100 lb, 60–120 lb**, with some rods rated
  **80 lb–unlimited** (`DuNkl8F45NE`).
- **Named rods:** United Composites' **Viper**, **Invictus**, and
  **Gladiator**; the **Calstar 775 double XH**, called "a very great
  unlimited rod" (`DuNkl8F45NE`).
- A named personal favorite (captioned "Shimano therese," read as the
  **Shimano Trevala** line from context; asr-uncertain), rated up to
  **200 lb braid**: its noticeably **parabolic** action lets the angler
  apply steady pressure to turn a fish's head toward the boat without
  pulling hard enough to rip the jig from a softly hooked fish — useful
  because the hook point on a bluefin isn't always known (`DuNkl8F45NE`).
- The rod carries an **extended foregrip** for bracing against the
  rail/gunnel rather than hand-holding through the fight (see below)
  (`DuNkl8F45NE`).

## Fight timing — stay in high gear through the run, shift to low only at the "pinwheel"

A two-speed-specific fight-timing rule, consistent with but distinct from
the shift-to-low-when-circling doctrine in [two-speed low-gear
fight](../techniques/two-speed-low-gear-fight.md):

- **Stay in high gear** through the fish's first deep run(s), even once it
  turns and charges back at the boat and the line goes visibly slack — the
  fix for that slack is winding faster in high gear to catch back up, not
  shifting to low, so the line comes tight again as soon as the fish turns
  and the rod loads back up (`DuNkl8F45NE`).
- **The "pinwheel":** when a fought-out bluefin settles roughly
  **100–200 ft** down and starts spiraling toward the surface rather than
  running, that's the cue to shift to **low gear** — low gear trades
  retrieve speed for torque to turn the fish's head and lift it out of the
  depths, applied specifically in the last minutes of the fight when a
  stalled fish risks a second wind and another run. This names the same
  circling/stalled-fish trigger already documented in [two-speed low-gear
  fight — timing the
  shift](../techniques/two-speed-low-gear-fight.md#timing-the-shift--stay-high-through-the-run-drop-to-low-once-its-close-and-circling),
  adding the "pinwheel" name and the **100–200 ft** depth band
  (`DuNkl8F45NE`).
- **Pull hard, early:** with drag set properly, pull as hard as physically
  possible within the **first five minutes** — if the hook is going to pull
  free, better to find that out in the first five minutes than after
  draining the angler's energy over a much longer fight (`DuNkl8F45NE`).
- **Rail-rod bracing** ("railroading" in this transcript's own framing,
  distinct usage from "railroading" in [fighting a big
  bluefin](../techniques/fighting-big-bluefin.md#naming--railroading-stokedonfishing-mdmbgdqay-4-2025-10-06-upload-socal-ne-of-santa-barbara-island---catching-monster-bluefin-using-a-flying-bait--catch--cook)):
  brace the rod's extended foregrip on the rail, keep a bend in the rod, and
  turn the handle — no angler is stronger than a triple-digit bluefin, so
  the rod/rail combination, not raw arm strength, brings the fish in as
  long as the bend and the crank stay steady (`DuNkl8F45NE`).
