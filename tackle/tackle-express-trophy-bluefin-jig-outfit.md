# Trophy (Triple-Digit) Bluefin Jig Outfit — Jig Selection, Rigging, Reel/Rod Ladder, and Fight Timing (Tackle Express)

Tackle Express, "How to Catch a Trophy Bluefin Tuna on a Jig" (James, on
camera; `DuNkl8F45NE`, uploaded 2022-06-04). `tackle-express` is an
**unregistered** channel, single mention **per video** — **medium** confidence
throughout (an unregistered channel caps at medium regardless of repeat
mentions). Framed on camera as an intro-level walkthrough for jigging
**triple-digit (100 lb+) bluefin**: jig selection, rigging upgrades, and the
reel/rod/fight package around them. This predates, and partly corroborates,
the later Tackle Express "Jarry rig" clips already logged in [Tungsten (TG)
jig rig for bluefin](tackle-express-night-bluefin-tg-jig-rig.md) — see the
leader section below. A much shorter 2025 Tackle Express clip, "How to Catch
100lb+ Bluefin on Jigs" (James, on camera; `Q4sW6JRQzAY`, uploaded
2025-08-04), restates this same doctrine in miniature — jig weight range,
rigging upgrade, and reel/line class — three years later; folded into the
relevant sections below, dated, rather than logged as independent
confirmation (re-cut/recap footage from the same shop is not a second
source).

## Jig selection is condition-dependent — carry a spread, not one jig

Stated up front as the organizing idea: jigging big bluefin is "very
condition oriented" — a jig that got bit yesterday is not a guaranteed bite
today, and might still be tomorrow's jig; there's no way to know in advance,
so the fix is carrying a range of options rather than committing to one jig.

- Carry jigs spanning **~200 g flat-fall** (a broad-profile, slow-falling
  jig through the water column) up through **320 g, 400 g, and 500 g knife
  jigs** for punching down faster through the water column — pick by where
  the fish are actually sitting, since how fast the jig needs to reach them
  determines which end of that range to fish.
- **On-the-water jig swap, by depth and fish behavior (same session,
  narrated live):** started on a **250 g "SK"** jig with a **zebra glow**
  pattern; when the fish moved higher in the water column and were seen
  swimming faster past the boat, switched to a **200 g "SL"** — same knife
  profile, still darting down through the column but not so fast it
  "rockets past" faster-moving, shallower fish. When the captain then began
  calling fish **300–400 g, 400 ft** deep, swapped again to something like a
  **300 g West Coast Jiggerz** or a **Mustad Rip Roller** — named as the next
  step up once the captain's called depth/grade outgrows the 200 g jig
  already on the line. This day-of ladder sits beside, not merged into, the
  differently-sourced [Searcher bluefin jig ladder by daypart and
  depth](searcher-bluefin-jig-ladder-by-daypart-and-depth.md) (100 g–400 g,
  organized by light level rather than a live depth call) and the **150–500 g
  by depth and current** range already logged in [knife
  jigs](../lures/knife-jigs.md) — a third source's own weight/depth framing,
  not reconciled into either.
- **2025 recap widens the named range (`Q4sW6JRQzAY`, 2025-08-04):** the
  shorter, later Tackle Express clip names the box as spanning **160 g
  flat-fall all the way up to a 560 g knife jig** — a wider band than this
  2022 video's named **~200 g–500 g** range, on the same "carry a spread"
  framing. Kept side by side, dated, rather than reconciled to one fixed
  range: this shop's own doctrine now has three overlapping but distinct
  weight/depth citations (this note, the daypart/depth ladder above, and this
  wider 2025 recap).
- The quick jig-swap above was made practical specifically by the
  quick-change leader rig described below — swapping a fully-rigged jig for
  another took only a pair of split-ring pliers.

## Hook and leader upgrades — every jig in the box gets rebuilt before it's fished

Every jig gets pulled off the shop shelf and re-rigged before it's fished —
none are fished on their stock hooks/leader:

- **Bottom hook:** a "big, nice, upgraded" **Mustad J hook** (transcript
  captions render this "mustang j hook" — read as Mustad per the channel's
  established Mustad-branding elsewhere in this corpus; flagged
  **asr-uncertain**), or, depending on the jig style, a hook the transcript
  captions as a **"travel hook"** — exact model/brand not resolved,
  flagged **asr-uncertain**.
- **Top assist hook, a separate hook from the bottom hook:** a "Must-have J"
  upgraded assist hook (named product not independently confirmed —
  asr-uncertain). **Stated function, in the angler's own words:** the assist
  hook (1) counterweights the jig as it's thrown around by a fighting fish,
  and (2) "digs a hole" somewhere in the fish's mouth that keeps it from
  breathing, driving it toward the boat rather than away — described as not
  always working that reliably, which is the stated reason the leader is
  upgraded as heavily as it is (below).
- **Leader:** **200–300 lb** as the standard bite-leader class, stepped up to
  as heavy as **400 lb** for heavy night fishing. **Stated reasoning:** a
  swallowed small jig (the video's own example: a 160 g flat-fall) can end up
  down the fish's gullet and halfway through its belly, putting the leader in
  contact with the fish's teeth for the length of the fight — bluefin "do
  have teeth," and the longer the fight runs, the more chance the fish chews
  through the line. The heavy leader is framed explicitly as a chafing
  leader bought for fighting-chance insurance, not a stealth trade-off.
- This **200–400 lb** range sits beside, not reconciled with, the fixed
  **200 lb Seaguar Blue Label** "Jarry rig" spec already logged in [Tungsten
  (TG) jig rig for bluefin — leader](tackle-express-night-bluefin-tg-jig-rig.md#leader--the-jarry-rig) —
  same shop, same underlying build (see next section), but this earlier
  video gives a range rather than one fixed test.
- **2025 recap (`Q4sW6JRQzAY`, 2025-08-04) — plain statement of why every jig
  gets re-rigged, plus a widened hook list:** stated directly on camera:
  stock jigs "come stock off the shelf ready to catch local island fish and
  smaller grade bluefin. These are not 100 lb fish ready" — the same
  reasoning this note already carries for rebuilding every jig before it's
  fished. Re-rig named as **200–300 lb leader** (sitting inside, not
  exceeding, this note's already-logged 200–400 lb range) paired with "heavy
  duty hooks, whether it be a big treble, a big offshore hook, or something
  like a Mustad Tuna J hook" — naming a **treble hook** as a third bottom-hook
  option alongside the Mustad J hook and unnamed "travel hook" already logged
  above, not reconciled to one fixed hook choice.

## The quick-change rig — an earlier citation of the "Jerry/Jarry rig"

This video independently corroborates, **three years earlier** than the
2025 clips already logged, the same shop-built quick-change leader system
already documented as the **"Jarry rig"** in [Tungsten (TG) jig rig for
bluefin — the "Jarry rig"](tackle-express-night-bluefin-tg-jig-rig.md#leader--the-jarry-rig):

- Same on-camera builder attribution — **"here at Tackle Express, James and
  Harry put together the [Jerry/Jarry] rig"** (this transcript's own ASR
  spells it "jerry rig," matching the ASR-only spelling in the two 2025
  clips before the `BsWb5BQ1Tdc` title correction to "Jarry").
- Same stated reason to go to a **solid ring rather than a split ring** at
  the hook: "we've heard and seen split rings bind." The hook itself rides
  on a swivel, with a second swivel at the top of the chafing leader — "there
  is no way this guy is going to bind" — worded almost identically to the
  later clip's version of the same claim.
- Names the same **350 lb Mustad Saltism split ring** as a component of the
  build (the earlier clip's captions render the brand "mustache saltism,"
  corrected here the same way the later note already corrects it).
- **Stated payoff, this video's own framing:** the rig lets an angler swap a
  fully rigged jig for another with just a pair of split-ring pliers — pull
  the rig off the spent jig, clip it onto the next one, back on the line —
  fast enough to matter because these fish "come under the boat and they're
  gone just as fast as they came under." It also lets the jig itself be
  unclipped and stowed at night/during long runs so it isn't banging around
  the boat or scratching reels, leaving just a bare hook on the rod ready to
  re-rig fast when fish show.
- Not reconciled beyond this: the two 2025 citations give one fixed leader
  test (200 lb) and a "welded" ring/hook connection, where this 2022 video
  gives a leader range (200–400 lb) and doesn't specify weld vs. crimp at
  the ring. Kept side by side as the same shop's build described three years
  apart, not as a single settled spec.

## Reel and line ladder

Stated goal: a reel putting out **30 lb+ of drag** and **400–800 yd** of line
capacity.

- **Named reels:** **Shimano Talica 20**; an item this list's captions render
  **"HXWs"** — not a Shimano model, and almost certainly the same **Avet
  HXW** named again later in this clip ("your talikas... your
  internationals... your avid hxws," grouping it with Talica/International
  as one class of two-speed reel — see the dedicated Avet HXW bullet below,
  which pairs it with its own line/capacity spec); **Speedmaster 25**;
  **Penn International 30**; **International 50**.
- **Talica 20, line pairing:** spooled with **80 lb or 100 lb Power Pro Max
  Quattro** — described as a thinner braid for its test, chosen specifically
  to hit the reel's stated line-capacity target on a compact-bodied reel.
- **A second reel class, single-speed, named for its retrieve speed:** a reel
  captioned "the osha [bleeped] 4000" — not independently resolved, flagged
  **asr-uncertain** — described as picking up "a lot of inches per crank,"
  holding a lot of line, and suited to the speed-jigging retrieve
  specifically. Stated trade-off: being a **star-drag, single-speed** reel,
  it can mean a harder, more effort-intensive fight than a two-speed,
  depending on the grade of fish hooked.
- **Avet HXW** (captioned "avid hxw" — read as Avet's HXW model line;
  flagged **asr-uncertain**): called out as one of the smallest, least
  expensive reels the speaker has personally seen land some of the biggest
  fish in this class — spooled with **100 lb Power Pro Max Quattro**,
  holding **just under 700 yd**.
- **Two-speed feature is treated as a requirement, not an option,** on this
  program's Talica/International-class reels for this grade of fish — see
  the fight-timing section below for why.
- **A separate, later Tackle Express clip gives a stated reason to run thin
  braid specifically** (`XtPUnC5jQnM`, "180lb Tuna on *that* setup? The secret
  revealed!," uploaded 2026-02-09; unregistered channel, single mention —
  medium confidence; no on-camera name given, so cited to the channel only):
  the narrator recounts anglers being surprised he landed a fish — the
  video's own title states **180 lb**, a number the 32-second clip's audio
  never restates, so it is carried from the human-typed title only — on an
  **80 lb** outfit. He then recalls that a year earlier (**2025**, resolved
  against the 2026-02-09 upload date) he landed a **140 lb** fish (species not
  named on camera in this 32-second clip — placed in this bluefin-specific
  note on context, since this is the same channel's established
  triple-digit-bluefin jigging subject matter elsewhere on this note; flagged
  here rather than asserted as confirmed) in the daytime on **65 lb braid**,
  still paired with a **100 lb top shot**. **Stated reasoning, in his own
  words:** he runs the thinner braid specifically so a smaller, lighter jig
  can get down to the strike zone, and so working the jig back up afterward
  doesn't wear him out. This is a **distinct** stated reason to prefer
  thinner braid from the
  reel-capacity reasoning already logged above for the Talica 20's 80/100 lb
  Power Pro Max Quattro pairing — kept side by side, not reconciled, as this
  same shop's second stated reason (jig-descent/retrieve effort, not reel
  capacity) to run a thinner braid test on this class of gear.
- **2025 recap (`Q4sW6JRQzAY`, 2025-08-04) — same reel class, first spectra
  test range logged on this note:** reels restated as **size 20 Shimano
  Talica** (captions render this "talas") up through an **International 50
  wide**, spooled with **80 lb up to 250 lb spectra** — this note's first
  citation of a spectra test range; sits alongside, not reconciled with, the
  Talica 20's specific 80/100 lb Power Pro Max Quattro pairing logged above
  from the 2022 video.

## Rod ladder — rated to the top shot, not just the line class

**Stated framing, worth preserving as its own rule:** "our rods are actually
rated to the top shots and stuff we are fishing" — i.e., read the printed
rating against the top-shot leader class actually being run on this program,
not just against the mainline braid test.

- **Rating classes named:** double XH up through **4XH**, spanning printed
  ranges of roughly **40–100 lb, 50–100 lb, 60–120 lb**, and some rods rated
  **80 lb–unlimited**.
- **Named rods:** United Composites' **Viper**, **Invictus**, and
  **Gladiator**; the new **Calstar 775 double XH**, called "a very great
  unlimited rod."
- **A named personal favorite, with a stated reason (captioned "Shimano
  therese" — read as the **Shimano Trevala** line from context; flagged
  **asr-uncertain**):** rated up to **200 lb braid**. Stated reasoning for
  favoring it: it has a noticeably **parabolic** action, which lets the
  angler apply steady pressure to turn a fish's head toward the boat without
  pulling hard enough to rip the jig out of a softly hooked fish — framed as
  useful specifically because you don't always know where on the jig a
  bluefin got hooked.
- Rod carries an **extended foregrip**, stated purpose: bracing the rod on
  the rail/gunnel rather than hand-holding it through the fight (see below).

## Fight timing — stay in high gear through the run, shift to low only at the "pinwheel"

A two-speed-specific fight-timing rule, distinct from but consistent with
the shift-to-low-when-circling doctrine already logged in [two-speed
low-gear fight](../techniques/two-speed-low-gear-fight.md):

- **Hook the fish and stay in high gear** through the fish's first
  deep run(s), even once it turns and charges back at the boat and the line
  goes visibly slack. **The stated fix for that slack is winding faster in
  high gear to catch back up** — not shifting to low — so the line comes
  tight again as soon as the fish turns and the rod loads back up.
- **Named end-of-fight stage — the "pinwheel":** when a fought-out bluefin
  settles roughly **100–200 ft** down and starts spiraling its way toward
  the surface rather than running, that's the cue to bump the reel into
  **low gear**. Stated reasoning: low gear trades retrieve speed for more
  torque to turn the fish's head and physically lift it out of the depths —
  applied specifically in "the last last minutes" of the fight, when a
  stalled fish risks catching a second wind and taking another run back out.
  This names the same circling/stalled-fish trigger already documented as
  the shift point in [two-speed low-gear fight — timing the
  shift](../techniques/two-speed-low-gear-fight.md#timing-the-shift--stay-high-through-the-run-drop-to-low-once-its-close-and-circling),
  giving it its own name ("pinwheel") and adding the specific **100–200 ft**
  depth band that stage tends to start at on a jig-hooked bluefin.
- **Pull as hard as you can, early:** with drag set properly, the stated
  advice is to pull as hard as physically possible within the **first five
  minutes** of the fight. Reasoning: if the hook is going to pull free, it's
  better to find that out in the first five minutes than after draining all
  the angler's energy fighting the fish for much longer — an early-pressure
  test rather than an easing-in approach.
- **Rail-rod bracing ("railroading" in this transcript's own framing,
  distinct usage from the "railroading" term already logged in [fighting a
  big bluefin](../techniques/fighting-big-bluefin.md#naming--railroading-stokedonfishing-mdmbgdqay-4-2025-10-06-upload-socal-ne-of-santa-barbara-island---catching-monster-bluefin-using-a-flying-bait--catch--cook)):**
  set the rod's extended foregrip on the rail, keep a bend in the rod, and
  turn the handle — the stated framing is that no angler is stronger than a
  triple-digit bluefin, so the rod/rail combination (not raw arm strength)
  is what brings the fish in as long as the bend and the crank are kept
  steady.
