# Searcher Finesse Live-Bait Outfit — 20 lb and 15 lb

Steve Carson's answer to bluefin that refuse anything heavier than 20 lb
test — schools of 15, 20, 30, and sometimes 40 lb tuna that "just won't bite
anything heavier than this" over difficult bait (`ptoIvB2MspE`). It steps
down a further notch to 15 lb test on days the bait or the fish get
especially finicky. Framed as a fall offshore-trip essential most anglers
don't already own, distinct from the calico-bass/bay gear they're likely to
already carry (`ptoIvB2MspE`); no location beyond "aboard the Searcher" is
named on camera. This rig closely matches, and is the earliest-dated
citation in this KB of, [Rig 1 — 15 lb
finesse](bluefin-rig-ladder-by-grade.md#rig-1--15-lb-finesse-the-rig-needed-most),
the same speaker's later (2023-11-14) talk, where the repeated parameters
reach high confidence even though this note's own single-mention figures
stay medium; the two notes cite each other as corroboration rather than
merging, per this repo's note-size guideline.

## The 20 lb outfit

- **Reel:** two named product picks across two citations — a small
  conventional live-bait reel, no level wind, his named pick the **Penn
  Squall II 15** (`ptoIvB2MspE`); a later citation instead names the **Penn
  Fathom 15, star drag, single-speed** — "you don't need a two-speed reel in
  this line category" (`QHY5kmU7OTU`). Both are product/model picks and stay
  low confidence regardless, per the channel's sponsored-claim caveat (Carson
  is Penn's rep).
- **Line:** reel filled about 3/4 of the way up with braid, topped with a
  monofilament top shot — **~50 yd of 20 lb test** in the earlier citation,
  his own emphasis on the exact number, "not 25, 20" (`ptoIvB2MspE`); a later
  citation gives a **25–50 yd** top-shot range instead of the single 50 yd
  figure (`QHY5kmU7OTU`). **General braid-sizing rule, not specific to this
  rig:** run the braid one class heavier than whatever top-shot test sits on
  top of it — e.g. not 20 lb braid under a 20 lb top shot — a further
  citation of the same rule already logged for the 40 lb starter rig in
  [Rig 3 — 40 lb (the starter
  rig)](bluefin-rig-ladder-by-grade.md#rig-3--40-lb-the-starter-rig)
  (`QHY5kmU7OTU`).
- **Rod:** **8 ft**, rated roughly **12–30 lb** — "as close to the middle of
  the line rating as you can get"; some anglers prefer 7 ft or, occasionally,
  9 ft (`QHY5kmU7OTU`). A rod tip too heavy for this small a hook rips the
  hook out of the bait or the fish (`QHY5kmU7OTU`).
- **Leader:** **4- to 5-ft of 20 lb fluorocarbon**, tied to the top shot,
  knot not shown clearly on camera in the earlier citation (`ptoIvB2MspE`); a
  later citation narrows it to **~4 ft**, tied with a single San Diego jam
  knot at the hook end (see [San Diego jam
  knot](../rigging/san-diego-jam-knot.md)) and a four-turn surgeon's knot
  joining the mono top shot to the fluoro — a further citation of the
  4-turns/20–100 lb rule already logged in [essential SoCal
  knots](../rigging/essential-knots.md#parameters) (`QHY5kmU7OTU`).
  **Leader-length rule, stated generally, not just for this rig:** size the
  fluoro so the connecting knot stays outside the rod tip on a cast — roughly
  4 ft on an 8 ft rod, 3 ft on a 7 ft rod, up to 5 ft on a 9 ft rod, varying
  by casting style (`QHY5kmU7OTU`) — a further, earlier-dated citation of the
  knot-clears-the-tip rule already logged in [Searcher 40 lb all-around tuna
  outfit](searcher-40lb-all-around-tuna-outfit.md#the-outfit) and [line &
  leader](line-and-leader.md#when-mono-top-shot).
- **Hook:** **Owner Gorilla Light**, fine wire — "that is a small hook"
  (`ptoIvB2MspE`). **Size discrepancy, kept side by side rather than
  reconciled:** the earlier (2020-10-01) citation has Carson naming and
  actually fishing **size 4**; the later (2021-01-28) citation instead names
  **size 2**, matching a further (2023-11-14) citation logged in [bluefin rig
  ladder by grade — Rig
  1](bluefin-rig-ladder-by-grade.md#rig-1--15-lb-finesse-the-rig-needed-most),
  where Carson names size 2 and notes he'd use a 4 if it came in that size —
  read as implying a 4 wasn't available to him by that later point
  (`ptoIvB2MspE`, `QHY5kmU7OTU`). Not resolved in this corpus — may reflect a
  product-line change between 2020 and 2023, or simply which size he had
  rigged that day.
- **When (his own framing):** when the bait isn't very good and/or the fish
  are finicky — schools of **15, 20, 30, maybe even 40 lb** tuna that won't
  bite anything heavier than this rig; explicitly not built for 100 lb tuna
  (`ptoIvB2MspE`). A later citation frames the same class as the **"20/25 lb
  rig,"** reached for when 25–30 lb tuna "are just being fish" and won't eat
  anything heavier, but not the rig once fish run **over 50 lb** — a reminder
  from Capt. Art on camera (`QHY5kmU7OTU`). That citation ties the pattern to
  a recurring several-week window, the **last week of August to the last
  week of September** in 2020 and the summers before, tuna running **20, 25,
  30, sometimes up to 40 lb**, refusing anything heavier than 20–25 lb test
  (`QHY5kmU7OTU`).
- **Drag and knots:** pair it with a reel that has a very good drag, and have
  a crewman set the drag if unsure how; every knot has to be perfect —
  "everything has to be perfect if you're going to catch a 30 or 40 lb tuna
  on one of these finesse rigs" (`ptoIvB2MspE`).
- **The tradeoff, stated explicitly:** fishing this light risks not landing a
  bigger fish that happens to eat — but without it, some days nothing gets
  bit at all: "if you don't get a bite, you can never land anything... some
  days this is just the only thing they'll bite" (`ptoIvB2MspE`).
- **Casting preference (general, not specific to this rig):** Carson prefers
  underhand casting over overhead — a further, independent citation of the
  preference already logged in [underhand
  casting](../techniques/underhand-casting.md); with a live bait, landing it
  gently and letting it swim away on its own matters more than casting
  distance, though overhand is fine for anglers who need the extra reach
  (`QHY5kmU7OTU`).

## A lighter step-down — 15 lb

For days when the bait is "really not too good" or the fish are "really
extra finicky" and not too big, Carson re-spools the same reel and setup one
step lighter, construction otherwise identical to the 20 lb outfit above
(`ptoIvB2MspE`):

- **Line:** **15 lb test** monofilament top shot, in place of the 20 lb top
  shot (`ptoIvB2MspE`).
- **Leader:** **4-ft of 15 lb test fluorocarbon** (`ptoIvB2MspE`).
- **Hook:** the same **Owner Gorilla Light** fine-wire hook — "I bet that
  barely even shows up on camera... but if you can't see it, neither can the
  fish. Plus, your bait can swim a lot more easily with that" (`ptoIvB2MspE`).
- **Grade expectation:** "lots and lots of schools, not any giant ones" — not
  a 100 lb-tuna rig, same as the 20 lb version (`ptoIvB2MspE`).

A 44 lb bluefin has been landed on this exact 15 lb rig when nothing else
would get bit, corroborating the tradeoff above (`ptoIvB2MspE`; full account
in the evidence file).
