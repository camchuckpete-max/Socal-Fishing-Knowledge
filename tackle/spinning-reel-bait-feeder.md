# Spinning-Reel Live-Bait Feeder — Mechanism & Use

A **live-bait feeder** (sometimes called a bait-runner feature) is a spinning
reel option worth selecting for if you fish live bait often — see
[rod & reel selection](rod-and-reel-selection.md) for how application drives
reel choice generally. Attribution: Lori Sackow, Turner's Outdoorsman (via
SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 187 (Spinning Reels),"
`NGtja-dCiC8`, 2022-06-23); unregistered channel, single mention — medium
confidence.

## The mechanism

The reel carries **two independent drag settings**, stacked so you don't have
to touch a dial mid-bite:

- A **top/main drag** — set to the fighting tension you want once you're
  hooked up, exactly as you would on a reel without the feature.
- A **lower feeder drag** — a separate, deliberately light drag path,
  engaged/disengaged with its own lever, independent of the main drag.

## How to fish it

1. Set the main drag first, to the tension you'd normally fight the fish at —
   not light.
2. Engage the live-bait feeder (a second, dedicated lever).
3. Pin the bait and put it out. With the feeder engaged, the bait swims and
   pulls line under the feeder's light tension only — the angler doesn't have
   to open the bail and hold the line by hand, and doesn't have to back the
   main drag off and reset it later.
4. On the bite, **turn the reel handle.** That single motion disengages the
   feeder and re-engages the main drag in one step — you're immediately
   fighting the fish at the pre-set fighting tension rather than the feeder's
   light tension.

## Why it's worth choosing this feature

Stated benefit: it removes two separate live-bait-fishing chores a
conventional single-drag spinning reel forces on the angler — holding an open
bail while waiting for the bite, or fishing on a deliberately light drag and
having to tighten it up only after hookup. The dual-drag design does both the
"let it swim free" job and the "fight it at full tension" job off one reel,
switched by a single turn of the handle.
