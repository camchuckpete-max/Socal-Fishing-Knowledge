# Spinning-Reel Live-Bait Feeder — Mechanism & Use

A **live-bait feeder** (also called a bait-runner) is a spinning-reel feature
worth selecting for if you fish live bait often: two independent drag
settings let the bait swim out under light tension until the bite, then
convert to full fighting tension with a single turn of the handle
(`NGtja-dCiC8`). See [rod & reel selection](rod-and-reel-selection.md) for how
application drives reel choice generally.

## The mechanism

The reel carries two independent drag settings, stacked so the angler doesn't
have to touch a dial mid-bite (`NGtja-dCiC8`):

- A **top/main drag** — set to the fighting tension for after hookup, exactly
  as on a reel without the feature.
- A **lower feeder drag** — a separate, deliberately light drag path,
  engaged/disengaged with its own dedicated lever, independent of the main
  drag.

## How to fish it

1. Set the main drag first, to the tension for fighting the fish — not light.
2. Engage the live-bait feeder with its dedicated lever.
3. Pin the bait and put it out. With the feeder engaged, the bait swims and
   pulls line under the feeder's light tension only; the angler doesn't have
   to open the bail and hold the line by hand, or fish on a light main drag
   and reset it after hookup.
4. On the bite, turn the reel handle. That single motion disengages the
   feeder and re-engages the main drag in one step, so the fight starts
   immediately at the pre-set fighting tension rather than the feeder's light
   tension (`NGtja-dCiC8`).

## Why it's worth choosing this feature

The dual-drag design removes two separate live-bait chores a conventional
single-drag spinning reel forces on the angler: holding an open bail while
waiting for the bite, or fishing a deliberately light drag and tightening it
only after hookup. One reel does both the free-swimming job and the
full-tension fighting job, switched by a single turn of the handle
(`NGtja-dCiC8`).

## Where this applies

This is reel-selection guidance for spinning-reel live-bait fishing generally,
saltwater or freshwater; it is not tied to a specific SoCal technique, species,
or region (`NGtja-dCiC8`). See [gear classes](gear-classes.md) for where
