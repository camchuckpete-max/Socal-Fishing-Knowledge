## Cameron — article critique (operative doctrine + editorial rules)

**Overall:** big improvement but falls short on being direct and easy to
understand; still a big reliance on single-sourced observations. Get this one
right — it's the base of everything else.

**Editorial rules extracted (now in `templates/style-guide.md` §v2.1):** no
filler/vacuous sentences; mechanism or explicit gap, never bare correlation;
scope every claim; single observation ≠ doctrine (evidence file);
no meta-attribution — don't put Cameron's name in prose or narrate "the
corpus/the source says"; no trip narration inside doctrine; jargon links or
dies; every situations row answers gear/size/color/when/why.

**Operative doctrine/corrections (citable as `(cameron)`):**

- Yellowtail seasonal presence should be stated as water-state mechanics —
  the shape is "migrate north at X °F water temp and bite best at X–X °F" —
  not as a calendar or a "north to south" line. (Numbers still corpus-gapped;
  don't over-rotate — but stop attributing the water-state model to him by
  name.)
- Paddy yellowtail do not "appear with the incoming bluefin" — coincident
  timing is not causation.
- La Jolla (and entries like it) must separate PRESENCE from CATCHABILITY:
  some yellows are in La Jolla year-round, but half-day boats can go months
  without one. Presence ≠ bite.
- The hard-bottom rule is structure-fishing scope only — it has no meaning on
  a paddy over 4,000 ft of water.
- "Exotics", "boils" and similar terms must be linked/defined, not assumed.
- Squid beds are a great place to target yellowtail, but "squid in = yellowtail
  chance" as a law is over-claimed from a single observation.
- Feeding state is the insight, not the sonar shape: the difference between
  "feeding" and "will eat" — understand and write WHY they flip into feeding,
  not "this mark at this depth means they'll bite." Not every bottom-hugging
  school is a wide-open bite waiting for a good angler.
- Moon-phase gap framing: what matters is how they react to nighttime light
  levels and tide activity by phase; an offshore school could behave
  differently from structure fish — genuinely unknown.
- Flyline for most yellowtail presentations is LIGHTER than 40–50 lb (that
  bracket is heavy); hook size follows bait size — say so.
- 80–100 lb line for slow-trolling is not a general recommendation
  ("ridiculous") — single-crew data point, not a class.
- Yo-yo vs dropper on bottom marks: check the logic — a dropper loop is not a
  "reaction bite" presentation.
- Slow-pitch vs yo-yo needs its decision split stated.
- The cast-at-a-troll-strike play is not panga-specific.
- Tuna-pen row = single observation, not a standing situation.
- The sea-lion free-spool move is fight-stage handling, not a
  technique-selection row.
- "Skip jigging" is not a term he has ever heard in a lifetime of SoCal
  fishing — verify/flag the term.
- The tube-bait row said "glide baits" — the C-1 reclassification echo.
- Cheater troll: fine, but if everyone did it on a sportboat it'd be a mess —
  keep the etiquette caveat.
- Zone guides: "we need guides per spot like 'targeting yellowtail, Bahía de
  Los Ángeles'. We don't need it at a spot level, but we definitely need it
  at a region level for species like yellowtail."
- Spawning gap: correct call-out; closing it is a later job, not this
  session's.
