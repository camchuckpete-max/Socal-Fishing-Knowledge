# Evidence — Electronics and Sounder

Trip reports and per-source provenance backing
[electronics and sounder](../electronics-and-sounder.md). One line per
observation/demo, grouped under the parent note's section headings, in its
order. Observations never change the parent's stated doctrine.

## Terminology: fathoms and the fathometer

- `Rf1HKJG-SDg` (Capt. Dave Hansen, Your Saltwater Guide, 2020-12-15):
  states the fathometer/fathoms etymology — the machine was originally built
  to report depth, and flasher-era operators incidentally learned to grade
  fish by how a mark flashed on the same bottom-return signal. Registered
  voice.
- `9hEa3sGTh40` (Capt. Dave Hansen, Your Saltwater Guide, 2019-08-20): the
  same etymology given in briefer form roughly a year earlier — repeated
  doctrine across two videos from the same registered channel, high
  confidence on the core etymology.
- `HGyL7pXy3Ts` (Dave Hansen, Your Saltwater Guide, 2019-05-20): worked
  example — running onto a close-range rock at Catalina, the meter reads 9
  fathoms, converted on the fly to 54 ft, and the boat slides in to 48 ft as
  it settles over a rock whose crest sits ~8 ft below the surface.

## Reading the bottom and grading marks

- `yLpDI8jnizU` (Capt. Duane Diego Mellor, "Fish Finder Tips and Tricks,"
  BDOutdoors, 2020-07-29): source of the Furuno bottom-discrimination
  (hard/soft seam) doctrine and the photograph-the-bite-mark habit.
- `11npFUPOJKU` (BDOutdoors, "Furuno Fish Finder Sounder For Rockfish,"
  2020-12-15): corroborates the same bottom-discrimination and
  mark-grading doctrine from a rockfish-specific angle.
- `6DzbsElGE7E` (Dave Hansen, Your Saltwater Guide, 2021-04-13): the
  brand-agnostic version of the hard/soft-bottom read by return-line
  thickness ("I don't care if you have a Hummingbird fish finder, they're
  all going to show that"); registered voice, single mention of this
  specific brand-agnostic framing — medium confidence on the method itself,
  distinct from the higher-confidence Furuno-discrimination doctrine above.
- `KjVLn4cWHbc` (Tackle Express, "on-the-water" clip, 2026-02-26) and
  `QP6c8vcslVs` (Tackle Express, a second, overlapping re-cut of the same
  footage with a slightly longer intro, 2026-02-19): worked example of
  stacking marks depth by depth (~20 ft near the boat, more at 40 ft, more
  again at 60 ft) before picking a spot to fish, framed as circumstantial
  ("assuming there's fish there") rather than confirmed. Unregistered
  channel; the two uploads are the same footage re-cut a week apart, so this
  is a single mention for confidence purposes, not independent confirmation
  — medium confidence.
- `bM2vUS1B-yQ` (dave-hansen, Your Saltwater Guide, live seminar, PCS Show,
  2023-03-03): the present-to-the-mark rule, framed as a mistake he says he
  watched deckhands make repeatedly running sportboats — told the meter is
  marking well directly under the boat at a known depth (his example: 30 ft
  down), the reflex is to cast far away instead of presenting right there.
  Registered voice, single mention — medium confidence.
- `SgF5hRlEGqU` (Dave Hansen, Your Saltwater Guide, 2022-02-27): the
  manual 0–150 ft range example working tuna schooling under dolphin pods
  offshore.
- `9hEa3sGTh40` (as above, 2019-08-20): the earliest tutorial statement of
  the manual-range rule, with the bait-budget reasoning — a private boat
  can't hold and chum fish up from depth the way a sportboat can ("they can
  look at fish at 30 fathoms and set on it long enough... the fish will come
  up"); working range 0–120 ft on older machines, 0–150 ft on newer.
- `8XrMkWPRxgs` (Capt. Dave Hansen, live seminar, Fred Hall Show 2019,
  filmed by Utah Spahghettzi Björker, 2019-03-12): antedates the tutorial
  above by roughly five months with the same bait-budget reasoning and the
  same 0–150 ft figure — "you don't have enough bait to draw fish up from
  250 feet down... if you're chumming, you don't carry enough bait, you're
  just wasting your time." Most offshore water runs far deeper than that
  working window (he cites ~3,000 ft). Also gives the reason most anglers
  never leave auto in the first place: fear of the machine's buttons, and
  units commonly arriving pre-set to auto from the dealer/installer.
- `m-M0iwX8DjA` (dave-hansen, Your Saltwater Guide, live seminar, 2024 Fred
  Hall Show, 2024-01-27): the 0–300 ft bluefin manual-range window, reasoned
  from a typical bluefin bottom of 2,000–3,000 ft; fish below 300 ft framed
  as "traveling," not biting — "the last thing they want to do is get in the
  [ice bag]" — while fish holding in the 300 ft-to-surface band are framed in
  a half-joking personification as eager to bite: "this water is so hot, we
  got to get out." Gives the mark-button-then-troll-back-with-a-Mad-Mac
  workflow. Registered voice — the core manual-range-for-bluefin rule is
  repeated/high per the citations above; this specific reasoning and
  workflow are a single mention, medium confidence.
- `FEXgl0eQCa8` (Dave Hansen, Your Saltwater Guide, 2021-05-17): the
  mid-screen range-sizing method (15 ft spot → 30 ft range; 25 ft spot →
  40 ft range; offshore 0–300 ft to look for tuna at ~150 ft) and the
  harbor-practice-before-offshore doctrine. Registered voice, single mention
  on each of these two specific methods — medium confidence, distinct from
  the higher-confidence core manual-range rule above.

## Searching range while running, and how sea state limits shallow reads

- `fgTmUq78ofQ` (Art Taylor/Mike, SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday
  Vol. 147 — Live at Turner's Outdoorsman," 2021-05-14): the *Searcher*'s
  wheelhouse sounder is typically run to about 80 fathoms (480 ft) while
  searching for bluefin schools, watching the whole band from the surface
  down; and the sea-state cone-geometry doctrine (flat conditions read the
  full column, choppy conditions tip the cone lower and miss shallow fish
  until the boat is close). Registered channel (`searcher-sportfishing`) —
  medium confidence, single mention of this specific window.
- `ZIJAvAEW_tU` (SearcherSportfishing, "new sonar," 2010-06-01): in 2010 the
  *Searcher* replaced its Westar scanning sonar with a Furuno CH250
  gyro/roll-stabilized unit specifically to address the fixed-transducer
  shallow-fish-miss problem — "we won't miss fish like we have in the
  past." Deckhand identified on camera only as "Kenny" (full name not
  given) explained that without stabilization a rolling boat in rough
  weather returns "annoying air" (aerated-water noise) that swamps the
  signal, effectively "looking for birds through the bottom of the ocean
  from under the boat"; the stabilized unit was expected to keep the boat
  "more in the zone where the fish are." Registered channel, single
  mention, speakers not fully identified — medium confidence on the
  mechanism, low on the specific product praise per the sponsored-claim
  rule. Carried as the equipment-generation reasoning behind the sea-state
  limitation logged above — not a claim that stabilization fully eliminates
  it: the 2021 Searcher doctrine above still describes sea state limiting
  how shallow the sounder can read, so the two are kept side by side rather
  than treated as resolved by this 2010 upgrade.

## Anchor vs. drift call off a live sounder read

- `97clKtVsEOs` (StokedOnFishing, "Intrepid Style 8 Day, part 2," 2015-10-29
  upload, Benito's Island (San Benito Island), Baja, aboard the Intrepid,
  Captain Kevin Osborne): a surface-iron yellowtail bite went quiet once the
  boat anchored, even though the fish were still visibly marking on the
  sounder. The crew's read: fish still on the meter but not responding at
  anchor means try drifting over them instead — position the boat up above
  the marks and let it drift through rather than staying pinned on the
  hook, on the idea the fish want a little more motion in the presentation.
  Making that move brought the bite back on. Unregistered channel,
  sponsor-heavy episode (Simrad, Okuma, Davey's Locker, a car dealership) —
  medium confidence. See
  [yellowtail](../../species/yellowtail.md#where--when) for the full
  session.

## SST and wind overlay for finding the break, and reading calmer water

- `uyjTdgIw-1k` (StokedOnFishing, "Stoked On Simrad, part 2," Kent Joplin of
  Simrad presenting, 2014-11-03, Mission Bay, SoCal): demonstrated the
  chartplotter's weather-overlay screen (Simrad NSO Evo2 → weather
  options), reading SST as a color chart (default Celsius, switched to
  Fahrenheit: 69°F near the boat leaving Mission Bay, breaking to 71°F at
  roughly 7 nm, 72°F by roughly 12–13 nm) and true wind speed as
  color-coded barbs (~11 kt near the boat, easing roughly 7 mi further out;
  flag tips show direction-from and speed by feather count, roughly one
  feather ≈5 kt, two feathers ≈10–15 kt). The crew routed toward the calmer
  zone for passenger comfort as well as toward the fish. Not independently
  corroborated elsewhere in this corpus. Unregistered channel, sponsor-heavy
  Simrad demo — medium confidence on the underlying mechanism, low on the
  product claim.
- `-bw1KDfDjv4` (StokedOnFishing, "Simrad Summer, part 1," 2015-09-27,
  offshore San Diego — the 14 Mile Bank / 209 / 267 bank complex): with the
  chartplotter's SST readout left in "text mode" over the chart, the crew
  read 66–67°F on the 14 Mile Bank itself, climbing to 68°F a little off the
  bank — describing that whole area as one pocket of comparatively warm
  water worth working. Unregistered channel, sponsor-heavy Simrad/Okuma
  episode — medium confidence on the mechanism, low on the product claim.

## Buoy overlay for wave height/period, plus AIS and structure-scan

- `MUpvP-Yl2R0` (StokedOnFishing, "Catalina Island Fishing," 2016-11-23, en
  route to Catalina Island, SoCal, sponsor-heavy Navico/Simrad electronics
  demo): pulled up an on-chart buoy overlay (refreshing roughly every two
  hours) and read a nearby buoy's data straight off the plotter — wave
  height 3 ft at 17 seconds ("below average steepness"), plus a separate
  wind-wave reading of 2 ft at 7 seconds. The same screen carried the SST
  color-shading ("temp breaks") used to help pick the day's zone. The
  captain also named the plotter's AIS layer and a structure-scan sonar
  channel run alongside the main sounder, without demonstrating either with
  a worked example. Not independently corroborated elsewhere in this
  corpus. Unregistered channel — medium confidence on the mechanism, low on
  the product claim.

## Closure boundaries as a chart layer

- `iAr6sbRC384` (Fisher Newb, 2020-03-16, Fred Hall Show 2020 modern
  rockfishing seminar, presenter Bobby Martinez; unregistered channel):
  recommended loading the CDFW depth-closure boundary into a chart app — he

## Bird radar

- `yLpDI8jnizU` (as above): source of the bird-mode radar settings (gain
  near max, range 4–5 mi) and the red-blotch bird-school description.
- `MUpvP-Yl2R0` (as above, same Catalina Island trip, front side of
  Catalina, Halo radar): with a bird school working close off the port
  side, the radar's bird mode painted the birds as small moving blue dots,
  tracked in real time, unit zoomed to 1/2 mile for visibility on camera;
  the presenter cited the unit's spec'd detection range as up to 5 mi. Not
  an independent extension of the 4–5 mi working envelope — a spec claim
  plus a close-in demo, not a longer-range field test. Unregistered
  channel — medium confidence on the mechanism, low on the product claim.
- `SczdZIq3UmE` (StokedOnFishing, 2019-10-02, offshore SoCal,
  Simrad-sponsored demo of a Halo 24 radar unit): the presenter described
  zooming the radar picture in to as close as ~200 ft, versus roughly
  1/8 mile as the closest usable range he attributed to "traditional
  magnetron" radar, and said he had used the unit to run up on dolphin
  schools marking on radar before spotting them visually. Not verified
  against other sources in this corpus. Sponsor-heavy — low confidence on
  the product claim, medium on the underlying mechanism/parameter.

## Search sonar side-to-side scan range and audio bite cue

- `nkJNzdNlm_c` (StokedOnFishing, "Top Gun 80 5-day Fun, part 1," trip
  framed on camera as summertime, resolved from the 2015-01-18 upload date
  to summer 2014, roughly 175–180 mi offshore San Diego, aboard the Top Gun
  80 out of H&M Landing; captains named on camera as Bobby Tapp and Scott
  Hart, ASR-uncertain spelling): asked on camera how far his sonar reaches,
  the captain described running it 400–600 ft side to side — it can be
  opened out further, but the return sweeps too slowly at longer range and
  "you miss a lot of fish" in the meantime. He also described leaning on
  the unit's audio as much as the screen: the normal side-to-side sweep
  makes one sound that changes distinctly when it hits fish, so he doesn't
  have to watch the screen continuously. Unregistered channel, sponsor-heavy
  episode (Simrad, Okuma, Davis Locker, Softsteel monofilament) — medium
  confidence.

## Radar for fog navigation (Baja)

- `d0yGBQDeY_4` (StokedOnFishing, "Big Yellowtail Part 1," 2019-03-05,

  sponsor-heavy Simrad-sponsored episode — medium confidence.
