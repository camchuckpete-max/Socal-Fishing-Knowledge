# San Nicolas Island

**A zone is a run grouping.** San Nicolas — "Nick" or "San Nick" in the fleet's
shorthand (`XLVUhV8DW64`, `5p6gu14ZC4w`) — is the Bight's outside island. Ten
charted spots sit in this zone (cameron): eight around the island itself,
including a named squid ground and a named white seabass zone, plus two
standalone banks the census assigns here on distance. The island fishery is a
squid-driven summer [white seabass](../species/white-seabass.md) and
[yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md) game with rockfish behind it; the water
around it is where the Bight's [bluefin](../species/bluefin-tuna.md) concentrate
in August and hold grade into September (`CMQkHQMxbXM`, `5to3Q5P7w90`).

Two things set the character of the zone. It is far enough out that **weather,
not the bite, usually decides whether anyone fishes it** — a good bite here goes
unfished when the wind comes up (`Blh2BA-7Ono`). And it gets **thin fleet
coverage**, so a quiet week from Nick is a read on how many boats went, not on
how many fish are there (`5p6gu14ZC4w`, `XLVUhV8DW64`; see
[report reading](../planning/report-reading-and-forecasting.md)).

## Getting there

**The run is the gate.** With a forecast of 15–20 kt gusting 25 over a July 2022
weekend, an in-progress white seabass bite at Nick was written off as
unreachable rather than over — "I don't really see any boats making that run out
there in that weather," with the advice to wait for the wind to lie down and
then jump on a trip (`Blh2BA-7Ono`). An August 2022 wind event blew out the
Channel Islands, San Clemente, Nick, Santa Barbara Island and the tuna zone off
Catalina together (`CMQkHQMxbXM`) — this zone goes down with the outside water,
not with the coast.

**Check the swell upstream, not just the point forecast.** The worked example in
the forecast curriculum clicks the **West End of San Nicolas Island** and finds
a benign local wind picture hiding a swell that climbs from **4 ft on a Thursday
afternoon to over 12 ft by Monday**, generated by a system up off Oregon days
earlier (`zKmZ4zql2ws`) — the reason
[sea state](../conditions/sea-state.md#upstream-fetch-check-swell-arrives-after-the-wind-that-made-it)
puts a 48–72 h upstream fetch scan in front of any run to the outside islands.

**Who runs here.** Sportboats out of the Channel Islands landings take trips to
San Nick (`O5aQkex0qGg`); a Channel Islands boat working the island in July 2022
picked up a bluefin on the way home, which is how the report placed fish between
Nick and Ventura Harbor (`Blh2BA-7Ono`). A private 39 ft center console running
charters made San Nicolas the longest run of its 2024 season, called roughly
**115 mi offshore from its launch** for a point a little past the island, and
described the crew as totally exposed out there overnight (`ouBrIdO7d4k`). From
the south the island is reachable and back inside a fishing day: a two-day
calico tournament boat ran to San Nicolas on day one, fished about **3 hours**
until the bite died, and ran back to Santa Barbara Island the same day
(`IATPg9110CE`).

## Structure & bathymetry

The charted library carries **named shore features around the island's east
half** — Blockhouse, Airplane Rock, Seabass Rock, the East End Buoy, Daytona
Beach and Dutch Harbor — plus two named fishing areas rather than points: the
**East End squid grounds**, logged as a big area, and the **Daytona WSB zone**
(cameron). A squid ground and a seabass zone carrying their own names is the
structural fact that explains the fishery: this is island squid-bed water, the
same nest-and-structure pattern the
[white seabass](../species/white-seabass.md#finding-them-sign--sonar) note works
from, where the fish hold near structure and kelp at depth over spawning squid.

**The two banks in this zone are not shoreline.** Potato Bank and Cherry Banks
are catalogued in the spot library's offshore-banks section and land here only
because this is the nearest zone centre — the census flags both as
parent-distance outliers, at **17.5 nm** and **20.3 nm** from the island, both
figures computed from the library's own charted positions (cameron). A
standalone bank rises off the plain and can fish on current from any direction,
each with its own preferred set
([current structure](../conditions/current-structure.md),
[island structure](island-structure.md)), so their read never starts from the
island's shoreline orientation.

Fall coverage is thin enough that the rockfish ground here is described as
presumed rather than reported: "I'm sure it's good rock fishing" at Nick and
Santa Barbara Island, in a week when nobody was up there to say (`5p6gu14ZC4w`).
The one depth figure the corpus attaches to the island is a tackle threshold —
**under 300 ft** is where a 7'10"-heavy bass-style rod stays in hand instead of
dedicated deep rockfish gear on a San Nick sportboat trip (`O5aQkex0qGg`).

## What's there

| species | season | what this zone does for them |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Bluefin tuna](../species/bluefin-tuna.md) | July–October | The August anchor. In early August 2022 the San Nick ground held "most of the bluefin tuna action… there's just a huge volume of fish out there" (`CMQkHQMxbXM`), with fish scattered from Catalina to San Nicolas and up to Ventura; the island is a corner of the September **volume square** SBI–Nick–Clemente–Catalina that holds the bigger fish (`5to3Q5P7w90`, [zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md#the-volume-square)). By mid-October 2022 the fish had left it for the back side of Clemente, with Nick named as a place they could already be back without the coverage to know (`XLVUhV8DW64`) |
| [White seabass](../species/white-seabass.md) | summer, moon-gated | The island's headline fishery. In July 2022, boats that made the run found "really good sea bass fishing" over a weekend and through the following week — while the northern Channel Islands bite had dropped off after the same full moon (`Blh2BA-7Ono`). The library's named squid ground and Daytona WSB zone are where that fishery lives (cameron) |
| [Yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md) | fall, while squid is around | A live chance rather than a report: with squid around the islands in late October 2022, taking squid to Nick was rated a good shot at yellowtail even with nobody covering the zone (`5p6gu14ZC4w`) |
| [Rockfish & lingcod](../species/rockfish-lingcod.md) | season-gated | Presumed good and unreported in late October 2022 (`5p6gu14ZC4w`); San Nick sportboat trips are named as the setting for shallow, under-300 ft rockfish work (`O5aQkex0qGg`) |
| [Calico bass](../species/calico-bass.md) | ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: season shape here | A tournament boat's day-one run reached San Nicolas and put fish on the boat before the bite died after about 3 hours (`IATPg9110CE`) — enough to say the island is fished for bass, not enough to say when or how |
| [Yellowfin tuna](../species/yellowfin-tuna.md) | late summer | Incidental in the bluefin mix: a charter working the ground in early August 2022 went two for three on tuna, one of them a smaller yellowfin (`CMQkHQMxbXM`) |
| [California halibut](../species/california-halibut.md) | summer | One July 2022 trip in this stretch of the Bight weighed bluefin, white seabass, halibut and yellowtail in a single day; the boat was not identified and the day was not pinned to the island, so read it as the mixed-bag shape of the zone rather than a halibut report (`Blh2BA-7Ono`) |

## How it fishes

**The zone is two fisheries stacked on one run, and the day is built to use
both.** Boats fishing white seabass at Nick could slide off the island and add
bluefin to the same trip in July 2022 — the pairing that produced the
bluefin/seabass/halibut/yellowtail day above (`Blh2BA-7Ono`). Island structure
first, offshore water second, is the shape the corpus shows.

**Offshore of the island, tuna is a search problem, not a spot.** The fish "aren't
really holding hard to structure," can move **10–15 miles overnight**, and are
looked for across a block drawn from Catalina to Osborn Bank to Nick to the
"7-Eleven" outside Clemente — an area big enough that boats already on fish lose
them (`Blh2BA-7Ono`). Plan hours of looking against
[search and glassing](../planning/search-and-glassing.md), and read the block the
way [zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md) reads the other named search boxes.

**The moon runs the bite window, and the fleet misreads what follows it.** The
days leading up to the full moon are the best windows; after the moon passes,
the fish are declared gone when what actually happened is that they moved to
water nobody is covering — possibly back up by San Nick (`XLVUhV8DW64`). That is
the same coverage-bias trap the zone sets all fall
([moon](../conditions/moon.md),
[report reading](../planning/report-reading-and-forecasting.md)).

**Trolling is the default program here, and it is not the only one.** Most boats
working the San Nick bluefin ground in August 2022 were pulling
[Mad Macs](../lures/mad-mac.md),
[spreader bars](../lures/spreader-bar.md) or flying-fish rigs — "but you don't
need to do it" (`CMQkHQMxbXM`, [trolling](../techniques/trolling.md)). In the
same week a **250 lb** fish was taped off a spinning rod, a 100–150 lb fish was
hooked on a 10 ft heavy crankbait rod, and a **200 lb** fish came trolling Mad
Macs (`CMQkHQMxbXM`). Grade in that week ran from a **70–75 lb** bluefin
alongside a smaller yellowfin up to those 200 lb-plus fish, which is what makes
the tackle call here a mixed-grade one: the stated answer for casting into a mix
like that is a **25-narrow two-speed class reel, 80 lb braid to an 80 or 100 lb
leader, on an 8 ft graphite-composite rod** — cast stability that still gives a
chance of landing a big one (`CMQkHQMxbXM`,
[gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md),
[bluefin rig ladder by grade](../tackle/bluefin-rig-ladder-by-grade.md)).

**On the island, the program is the general squid-bed one.** Nothing in the
corpus describes a San Nicolas-specific white seabass or yellowtail method, so
work the [white seabass](../species/white-seabass.md#situations--techniques)
router's gray-light and night squid-bed program on the named squid grounds, and
the squid-driven [yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md) program with squid
carried from a bait receiver when the islands have it (`5p6gu14ZC4w`).

## Spots

Ten spots, in two groups: eight on the island itself, and two outer banks the
census attaches to this zone on distance.

- **The east end** — the East End squid grounds (logged as a big area, not a
  point), Seabass Rock and the East End Buoy. The squid ground and the rock's
  name are the zone's white seabass fishery in the library's own vocabulary
  (cameron).
- **The Daytona stretch** — Daytona Beach and the Daytona WSB zone, a second
  named white seabass area rather than a mark (cameron).
- **Named shore features** — Blockhouse, Airplane Rock and Dutch Harbor.
  ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: how any of these three fish, or what the
  names refer to.
- **Outer banks, census-assigned** — Potato Bank and Cherry Banks, both
  catalogued under the library's offshore-banks section and attached here as the
  nearest zone at 17.5 nm and 20.3 nm; the census flags both as parent-distance
  outliers and the assignment is queued rather than re-drawn here (cameron).

Charted positions for all ten are in
[the spot library](../sources/spot-lists.md); the per-spot pages carry them.
