# Baja - 230 / Peanut Bank / 60

**A zone is a run grouping.** Baja - 230 / Peanut Bank / 60 is the innermost
piece of the numbered offshore-bank grid in
[Baja Pacific — North](baja-pacific-north.md) — three charted positions
(cameron) lying straight out from the Punta Banda to Punta Santo Tomás shore,
inside **7.0 minutes of latitude** (31°23.833'N to 31°16.800'N) and **5.8
minutes of longitude** (116°45.000'W to 116°50.800'W).

**Two things separate it from the rest of the grid.** It is the **tightest
three-spot cluster** in the region's bank grid at **8.3 nm** corner to corner —
every other three-or-more-spot bank zone is looser, the nearest being
Sniffer / West 400 / 300 (The Rampart) at 10.8 nm — and it is the one that lies
**closest to the coast**: the **60** sits **5.7 nm** from the **33 Fa. Spot**,
the southernmost mark of
[Punta Banda / Santo Tomas](punta-banda-santo-tomas.md). Only the Banda Bank /
Todos Santos Island pair reaches nearer a charted coastal position (cameron).

numbered grid is a run offshore; this is water a coastal day already passes.

**Nothing in the corpus describes fishing any of these three marks.** The
[region page](baja-pacific-north.md) carries the same gap across the whole
numbered-bank grid — fifty-one charted positions over seventeen bank zones with
no depth, no target species and no doctrine attached to one of them. This is
therefore a **position-and-ladder page**: it says where the zone is, what the
spot names claim, and what is missing.

## Structure & bathymetry

**The zone is a compact triangle, not a line.** The **60** at
**31°23.833'N 116°45.700'W** lies **3.9 nm** from the **Peanut Bank** at
**31°20.000'N 116°45.000'W** on **171°**, the Peanut Bank lies **5.9 nm** from
the **Baja - 230** at **31°16.800'N 116°50.800'W** on **237°**, and the long
side back from the Baja - 230 to the 60 is **8.3 nm** on **032°** (cameron).
All three legs sit inside the census's **12 nm** zone-diameter cap, so all three
marks clustered on each other with nothing attached from outside — unlike
[1140 Finger / 450](1140-finger-450.md) to the west, which is a pair plus an
attachment, or [483/500 / 437](483-500-437.md), which spans 20.7 nm.

**The 60 and the Peanut Bank sit on the same meridian.** They are **0.7 minutes
of longitude** apart (116°45.700'W against 116°45.000'W) and **3.8 minutes of
latitude** apart, so the northern two marks are a straight north–south pair and
the Baja - 230 is the southwest corner that opens the triangle (cameron).

**Two of the three names are depth claims and the third is a shape.** A bank
takes the **fathom depth of its shallowest point** as its name, at six feet to a
fathom ([zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md), `Rf1HKJG-SDg`):

| mark | name read as depth | at six feet to a fathom |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 60 | 60 fathoms | about 360 ft |
| Baja - 230 | 230 fathoms | about 1,380 ft |
| Peanut Bank | not a number — a shape name | ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source |

"most of them / are the banks are called after the highest part of the bank / on

"the 14" being named for miles off the coast. "Six feet is in a fathom" is

[Punta Banda / Santo Tomas](punta-banda-santo-tomas.md).

**Read literally, the 60 is the shallowest bank name in this half of the
library except one.** Of every numerically named position in the spot library's
offshore-banks section south of the border, only the **52** — which the census
puts in [Colonet](colonet.md) — reads shallower (cameron). At about **360 ft**
the 60 lands inside the **300–900 ft** band the KB calls the deep rockfish game
([rockfish & lingcod](../species/rockfish-lingcod.md)) and just outside the
**about 300 ft** rock drop-off fished for bottomfish down-coast of Ensenada
(`0dIwWiOc1NY`). It reads deeper than the yellowtail yo-yo water on this coast:
the spots that hold them off Colonet run **120–180 ft, sometimes 220 ft**
(`YAKOv9bXKO0`), and the heaviest of the three yo-yo depth tiers still tops out
below the 60's name (`hteLeDIy9Qs`; [yo-yo iron](../techniques/yo-yo-iron.md)).
The **Baja - 230** at about **1,380 ft** is a different kind of number
altogether — inside the **150–350 fathom** contour band the KB gives for
[swordfish](../species/swordfish.md), and below the deepest working depth
anything else in the KB records.

**The 60 continues the coastal fathom ladder outward.** The five fathom-named
marks in [Punta Banda / Santo Tomas](punta-banda-santo-tomas.md) run **15, 33,
38, 47 and 48 fathoms** — about 90 to 288 ft — and the 60 sits **12.1 nm** from
the **48 Fa. Spot**, the outermost of them, at the next step down (cameron).
⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source confirms the 60 or the Baja - 230 as a charted

**The Peanut Bank is the zone's only structural claim.** A depth earns the word
*bank* only when it is an isolated high spot rising out of otherwise deep water —
other places read the same fathom figure without being "a bank"
([zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md), `Rf1HKJG-SDg`). The Peanut Bank is one of a
handful of Baja marks in the library named for a shape rather than a number,
alongside the Elephant, the Mushroom, the Worm and the Pistol. ⚠ Flagged gap —
no corpus source: what the Peanut Bank's shape, depth, relief or extent actually
is, or whether the name describes the bank's outline on a chart or something
read on the sounder.

**Nothing here is attached to land, which sets how it would be worked.**
Standalone structure rising off the plain fishes in **any** direction, but each
individual bank still has its own **preferred directions** learned over repeat
trips ([island structure](island-structure.md)). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus
source: bottom composition, relief, contour, high-spot shape, surrounding depth,
preferred directions, drift or current behaviour at any of these three marks.
The `depth_band` is unknown for that reason, not because the names are missing.

**The zone's charted neighbours are the coast on one side and unbuilt banks on
the other.** The **Lower 500** is the nearest foreign bank at **11.8 nm** from
the Baja - 230 on **238°**, in [1140 Finger / 450](1140-finger-450.md), whose
page flags the same overlap from its side; the **52** in [Colonet](colonet.md)
is **16.1 nm** from the Peanut Bank, the **450** **16.1 nm** from the Baja - 230
and the **Banda Bank** **16.1 nm** from the 60 (cameron). The Colonet high spots
lie **30.1 nm** from the zone centre on **151°** — closer than the **32.3 nm**
that [1140 Finger / 450](1140-finger-450.md) measures from its own southern mark
(cameron). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source connects these banks to the Colonet
material, to the coastal spots inside them, or to each other.

**One naming question is unresolved in the library.** The **60** here and the
**60 Mile Bank (53 Fa.)** on the SoCal side are **85.8 nm** apart and mean
different things — the second is named for distance off the coast, the pattern
the lexicon records for Nine Mile Bank and "the 14," and carries its fathom
reading in the parenthesis instead ([zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md),
`Rf1HKJG-SDg`). The **Baja - 230** carries the landmass prefix that separates it
from the **Coronados - 230**, **78.1 nm** north (cameron). ⚠ Flagged gap — no
corpus source: the library's collision rules resolve *475* against *475 Knuckle*
and *220* against *Double 220* but address neither the two 60s nor whether the
60 is a depth name at all.

## Spots

Three charted spots (cameron). They group as a north–south pair plus the
southwest corner:

- **The pair — the 60 and the Peanut Bank**, **3.9 nm** apart on the same
  meridian (cameron). The **60** is the northern, inshore and shallowest-named mark, the
  closest position in the zone to the coast, to the border and to the coastal
  fathom ladder outside Santo Tomás. The **Peanut Bank** sits nearest the zone
  centre at **1.9 nm** and is the only mark here named for a shape rather than a
  depth.
- **The Baja - 230** — the southwest corner, **5.9 nm** from the Peanut Bank and
  **8.3 nm** from the 60. It is the deepest-named, furthest-offshore and
  furthest-from-shore mark in the zone, the closest of the three to the Colonet
  stretch and to the [1140 Finger / 450](1140-finger-450.md) banks, and it
  carries the landmass prefix that separates it from the Coronados - 230.
