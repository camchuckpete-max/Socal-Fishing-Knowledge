# Tanner Bank

**A zone is a run grouping.** This is the Bight's outer pair — **Tanner Bank**
at **32°42.000'N 119°08.000'W** and **Cortes Bank** at **32°26.417'N
119°07.833'W** (cameron), **15.6 nm** apart on a **179°/359°** line that is very
nearly due north–south, with **15.6 minutes** of the separation in latitude and
**0.2 minutes** in longitude. They are the last structure in the
[Southern California Bight](socal-bight.md) before open ocean, and the terminus
of the fall condensation: as the coast cools and fragments, the bluefin condense
outward toward Tanner and Cortez (`XLVUhV8DW64`, `OYOda6T3f-8`,
[bight geography](bight-geography.md), [October](../seasonal/october.md)).

**The fleet works the two as one complex, and the names blur.** A captain naming
the grounds for a three-day charter called them "Cortez and Tanner Bank"
together (`LsFMBCa9DOQ`); a hooked fish on a trip logged as Cortez Bank was
called a "Tanner Bank yellow" on camera (`c3NFkQbdDy0`); the boat, captain, home
port, trip length and run distance of that trip match a later charter logged as
Cortez (`nQvJnfb5jQ4`), and both videos describe the trip as annual. Either the
target bank varies year to year or the crews use the two names loosely for one
bank complex — **flagged, not resolved**
([yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md#where--when),
[bluefin evidence](../species/evidence/bluefin-tuna.md)). Read every claim on
this page as scoped to the complex unless it names a bank.

**Spelling.** The spot library and the BightSST target list write **Cortes**;
every fishing note in this repo writes **Cortez** (cameron,
[eval targets](bightsst-eval-targets.md)). One bank, two spellings, no
distinction intended.

**This is a zone by attachment, not by cluster.** At **15.6 nm** the pair is
past the census's **12 nm** zone-diameter cap, so the two never clustered:
Tanner was left over as a lone bank and minted a zone in its own name, and
Cortes then attached under the **25 nm** rule that puts a lone bank in the
nearest zone it could realistically be fished with (cameron). The zone therefore
carries Tanner's name while most of the corpus material is logged at Cortez —
the naming tension above is the reason that is survivable rather than confusing.

## Getting there

**Every account of this water is an overnight run or longer, from a big boat.**
The corpus states the run in statute miles, never in nautical miles or hours of
transit for a sportboat:

| from | to | stated run | cite |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| San Pedro | Cortez Bank | 96 mi SW | `QSvzVHW9UMk` |
| Point Loma / San Diego | Cortez Bank | 111 mi west | `QSvzVHW9UMk` |
| San Diego (Fisherman's Landing) | Cortez Bank | ~110–115 mi, "a full overnight sportboat run" | `UfuiWFVvz2E` |
| Long Beach | Cortez Bank | ~90 mi | `nQvJnfb5jQ4`, `c3NFkQbdDy0` |
| Long Beach | Cortez Bank | 110 mi | `fDSd9kqwYW0` |
| Long Beach | Tanner Bank | roughly 90–100 mi west | `ntQXxcH5sjI` |
| San Clemente Island | Cortez Bank | 47 mi SW | `QSvzVHW9UMk` |

**The two Long Beach figures for Cortez disagree by 20 miles** — ~90 mi
(`nQvJnfb5jQ4`, `c3NFkQbdDy0`) against 110 mi (`fDSd9kqwYW0`), all three aboard
the same boat out of the same port — so the Long Beach run is stated in the
corpus as anywhere from **90 to 110 mi**. ⚠ Flagged — unresolved, queued in
[the fact-check ledger](../sources/fact-check-ledger.md).

The San Clemente figure is the one the charts can check: **40.8 nm** separates
Cortes Bank from Eel Point on San Clemente's back side (cameron), which is 47
statute miles converted — the on-camera number and the spot library agree. ⚠ Flagged
gap — no corpus source: a run stated from Dana Point, Oceanside or any Orange
County port, and no fuel figure or sea-state envelope for the crossing itself.

**The trip shape the sportboats sell is 2.5 days, and the bank is one leg of
it.** A limited-load charter out of Fisherman's Landing runs overnight to Cortez
Bank, anchors, fishes yellowtail from sunup to roughly 11:00 a.m. or noon, then
moves into deeper water to search for bluefin through the second night, with the
boat pointed south into Mexican waters around 3:00 a.m. on day two for a Baja
kelp-paddy program (`UfuiWFVvz2E`). A day-and-a-half charter fishes the bank's
bottom and its yellowtail in one shot (`fDSd9kqwYW0`); the two- and three-day
charters add the night bluefin program (`oXunQKSbc2g`, `LsFMBCa9DOQ`).

**A private boat can do it, and the accounts that describe one describe a big
one.** A group entirely new to bluefin ran **2.5–3 hours** out to Tanner Bank on
a captain's own triple-outboard boat and landed multiple 100 lb-plus fish in a
day, the crew calling the return leg alone over 100 mi (`vCskOx6N-XM`). That is
the only non-sportboat trip in the corpus for this zone.

**Expect a fleet when the bite is on.** Anchoring at Cortez in August 2024 meant
picking a slot among "northern boats… southern boats… middle boats," private and
commercial sportboats together on the same structure (`nQvJnfb5jQ4`). Where the
other boats sit relative to the high spot is itself a positioning input here —
see [How it fishes](#how-it-fishes).

**Regulatory — United States. Jurisdiction: California / CDFW. Checked as of
2026-08. Verify current rules before you fish** ([US waters](us-waters.md)).
Three limits are stated on camera at these banks and none is stamped here as
current regulation: a **two-fish-per-person daily bluefin limit**
(`947solNfiPw`), a **10-fish** rockfish allowance (`fDSd9kqwYW0`) and a **20
fish per angler** boat allowance on a rockfish stop (`Y1xeieQI3B4`). Confirm all
three against CDFW and the CPFV rules for your trip length before you fish.
⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: MPA boundaries, closures or restricted areas
stated for either bank. The corpus names the **Cow Cod Conservation Area** as an
offshore closure with its own depth limit and its own as-of date
([rockfish & lingcod](../species/rockfish-lingcod.md)) but gives no boundary for
it, so whether either bank falls inside it is unverified — **check the CCCA
boundary against your position with CDFW/NOAA before you drop.** Compliance is
positional, not depth-based ([US waters](us-waters.md)). Note also that the
2.5-day program crosses into Mexican waters on its second leg (`UfuiWFVvz2E`),
which is a different jurisdiction and a different paperwork set
([Mexican waters](mexican-waters.md)).

## Structure & bathymetry

**Two isolated highs, one leg apart.** Working both is a single north–south run
of **15.6 nm** with open water between them (cameron), and no third charted mark
in the middle to break it. A bank is an isolated high spot rising out of
otherwise deep water, named for the fathom depth of its shallowest point at six
feet to a fathom (`Rf1HKJG-SDg`, [zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md)) — but neither
of these carries a number in its name, so the convention that reads the 209 or
the 43 says nothing here. Both are proper-named banks, and their depths come
from the sources instead.

**The depth bands the corpus actually states, shallowest first:**

| band | what happens there | cite |
| --- | --- | --- |
| the shallows at the bank's far end, near a buoy | anchored for the yellowtail bite | `fDSd9kqwYW0` |
| ~120 ft over the high spot | anchored at Tanner; yellowtail marked at 60–80 ft in this water at Cortez | `ntQXxcH5sjI`, `UfuiWFVvz2E` |
| 15–25 fathoms | the anchor zone at Cortez for the morning yellowtail program | `UfuiWFVvz2E` |
| ~150 ft on a ridge on the bank's edge | a drift for reds, sheephead and whitefish | `fDSd9kqwYW0` |
| ~50 fathoms | the water an anchor job and a drift both produced in on the same day | `zBd1mayUt_I` |
| 50–60 fathoms, the drop-off edge | where a repositioned boat found bluefin and yellowtail "right out the gate" | `nQvJnfb5jQ4` |
| 50 fathoms down to 80, "even below" | night jig marks read 360° around the boat | `LsFMBCa9DOQ` |

That is a bank with a shallow, anchorable top in the 15–25 fathom range, a
named high spot, and an edge that falls away to 50–60 fathoms close enough to
reposition onto within a session (`nQvJnfb5jQ4`, `UfuiWFVvz2E`). The night jig
column running to 80 fathoms and below is deeper than the 30–50 fathom
sounded-school range the species note gives, and is kept as a bank-specific
extension rather than a correction ([bluefin tuna](../species/bluefin-tuna.md),
[electronics and sounder](../planning/electronics-and-sounder.md)).

**The high spot has sides, and the sources use them.** One boat anchored "on the
Eastern side of the high spot" at Tanner (`947solNfiPw`); another read where the
fleet sat relative to the high spot and the drop-off before moving onto the
edge (`nQvJnfb5jQ4`). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: preferred directions
per bank, bottom composition, relief profile, kelp, or any named sub-feature of
either bank — no source in this corpus names a rock, pinnacle or high spot by
name at Tanner or Cortez.

**Nothing charted is close.** Nearest positions in the spot library, measured to
whichever bank is closer (cameron): **Cherry Banks** at **17.3 nm** off Tanner,
which is parented to [San Nicolas Island](san-nicolas-island.md) rather than out
here; **267 (The Condom)** at **22.9 nm** off Cortes, in the
[81 / 381](81-381.md) zone; **711** at **26.3 nm** off Tanner
([474 / 711](474-711.md)); **Northeast Bank** at **26.4 nm** off Cortes; **381**
at **26.5 nm** off Tanner; and the back side of San Clemente Island at
**31.9 nm** off Tanner, Eel Point being the nearest island position
([San Clemente Island — back side](san-clemente-island-back-side.md)).
⚠ Flagged stub — no corpus source yet: Northeast Bank and Hancock Bank are
charted positions with no page on this ladder, so the water immediately west and
south of Cortes has no built fallback. The realistic fallback from here is the
island, and it is over 30 nm away.

**Forage.** The boats fish sardines, and the bite is a chum bite: bluefin came
off bait marks sitting around **60 ft** while the boat chummed sardines at
Tanner (`ntQXxcH5sjI`), and sardines running **6–8 in** were the flyline bait on
the bank (`fDSd9kqwYW0`). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: what the fish are
eating natively out here — no squid, no flying fish sighting, no bait-ball or
paddy description scoped to either bank. The Bight's best paddy water is south
of San Clemente and Catalina and along the coastal strip (`HzE4FHHcvTk`,
[bight geography](bight-geography.md)), which is not this water.

**One water-temperature record exists for the zone, and it is not a bluefin
one.** Day one at Cortez Bank fished **74–75 °F** for the mixed
yellowtail/yellowfin/skipjack bite, and the boat then ran to Osborn Bank
"chasing some cooler water," finding **70 °F** at the bank edge and **72 °F**
where it sat, where the graylight bite was bluefin (`i3qIAHW-SJc`,
[water temperature](../conditions/water-temperature.md)). In the fall the outer
banks read the other way: the Tanner end showed as the lighter orange band on
the chart, 60-something degrees and the same shade as the back side of Santa
Cruz, and that slightly cooler water was called the more ideal water for those
bluefin (`OYOda6T3f-8`).

## How it fishes

**Anchor and chum is the program, and current gates it.** The bank is fished as
a flyline with the current — hooked sardine, 25 lb test, No. 2 hook — and when
the current is slack the exotics do not go: a morning that was "missing the
current" was spent scratching rockfish until it moved, after which a **five-hour
yellowtail bite** closed the charter (`fDSd9kqwYW0`,
[flyline](../techniques/flyline.md),
[current & structure](../conditions/current-structure.md)). Anchor is not the
only answer — a day at Cortez produced on both a drifting job and an anchor job
in water around 50 fathoms, and when the anchor spot slowed the crew moved off
to search rather than sit (`zBd1mayUt_I`).

**Positioning is read off the structure and off the fleet.** Mid-trip
repositioning came from reading where other boats sat relative to the high spot
and the drop-off, then working the **50–60 fathom** edge, which produced "right
out the gate" (`nQvJnfb5jQ4`). Another captain moved onto anchor mid-morning
after hearing a nearby boat was stacking fish "1 to two at a time or 5 to 10 at
a time" on an established chum line, judging his own drifting boat was picking
off only a little piece of that school from outside the spot (`QSvzVHW9UMk`).
Once anchored, with the current pulling down the left-hand side, the deck call
was to spread out along that side including from the bow rather than stacking
the stern (`QSvzVHW9UMk`).

**The day has a shape, and the night has two windows.** Yellowtail from sunup to
roughly 11:00 a.m. or noon, then deeper water for bluefin through the night
(`UfuiWFVvz2E`); a sardine bite from first light at anchor (`947solNfiPw`); an
overnight run that started yo-yo and jig fishing around **1:00 a.m.** and put
roughly half a dozen 30 lb-class fish aboard on that first session
(`LsFMBCa9DOQ`). One captain's read of the night bite ran two windows — roughly
**9:00 p.m. to 11:00 p.m. or midnight**, a lull, then **3:30 a.m. to sunrise**
(`LsFMBCa9DOQ`) — ⚠ single-source, one season, queued in
[the fact-check ledger](../sources/fact-check-ledger.md); it is the corpus's
only clock-time night guidance. At the other end of the day, an intense sundown
bite produced multiple bluefin over **100 lb** on the kite and the flyline in
the same window (`zBd1mayUt_I`).

**Fish arriving here take their time coming up onto the bank.** One captain
described the school holding outside the bank for several days, close to a week,
with a further **10 to 14 days** passing before they pushed in close enough for
the boat to anchor directly on the structure (`eL1Qm33-Mj0`). When they are
established the distribution can be unlike anything inshore: driving through
**20 to 30 miles** of fish was called "not very common," with a boat **15 miles
away** seeing the same thing rather than the usual isolated **4–5 sq mi** patch,
on **50 to 100 fish** days (`LsFMBCa9DOQ`). That is the opposite end of the
scale from the Bight's compact spot-check, and bigger even than the
**~50–60 square-mile** search box the region's other complexes are planned
around (`Blh2BA-7Ono`, [zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md),
[search and glassing](../planning/search-and-glassing.md)).

**The mixed grade is this zone's defining tackle problem.** At Tanner a trip
faced "10 pounders all the way up to 80 pounders, maybe even 100" moving through
on "one size line," and matching hook, leader and weight without going
under-gunned was called "a total game" (`947solNfiPw`). The corpus's answers
sit side by side rather than reconciled, and what they turn on is grade and bite
quality, not section order:

- **Go down in hook size, not in line class** — a 1/0 circle stepped down to a
  No. 1 when bites are not coming, because line too light to land the fish
  wastes the trip: "you're gonna flag a fish for two hours" (`shZCjX2-fkI`,
  registered channel, [hooks](../tackle/hooks.md)). The **Tanner Bank data
  point** for that rule is the 2020 season — 40–60 lb bluefin at Tanner, where a
  smaller hook got bit better than a larger one regardless of bait size.
- **Step the line up when the grade is up** — on the same Cortez trip, 100 lb
  line was called easier for hooking the bigger fish being seen than 30 lb
  (`nQvJnfb5jQ4`, [double trouble](../rigging/double-trouble-rig.md)).
- **What the 40 lb class actually costs on big fish:** during an evening
  "Sundowner" bite on the bigger grade, the crew estimated they hooked about six
  and landed one on 40 lb with a tiny circle hook — "really tough"
  (`nQvJnfb5jQ4`, [fighting big bluefin](../techniques/fighting-big-bluefin.md)).

**The daytime numbers the corpus gives for this bank**, all from anchored or
adrift bait fishing: flyline **25 lb** mono or fluorocarbon to a **No. 2** hook
(`fDSd9kqwYW0`, `nQvJnfb5jQ4`); a **40 lb** flyline floor for bluefin in
daylight, droppable to 30 lb only if the fish are line-shy, with the caveat that
Cortez yellowtail "swim straight down" into the rocks on lighter line
(`UfuiWFVvz2E`); **20 lb** fluorocarbon to No. 4 and No. 2 hooks on a 20–30 lb
grade day, with a caution that some No. 4s run light wire (`QSvzVHW9UMk`); and
**circle hooks for bluefin, J hooks for yellowtail** as the trip-wide briefing
(`UfuiWFVvz2E`), against which one crew's stated preference for J hooks on a
finicky bluefin bite is kept side by side (`LsFMBCa9DOQ`,
[hooks](../tackle/hooks.md)).

**The night program is a jig program, and it is heavier here than the general
note's middle.** A **300 g** floor for night jigs, with 250 g allowed only for
anglers savvy enough to fish it well, over **80 lb** braid and **130 lb** mono
or fluorocarbon (`UfuiWFVvz2E`); on a windy, hard-drifting night at
Cortez/Tanner the standard 250–350 g jigs would not hold the zone and the boat
stepped up to **500 g** (`LsFMBCa9DOQ`,
[knife jigging](../techniques/knife-jigging.md)). Colour was called close to
irrelevant, with white-with-polka-dots named if pressed (`UfuiWFVvz2E`,
[knife jigs](../lures/knife-jigs.md)).

**Sea state is the gate on a bank this far out.** A day at Cortez was fished
with the weather up and an uphill current against a light wind — chop, but
fishable (`QSvzVHW9UMk`); a night on the same complex drifted hard enough to
break the jig program (`LsFMBCa9DOQ`); a Tanner day ran heavier-than-usual swell
and the anglers were coached to pace themselves through the fights
(`vCskOx6N-XM`). Nothing out here is sheltered by land in any direction, so the
upstream fetch scan belongs in front of the run
([sea state](../conditions/sea-state.md)). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source
states a wind or swell number that turns this zone off.

**Conditions read.** From October the regime flips to reading the temperature
chart and the chlorophyll chart, and across a big area with no temperature
differentiation the fish are most likely on structure like the banks
(`OYOda6T3f-8`, [water regimes](../conditions/water-regimes.md),
[report reading & forecasting](../planning/report-reading-and-forecasting.md)).
**Cortes Bank is a BightSST eval target**, one of the original eight
([eval targets](bightsst-eval-targets.md), cameron), so this zone is one of the
few where the conditions pull is a scored spot rather than a per-position
lookup; Tanner is not on that list, and at **15.6 nm** away it should not be
read off Cortes without saying so.

## Spots

Two charted spots (cameron), north to south — the axis the zone is drawn on:

- **Tanner Bank** — the northern mark and the one the zone is named for, at
  **32°42.000'N 119°08.000'W**. The August anchored bite is logged here: a
  multi-day trip out of Long Beach anchored in **~120 ft** with bluefin taken off
  bait marks around **60 ft** over chummed sardines (`ntQXxcH5sjI`), and another
  boat anchored "on the Eastern side of the high spot" (`947solNfiPw`). The
  private-boat kite trip and the 2020 small-hook data point are both scoped to
  this bank (`vCskOx6N-XM`, `shZCjX2-fkI`).
- **Cortes Bank** — the southern mark, **15.6 nm** at **179°** from Tanner, at
  **32°26.417'N 119°07.833'W**. It carries most of the corpus: the 15–25 fathom
  anchor zone, the 120 ft high spot, the 50–60 fathom drop-off edge, the
  yellowtail program and the bottom stops (`UfuiWFVvz2E`, `nQvJnfb5jQ4`,
  `fDSd9kqwYW0`, `Y1xeieQI3B4`). It is also the zone's BightSST eval target
