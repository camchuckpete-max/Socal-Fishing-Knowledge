# "La Rasa" high spot

**[Baja only]** — a **high spot** in the
[Bahía de los Ángeles](bahia-de-los-angeles.md) zone, called at **40–60 ft** and
named on camera by the panga captain when an angler asked what the ground was
(`odiIWmut6gM`). It was the first stop of day four of one trip, fished by
casting, and the captain's plan for it was the bigger bottom fish first with
[yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md) to follow. What it produced on camera was
a [skipjack](../species/skipjack-tuna.md) and a
[cabrilla](../species/cabrilla.md); the trip's yellowtail came only after the
crew gave up on it and went deeper.

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): *the trip's* yellowtail. Yellowtail were
already landed earlier in the same trip, before this stop — *"and a couple big
yellows"* and *"Jamie just got that last yellow"* both fall on an earlier day
(`odiIWmut6gM`). What the record supports is that the yellowtail **of day four**
came after the move, not the trip's. The same scope claim is repeated in the
yellowtail row of [What's there](#whats-there).

⚠ Fact-check (unverifiable): the name. The captain's answer is rendered by the
auto-captions as *"right here is a is the La Rasa Part"* (`odiIWmut6gM`), and
that rendering is the only one the corpus offers — this page carries it rather
than correcting it to a guess. It resolves to no charted position in the KB.
Queued in [the fact-check ledger](../sources/fact-check-ledger.md).

**It carries no charted position in [the spot library](../sources/spot-lists.md).**
That library stops at San Quintín on the Pacific side and carries nothing on
this coast, so a conditions pull has to run against
[the zone](bahia-de-los-angeles.md#structure--bathymetry) rather than against
this mark.

The whole record here is **one stop, on one morning of one trip** whose month is
never established on camera (`odiIWmut6gM`). Read the page as that stop, not as
the ground's fishery.

## Getting there

**You arrive in the zone's panga program, and the crew navigates none of it.**
Every documented day in this zone is fished from a local panga that collects you
at your hotel room before dawn, with the captain choosing the ground — the
drive, the lodging, the operators and the day shape are all on
[the zone page](bahia-de-los-angeles.md#getting-there).

**This was where a day started, not where one ended up.** Day four opens here on
camera, with the crew already fishing before the ground is named (`odiIWmut6gM`);
the move off it, later the same morning, is what put them on
[Cardonosa](cardonosa-isla-cartito.md).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-internal): "first stop", "opens here" and "morning".
*"Day four"* is verbatim (`odiIWmut6gM`), but the transcript never calls this the
day's first stop and never says morning; fishing calls run between the day-four
card and the naming, and the captain's line right after the depth call is
*"Let's go fishing."*
[Cardonosa](cardonosa-isla-cartito.md#getting-there) already carries the same
finding from the other side, stating the corpus "never calls it morning, never
says the shallower high spot was the day's first stop". The lead of this page
carries the same "first stop of day four" claim.

## Structure & bathymetry

**A high spot at 40–60 ft, and that is the entire description.** Asked *"what's
it called?"*, the captain gives the name and the depth and nothing else, having
just said *"we're fishing the high spot"* (`odiIWmut6gM`) — the captions render
the sounding "40T 60 ft", read here as 40 to 60 ft. No relief, bottom
composition, extent or surrounding depth is stated.

**The depth band is what places it in the zone's shallow program.** High spots
under 100 ft are where the zone's yellowtail fishing lives, and in that water the
fish respond to a surface bait immediately even when they are marked deep
(`T3cEQUqiH8E`) — [the zone page](bahia-de-los-angeles.md#structure--bathymetry)
holds that read and the rest of the zone's depth programs.
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): "marked deep", and the depth condition.
The source says *"if we're in less than 100 ft of water, these fish will respond
to a surface bait immediately even if they're deep"* (`T3cEQUqiH8E`) — a
condition on **water depth**, with no sounder, meter or marking language anywhere
in the transcript, and "these fish" not named as yellowtail until ten lines
later. The adjacent *"The on high spots"* is a caption fragment, not the stated
condition, so "high spots under 100 ft are where the yellowtail fishing lives" is
a reading rather than a statement.
[The zone page](bahia-de-los-angeles.md#structure--bathymetry) quotes the line
correctly as "even if they're deep"; the sonar framing is added here.

**Whether this is a separate ground from Cardonosa is unresolved.** The move off
here was described only as going to look in deeper water, yet the shelf the crew
then named is called at 60 ft as well (`odiIWmut6gM`) — the top of this mark's
own range. Nothing on camera says whether the two are the same structure, two
marks in sight of each other, or a long run apart. The same question is flagged
from the other side on
[Cardonosa](cardonosa-isla-cartito.md#structure--bathymetry). ⚠ Fact-check
(unverifiable): a page each is published because the corpus names two grounds;
if they are one, one page is a duplicate under a garbled name.

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: bottom composition, relief, the extent of the
high spot, what it drops into, or any sounder picture at this mark — nothing in
the record describes what the meter showed
([electronics & sounder](../planning/electronics-and-sounder.md)).

## How it fishes

**The documented program is casting, and the stop was slow.** The narration
marks *"a considerable amount of time casting"* before anything changed
(`odiIWmut6gM`). Two fish over that stretch is the whole catch record.
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the two-fish count. Two fish are the only
ones **identified** at this stop, but the same stretch carries an *"Oh, double
up"* and further hookup calls — *"There we go"*, *"He did it. Mitch"* —
(`odiIWmut6gM`), so the transcript does not support two as the total landed. The
lead's "what it produced on camera was a skipjack and a cabrilla" rests on the
same count. The
zone's shallow cast-and-wind doctrine — speed above all, twitch baits burned at
structure, heavy line because fish rock you — is on
[the zone page](bahia-de-los-angeles.md#shallow--cast-and-wind-fast), with the
casting mechanics in
[surface iron casting](../techniques/surface-iron-casting.md) and the baits in
[jerkbaits](../lures/jerkbaits.md). Nothing states which of those was thrown
here.

**The tide read is what ended the stop.** The crew noticed a correlation between
that particular tide and previous slow periods on the trip, and asked the
captain if they could go in search of deeper water and potentially find yellows
(`odiIWmut6gM`) — which worked. That is one crew's read on one trip, not an
established property of this ground; the general layer is
[tide & slack](../conditions/tide-and-slack.md), and the zone-level version of
the same episode is on
[the zone page](bahia-de-los-angeles.md#how-it-fishes).

**Read that as a reason to leave, not as a verdict on the mark.** The corpus
records this ground being abandoned once, mid-tide, on a day the whole trip had
been reading as slow — it never records it being given a second chance under
different water.
