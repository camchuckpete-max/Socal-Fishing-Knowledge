# Bahía de los Ángeles (BOLA / Bay of LA)

**A panga fishery on the Sea of Cortez side of northern Baja, ~8 hours' drive
from San Diego, fished out of a hotel strip with day-boat operators.** Yellowtail
and cabrilla are the two things you go for; they are **not in the same places**,
so you pick a target each morning rather than expecting both.

This note is the trip-planning entry point — when to go, what it costs, what
paperwork you need, how the days run. Species decision logic stays in the
routers ([yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md),
[cabrilla](../species/cabrilla.md)); execution stays in the technique notes.

**Not to be confused with San Felipe.** The San Felipe mothership fishery
(Tony Reyes-style multi-day trips out of the northern Sea of Cortez) is a
different operation ~4 hours further north, and video titles conflate the two —
see [Sea of Cortez](sea-of-cortez.md).

## When to go

Primary source is a 2026-08-06 trip-planning walkthrough from Strictly Irons
(`P36VGPPf120`), answering viewer questions on the drive down. Unregistered
channel — `medium`. The hosts are explicit that they can't give "a hard fast
answer."

| Window | What it fishes like |
| --- | --- |
| **Through April** | Get in **before** the wind season if you want surface fish. |
| **April–May** | **Hit or miss — wind is the biggest factor.** Local saying: *"Mayo es malo"* — May is bad, because of wind. Expect days sitting on the beach waiting for it to lie down, then relocating the fish. |
| **End of April → early June** | Called out as the good window, wind permitting. |
| **May–June** | **The prime months.** |
| **July on** | "Everything starts to go down." Water heats, **fish drop out of the surface program.** |
| **August–October** | The late-season play, if you want **dorado, marlin, sailfish, and occasionally tuna** — how hot the water gets decides it. |
| **Winter** | Normally the slow season — "it tapers off, all the bonitos get to rest." |

**The mechanism that matters:** as the water heats through summer, **the fish
sound**. You lose the surface bite and move to
[dropper loop](../techniques/dropper-loop.md), more live bait, some
[yo-yo iron](../techniques/yo-yo-iron.md) and
[knife jigs](../lures/knife-jigs.md) — "anything heavy on the bottom."

> **This independently corroborates Cameron's own BOLA read.** Cameron's
> September program (logged in [September](../seasonal/september.md) and the
> [yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md) router) is that the fish are **on the
> bottom** that time of year, worked with dropper loops and yo-yo iron. Same
> pattern, same mechanism, arrived at independently — a second source for what
> had been a single-angler observation.

**Anomalous years happen.** The 2026 season ran against the pattern — biting
through the winter from November, with consistent yellowtail limits posted
"every other day." Treat the table as the prior, not the forecast; the hosts'
own advice is to plan 2–3 months out, then follow the operators and talk to
the captains close to the date.

## Paperwork

Mexican entry and fishing paperwork — the FMM, the CONAPESCA licence, the
passport and mandatory vehicle insurance — is the same for every Mexican trip
and lives one rung up, in [Mexican waters](mexican-waters.md). Nothing on this
page is BOLA-specific paperwork; go there before you drive.

## Getting there

Route from San Diego: the 8 east to **Calexico**, cross at Mexicali, then south.

| Leg | Time |
| --- | --- |
| San Diego → Calexico | 1.5–2 h |
| Calexico → San Felipe | 2 h |
| San Felipe → Gonzaga Bay | 2 h |
| Gonzaga → Bahía de los Ángeles | 2 h |
| **Total** | **~8 h without stops; 9–10 h at a relaxed pace** |

- **Exchange money right after the border** — there's a spot about 3 minutes
  past the crossing. **$300–400** covers a 4-day trip.
- **Wind there is intensely local.** *"This area is weird, because you go 5
  miles up the beach and then it's totally calm"* (Nate Winnicki, BD Outdoors,
  `T3cEQUqiH8E`). A blown-out morning at the bay does not mean a blown-out
  day — the operators move. On tournament days the crews also simply **start
  earlier** to get the morning window before it builds (`6P34jI9q1tE`).
- **Fuel discipline:** top off before crossing, and again in San Felipe.
  Stations are "few and far between" once you're past San Felipe city.
- **The last stretch has the potholes.** From Gonzaga: two lefts, straight, a
  left, then 20–30 minutes, one more left and it's a straight shot in. Partly
  repatched as of 2026-08; the rest of the road was reported fine.
- Food/stops used on this trip: **Tacos El Güero** in San Felipe (across from
  a gas station), **Calimax** for groceries, snacks, and water.
- **Military checkpoints** are on the route — reported as routine and "no big
  deal" (`T3cEQUqiH8E`).
- **It is all paved** — "no dirt roads down there"; past San Felipe the road
  runs along the coast (`wX7jUycIJ-U`). Door-to-door reality check: one crew
  left at 06:30 and reached the hotel at 17:31 — an **11-hour travel day**
  with a lunch stop (`T3cEQUqiH8E`).

**Buy everything before you cross.** Tackle and food further from the border
run up to roughly **twice** the price — bring gear, ice, coolers, and beer with
you. A second source puts it starker: *"there's no tackle shop to really speak
of… everything is really limited, so you pack up extra. Not the trip you want
to pack light for"* (`odiIWmut6gM`).

## Comms and safety

- **There is no cell service** through the desert, at Gonzaga, or at Bahía.
  You keep coverage around Calexico and San Felipe, then lose it.
- **Wi-Fi at the hotel is spotty** — enough to text and check in, not to stream.
- Starlink, Roam, and iPhone satellite messaging were all named as working.
- **Travel in a group, minimum two vehicles.** A tyre problem in the desert
  puts you a long way from help.
- Text your lodging address to someone at home before you lose service.
- Some Spanish helps — enough to ask for help, handle fuel, and exchange money.

## Lodging and operators

**Hotel Villa Vita** is a second option named on a different trip
(`odiIWmut6gM`). **Costa del Sol** is the Strictly Irons default, chosen for
access rather than
luxury: the panga operator picks up **directly in front**, the mercado is a
2-minute walk, the launch ramp 5–7 minutes. On-site restaurant and bar, and
they will **cook your catch**. AC, ceiling fans, soap and shampoo.

- **~$90–120/night** depending on room size (two-bed ≈ $90, four-bed ≈ $120).
  Quoted in pesos on this trip: two rooms 1,800 pesos ≈ $110 at a ~16:1 rate;
  the triple 2,000 pesos.
- A deposit is taken at check-in. The source's practice is to **settle
  everything — rooms and pangas — on arrival** rather than collecting money on
  the last day.
- Several other hotels and motels run along the same strip, same basic setup.
- **Campo Archelón** is a different kind of base — an eco-camp on the beach
  offering casitas and camping with a communal feel, run by **Antonio
  Resendes** and family. The family came to Bahía in 1979 doing sea-turtle
  conservation work, and now run it as ecotourism on the reasoning that
  conservation needs local economic opportunity behind it (`8c07sN9wxGM`).
- **Shore fishing is walkable** from the hotel strip — one crew "snuck across
  the street" for an evening session on arrival (`T3cEQUqiH8E`).

**Panga operators named, all spoken of as good:** Fish Arms (the Strictly
Irons regular), FG's, Rick's, Hoy, Dagots, Eigor, Ivan. **They talk to each
other** about where fish are showing on the day. Also documented working out
of Bahía: **Bola Sport Fishing** (Joe — a new panga, the *Guardian*, with
current electronics and a new Suzuki outboard, `XRIjUgpuyZ8`), and captains
named **Joel** and **Nego** (`wX7jUycIJ-U`, `ojE5H6AdGGo`).

## What a fishing day looks like

- Wake ~04:30, gear staged on the sidewalk, **depart 05:30**.
- **The boat comes to you.** "There's no meeting the captain at the ramp. They
  pull the boat right up to your hotel room, load you up, and you are on your
  way" (Capt. Luke, Artemis Charters, `odiIWmut6gM`, uploaded 2026-07-02; on-camera dates are day-of-month only — "the 22nd", "Wednesday the 24th" — so the trip month is not established). Retrieval
  works the same way — the boat is run up onto the beach and trailered off it,
  by a local whose job that is.
- The operator runs to the grounds — one trip ran **~20 miles north** of the
  bay for cabrilla and yellowtail.
- **A stated day-order from one captain:** cabrilla first thing, then after
  roughly two hours move and try for yellowtail and snapper (`odiIWmut6gM`).
- **The fleet makes bait first.** Asked why every boat was stopped in the same
  place, the answer was "all the guys is catching bait" — for the dropper-loop
  program. A crew fishing lures can skip it and run straight to the rock.
- **Yellowtail are pelagic and move.** The boats check multiple areas and
  radio each other. It can take a long time to locate them, "but once they do,
  the bite's usually pretty good."
- **Cabrilla and yellowtail sit in different areas** — you choose which to
  target for the day rather than expecting to cover both.
- Tide state is read on the day and changes the plan.

**Book with someone you have a relationship with, and have a fallback.** One
documented trip had its pre-booked operator simply not show for a 14:00–15:00
departure; the day was saved only because a captain they knew from a previous
trip (Jorge) came off the water, dropped his own clients, and took them out at
17:45 (`odiIWmut6gM`). The Strictly Irons crew's stated reason for staying with
Fish Arms across trips is the same thing from the other side — a built
relationship. A second contact named on that trip is a Fish Arms captain whose
surname is rendered "Morales" in the captions (given name ASR-garbled — treat
the handle as unverified).

## Cost

For a **4-day trip** (4 days of driving/travel, 3 nights, **2 fishing days**),
splitting shared costs across a group of 13:

- **$800–$1,000 per person, all in** — roughly **$250/day** — including
  licences, fuel, tips, and spending money.
- **Panga: $550/day**, plus ~$100 tip.
- The variable that moves the number is food and drink: eating at the
  restaurants and drinking through the trip is what pushes it toward $1,000.

## What you bring

- **A kill bag.** The pangas carry coolers, but they "only fit so many fish and
  ice." A kill bag is what lets you keep a full day's catch properly.
- **Ice, and more than you think** — fish "burn up in the fish holds" otherwise.
  The source's own lesson learned across trips was to bring more ice, more
  coolers, more kill bags each time.
- All tackle, bought before crossing. Renting gear locally needs arranging in
  advance.
- Breakfast, snacks, water, electrolytes, beer.

## Two programs, not one

The trip has a **shallow** program and a **deep** program, and they use
different gear and different water. Plan which you want.

### Shallow — cabrilla and yellowtail on structure

Cast-and-wind at rock and boiler structure, the same read as the
[cabrilla](../species/cabrilla.md) router. *"We're going to start doing the
exact same thing we did yesterday — casting and winding until we get bit"*
(Nate Winnicki, BD Outdoors, `6P34jI9q1tE`).

- **Expect to get rocked.** Big yellowtail bury you in the structure — "big
  yellow just rocked your ass." This is the same reason the Sea-of-Cortez
  shallow program runs 80 lb (see [Sea of Cortez](sea-of-cortez.md)).
- **Retrieve speed selects for size.** One angler tried slowing down, then
  went the other way: *"faster, so the slow — the little guys can't catch
  them"* (`K22a8Ui8tWg`). Burning it culls the small fish.
- **Read the lee of a point.** "It swells a lot less on the back side of this
  point" — on a windy day the back of a point is the fishable water
  (`K22a8Ui8tWg`).
- A **large twitch bait** was noted to cast roughly **twice as far** as the
  smaller size, which matters when you need to reach structure from a drifting
  panga (`6P34jI9q1tE`).

### Deep — grouper and pargo on the dropper loop

A completely separate program, and one the KB had no BOLA record of:

> **600–700 feet of water, single high dropper loop, live mackerel, "slow and
> steady"** — targeting big grouper and pargo (`XRIjUgpuyZ8`, Bola Sport
> Fishing).

See [dropper loop](../techniques/dropper-loop.md) for the rig and
[sliding sinker](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md) for the heavy-leader grouper
variant. **Colt Sniper 150 g** and yo-yo iron were the jig side of the same
day.

### Yellowtail on bird schools

The other way yellowtail come, and it is a timing game:

> *"Chasing bird school after bird school… there is good yellowtail bird
> schools all around us right now — **very, very short windows**. They come up
> for maybe a couple seconds and then dive out. So waiting your shot, **proper
> placement with your jig is very key**."* (`ojE5H6AdGGo`)

Four or five fish in the box by 07:55 working that pattern; fish to ~25 lb,
with 13-pounders released. General method in
[bird reading](../conditions/bird-reading.md) and
[search & glassing](../planning/search-and-glassing.md).

**Making bait is part of the morning** on most programs — one crew's routine
was to meet the captain at the ramp, go over the plan, and start by making
bait (`wX7jUycIJ-U`). See [making bait](../bait/making-bait.md).

## Species

Primary targets are **[yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md)** and
**[cabrilla](../species/cabrilla.md)** (leopard grouper). Also taken:
grouper and pargo on the deep program, other bass, and **white seabass turns
up randomly**. Late season adds dorado, marlin, and sailfish.

**The Puro Fierro tournament** is fished there in May — format is **one fish
per team per day per species, and only two species count: yellowtail and
cabrilla** (`T3cEQUqiH8E`, `6P34jI9q1tE`).

**Roosterfish** are present but are a matter of luck — the source had not
landed one there, describing them as migratory, condition- and bait-driven,
and more reliable further south in the Sea of Cortez. See
[Pacific crevalle jack](../species/pacific-crevalle-jack.md) for the related
inshore jack, and [Loreto](loreto.md) for the southern Cortez fishery where
roosterfish are the actual target.
