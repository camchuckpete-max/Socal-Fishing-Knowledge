# Bahía de los Ángeles (BOLA / Bay of LA)

**The drive-in panga zone of the northern Sea of Cortez, and the one ground in
[Cortez North](cortez-north.md) the corpus documents in depth.** You stay on a
hotel strip on the bay, the operator collects you at your room before dawn, and
you fish an open boat over island rock and high spots. It is called "legendary
yellowtail grounds" (`vqsD0qpwcJA`), and [yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md)
and [cabrilla](../species/cabrilla.md) are the two targets — but they are not in
the same water, so you pick one for the day rather than expecting to cover both
(`odiIWmut6gM`, `P36VGPPf120`). Under that shallow program sits a separate
600–800 ft bottom fishery for snowy grouper and pargo out of the same boat
(`XRIjUgpuyZ8`, `8c07sN9wxGM`).

Season shape for this zone is the region's season table — nearly all of it was
sourced here — in [Cortez North](cortez-north.md). Paperwork for every Mexican
trip is a rung further up, in [Mexican waters](mexican-waters.md). Species
decision logic stays in the routers; execution stays in the technique notes.

**Not to be confused with San Felipe.** The mothership fishery out of San Felipe
is a different operation two driving legs — about 4 hours — back up the coast
(`P36VGPPf120`), and titles and narration conflate the two — see [Sea of Cortez](sea-of-cortez.md) and the
naming caution on [Cortez North](cortez-north.md).

## Getting there

**The drive is the trip, and the region page owns it** — route, legs, fuel,
money and comms are on [Cortez North](cortez-north.md). What is specific to
arriving here:

- **The last stretch carries the potholes.** From Gonzaga Bay it is two left
  turns, straight, a left, then another 20–30 minutes, one more left and a
  straight shot in. About a mile of that final road had been patched, and the
  rest was reported fine as of 2026-08 (`P36VGPPf120`).
- **Coverage is gone before you get here.** There is no cell service through
  the desert, at Gonzaga or at the bay; the hotel Wi-Fi is enough to text and
  check in, not to stream (`P36VGPPf120`).
- **Door to door runs longer than the leg times suggest.** One crew left at
  06:30 and was at the hotel at 17:31, an 11-hour travel day with a lunch stop
  in San Felipe (`T3cEQUqiH8E`).
- **Everything you fish with comes across the border with you.** *"There's no
  tackle shop to really speak of… everything is really limited, so you pack up
  extra. Not the trip you want to pack light for"* (`odiIWmut6gM`).
- **You do not use the ramp.** The panga is driven to your hotel room, loaded
  there, and trailered off the beach at the end of the day (`odiIWmut6gM`,
  `P36VGPPf120`). The ramp is a 5–7 minute walk from the Costa del Sol strip and
  the mercado a 2-minute walk, but the boat coming to you is what the operators
  actually do (`P36VGPPf120`).
- **Shore fishing is walkable from the strip** — one crew crossed the street for
  an evening session on the day they arrived (`T3cEQUqiH8E`).

**Regulatory — Mexico. Jurisdiction: CONAPESCA for fishing, INM for entry.
Checked as of 2026-08. Verify current requirements before you fish
([Mexican waters](mexican-waters.md)).**

## Lodging, operators and cost

**Costa del Sol** is the hotel the trip-planning source uses, chosen for access
rather than luxury: rooms run about **$90–120/night** (two-bed ≈ $90, four-bed
≈ $120; quoted in pesos on that trip as 1,800 for two rooms and 2,000 for the
triple), there is AC, ceiling fans, Wi-Fi, soap and shampoo, an on-site
restaurant and bar, and they will cook your catch (`P36VGPPf120`). A deposit is
taken at check-in, and the source's practice is to settle rooms and pangas on
arrival rather than on the last day (`P36VGPPf120`). **Hotel Villa Vita** is a
second hotel on the same strip used by another crew (`odiIWmut6gM`), and several
more run along it with the same basic setup (`P36VGPPf120`).

**Campo Archelón** is a different kind of base — an eco-camp on the beach run by
**Antonio Resendes** with his mother and sister, offering palapas at **$24** and
cabins at **$120–160** with AC, Wi-Fi and kitchens, plus a café-restaurant open
07:00–20:00 and closed Wednesdays. The family arrived in 1979 doing sea-turtle
conservation work and now run ecotourism on the reasoning that conservation
needs local economic opportunity behind it (`8c07sN9wxGM`).

**Panga operators named, all spoken of as good:** Fish Arms, FG's, Rick's, Hoy,
Dagots, Eigor and Ivan — and **they talk to each other** about where fish are
showing on the day (`P36VGPPf120`). Also documented working out of the bay:
**Bola Sport Fishing** (Joe, running a new panga, the *Guardian*, with current
electronics and a new Suzuki outboard, `XRIjUgpuyZ8`) and captains named
**Joel**, **Nego** and **Yoshi** (`wX7jUycIJ-U`, `ojE5H6AdGGo`, `8c07sN9wxGM`).
The Fish Arms captain who appears on two separate trips gives his name as
"Hamed" and his social handle as Capt. Morales (`odiIWmut6gM`, `6P34jI9q1tE`) —
ASR-uncertain spelling, so treat the handle as unverified.

**Book with someone you have a relationship with, and carry a fallback.** One
crew's pre-booked operator simply never showed for a 14:00–15:00 meet; the day
was saved because a captain they knew from an earlier trip, Jorge, came off the
water, dropped his own clients and had them fishing by 17:45 (`odiIWmut6gM`).

**Cost, for a 4-day trip** — 4 days of travel, 3 nights, **2 fishing days**,
shared across a group: **$800–1,000 per person all in**, roughly **$250/day**,
including licences, fuel, tips and spending money. The **panga is $550/day**
plus about a **$100** tip, and Mexican vehicle insurance ran **$90** for 4 days
on a 2021 car and **$130** on a new truck. Food and drink are the variable that
pushes the number toward the top (`P36VGPPf120`).

**What you bring beyond tackle:** a kill bag, because the pangas' coolers "only
fit so many fish and ice"; and more ice than you think, because fish otherwise
burn up in the holds — the source's own lesson across trips was more ice, more
coolers, more kill bags each time (`P36VGPPf120`). A filleting, vacuum-sealing
and freezing service in town will prepare the catch for the drive home
(`8c07sN9wxGM`).

## Structure & bathymetry

**The zone is a bay with islands off it, and the grounds are a boat ride from
the hotel.** One trip's plan was to run about **20 miles north** of the bay for
cabrilla and yellowtail (`P36VGPPf120`); another ran about **20 miles** the
other way to an island high spot (`wX7jUycIJ-U`). The bay itself is the shelter,
not the fishery — every documented fishing day starts by leaving it.

**Four depth programs stack in the same zone:**

- **Shoreline rock.** Cast to the rocks and the shoreline for cabrilla
  (`T3cEQUqiH8E`); protected coves and the lee of every island hold cabrilla and
  are what a crew works on the way back into town (`6P34jI9q1tE`).
- **Island shelves.** At **Cardonosa**, an island named on camera, one crew
  fished a rising shelf that comes up to about **60 ft**, on what they took to
  be its south side (`odiIWmut6gM`). ⚠ Fact-check (unverifiable): the same
  sentence renders the island as "Isla Cartito" — one of the two names is ASR
  garble, and the KB carries no charted position for either.
- **High spots under 100 ft.** These are where the yellowtail program lives.
  *"If we're in less than 100 ft of water, these fish will respond to a surface
  bait immediately even if they're deep"* (`T3cEQUqiH8E`). One little high spot
  held yellowtail running **10–15 lb** (`wX7jUycIJ-U`); another day's first stop
  was "a shallow reef kind of in the middle of nowhere" that produced snapper
  and yellowtail (`6P34jI9q1tE`). A crew that has fished it repeatedly calls one
  island "the big island" and runs there for bigger fish when the weather allows
  (`wX7jUycIJ-U`).
- **The deep bottom.** Grouper are worked at **600–700 ft**, with 700 and 800 ft
  called on camera on the same day (`XRIjUgpuyZ8`); another crew put snowy
  grouper at **over 600 ft** (`8c07sN9wxGM`).

A **150–300 ft jigging band** on the high spots sits between the last two, bit
best at **150–180 ft** on the one trip that recorded it (`vqsD0qpwcJA`). That is
a single-trip data point, not established zone doctrine — see the
[yellowtail evidence file](../species/evidence/yellowtail.md).

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: charted positions for anything in this zone.
[The spot library](../sources/spot-lists.md) stops at San Quintín on the Pacific
side and carries nothing on this coast, so a day plan cannot pull per-spot
BightSST conditions here the way it can in the [Bight](socal-bight.md).

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: bathymetry proper — what the bottom does
between the shoreline rock and the 600 ft ground, where the high spots sit
relative to the islands, and whether the deep ground is a drop-off, a canyon
edge or a flat.

Also recorded: other bass (`P36VGPPf120`), a small Mexican hogfish seen on the
rock (`ojE5H6AdGGo`), and squid coming up on the deep drops (`8c07sN9wxGM`).
Yellowtail here run from the 10–15 lb high-spot grade (`wX7jUycIJ-U`) up through
an 18-lb tournament weigh-in (`6P34jI9q1tE`) to fish of **24–25 lb** and a 28–29
lb fish taken on a 200 g jig, with 13-pounders released on a good morning
(`ojE5H6AdGGo`, `odiIWmut6gM`).

**The Puro Fierro tournament** is fished here in May. The format is a **single
fish per team per day per species**, and fish are weighed **gilled and gutted**
so nobody can weight them; yellowtail and cabrilla are what gets weighed
(`T3cEQUqiH8E`, `6P34jI9q1tE`).

## How it fishes

**The day starts before light and the boat comes to you.** Wake around 04:30,
gear staged on the sidewalk, depart 05:30 (`P36VGPPf120`) — *"there's no meeting
the captain at the ramp. They pull the boat right up to your hotel room, load
you up, and you are on your way"* (`odiIWmut6gM`). Crews that do meet at the
ramp use the time to go over the plan before making bait (`wX7jUycIJ-U`).

**Then you choose.** Yellowtail and cabrilla hold in different areas, so the
captain picks a target: *"you kind of got to pick and choose what you want to
catch and they'll go for it"* (`P36VGPPf120`). One captain's stated day-order is
cabrilla first thing, then a move after roughly two hours to try for yellowtail
and snapper (`odiIWmut6gM`).

**The fleet makes bait first.** Asked why every boat was stopped in the same
place, the answer was that they were all catching bait for the dropper-loop
program; a crew fishing lures skips it and runs straight to the rock
(`odiIWmut6gM`). Where bait is made, everyone aboard helps, which one visitor
noted as unlike a SoCal boat (`wX7jUycIJ-U`). See
[making bait](../bait/making-bait.md).

**Wind decides which zone you fish, and the captain works the lee.** When it
looked different one morning the plan changed to running south to new water
(`6P34jI9q1tE`); on a day blowing **25 kt with gusts to 40** the guide used the
islands and coves to keep the ride tolerable and the crew fished every protected
cove and island lee on the way home (`6P34jI9q1tE`). The zone-wide caution is on
[Cortez North](cortez-north.md): a blown-out morning at the bay is not a
blown-out day, because 5 miles up the beach can be calm (`T3cEQUqiH8E`).

**Tide is read on the day.** One crew tied the trip's slow periods to a
particular tide and asked the captain to go looking in deeper water for yellows,
which is where they found them (`odiIWmut6gM`); another treated a day of unusual
tides as a study day for the days after (`P36VGPPf120`). See
[tide & slack](../conditions/tide-and-slack.md).

### Shallow — cast and wind, fast

The shallow program is [jerkbaits](../lures/jerkbaits.md) and twitch baits cast
at rock and burned back, on both cabrilla and yellowtail
([surface iron casting](../techniques/surface-iron-casting.md) covers the
casting mechanics).

- **Speed is the doctrine.** *"Speed is the name of the game here… you got to
  move these baits as quickly as possible"*, and on a high spot in under 100 ft
  the fish come up for a surface bait even when they are deep (`T3cEQUqiH8E`).
  The same read holds on the jig: the fish "wanted it really fast", and worked
  slowly the jigs drew little (`wX7jUycIJ-U`).
- **Expect to get rocked, and bring line for it.** *"Big yellow just rocked your
  ass"* (`6P34jI9q1tE`). A visiting angler fishing **40–50 lb** broke off
  cabrilla repeatedly and concluded he needed heavier line (`wX7jUycIJ-U`); the
  tournament crew fished **80 lb** on a stiff rod and a Fathom 400
  (`6P34jI9q1tE`). The region's shallow-rock line doctrine is on
  [Cortez North](cortez-north.md) and
  [line & leader](../tackle/line-and-leader.md).
- **A large twitch bait casts roughly twice as far as the smaller size**, which
  is what lets you reach structure from a drifting panga (`6P34jI9q1tE`).

### Yellowtail on bird schools

The other way the yellowtail come, and it is a timing game rather than a search:

> *"Chasing bird school after bird school… there is good yellowtail bird schools
> all around us right now — **very, very short windows**. They come up for maybe
> a couple seconds and then dive out. So waiting your shot, **proper placement
> with your jig is very key**."* (`ojE5H6AdGGo`)

Four or five fish in the box by 07:55 working that pattern. General method in
[bird reading](../conditions/bird-reading.md) and
[search & glassing](../planning/search-and-glassing.md).

### The 150–300 ft jigging band

[Slow-pitch](../techniques/slow-pitch-jigging.md) and
[knife jigs](../lures/knife-jigs.md) on the high spots: **130–250 g** matched to
the rod's jig rating, vertical/knife shapes to punch down through current and
wing-fall shapes to flutter, with the bump felt on the fall and the answer being
to wind tight (`vqsD0qpwcJA`). A second crew ran **170–220 g** Shimano Shimmer
Falls and found the long, skinny jigs outproduced the flatter falling shapes
(`wX7jUycIJ-U`). Working a vertical-jig cadence with an **8 ft** rod was hard
enough that the angler gave up and burned the jig like a
[yo-yo](../techniques/yo-yo-iron.md) instead — the region's short-rod argument,
in [rod & reel selection](../tackle/rod-and-reel-selection.md), is about exactly
this.

### Deep — grouper and pargo at 600–800 ft

A separate program out of the same boat: a **single high
[dropper loop](../techniques/dropper-loop.md) with live mackerel, worked "slow
and steady"** in 600–700 ft for big grouper and pargo, with cut bait on single
and double dropper loops picking away alongside it (`XRIjUgpuyZ8`). The heavy-
leader variant is [sliding sinker](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md).

**Jig weight for the deep program is unresolved in the corpus.** Section order
below is not a ranking:

- **150–200 g.** A **Colt Sniper 150 g** and a **170 g** knife jig fished the
  600–700 ft ground beside the dropper loops (`XRIjUgpuyZ8`); a tournament crew
  moving to deeper water for baqueta and snowy grouper reached for **180–200 g**
  knife jigs to slow-pitch (`T3cEQUqiH8E`).
- **400–500 g.** A crew fishing over 600 ft recommends knife jigs of **400–500
  g** for snowy grouper here, with a 500 g red-and-white-polka-dot jig the
  producer on their day (`8c07sN9wxGM`). ⚠ Fact-check (single-source): a
  recommendation from one trip on a channel monetising gear links; the twofold
  gap against the other reports is unexplained.

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: what selects jig weight in the deep program.
No source states the current, drift speed or jig style behind either bracket, so
the corpus cannot say whether these are two conditions or two opinions.

## Spots

**No spot in this zone has a charted position in the KB, so no spot pages hang
below it.** The named grounds the corpus does record are:

- **Cardonosa** — an island with a rising shelf up to about 60 ft, fished on
  what the crew took to be the south side (`odiIWmut6gM`).
- **"The big island"** — the crew's own name for the run they make for bigger
  fish on a good-weather day (`wX7jUycIJ-U`).
- **The high spot ~20 miles out** — an island high spot holding 10–15 lb
  yellowtail, run to on day one of a trip; the same trip's crew also fished
  "inside" to an island for cabrilla when it got rough (`wX7jUycIJ-U`).
- **The grounds ~20 miles north of the bay** — where one trip planned its
  cabrilla and yellowtail day (`P36VGPPf120`).
- **Playa La Gringa** — a beach in the bay, named as a swimming stop rather than
  a fishing spot (`8c07sN9wxGM`).

None of these carries a public name plus a charted position, which is what a
spot page needs, so they are logged in
[the spot harvest](../sources/spot-harvest.md) for the gazetteer rather than
written up here.
