# 279 / 267 / 14 Mile Bank

**A zone is a run grouping.** This is two charted marks in the open water between
the south Orange County beach and Catalina's east end — the **14 Mile Bank** at
**33°23.833'N 118°00.000'W** and the **279 / 267** at **33°17.800'N
117°49.150'W** (cameron). They lie **10.9 nm** apart, the 279 / 267 bearing
**124°** from the 14 Mile Bank and the 14 Mile Bank **304°** back, which puts the
whole zone inside the census's **12 nm** diameter cap on a single southeast-
northwest line.

**The zone's name is its distance, and the coordinates prove it out.** A bank is
normally named for the fathom depth of its shallowest point, but two SoCal banks
are named for distance instead — Nine Mile Bank sits **9 statute miles** off the
coast, and "the 14" is **14 miles off the beach** (`Rf1HKJG-SDg`). The nearest
charted mainland-side position in the spot library, the **Hospital 100-fathom
curve** off [Dana Point](dana-point.md), lies **12.2 nm** from the 14 Mile Bank
(cameron) — **14.0 statute miles**, the name read back off the chart.
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): `Rf1HKJG-SDg` never says **statute**. It
says "the nine mile bank that's not nine miles deep that's nine miles off the
coast" and "it's nine miles off the beach" — no unit qualifier anywhere in the
transcript. The statute reading is this page's inference, and the whole
"coordinates prove it out" derivation (12.2 nm = 14.0 statute miles) rests on it;
read as nautical miles the name would not check out.
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the source never counts **two**
distance-named banks. It hedges the convention — "there's banks like that up and
down the coast but most of them are the banks are called after the highest part
of the bank" — and names the nine mile and the 14 as examples, not as an
exhaustive pair. The cameron geometry itself is clean: 12.17 nm / 062° to the
Hospital curve, 14.01 statute miles.

**This is one of the few offshore-bank zones the corpus actually fishes on
camera.** A summer trip worked the warm pocket over these marks and the 209 next
door, found boiling bluefin that would not bite, and salvaged the day on a kelp
paddy with yellowtail and bonito (`-bw1KDfDjv4`); a separate report walks through
exactly how to approach this bank and glass it (`DnSqw4r7A1s`); a seminar puts a
depth on the 267 (`m-M0iwX8DjA`). What the corpus does **not** carry is a run, a
port, a season or a bottom description, so the gaps below are about access and
pattern rather than about whether anyone fishes here. They aggregate in
[the gap report](../sources/gap-report.md).

## Structure & bathymetry

**The two positions, and the line they sit on.** The 14 Mile Bank is at
**33°23.833'N 118°00.000'W** and the 279 / 267 at **33°17.800'N 117°49.150'W**
(cameron) — **10.9 nm** apart on a **124°/304°** axis that runs from Catalina's
east-end water down toward the Oceanside side of the coast. The line does not
stop at the zone edge: the **209** continues it another **12.4 nm** at **193°**
from the 279 / 267, and the **312** another **16.4 nm** (cameron).

**Bank names are depths, except when they are distances — and this zone has one
of each.** A bank is an isolated high spot rising out of otherwise deep water,
named for the **fathom depth of its shallowest point**, at six feet to a fathom
(`Rf1HKJG-SDg`, [zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md)). Read that way:

| spot | name reads as | in feet | source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 14 Mile Bank | a distance, not a depth — 14 miles off the beach | no depth claim | `Rf1HKJG-SDg` |
| 267 | 267 fathoms | about 1,602 ft | stated on camera, `m-M0iwX8DjA` |
| 279 | 279 fathoms | about 1,674 ft | the naming convention only — unconfirmed |

middle of the ocean" lying at **209, 181 and 267 fathoms** (`m-M0iwX8DjA`), in a
passage about the sounder being a fathometer. That grouping is the San Diego-side
local banks, which is what attaches the depth to **this** 267 rather than to the
southern one ([81 / 381](81-381.md)).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): `m-M0iwX8DjA` attaches no region to the

the middle of the **150–350 fathom** contour the SoCal daytime swordfish fishery
works ([swordfish](../species/swordfish.md),
[deep-drop swordfishing](../techniques/deep-drop-swordfishing.md)) — deep water
by the standards of this zone's inshore neighbours. ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus
source gives relief, bottom composition, surrounding depth or high-spot shape at
either mark, and nothing states a depth for the 14 Mile Bank at all.

**One charted position, two bank numbers.** The spot library carries "279 / 267"
as a single line at a single coordinate (cameron), unlike the zone's other
paired names, which get a position each. ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source
separates the 279 from the 267 on the water, gives a distance between them, or
says whether the pair is two high spots on one structure or two names for the
same one.

**The 267 collides across the library, and the collision rules do not cover
it.** A different **267 (The Condom)** is charted at **32°30.000'N
118°41.000'W** in the [81 / 381](81-381.md) zone, **64.7 nm** at **223°** from
this one (cameron). Cameron's landmass-prefix collision rule and his list of
look-alike-but-distinct names do not name this pair
([zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md)), so nothing but context separates them —
confirm which 267 a report means before you plot it.

**Standalone structure fishes any direction, but each bank has its own.** A bank
rising off the plain is not direction-locked the way a land-attached
perpendicular ridge is, yet each individual bank still has **preferred
directions** learned over repeat trips ([island structure](island-structure.md)),
and the working bias on any bank is the **up-current face**, where nutrients are
being forced up ([current & structure](../conditions/current-structure.md)).
⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source names the preferred directions, drift or current
behaviour at either mark.

## How it fishes

**The approach to this bank is one of the few spot-specific search procedures in
the corpus, and it is named on this bank.** Running out to the 14 Mile Bank, stop
somewhere within about **two miles** of the **highest part** of the bank and stop
the boat entirely if it is calm; if it is not calm, angle the boat for the
easiest ride and barely leave it in gear at **1–1.5 mph**. Then glass for an
extended period — small increments all the way around, **three or four minutes**
for a full circuit — looking not only for foamers (`DnSqw4r7A1s`). Once you are
on the **inside edge** of the bank, the majority of the looking goes at the
**bank itself** rather than at open water, because the chance of finding fish off
the structure is slimmer than right on it (`DnSqw4r7A1s`). The general method,
including the **~30° off each bow** lookout stations and the **16–18 mph** contour
run, is [search and glassing](../planning/search-and-glassing.md).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): `DnSqw4r7A1s` frames this as a **generic**
bank approach illustrated by the 14 Mile Bank, not as spot-specific doctrine —
"let's say i'm going to approach a bank to go look for something or i have a plan
like i was talking last week about running out the 14 mile bank". This page's own
evidence file records it correctly as "the worked example for approaching a
bank"; the section lead hardens that into "one of the few spot-specific search
procedures in the corpus". Every parameter itself verifies verbatim (two miles
off the highest part, stop if calm / barely in gear at one to one and a half
miles an hour if not, three or four minutes to glass around the entire boat,
inside edge), except that the source hedges the open-water comparison as "a
little bit slimmer" and adds that he still looks at open water.

**On the way out, anything that breaks the pattern stops the boat.** Birds, bait,
a scum line off a current break, a couple of paddies in a row, a temperature or
colour break — any deviation from what you have been seeing is worth stopping and
looking around the immediate area (`DnSqw4r7A1s`). Reading those signs is
[bird reading](../conditions/bird-reading.md),
[water colour](../conditions/water-color.md) and
[current & structure](../conditions/current-structure.md).

**Read the bank against the water just off it, not against an absolute number.**
The on-camera day worked exactly that way — the SST readout in text mode over the
chart, **66–67 °F** on the bank against **68 °F** off it, and the differential is
what identified the pocket (`-bw1KDfDjv4`,
[electronics and sounder](../planning/electronics-and-sounder.md),
[water temperature](../conditions/water-temperature.md)).

**Radar is a fleet tool out here.** Running the radar while searching this
complex doubles as a way to see where the rest of the fleet is sitting — the
stated payoff was spotting a boat parked on a kelp paddy on the radar screen
before that boat noticed the approach (`-bw1KDfDjv4`,
[fleet intelligence](../planning/fleet-intelligence.md)).

**Not every school you find here will bite, and the answer is to keep moving.**
The trip found bluefin boiling and jumping and could not get them to eat, and the
stated read was that not every school of fish you find is going to bite — stay
positive, move on, and look for new fish or paddies (`-bw1KDfDjv4`). Casting
mechanics for the schools that do eat are
[foamer casting](../techniques/foamer-casting.md).

**A paddy that already produced gets a second pass.** If you catch a yellowtail
off a paddy and then drift off it, circle back and give the same piece another
try rather than treating the one fish as the end of the stop — the stated payoff
was a few more (`-bw1KDfDjv4`, [kelp paddies](../conditions/kelp-paddies.md)).
The paddy build that worked here was light: **25 lb** fluorocarbon to a **No. 2
or No. 3** J hook, sardines **nose-hooked** (`-bw1KDfDjv4`,
[flyline](../techniques/flyline.md)).

looking across — and the compact spot you idle onto, grade on the meter, and
either commit to or leave in **20–30 minutes** (`Blh2BA-7Ono`,
[zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md)). Two marks **10.9 nm** apart, with the two-mile
stand-off and the extended glass the bank's own approach doctrine calls for
(`DnSqw4r7A1s`), reads as neither cleanly.
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): only the complex half is in

## Spots

Two charted spots (cameron), listed northwest to southeast down the zone's axis:

- **14 Mile Bank** — the northwestern mark and the one the corpus names, at
  **33°23.833'N 118°00.000'W**. Its name is a distance rather than a fathom
  reading (`Rf1HKJG-SDg`), and the chart bears that out at **12.2 nm** —
  **14.0 statute miles** — from the Dana Point hundred-fathom curve (cameron).
  It is the zone's Catalina-side end: **11.3 nm** to Avalon Bank, **15.9 nm** to
  Jewfish Point on
  [Catalina Island — Front Side](catalina-island-front-side.md) and **26.1 nm**
  to Farnsworth Bank. It is also a **BightSST eval target**
  ([eval targets](bightsst-eval-targets.md)) and the position the **66–67 °F**
  reading and the **8 lb** bonito jig stop attach to (`-bw1KDfDjv4`).
- **279 / 267** — the southeastern mark, **10.9 nm** at **124°** from the 14 Mile
  Bank, at **33°17.800'N 117°49.150'W**. One charted position carrying **two**
  bank numbers, both of which read as fathoms — and the **267** is the rare case
  where a source states the depth outright, **267 fathoms** (`m-M0iwX8DjA`). It
  is the zone's inshore end, **10.7 nm** off the Dana Point kelp red buoy, and
  its southern hand-off, **12.4 nm** north of the 209 that the on-camera warm
