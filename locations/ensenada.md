# Ensenada & the Tuna Pens

**[Baja only]** — the **drive-in** northern-Baja Pacific day-trip fishery,
roughly **2 hours south of the border**, versus the ~4-hour drive needed to
stage for a [Cedros](cedros-island.md) trip on the same coast. Fished out of
Ensenada sportfishing landings on local pangas and sport boats. The defining
structure is not a reef or an island — it is the **offshore tuna pens**.

Single-source note (Strictly Irons, `Ob45hK4HSWc`; unregistered channel, one
trip) → `medium`, and the specifics below are one day's program, not a season.

## Where & when

- **The tuna pens hold yellowtail.** The pens are the target structure; the
  fish sit on them and meter **straight down at about 120 ft**. The plan on the
  filmed trip was built around getting on them **early in the morning**.
- **Bluefin are on the same pens** — a bluefin was sighted right alongside them
  on the same day. See [bluefin tuna](../species/bluefin-tuna.md).
- Conditions on the filmed trip: very light wind. No seasonal window is
  established by this source.

## Situations → techniques

| Situation (conditions) | Do this (ranked) | Gear class | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Yellowtail metered on the pens, ~120 ft, calm morning | 1) [Yo-yo iron](../techniques/yo-yo-iron.md) 2) [surface iron](../techniques/surface-iron.md) | 40–60 lb class stout rod, high-speed reel; jig-stick class | The planned program on the filmed trip — see the failure mode below before committing to it |
| **Bonito owning the water column** — the iron gets intercepted before it reaches the yellowtail | Switch off metal entirely: **dead sardines** | Bait class | The switch that actually produced. Once the yellowtail went wide open the crew moved to **hand-lining** them |
| Hooked fish worked by a **sea lion** at the boat | Free-spool slack counter-move — see [yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md) | No gear change | A ~50 lb yellowtail was lost to a sea lion on this trip |

## Doctrine & conflicts

- **The useful part of this trip is the abandoned plan.** The crew came to fish
  iron — it is a surface-iron channel — and stated on camera that yo-yo
  *"didn't go well, a lot of bonito."* The bycatch species was intercepting the
  jig before it reached the target, and the fix was to stop fishing metal
  rather than to change metal. That is a worked example of a **bycatch-driven
  presentation switch**, and it belongs beside the general
  [yo-yo iron](../techniques/yo-yo-iron.md) doctrine rather than inside it.
- **This is a bait fishery on the day it was filmed, not an iron fishery.** No
  claim is made here that the pens are generally poor iron water; one trip is
  one trip.
