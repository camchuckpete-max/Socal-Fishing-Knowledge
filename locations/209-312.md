# 209 / 312

**A zone is a run grouping.** This is two charted marks in the open water
south-southwest of Dana Point — the **209** at **33°05.750'N 117°52.350'W** and
the **312** at **33°01.417'N 117°48.500'W** (cameron). They lie **5.4 nm** apart,
the 312 bearing **143°** from the 209 and the 209 **323°** back, so the whole
zone spans less than half the census's **12 nm** diameter cap.

**The 209 is the corpus's default example of an offshore bank.** It is the mark
the naming-convention source reaches for after defining the term — "another
mountain in the middle of what you would call a desert" (`Rf1HKJG-SDg`). It is
the mark used to explain what a fathom is (`9hEa3sGTh40`), it heads the "have you
ever heard of the 181? The 209? The 267?" roll-call of what a spot even is
(`BskCsKaTiZw`), and it is the destination in the corpus's worked example of
being lied to on the VHF (`9hEa3sGTh40`, `ztpj1Xll0-U`). Three separate videos
state its depth outright, which almost no other bank in the library gets.

**The 312 is the silent half.** No corpus source names it, in any context. Its
name, its position and its relationship to the 209 are all this page can say
about it, and the gaps below say so line by line. They aggregate in
[the gap report](../sources/gap-report.md).

## Getting there

**This zone carries the only offshore-bank run in the corpus given as a compass
course, and the chart backs it.** The stated picture is leaving Dana Point
Harbor at **five o'clock in the morning** with a couple of scoops from the bait
barge and heading out to the 209 at **200 degrees** (`9hEa3sGTh40`). Plotted
from the **Dana Point kelp red buoy**, the 209 bears **199°** at **22.8 nm**
(cameron) — the stated course read back off the chart to within a degree. The
**312** lies **26.3 nm** at **189°** from the same buoy, and no source runs to it
([Dana Point](dana-point.md)).

**The five-a.m. departure is doctrine, not detail.** Pull up to the Dana Point
bait barge at **five**, not six, "because that's when everybody goes"
(`ztpj1Xll0-U`); the sportboat fleet leaves at **6:00 a.m.** and the private-boat
crowd converges on the barge at the same hour
([Dana Point](dana-point.md)). Bait handling and tank set-up are
[bait tanks](../bait/bait-tanks.md).

**The North County beach is closer than the port the corpus runs from.** From
the 209 the nearest charted coastal positions are the **Box Canyon rockfish
area** at **19.1 nm / 053°** and **San Onofre Kelp** at **21.5 nm / 045°**; from
the 312 they are **19.9 nm / 037°** and **22.9 nm / 032°**
([Oceanside / North County](oceanside-north-county.md), cameron). The Dana Point
run is longer than either, and it is the one the corpus describes — ⚠ Flagged
gap — no corpus source names Oceanside, Newport or any San Diego port as a
departure for these marks.

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: run time, fuel figure or sea-state envelope for
the Dana Point course, and nothing states whether this zone is a full-day or a
half-day proposition.

**Regulatory — United States. Jurisdiction: California / CDFW. Checked as of
2026-08. Verify current rules before you fish** ([US waters](us-waters.md)).
⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: closures, MPA boundaries or restricted areas
stated for either position. The MPA lines the library carries on this transit are
**coastal** — stay south of **33 30.050** at the Hospital 100-fathom curve
(cameron, [Dana Point](dana-point.md)) — rather than over the banks. The corpus
also names the **Cow Cod Conservation Area** as an offshore closure with its own
depth limit and as-of date ([rockfish & lingcod](../species/rockfish-lingcod.md))
without giving a boundary, so whether either mark falls inside it is unverified;
**check the CCCA boundary against your position with CDFW/NOAA before dropping.**
Boundary compliance is positional, not depth-based ([US waters](us-waters.md)).

## Structure & bathymetry

**The two positions, and the line they sit on.** The 209 is at **33°05.750'N
117°52.350'W** and the 312 at **33°01.417'N 117°48.500'W** (cameron) — **5.4 nm**
apart on a **143°/323°** axis. That line is a continuation of the one running
down from the marks above it: the **279 / 267** is **12.3 nm** north of the 209 at
**013°**, and the **14 Mile Bank** **19.2 nm** at **341°**
([279 / 267 / 14 Mile Bank](279-267-14-mile-bank.md), cameron).

**A bank is named for the fathom depth of its shallowest point**, at six feet to a
fathom — an isolated high spot rising out of otherwise deep water, with places all
over the ocean reading the same depth without being banks (`Rf1HKJG-SDg`,
[zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md)). Read that way:

| spot | name reads as | in feet | source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 209 | 209 fathoms | about 1,254 ft | stated outright, `Rf1HKJG-SDg`, `9hEa3sGTh40`, `m-M0iwX8DjA` |
| 312 | 312 fathoms | about 1,872 ft | the naming convention only — unconfirmed |

**The 209's depth is one of the few in the corpus stated rather than inferred, and
it is stated three times by the same voice across four years.** The
naming-convention tutorial gives it as "209 fathoms deep" while defining the
fathom (`Rf1HKJG-SDg`), a separate tutorial repeats "the 209 209 fathoms" as the
worked example of the unit (`9hEa3sGTh40`), and a seminar names the **209, the
181 and the 267** together as high spots in the middle of the ocean lying at
**209, 181 and 267 fathoms** (`m-M0iwX8DjA`).

**The 267 in that trio is the northern one, 12.3 nm from here.** A different
**267 (The Condom)** is charted **54.3 nm** away at **229°** (cameron) in
[81 / 381](81-381.md), and Cameron's landmass-prefix collision rule
does not name the pair ([zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md)) — so context is all that
separates them, and the context here is the San Diego-side local banks.

**The mechanism the 209 is used to teach is the whole reason this zone exists.**
Water 2,000–3,000 ft deep with a mountain in the middle of it congregates bait
around the structure for safety, "and then the chain reaction just occurs, and
then all of a sudden here's the big fish" — current, tide and water temperature
all matter, but you still have to have structure for them to act on
(`Rf1HKJG-SDg`). The same frame is put more plainly at a later seminar: the ocean
is "a giant desert with these little tiny oases in it called spots," and the
spots are high spots where the bait gathers up (`BskCsKaTiZw`). Structure-plus-
current is [current & structure](../conditions/current-structure.md).

**Both names land inside the working sword band.** **209** and **312 fathoms**
both fall inside the **150–350 fathom** contour the SoCal daytime swordfish
fishery works (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`, [swordfish](../species/swordfish.md),
[deep-drop swordfishing](../techniques/deep-drop-swordfishing.md)), the 312 near
its deep end. That is a depth match, not a report.

**Standalone structure fishes any direction, but each bank has its own.** A bank
rising off the plain is not direction-locked the way a land-attached
perpendicular ridge is, yet each individual bank still has **preferred
directions** learned over repeat trips ([island structure](island-structure.md)),
and the working bias on any bank is the **up-current face**, where nutrients are
being forced up ([current & structure](../conditions/current-structure.md)).
⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source gives relief, bottom composition, surrounding
depth, high-spot shape, drift or current behaviour at either mark, and nothing
confirms the 312 as a charted crest reading at all.

**Charted neighbours, nearest first** (cameron): from the **209**, the **181** at
**11.8 nm / 183°** and the **277** at **12.2 nm / 301°**, then the **279 / 267**
at **12.3 nm**, the **289** at **14.8 nm**, the **284** at **15.5 nm**, the **51**
at **16.4 nm**, the **138** at **17.2 nm**, the **152** at **17.9 nm** and the
**14 Mile Bank** at **19.2 nm**. From the **312** the order shifts south: the
**181** at **8.3 nm / 207°**, the **51** at **11.9 nm**, the **138** at
**12.7 nm**, the **289** at **15.4 nm** and the **284** at **15.5 nm**. Of those,
[51 / 181 / 138](51-181-138.md), [The Slide / 152 / 277](slide-152-277.md) and
[279 / 267 / 14 Mile Bank](279-267-14-mile-bank.md) are built as zone pages on
this ladder. ⚠ Flagged stub — no corpus source yet: the **289 / 284** grouping
between this zone and the below-Clemente box is unbuilt.

**Neither mark is a BightSST eval target**, so conditions here are pulled per
charted position rather than read off a scored spot
([eval targets](bightsst-eval-targets.md)) — unlike the 14 Mile Bank one zone
north, which is scored.

**"Inside the 209" is where the dorado sit, and inside means shoreward.** Both
dorado calls put the fish between the bank and the beach rather than on the high
spot — "just inside of the 209 a little bit" in one telling (`ztpj1Xll0-U`) and
"three miles inside the 209" with GPS numbers to the kelp paddy in the other
(`sIoNELGlxmk`). Inside on the Dana Point course is back up the **019°**
return bearing (cameron), which puts that water between the 209 and the North
County beach.

**Warm water over these banks is what the summer trip was reading, and the pocket
does not respect the zone line.** With the plotter's sea-surface-temperature
readout in text mode over the chart, the crew read **66–67 °F** on the 14 Mile
Bank and **68 °F** a little off it, and called the whole area "between the 14 M
Bank, the 209, the 267" one pocket of comparatively warm water worth working
(`-bw1KDfDjv4`). The coordinate census splits that pocket across two zones — the
209 is **12.3 nm** from the 279 / 267 and **19.2 nm** from the 14 Mile Bank
(cameron). Plan the water as the source describes it, one pocket, and treat the
zone boundary as a filing decision rather than a feature of the ocean.

**Two temperature readings, four years and a season apart, are not a band.**
**66–68 °F** on the summer bluefin/bonito day (`-bw1KDfDjv4`) and **72 °F** on
the 2019-10-02 yellowfin day (`SczdZIq3UmE`) are the only numbers the corpus
attaches to this water. ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source gives the temperature
range that gates a bite here. Break-reading is
[water temperature](../conditions/water-temperature.md) and
[water colour](../conditions/water-color.md).

**This zone is in the good half of the paddy split.** The best kelp-paddy water
in the Bight is south of San Clemente Island and Catalina and along the coastal
strip from **Dana Point to Oceanside and southward**; north of Dana Point paddies
thin out and hold fewer fish (`HzE4FHHcvTk`,
[bight geography](bight-geography.md)). Both marks lie south of the Dana Point
kelp buoy's latitude — by **21.6** and **25.9 minutes** (cameron) — and the two
paddy stories the corpus attaches to this water, the summer yellowtail paddy and
the October dorado paddy, are both inside that strip. Paddy mechanics are
[kelp paddies](../conditions/kelp-paddies.md).

**The 209 works as a first stop a fast boat leaves.** A 2016-11-29 trip made the
jump from the 209 out to San Clemente Island, ending up about **six miles** off
the island outside Desperation Point in **1,200–1,300 ft** of water with fish
boiling around the boat (`AIHvJj-paoo`); the charted run is **33.7 nm** at
**233°** to Desperation Reef
([San Clemente Island — Back Side](san-clemente-island-back-side.md), cameron).
The 2022-10-12 report runs the other way — out to the 209 area for dorado, then
back inshore to find yellowfin within ten miles of Newport Harbor
(`XLVUhV8DW64`). Neither day treated this zone as the destination.

**It is not on the fall warm-band arc.** That arc runs backside Santa Cruz /
Rosa → Santa Barbara Island → backside Catalina → the 499 and Clemente, with the
bluefin condensing outward to Tanner and Cortez as the coast cools
(`OYOda6T3f-8`, [bight geography](bight-geography.md)). This zone sits inshore
and east of that path — **44.9 nm** from the 499 and **67.8 nm** from Tanner Bank
(cameron). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source places either mark on or off the arc.

## How it fishes

**The sounder rule for this water is stated on this bank by name.** Out on the
209 it is 209 fathoms deep, and the machine should come **off auto and onto
manual** so you decide what you are looking at rather than letting it hunt the
bottom — a private boat has no bait budget to hold a school and chum it up from
**30 fathoms** the way a sportboat can, so nothing below the top of the water
column is worth reading. Working range on manual is **0–120 ft on older machines,
0–150 ft on newer ones**, regardless of how deep the bottom actually is
(`9hEa3sGTh40`). At that range a school of tuna paints as a large distinct mark;
left on auto over 209 fathoms it shrinks to a speck
([electronics & sounder](../planning/electronics-and-sounder.md)).

**Stop on the marks, not on the plan.** The 2019-10-02 trip was "heading over the
209" and stopped short of it because "the marks were unbelievable," then found
white terns working over **72 °F** water (`SczdZIq3UmE`). The bird call given on
camera on that stop is the general one: look for the **white terns**, not
pelicans and not seagulls ([bird reading](../conditions/bird-reading.md)).
General search method is
[search & glassing](../planning/search-and-glassing.md).

**The VHF on this run is a hazard, and the corpus proves it on this bank.** "Anybody
out there on the 209?" is the standard call on the two channels recreational
boats default to, **72** and **65** (`9hEa3sGTh40`). The worked example runs the
Dana Point course: a boat on the **200°** line with day-old research on where the
fish bit is talked off it by escalating radio traffic — a wide-open bite on the
**181**, running out of bait, the boat sinking under the weight of tuna — and
turns **180 degrees down the coast**, at which point the caller admits the report
was fabricated and that he was himself running to the 209 (`9hEa3sGTh40`). The
same voice describes sitting on the 209 and broadcasting fake numbers for the
Orange County dump, sending five boats off (`ztpj1Xll0-U`). **Doctrine: turn the
VHF off, or at minimum weight your own pre-trip research and your own sign over
an unsolicited hot-bite call from a stranger**
([fleet intelligence](../planning/fleet-intelligence.md)). The chart makes the
scenario legible: the 181 is **11.8 nm** at **183°** from the 209 (cameron), so
the course change is a real and cheap-looking one from a boat already on the
200° line.

**Published numbers cut both ways on this same water.** The GPS numbers for the
dorado paddy inside the 209 were given out deliberately, on a subscription site,
by the same voice that says to turn the radio off (`sIoNELGlxmk`) — a paid,
attributable source rather than an anonymous broadcast. And the research the
fabricated call was designed to override was itself a **day-old** report of where
fish bit yesterday (`9hEa3sGTh40`), which is a report-aging problem in its own
right ([report reading & forecasting](../planning/report-reading-and-forecasting.md)).

**A paddy that already produced gets a second pass.** If you catch a yellowtail
off a paddy in this water and then drift off it, circle back and give the same
piece another try rather than treating the one fish as the end of the stop — the
stated payoff was a few more (`-bw1KDfDjv4`,
[kelp paddies](../conditions/kelp-paddies.md)). The paddy build that worked here
was light: **25 lb** fluorocarbon to a **No. 2 or No. 3** J hook, sardines
**nose-hooked** (`-bw1KDfDjv4`, [flyline](../techniques/flyline.md)). Casting
mechanics for schools that do eat are
[foamer casting](../techniques/foamer-casting.md).

**Radar is a fleet tool out here.** Running the radar while searching this water
doubles as a way to see where the rest of the fleet is sitting — the stated payoff
was spotting a boat parked on a kelp paddy on the radar screen before that boat
noticed the approach (`-bw1KDfDjv4`,
[fleet intelligence](../planning/fleet-intelligence.md)).

**Not every school you find here will bite.** The summer trip spotted bluefin
repeatedly and could not get them to eat, and the day was salvaged on a paddy and
a jig stop instead (`-bw1KDfDjv4`).

## Spots

Two charted spots (cameron), listed northwest to southeast down the zone's axis.
The corpus is entirely one-sided: every cite on this page attaches to the 209.

- **The 209** — the northwestern mark, at **33°05.750'N 117°52.350'W**, and the
  bank the corpus uses to explain what a bank is (`Rf1HKJG-SDg`). Its crest is
  stated at **209 fathoms — about 1,254 ft** in three separate sources
  (`Rf1HKJG-SDg`, `9hEa3sGTh40`, `m-M0iwX8DjA`). It is **22.8 nm** at **199°**
  from the Dana Point kelp red buoy, matching the **200°** course given on camera
  (`9hEa3sGTh40`, cameron), and it is the position the **72 °F** October
  yellowfin stop, the summer warm pocket and both "inside the 209" dorado calls
  attach to.
- **The 312** — the southeastern mark, **5.4 nm** at **143°** from the 209, at
  **33°01.417'N 117°48.500'W**. Its name reads as **312 fathoms — about
  1,872 ft** by the convention alone. It is the zone's southern hand-off, **8.3 nm**
  from the 181 in [51 / 181 / 138](51-181-138.md) — closer to that zone's
  northern mark than to anything else charted. ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source
  names the 312 in any context.

Charted positions for both are in
[the spot library](../sources/spot-lists.md). Neither carries a bottom
description, so relief and composition are gaps on both spot pages until sources
arrive.
