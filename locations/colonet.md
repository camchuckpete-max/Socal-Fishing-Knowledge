# Colonet

**A zone is a run grouping.** Colonet is the deep-water coastal grouping off
Punta Colonet: two charted high spots named for their fathom depth, **28** and
**41 fathoms** (cameron), plus the numbered banks **52** and **330** that the
census attaches here from the spot library's offshore-banks section. It sits
between [Punta Banda / Santo Tomás](punta-banda-santo-tomas.md) and
[San Quintín](san-quintin.md) on the mainland-coast ladder in
[Baja Pacific — North](baja-pacific-north.md).

Three fisheries are documented here. Colonet is a **winter and early-spring
yellowtail yo-yo destination** for San Diego long-range boats (`YAKOv9bXKO0`),
it is named among the **most productive rockfish grounds** a slow-pitch angler
will see (`PRNMGpLj7Pw`), and the water outside it is half of the
historically productive **bluefin** structure that went unfished in 2021
because closer grounds were producing (`fgTmUq78ofQ`).

## Getting there

**Colonet is reached as a trip, not as a run.** It is grouped with
long-range travel rather than with overnight fishing: a slow-pitch rig is
described as the one to carry "on an overnight boat out of the Channel Islands
or a Colonet trip" (`PRNMGpLj7Pw`), and the yo-yo tackle called for here is the
heavier long-range rig rather than the lighter one used at home
(`YAKOv9bXKO0`). Distance is what kept boats away in 2021 — with bluefin
showing west of San Diego and west of Ensenada, no boat had run down outside
Colonet or San Quintín "in a while… because they haven't had to go that far"
(`fgTmUq78ofQ`).

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: any distance, run time, port, ramp, fuel dock
or bait receiver for Colonet. The [region page](baja-pacific-north.md) carries
the same gap for the whole coast south of Ensenada, so a private-boat plan for
this zone has no supported staging point in the KB.

**Regulatory — Mexico. Jurisdiction: CONAPESCA for fishing, INM for entry.
Checked as of 2026-08. Verify current requirements before you fish
([Mexican waters](mexican-waters.md)).** Two rules are stated for this zone
specifically, and both are jurisdiction differences rather than doctrine
conflicts:

- **Cow cod.** Cow cod is legal to catch in Mexican waters, but a fish caught
  legally in Mexico **cannot be brought back into California**; the advice is
  to send it back down on a descending device instead (`PRNMGpLj7Pw`,
  checked on camera against the regulations as of 2026-01). California's
  side of the same rule — cow cod as a no-retain species and the Cow Cod
  Conservation Area's depth limit — is on
  [rockfish & lingcod](../species/rockfish-lingcod.md).
- **Hook count.** Anglers fishing down at Colonet "can use a little bit more
  hooks" than the **maximum of two** that applies in US waters once the
  California rockfish season opens (`PRNMGpLj7Pw`, as of 2026-01). ⚠ Flagged
  gap — no corpus source states what the Mexican hook limit actually is, so the
  KB can only say it is not the US two-hook rule.

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: closures, protected areas or restricted zones
anywhere in this zone.

## Structure & bathymetry

**Both named spots are crests, and they are deep ones.** Colonet High Spot 28
Fathoms sits at **30°53.800'N 116°30.200'W** and Colonet 41 Fathoms at
**30°50.600'N 116°30.350'W** (cameron). A mark named for a number is named for
the fathom depth of its shallowest point as read on the sounder, six feet to a
fathom ([zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md)) — read that way they crest at about
**168 ft and 246 ft**.

**The depth is the zone's defining feature.** Colonet is named in the deepest
of three yo-yo depth tiers, alongside "the ridge," Alijos and Guadalupe: water
deep enough that the pick is a **Salas 7X heavy** rather than the 6X fished at
120–150 ft or the 6X Jr fished at 60–100 ft, because a big heavy jig spends more
time in the zone on a straight up-and-down (`hteLeDIy9Qs`;
[yo-yo iron](../techniques/yo-yo-iron.md)).

**The charted crests bracket the depths the fish are actually found in.** The
yellowtail spots on this stretch run **120 to about 180 ft, sometimes as deep as
220 ft, but almost always within that range** (`YAKOv9bXKO0`) — 20 to 30
fathoms, which puts the 28-fathom high spot inside the band and the 41-fathom
spot below it.

**Two numbered banks are assigned here by the census**, both from the spot
library's offshore-banks section: **52** at **31°06.817'N 116°34.250'W** and
**330** at **30°45.000'N 116°34.700'W** (cameron). Under the same fathom
convention their names read as **52 and 330 fathoms** — the second one an
open-ocean depth rather than anything a yo-yo reaches. ⚠ Flagged gap — no
corpus source says anything about either bank beyond its position.

**Bottom character is described only obliquely.** The slow-pitch rig carried
for a Colonet trip runs 25 ft of 50 lb braid plus 3 ft of 80 lb at the business
end purely for abrasion, because thin main braid that touches "the pinnacles
and rocks" down there will break (`PRNMGpLj7Pw`). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus
source maps the reef, the kelp, the canyon edges or the shape of either high
spot.

## How it fishes

**The program is a deep yo-yo grind over the high spots.** Drop to the bottom,
put the rod under the left armpit with the tip down, and wind back as fast as
possible; when the bite comes, keep winding rather than swinging — the hook is
set once drag actually pays out in the other direction, because the fish sets
it better than the angler can (`YAKOv9bXKO0`, `hteLeDIy9Qs`). Full mechanics
are in [yo-yo iron](../techniques/yo-yo-iron.md).

**Work the strike zone, not the whole water column.** Because the fish sit
around 30 ft off the bottom, the productive pattern is to wind up only
**75–100 ft off the bottom**, flip out of gear and drop back down — more passes
through the zone, and far less tiring than winding all the way to the surface
(`YAKOv9bXKO0`).

**Anchored with current on both sides is a normal presentation here.** When the
boat is anchored and the current runs the same way down both rails, either side
is fishable: cast a few feet up-current so the jig sinks in front of you
(`YAKOv9bXKO0`). Yo-yo fishing tolerates an angled line in a way bluefin
jigging does not, so drift scope is not automatically a lost drop
(`YAKOv9bXKO0`; [current & structure](../conditions/current-structure.md)).

**Jig choice is set by depth, by current and by what else is biting.** The
Colonet-tier default is the heavy iron — Salas 7X heavy in the deep tier
(`hteLeDIy9Qs`), or a Salas 7X or brass JRI when the fish are deep or there is a
lot of wind or current (`YAKOv9bXKO0`). Colour matters less than retrieve speed
— scrambled egg or blue-and-white covers most of it — with two exceptions:
during a red-crab bloom the red-crab patterns are the pick, and chrome is
avoided anywhere bonito or barracuda are around because they are quicker to it
than the yellowtail (`YAKOv9bXKO0`;
[iron jigs](../lures/iron-jigs.md)).

**The rockfish program is slow-pitch, sized up on purpose.** On grounds this
productive the first fish to see the jig bites it, and it may be a small one,
so a **6 in grub tail** on the rear hook turns an 8 in jig into a 14 in
presentation — small fish hesitate and look, which is the window a better fish
comes in through (`PRNMGpLj7Pw`;
[slow-pitch jigging](../techniques/slow-pitch-jigging.md)).

**Gear class, zone-scoped.** Yellowtail: a fast, full two-speed reel with
**80 lb braid under a 50 or 60 lb mono top shot** — 50 lb minimum on a
long-range trip, 60 lb better, and 80 lb avoided only because it does not sink
as well — on a short **6'6", 30–80 lb** rod, with treble hooks unless tuna are
around (`YAKOv9bXKO0`; [gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md)). Slow-pitch:
**20 lb braid** for a thin diameter that sinks straight, a 25 ft **50 lb**
braid section and 3 ft of **80 lb** leader for abrasion, and a rod rated to
**350 g** of jig as the first one to own (`PRNMGpLj7Pw`).

## Spots

Four charted positions (cameron), in two groups:

- **The named high spots** — Colonet High Spot 28 Fathoms and Colonet 41
  Fathoms (cameron): the two crests the zone is named around, both labelled for
  their sounder depth, and the deeper of the two below the 120–220 ft band the
  corpus gives for yellowtail here (`YAKOv9bXKO0`).
- **The numbered banks** — 52 and 330: assigned to this zone by the census from
  the spot library's offshore-banks section, neither carrying a word of corpus
  detail beyond its position.
