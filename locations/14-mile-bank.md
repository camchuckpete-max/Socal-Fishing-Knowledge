# 14 Mile Bank

A charted offshore bank at **33°23.833'N 118°00.000'W** (cameron), the
northwestern mark of the
[279 / 267 / 14 Mile Bank](279-267-14-mile-bank.md) zone, sitting in open water
between the south Orange County beach and Catalina's east end. It is named for
its distance off the beach rather than for a fathom depth (`Rf1HKJG-SDg`), and
it is the one mark in its zone the corpus names repeatedly — on camera as a
summer bluefin/yellowtail/bonito day, in a bank-approach walkthrough, in an
August yellowfin report, and in the San Diego offshore swordfish rotation.

## Structure & bathymetry

**The name is a distance, and the chart bears it out.** A bank is normally an
isolated high spot rising out of otherwise deep water, named for the **fathom
depth of its shallowest point** at six feet to a fathom — with distance-named
banks the stated exception, and "the 14" sitting **14 miles off the beach**
(`Rf1HKJG-SDg`, [zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md)). The nearest charted
mainland-side position, the Hospital 100-fathom curve off
[Dana Point](dana-point.md), lies **12.2 nm** away — **14.0 statute miles**
(cameron), the name read straight back off the chart.

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: depth, relief, bottom composition,
high-spot shape, or the surrounding depth this bank rises out of. It is the one
mark in its zone with **no** fathom reading available even by naming convention,
because its number is a distance ([the zone page](279-267-14-mile-bank.md)).

**The bank has a "highest part" and an "inside edge," and both are working
terms here.** The corpus's bank-approach walkthrough is set on this mark and
uses both — stand off the **highest part**, and once on the **inside edge**
point the search at the bank rather than at open water (`DnSqw4r7A1s`). That is
the only shape information the corpus gives, and it is procedural rather than
bathymetric. A standalone bank is not direction-locked the way a land-attached
ridge is, but each bank still has preferred directions learned over repeat trips
([island structure](island-structure.md)), and the working bias on any bank is
the **up-current face** ([current & structure](../conditions/current-structure.md)).
⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source names the preferred directions or the drift
behaviour at this mark.

**Charted neighbours, nearest first** (cameron): the **279 / 267** at
**10.9 nm / 124°** — the zone's other mark — **Avalon Bank** at **11.3 nm** west,
which the census attaches to
[Catalina Island — Front Side](catalina-island-front-side.md) rather than to a
bank grouping; the **277** at **12.5 nm** and the **152** at **12.8 nm** in
[The Slide / 152 / 277](slide-152-277.md); **Jewfish Point** on Catalina's front
side at **15.9 nm**; the **209** at **19.2 nm / 160°**; and **Farnsworth Bank**
at **26.1 nm**.

**A scored spot, which is unusual for a bank.** It is one of the ~14 BightSST
evaluation locations the upwelling/turnover model is tested against, listed there
as "14 Mile Bank (LA)" (cameron) — see
[eval targets](bightsst-eval-targets.md) and
[upwelling and turnover](../conditions/upwelling-and-turnover.md) — so a day
plan pulls conditions for this mark off a scored position rather than a raw
per-coordinate lookup, and Cameron's turnover doctrine, that fishing is best
right **before** turnover, applies to a number this bank actually produces.

**The corpus labels its coast three different ways.** The eval list files it as
**LA**, the on-camera trip is framed as **offshore San Diego** (`-bw1KDfDjv4`,
[electronics and sounder](../planning/electronics-and-sounder.md)), and the
swordfish rotation groups it with the **San Diego** banks (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`) — while
the nearest charted coast in the spot library is **Dana Point / south Orange
County** at **12.2 nm**, with La Jolla **46.5 nm** and the Point Loma stretch
**54.1 nm** off (cameron). This is a labelling difference between a model list,
a trip framing and a fishery shorthand — not a doctrine conflict. Plot the
coordinate. ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source resolves which coast the fishery
considers this bank to belong to.

**The summer day's scope is the complex, not just the mark.** The
**66–67 °F** reading and the boiling bluefin attach to the bank itself, but the
crew described the whole section **"between the 14 M Bank, the 209, the 267"**
as one pocket of warm water and reported the later paddy and jig stop from
"offshore on the 209 area" (`-bw1KDfDjv4`) — so the bonito grade and the paddy
yellowtail are carried here as the complex's, even though
[bonito](../species/bonito.md) records the 8 lb fish as a 14 Mile Bank jig stop.
⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source separates the two marks' catches on that day.

**The August yellowfin report reached this bank late in a long run.** The day
worked Mackerel Bank, then the 289, then "the 14" (`CMQkHQMxbXM`) — **29.3 nm**
and **28.3 nm** from this position respectively (cameron), so the bank was the
inshore end of a big loop rather than a first stop.

**This is in the good half of the paddy split.** The best kelp-paddy water in the
Bight runs south of San Clemente Island and Catalina and along the coastal strip
from **Dana Point to Oceanside and southward**, thinning out north of Dana Point
(`HzE4FHHcvTk`, [bight geography](bight-geography.md)). This mark lies **3.5
minutes** of latitude south of the Dana Point kelp buoy (cameron), and the best
paddy the on-camera crew saw all day was in this complex (`-bw1KDfDjv4`). Paddy
mechanics are in [kelp paddies](../conditions/kelp-paddies.md).

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: any season here other than the two summer
days, or a temperature, moon or current band that gates a bite at this mark.

## How it fishes

**The corpus's bank-approach procedure is set on this bank by name.** Running out
to the 14 Mile Bank, stop somewhere within about **two miles** of the **highest
part** and stop the boat entirely if it is calm; if it is not calm, angle the
boat for the easiest possible ride and barely leave it in gear at
**1–1.5 mph**. Then glass for an extended period — moving the binoculars a couple
of inches at a time, **three or four minutes** to work all the way around the
boat — looking for more than foamers (`DnSqw4r7A1s`). Pulled up on the **inside
edge**, the majority of the looking goes at the **bank itself** rather than at
open water, because the chance of finding fish off the structure is slimmer than
right on it (`DnSqw4r7A1s`). The general method, including the **~30° off each
bow** lookout stations and the contour run, is
[search and glassing](../planning/search-and-glassing.md).

**On the way out, anything that breaks the pattern stops the boat.** Birds,
bait, a scum line off a current break, a couple of paddies in a row, a
temperature or colour break — any deviation from what you have been seeing is
worth stopping and looking around the immediate area (`DnSqw4r7A1s`). Reading
those signs is [bird reading](../conditions/bird-reading.md),
[water colour](../conditions/water-color.md) and
[current & structure](../conditions/current-structure.md).

**Read the bank against the water just off it, not against an absolute number.**
With the plotter's sea-surface-temperature readout left in text mode over the
chart, the on-camera crew read **66–67 °F** on the bank climbing to **68 °F** a
little off it, and it was the differential that identified the pocket worth
working (`-bw1KDfDjv4`,
[electronics and sounder](../planning/electronics-and-sounder.md),
[water temperature](../conditions/water-temperature.md)). That is one day's
reading, not a band — ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source gives the temperature
range that gates a bite here.

**Run the radar while you search.** It doubles as a way to see where the rest of
the fleet is sitting — the stated payoff on this bank complex was spotting a
boat parked on a kelp paddy on the radar screen before that boat noticed the
approach (`-bw1KDfDjv4`, [fleet intelligence](../planning/fleet-intelligence.md)).

**Wind is what ends the day here, twice over.** The August yellowfin report has
chop shutting down spotting entirely once it came up (`CMQkHQMxbXM`), and the
approach doctrine already assumes a sea state bad enough that you cannot stop
the boat and have to idle at 1–1.5 mph instead (`DnSqw4r7A1s`). Sea-state limits
are [sea state](../conditions/sea-state.md).

**Not every school you find here will bite, and the answer is to keep moving.**
Both dated days on this bank found fish on the surface that would not eat —
bluefin boiling and jumping in summer, yellowfin refusing surface lures in
August — and the stated read is that not every school is going to bite: stay
positive, move on, and look for new fish or paddies (`-bw1KDfDjv4`,
`CMQkHQMxbXM`). Casting mechanics for the schools that do eat are
[foamer casting](../techniques/foamer-casting.md).

**A paddy that already produced gets a second pass.** If you catch a yellowtail
off a paddy and then drift off it, circle back and give the same piece another
try rather than treating the one fish as the end of the stop — the stated payoff
was a few more (`-bw1KDfDjv4`, [kelp paddies](../conditions/kelp-paddies.md)).
The paddy build that worked was light: **25 lb** fluorocarbon to a **No. 2 or
No. 3** J hook, sardines **nose-hooked** (`-bw1KDfDjv4`,
[flyline](../techniques/flyline.md)).

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: a tide, moon or current window for this mark,

search box or as a spot-check. The zone-level program is on
