# East Cape

**[Baja only]** — The **offshore end** of [Cortez South](cortez-south.md), and
the one zone in the region where an open-water troll for tuna, wahoo, dorado
and marlin is the whole day rather than something that runs above a structure
program. The East Cape is the Sea of Cortez shore below La Paz, called on camera
*"the jewel to East Cape, the Sea of Cortez"* and framed as a region distinct
from the Cabo side of the peninsula (`84XPJAeH0Rw`, `mL4Ph7t0WcQ`). It is fished
off a beach resort: the boats leave in the morning for
[yellowfin](../species/yellowfin-tuna.md), [wahoo](../species/wahoo.md),
[dorado](../species/dorado.md) and [marlin](../species/striped-marlin.md), and
the same trip puts anglers on the sand for roosterfish and
[jack crevalle](../species/pacific-crevalle-jack.md) (`tcso7Lpm_Xs`,
`mL4Ph7t0WcQ`).

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the *"jewel to East Cape"* quote is
`84XPJAeH0Rw` alone — *"Buena Vista beach resort earns its name as the jewel to
East Cape the Sea of Cortez"*. `mL4Ph7t0WcQ` carries no such line (its nearest
is `mG8ZZLFGlT8`'s separate *"it is truly a gem in the east cape"*), so the
paired cite credits the quote to a source that does not carry it.

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the four-species morning list is not in
`mL4Ph7t0WcQ`, where wahoo is never mentioned at all and the stated plan is
squid-then-tuna-then-roosters — *"Squid from there we can fishing because it's
close to shore we can go for Roosters afterwards"*. Jack crevalle on the sand is
in neither cite: `tcso7Lpm_Xs` mentions the beach only as a joking aside and
lands no inshore fish.

**The grade is the reason to come, and it is caught close in.** Tournament
weigh-ins here recorded yellowfin at **37 lb**, **39.4 lb** and a fish corrected
on stage from **41 lb to 46 lb** in one year, and **72 lb, 63 lb and 63 lb** in
another (`mG8ZZLFGlT8`, `GptrotE0x5M`), with a day's catch across a boat running
**50–70 lb class** — taken *"not even two miles away from shore, 52 feet"*
(`_c6UI3lGBVg`). That grade is bigger than anything the corpus records in the
[Bight](socal-bight.md), and wahoo were caught **a mile to a mile and a half
offshore** (`YijeuGOYoVQ`).

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the weights verify verbatim but neither
event is a weigh-in — both are dockside prize recaps (*"I heard you caught a
39.4 pound yellowfin tuna"*), and the 41→46 lb move is stage banter adding a
bonus, not a re-weigh: *"and how big was he 41 pounds... oh that's five more
pounds so 46 Pounds"*. Already carried on [Cortez South](cortez-south.md).

**The whole record is one operation's tournament week, filmed across four
years.** Every documented day here comes from the Buena Vista Beach Resort and
its annual event — the first edition fished in December 2016, then trips in
March 2018, February 2019 and April 2020 (`mG8ZZLFGlT8`, `tcso7Lpm_Xs`,
`84XPJAeH0Rw`, `APsnsunT4gM`). Read the page as one resort fleet's program, not
as the zone's full fishery. Paperwork for every Mexican trip is
[Mexican waters](mexican-waters.md).

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): none of the four months is spoken on
camera — 2016-12, 2018-03, 2019-02 and 2020-04 are all YouTube upload dates, and
no East Cape transcript states a calendar date. The March 2018 pair calls itself
*"an absolute perfect summer getaway"* (`tcso7Lpm_Xs`) and the April 2020 pair
sells *"comes summertime"* (`BQ2U1PqxWi8`), so the sources' own season language
runs against the dates the page files them under. Already carried on
[Cortez South](cortez-south.md).

## Getting there

**This zone is a short flight and a van ride, not a crossing.** The party flies
into the **Cabo San Lucas** airport — named on camera as the gateway for Cabo,
the East Cape and La Paz alike (`ORC1A68cEeM`) — on a flight put at **less than
two hours** from John Wayne and likened to going to Denver (`mL4Ph7t0WcQ`). The
resort arranges the ground transfer as part of the booking and meets the group
with a van (`mL4Ph7t0WcQ`).

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the airport is misnamed. `ORC1A68cEeM`
names **San José del Cabo** and sets it explicitly *against* Cabo San Lucas as a
destination — *"we are at the San Jose the Cabo the airport that we have to come
in order to go to Cabo San Lucas or go to eastcape or in this case we're going to
[La Paz]"*. The gateway framing is right; the airport name inverts the source.
`mL4Ph7t0WcQ` renders its arrival airport only as the garbled *"Kon Lucas"*.

**The base is a family-run beach resort, and the fishing is packaged around a
tournament.** The property sits directly on the beach within short walking
distance of the water (`mG8ZZLFGlT8`). The trip format is stated as **six days
with three days of fishing** in one year and as **three full fishing days plus
a day off** in another (`mL4Ph7t0WcQ`, `tcso7Lpm_Xs`), and a later edition drew
**30–40 people** across **10–12 teams** (`BQ2U1PqxWi8`).

**The fleet runs both cruisers and pangas, and you can switch between them
mid-trip.** Having both is called out as the point — *"every fisherman is
different but the good news is they have both"* (`_c6UI3lGBVg`) — and one crew
changed boats mid-trip specifically to fish inshore from a panga
(`6j7V34GYzzw`). Named boats include a **22 ft** center console and an
**Everglades 323** (`84XPJAeH0Rw`). Boats are boarded at a **slip**, not
launched off the sand (`APsnsunT4gM`). One captain's tenure with the operation
is given as **30 years** (`YijeuGOYoVQ`).

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the slip/sand contrast is the page's, not
the source's. `APsnsunT4gM` says only *"we're gonna back that big boat out of the
slip"*, in a hype segment, and never mentions a beach launch — while another
episode in this same zone's source set shows the opposite: *"that's a good
oldfashioned beach launch... on the sand"* (`GptrotE0x5M`). Both boarding modes
are on record here.

**The runs are short, and that is the zone's defining practical fact.** Wahoo
came from **a mile to a mile and a half offshore** (`YijeuGOYoVQ`), tuna from
**under 2 mi off in 52 ft** (`_c6UI3lGBVg`), the blue marlin from roughly
**five miles offshore** (`mG8ZZLFGlT8`), and a **~20 lb dorado** ate about
**100 yards off the sand** (`APsnsunT4gM`). The summary the crew give it is
simply that *"the fishing spots are close"* (`6j7V34GYzzw`) — a different
proposition from [Baja Pacific — South](baja-pacific-south.md) at the same
latitude on the other coast.

**Fish go home through the resort.** Shipping the catch home is offered as a
resort service rather than left to the angler (`mG8ZZLFGlT8`;
[bleeding & general care](../fish-care/dorado-and-general.md)).

**Regulatory — Mexico. Jurisdiction: CONAPESCA for fishing, INM for entry.
Checked as of 2026-08. CDFW rules do not apply on this side of the line, and a
Mexican limit differing from a California one is two jurisdictions, not a
conflict. Verify current requirements before you fish
([Mexican waters](mexican-waters.md)).**

⚠ Fact-check (unverifiable): the CONAPESCA/INM agency split carries no cite and
nothing in this zone's twelve transcripts establishes it — it is inherited from
[Mexican waters](mexican-waters.md), where the same block is asserted rather than
sourced, and [Cortez South](cortez-south.md) already carries this flag on
identical wording. A regulatory claim stamped as checked that the corpus cannot
support.

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: closures, protected areas, park boundaries,
bag limits or depth restrictions anywhere in this zone. Not one of the eleven
sources mentions a reserve, a permit or a limit. This matters more here than in
most zones, because the panga grounds south of the resort may be Cabo Pulmo
(see [Spots](#spots)) and nothing in the corpus says what rules apply there.

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-internal): the source count is wrong in both places it
appears. The front matter lists **twelve** sources, and the
[evidence file](evidence/east-cape.md) counts **ten** East Cape episodes plus two
borrowed from La Paz and La Ventana — neither figure is eleven.

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: the harbor. Boats are boarded at a slip
(`APsnsunT4gM`) but no source names it, and no ramp, fuel, haul-out or marina
detail appears.

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: running your own boat here, and what a day
costs. Every documented day is aboard a resort boat, and no boat rate, tip

## Structure & bathymetry

**This zone has no documented offshore structure — it has a coast with deep
water against it.** Not one bank, high spot, ridge, canyon, contour or seamount
is named anywhere in the East Cape corpus. That is the sharpest contrast with
the rest of [Cortez South](cortez-south.md), where the day's plan *is* a named
ground — El Bajo at [La Paz](la-paz.md), the **200-to-80 ft** rise off La
Ventana (`1nK7vSPl2sg`), Six Mile Reef off [Loreto](loreto.md). Here the fish
are found by looking, and the depths on record are depths fish were caught in,
not depths of anything on the bottom.

**The drop comes to the beach, and that single edge is the inshore fishery.**
The lighthouse is described as one of the main fishing spots the operation has,
with *"a drop very close to Shore"* holding sharks, roosterfish, jacks, cabrilla
and dorado — and the claim that *"even the yellowfin tuna you can catch it from
the Beach here"* (`mL4Ph7t0WcQ`). The crew's phrase for it is that
*"everything's right here on this Edge"* (`mL4Ph7t0WcQ`). The beach dorado ate
in about **30 ft** roughly a hundred yards out (`APsnsunT4gM`).

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): two of the assemblage's terms are not what
the captions say. **Cabrilla** is a de-garbling of *"kuas"* — the word never
appears, and this note's own [evidence file](evidence/east-cape.md) logs the
identification as not carried. And the yellowfin quote is normalised, not
verbatim: the captions read *"even the yant tuna you can catch it from the Beach
here"*. Sharks, roosters, jacks and dorado are literal.

**The inshore tuna ground is 52 ft of water under 2 mi off the beach**
(`_c6UI3lGBVg`) — an open-coast position, not a structure stop, and the only
sounded depth in the zone.

**The tuna hold high in the column.** Fish taking flylined squid close to shore
were put at **30–60 ft** of water (`mL4Ph7t0WcQ`), and on a later trip the crew
put the squid the tuna were eating at **no more than 50 ft down**
(`GptrotE0x5M`). Both readings are shallow enough that the technique runs
without weight.

**There is rock down the coast toward the resort, fished on the way home.**
When the weather picked up, the crew ran back down the coast and fished all the
way in, throwing rip baits at rocks behind the boat (`APsnsunT4gM`). That is the
only hard structure named in the entire zone, and it is given no position, no
depth and no name.

**Offshore is "the point" and then open water.** The crew ran out toward a
feature they called the point, fished outside for about an hour with nothing
happening, and hooked the blue marlin roughly five miles out while cruising back
(`mG8ZZLFGlT8`). No depth, no bottom and no bathymetry is given for any of it.

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: any charted position in this zone.
[The spot library](../sources/spot-lists.md) stops at San Quintín on the Pacific
side and carries nothing on this coast, so a day plan cannot pull per-spot
BightSST conditions here the way it can in the [Bight](socal-bight.md).

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: bathymetry of any kind — how far the shelf
runs, where the drop-off sits, what depth the offshore troll works over, and
whether the fleet is fishing a contour, a temperature edge or simply the water
in front of the hotel.

## How it fishes

**The boat picks one of three fisheries in the morning**, and all three are
available from the same trip: an offshore troll, an inshore panga or bait
session, and the beach (`tcso7Lpm_Xs`, `_c6UI3lGBVg`).

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): neither cite describes a morning choice
of three. `tcso7Lpm_Xs` offers only the marketing framing *"world-class inshore
and offshore fishing"* and mentions the beach as a joking aside, with no panga
fishing in the episode at all; `_c6UI3lGBVg`'s only stated choice is **boat
type**, not fishery — *"cruisers are awesome [pangas] and every fisherman is
different but the good news is they have both"*. The three-program structure the
section is built on is the page's synthesis across episodes, not a source claim.

**Program one — the offshore troll.** Jigs go in on the run out, and on the
documented morning the first hookup came roughly **15 minutes** later; that
session produced tuna, roughly 15–16 dorado, roughly 15–20 skipjack and a marlin
hookup (`tcso7Lpm_Xs`;
[full execution](../techniques/trolling.md#east-cape-baja--buena-vista-beach-resort-offshore-troll-for-tunadoradowahoomarlin)).
A long, hard-running fish is given **free-spool** and allowed to run before being
worked back, with the **circle hook** credited on camera for the solid hookup
(`6j7V34GYzzw`; [hooks](../tackle/hooks.md)). **Hot pink is the colour the crews
keep coming back to** on the troll (`6j7V34GYzzw`, `YijeuGOYoVQ`), and the
standing advice is that *"you just got to find that magic lure"* (`YijeuGOYoVQ`).

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the troll session's tally is a **different

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): neither cite supports hot pink as an

roosters refused every lure, not on the troll.

**Program two — park on the fish and flyline over squid chum.** With the boat
sitting over actively feeding tuna, the crew **chum squid pieces continuously**
while hook-baiting squid on one of the smaller hooks in the spread, and the fish
take **no more than 50 ft down** (`GptrotE0x5M`;
[flyline](../techniques/flyline.md)). The presentation is a squid **drifted
slowly back with no weight**, likened on camera to a chunk bait, with chunks
going over the side alongside it (`mL4Ph7t0WcQ`, `_c6UI3lGBVg`). Live sardine
and live mackerel are flylined on circle hooks beside it — sized down for the
sardines and up to about **4/0–5/0** on fluorocarbon for the mackerel
(`84XPJAeH0Rw`).

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): *"continuously"* hardens the source, which
says *"I keep [chumming] a little bit of squid and put squid on another hook on
one of the small hooks"*, and the *"no more than 50 ft down"* call is a **separate
moment ~50 seconds later**, after a different instruction (*"let's do the two
with big hooks with circle hooks"*) — the two are fused here (`GptrotE0x5M`).

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the squid sentence takes half from each
cite and gives both to both. **No weight is stated in either** — it is inferred
from the word "flylining". The chunk-bait likening is `mL4Ph7t0WcQ` only

your squid floating down"* alongside thrown pieces), where neither the slow
drift-back nor the analogy appears.

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the circle hook and the fluorocarbon are

macro"*. The small sardine hook is never called a circle hook, and "flylined" is
never said in the passage (`84XPJAeH0Rw`).

**Find the line class by fishing three of them at once.** The crew ran **three
rods rigged to 30 lb, 40 lb and 25 lb** simultaneously and watched which got bit
more often before leaning the rest of the spread that way, with the explicit
framing that the answer changes day to day — *"what worked yesterday is
completely different today"* (`84XPJAeH0Rw`). Squid hook baits were fished on a
**40 lb** class (`GptrotE0x5M`), and one inshore day was fished at **20 lb**,
called *"light Tackle fishing at the finest"* (`mL4Ph7t0WcQ`).

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the 40 lb is never tied to the squid hook
bait. It is said of a fish already landed — *"that's on a circle hook yeah on a
circle 40 lb"* — while the squid-on-a-small-hook remark is a separate utterance
carrying no line class (`GptrotE0x5M`). The 20 lb is itself hedged in its source:
*"it's 20 lb is that right Ryan I think so he thinks so"* (`mL4Ph7t0WcQ`).

**Program three — slow-troll live bait and drop a jig under it.** Big **scad
mackerel** are slow-trolled while an angler drops a **flat-fall** under the boat
and rips it, the speed kept low enough that casting and dropping both stay
possible; the captain calls fish off the meter as they go (`BQ2U1PqxWi8`;
[knife jigging](../techniques/knife-jigging.md)). Where to start is decided by
the bait, not the chart — the crew fished a spot specifically because the boat
they got the baits from had already taken tuna there (`BQ2U1PqxWi8`).

**Program four — the beach, and it is a sight fishery on wheels.** Anglers rent
ATVs, cruise the sand and do not cast until they see a fish: *"it's sight
fishing, so cruising, we're not going to fish until we see something,"* looking
for **the shadow** rather than the fish itself (`YijeuGOYoVQ`). The clock rule
is the zone's only time-of-day doctrine and it is about light, not feeding —
start **after 9:00**, because you need the sun up to see into the water at all
(`YijeuGOYoVQ`). The surf kit is a **12 ft** rod throwing **1.5–2 oz**, up to a
**3 oz** skipped lure, and the retrieve for roosters is deliberately loud: the
**more erratic and splashy the better** (`YijeuGOYoVQ`). Some anglers come for
this alone and spend **three or four days on the beach** (`YijeuGOYoVQ`).

**The bait-soak version of the beach program is the opposite of that.** A short
**~30 ft** cast is held stationary and thumbed on the spool, and the circle-hook
coaching is to let the fish take it and run — *"give him a 10 count, at least 10
seconds"* — then just wind rather than swing (`mL4Ph7t0WcQ`;
[hooks](../tackle/hooks.md)). Once fish are located the bite window is fast: a
hookup inside seconds, at most a couple of minutes (`mG8ZZLFGlT8`). The beach
dorado was taken on **50 lb braid** to a **30 lb fluorocarbon** top shot and a
**#2 circle hook** sized for the sardine, on a **7'6" heavy** rod
(`APsnsunT4gM`). A second inshore rig ran the same 50 lb braid to a **40 or
30 lb** fluorocarbon top shot (`APsnsunT4gM`).

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the ~30 ft cast is coached **from the
boat** on live bait, not off the beach — *"like how far like uh 30 ft 30 ft okay
and then just kind of keep it there you keep it there and thumb it on it"*, with
the hookup running under the hull; the ATV beach session is only proposed for a
later day in that episode (`mL4Ph7t0WcQ`). The 10-count verifies verbatim.
Already carried on [Cortez South](cortez-south.md).

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the #2 rationale is inverted — the source
gives it as a species hedge, not a bait fit: *"the number two circle hook in case
we get any roosters"* (`APsnsunT4gM`). The 100 yd, 30 ft, ~20 lb, 50 lb spectra,
30 lb fluorocarbon and 7'6" heavy rod all verify verbatim. Already carried on
[Cortez South](cortez-south.md).

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the speaker corrects himself away from
seconds in the same breath — *"boom rooster within seconds not seconds but a
couple minutes couple minutes"* — and it is one anecdote about how fast that fish
came, not a stated rule about located fish (`mG8ZZLFGlT8`).

**Program five — the panga inshore troll.** Roosterfish are worked from a panga
on a **slow-trolled pink skirt**, which drew repeated strikes and several
releases on the documented session (`6j7V34GYzzw`).

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): "skirt" is never said — the lure is only
ever *"pinky"* — and the fish is **not confirmed as a roosterfish**, the crew
leaving the identification open on camera: *"I know if it was the jack of the
rooster too big jack"*. Panga (*"a little Ponca fishing today"*) and slow-trolling
(*"we are slow trolling through Kabul Eidos"*) verify (`6j7V34GYzzw`). Already
carried on [Cortez South](cortez-south.md).

**The fight here is hand-fought on light gear, and it is long.** A **30 lb mono
top shot over spectra** was fished with both thumbs on the spectra, pulling only
when the fish was not pulling, drag bumped up gradually only late; that produced
a **40-minute** fight on an estimated **60 lb** fish that broke the gaff
boatside, went back in, and was landed only after the angler pushed the lever
drag down and thumbed the spool, at **30 lb** of drag — the rod in use was
called *"a little light"* for the **60–80 lb** grade being caught that day
(`GptrotE0x5M`; [fight mechanics](../techniques/fighting-big-bluefin.md)). A
fish circling boatside is treated as the normal end of the fight rather than
something to prevent: walk it through **two more circles**, and call for the
gaff when no more line is going out (`84XPJAeH0Rw`). On wahoo the instruction
inverts — **no pumping at all**, just a steady reel keeping the rod loaded
(`YijeuGOYoVQ`).

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the fight description fuses **two
different fish ~15 minutes apart**. The both-thumbs demo is its own fish, on

Pounders"* verifies verbatim (`GptrotE0x5M`). Already carried on
[Cortez South](cortez-south.md).

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the coaching is exact on both counts —

to a cue the source leaves unattached, and the call *"all right now it's time"*
comes before the two-circles line, not after (`84XPJAeH0Rw`). Already carried on
[Cortez South](cortez-south.md).

**Hand the rod off before you are beaten.** The standing coaching on a big tuna
here is to pass the rod to a teammate rather than keep fighting fatigued
(`_c6UI3lGBVg`), on fights that run about **an hour** (`_c6UI3lGBVg`). The
paired lesson from the same session is why circle hooks carry the zone: a small
sardine-sized hook **pulled out of a big tuna mid-fight** (`_c6UI3lGBVg`).

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the hour is not a zone norm. It is one
offhand line about the single fight then in progress — *"yeah it's an hour worth
that"* — spoken about a minute before the hand-off coaching, which itself carries
no duration (`_c6UI3lGBVg`). The hand-off doctrine and the pulled small hook both
verify verbatim.

**Slack is not a lost fish.** A fish that ate and then swam **toward the boat**
produced slack the crew first read as a pulled hook; winding down showed it was
still on (`BQ2U1PqxWi8`).

**Take the small shot on the way to the big one.** Running toward a known pack
of working boats, the crew veered off to cast at a smaller separate patch of
surface activity first — a detour they put at *"only... a couple of minutes"* —
and that patch was the one biting, on the reasoning that *"it never hurts to
take a shot"* (`84XPJAeH0Rw`; [foamer casting](../techniques/foamer-casting.md)).
A small popper thrown at bait breaking the surface drew an immediate blow-up in
the same session (`84XPJAeH0Rw`), and a topwater stickbait thrown at boils
spotted off a slow-troll was eaten on the **second cast** (`BQ2U1PqxWi8`).

**Bait supply is the variable that can cost a day.** One trip gave a **whole
day** to gathering sardines because the bait situation had *"been kind of poor
lately"*, running to an island to get them (`APsnsunT4gM`;
[making bait](../bait/making-bait.md)).

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): no whole day went to bait. The source

Sardinas because the bait situation has been kind of poor lately so be an inshore
day and see what shows up"* — and the ~20 lb beach dorado is caught on those same
sardines in the same segment (`APsnsunT4gM`). Already carried on
[Cortez South](cortez-south.md).
