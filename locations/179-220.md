# 179 / 220

**A zone is a run grouping.** This is three charted positions on the numbered
offshore grid off the middle of the Baja Pacific coast (cameron) — the **179**,
the **220** and the **Tuna Hole (179)** — spanning **22.0 minutes of latitude**
(30°33.000'N down to 30°11.000'N) inside **5.8 minutes of longitude**
(116°23.200'W to 116°29.000'W). The 179 and the 220 are a tight pair
**2.7 nm** apart that clustered on their own coordinates; the Tuna Hole was
attached **21.2 nm** south of their midpoint and is what sets the zone's
**22.4 nm** diameter.

**No corpus source names any of the three.** Neither the 179, the 220 nor the
Tuna Hole appears in a transcript, a knowledge note or an evidence file — the
whole zone exists on coordinates from
[the spot library](../sources/spot-lists.md) (cameron). This is therefore a
**position-and-ladder page**: where the zone is, what its names claim, what it
sits between, and what is missing. The [region page](baja-pacific-north.md)
carries it as a flagged stub and this page does not lift that stub.

**Where it is is the water outside San Quintín.** The 179/220 pair lies
**14.7 nm** west of the San Martin Island Anchorage at almost exactly the same
latitude, and **18.4 nm** south-southeast of Colonet 41 Fathoms (cameron) — it
is offshore of [San Quintín](san-quintin.md) and south of
[Colonet](colonet.md), the two places the corpus names together as historically
productive bluefin structure that had gone unfished (`fgTmUq78ofQ`).

**The zone holds the southern end of the charted library.** The Tuna Hole at
**30°11.000'N 116°29.000'W** is the **southernmost charted position in the whole
spot library** (cameron). Below it the charted coordinates stop and
[Cedros / San Benitos](cedros-island.md) picks up as a corpus-only zone.

## Getting there

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: any run distance, heading, timing or fuel
figure to this zone from any port, and no source names a port these three marks
are fished out of.

**This is out past the Ensenada day-run band.** The offshore grounds worked out
of that port run **20–50 mi** west with a tournament bank at **42–43 mi**
(`0bcDBGzQnGw`, `Ix0gG0-l3v0`, [Ensenada](ensenada.md)). The marks here sit
**83.1–104.0 nm** from **Punta San Miguel** at **31°54.110'N 116°44.980'W**
(cameron) — about **96–120 statute miles** — roughly double the far edge of that
band on a statute reading, so the Ensenada offshore program does not reach this
water however the units are read. ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source says whether
those port figures are statute or nautical miles.

**The travel band the corpus does put this latitude in is long-range.** Colonet,
**29.9 nm** north of this zone centre to centre (cameron), is grouped with
long-range travel rather than overnight fishing — the rig to carry "on an
overnight boat out of the Channel Islands or a Colonet trip" (`PRNMGpLj7Pw`) —
and the coast just inshore of here was fished off a 90-ft mothership with skiffs
launched from her (`L3tkGVu516A`, [San Quintín](san-quintin.md)). The one
distance figure in the corpus for this stretch belongs to that trip: **188
miles** from San Diego to its first stop at Dono, units never stated on camera
(`_r_qKX_7080`). The **Point Loma Pipe** at **32°40.350'N 117°17.000'W** is
**135.1 nm** from the 179, **137.8 nm** from the 220 and **154.9 nm** from the
Tuna Hole (cameron) — about **155–178 statute miles** — so on either reading of
those 188 miles this zone falls just inside the same travel band, not inside a
day run.

**Distance is the stated reason this water goes unfished, not the fishing.** In
2021-05 the fleet was working one area west of San Diego and two west of
Ensenada, and no boat had run down outside Colonet or San Quintín "in a while…
because they haven't had to go that far" (`fgTmUq78ofQ`). That is said about the
water this zone sits in, and it is a statement about effort rather than about
the marks.

**Regulatory — Mexico. Jurisdiction: CONAPESCA for fishing, INM for entry.
Checked as of 2026-08. Verify current requirements before you fish**
([Mexican waters](mexican-waters.md)). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source:
closures, protected areas or restricted zones anywhere in this zone.

## Structure & bathymetry

**The zone is a tight pair plus a long southern leg.** The 179 at
**30°33.000'N 116°24.000'W** and the 220 at **30°30.350'N 116°23.200'W** are
**2.7 nm** apart — among the closest charted neighbours anywhere on the numbered
grid — while the Tuna Hole (179) at **30°11.000'N 116°29.000'W** lies
**22.4 nm** from the 179 and **20.0 nm** from the 220 (cameron), past the
census's **12 nm** zone-diameter cap, which is why it was attached rather than
clustered.

**All three names claim a depth, and two of them claim the same one.** A bank is
an isolated high spot rising out of otherwise deep water, named for the **fathom
depth of its shallowest point** as read on the sounder, at six feet to a fathom
([zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md), `Rf1HKJG-SDg`):

| spot | name read as fathoms | in feet |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 179 | 179 fathoms | about 1,074 ft |
| 220 | 220 fathoms | about 1,320 ft |
| Tuna Hole (179) | 179 fathoms | about 1,074 ft |

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source confirms any of the three as a charted reading,
and none of them is given a depth independently of its own name.

**Read as depths, these are deep marks by the KB's working bands.** The SoCal
swordfish fishery works the **150–350 fathom** contour
([swordfish](../species/swordfish.md)), and 179 and 220 fathoms both sit inside
it, at six feet to a fathom (`Rf1HKJG-SDg`). About **1,074 ft** falls within
the **800–1,100 ft** band SoCal daytime
deep-drop baits average and just below the **~950 ft** the deep scattering layer
prefers ([deep-drop swordfishing](../techniques/deep-drop-swordfishing.md),
[swordfish](../species/swordfish.md)); about **1,320 ft** is past both. The deep
rockfish game runs **300–900 ft** ([rockfish & lingcod](../species/rockfish-lingcod.md)),
and every figure here reads past it. These are readings of names against
doctrine stated for other water, not a claim that anything is fished this way
here — and the shallowest point of a bank is not the depth you fish over it.

**Two marks share a number and sit 22 nm apart, and the library says that is on
purpose.** The **179** and the **Tuna Hole (179)** are recorded as **distinct
names, not a collision** (cameron), in [the zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md) —
the same ruling given to the 475 against the 475 Knuckle and to the three
300s. This is the only such pair the
census puts inside **one** zone: the 475s, the 300s, the two Kidney Banks and
the 220 against the **Double 220** all land in different zones. ⚠ Flagged gap —
no corpus source says what relationship, if any, the two 179s have beyond the
shared number — whether they are two crests on one structure, a transcription of
one mark twice, or two unrelated banks that read the same on the sounder.

**The plain 220's namesake is 92 nm away.** The **Double 220** at
**31°35.000'N 117°39.000'W** sits **91.6 nm** north-northwest of the 220, in
[Baja - 270 / Double 220 / 295](baja-270-double-220-295.md) (cameron), which
records the same distinct-names ruling from the other end.

**"Tuna Hole" is the only spot name in the grid that states a target, and the
only "hole" in the library.** Across **125** charted offshore-bank positions no
other name mentions a species, and no other name anywhere in the spot library
uses *hole* as a structure term (cameron). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source
defines *hole* as a structure word, says which tuna the name refers to, or
states whether the name reflects a fishery or a chart label.

**Nothing here is attached to land, which sets how it would be worked.**
Standalone structure rising off the plain fishes in **any** direction, but each
individual bank still has its own **preferred directions** learned over repeat
trips ([island structure](island-structure.md)). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus
source: bottom composition, relief, contour, high-spot shape, surrounding depth,
preferred directions, drift or current behaviour at any of these three marks.
The `depth_band` is unknown for that reason, not because the marks are shallow.

**Charted neighbours, nearest first** (cameron): the **304** at **12.5 nm** from
the Tuna Hole, the **6.5 Fathom Spot** at **13.0 nm** from the 220, **Roca Ben**
at **14.5 nm**, the **San Martin Island Anchorage** at **14.7 nm**, the **330**
at **15.1 nm** from the 179, **Breakers Reef** at **17.5 nm**, **Colonet 41
Fathoms** at **18.4 nm** and **Colonet High Spot 28 Fathoms** at **21.5 nm**.
Both zones those belong to are already built —
[San Quintín](san-quintin.md) carries the 304 and the San Martin marks,
[Colonet](colonet.md) the 330 and the two high spots. The 304 is the closest
charted position to the Tuna Hole of anything in the library and still went to
San Quintín, which is where the zone line between this water and the coast runs.
Offshore of here the next groupings are **The Bumps** **42.4 nm** north of the
179 and the **380** **57.1 nm** west of the Tuna Hole — ⚠ Flagged stub — no
corpus source yet: neither has a page on this ladder to hand off to.

**The one corpus statement that scopes to this water is about bluefin, and it is
about the water rather than the marks.** The structure outside Colonet and San
Quintín is described as "a good structure of water down there that, in the
beginning when this all started, that's where that fish was at, and if that
water structure is still there then I'm sure there's bluefin down there too"
(`fgTmUq78ofQ`, 2021-05-14). Every mark in this zone lies in that water — the
pair **14.7 nm** outside San Martin Island, the whole zone **18–22 nm** south of
the Colonet crests (cameron) — so the expectation covers it geographically.
⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source puts a [bluefin](../species/bluefin-tuna.md) on
any of these three positions, gives a month for them, or says how far offshore
"outside" reaches.

**The region-level grade pattern applies here by latitude and nothing narrower.**
Grade improves as you go south on this coast, and Baja coastal structure from
Ensenada southward holds [yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md) more or less
year-round with the grade improving south (cameron,
[Baja Pacific — North](baja-pacific-north.md), [yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md)).
That is stated for coastal structure; these are offshore banks, and no source
extends it to them.

## How it fishes

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: the zone's program in full. Nothing states
which mark to start on, whether the 179 and the 220 are graded as one stop,
whether the **21 nm** leg down to the Tuna Hole belongs in the same day, or what
tide, light, current or swell do here.

**The geometry splits the zone into two different search problems.** The Bight's
vocabulary separates the complex — a **~50–60 square-mile** box you plan hours of
looking across — from the compact spot you idle onto, grade on the meter, and
either commit to or leave (`Blh2BA-7Ono`, [zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md)). The
179 and the 220 at **2.7 nm** apart are close enough to be graded on one pass and
read as a single compact check; the run south to the Tuna Hole is a transit
between two of them, not a box (cameron). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source places
any mark here on either side of that split, and the vocabulary is stated for the
Bight rather than for this coast.

**With no corpus program, the general layers are what a plan has.** Structure is
where bait stages for shelter and gamefish follow it
([zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md), `Rf1HKJG-SDg`), and reading a day out here
falls to [current & structure](../conditions/current-structure.md),
[water temperature](../conditions/water-temperature.md),
[water colour](../conditions/water-color.md),
[search and glassing](../planning/search-and-glassing.md) and
[electronics and sounder](../planning/electronics-and-sounder.md). None of them
is scoped to this water.

**No mark here is a BightSST eval target**
([eval targets](bightsst-eval-targets.md)), so conditions for this zone are
pulled per charted position rather than read off a scored spot — and the three
positions span **22 nm** (cameron), far enough that the pair and the Tuna Hole
should be pulled separately.

## Spots

Three charted spots (cameron). They group as a pair plus one southern
attachment, listed north to south:

- **The 179** — the northern mark of the pair and the closest position in the
  zone to [Colonet](colonet.md), **15.1 nm** from the 330 and **18.4 nm** from
  Colonet 41 Fathoms (cameron). Its name reads as **179 fathoms**, about
  1,074 ft. Coordinates only; the corpus never names it.
- **The 220** — **2.7 nm** south-southeast of the 179, the mark closest to
  [San Quintín](san-quintin.md) at **13.0 nm** from the 6.5 Fathom Spot and
  **14.7 nm** due west of the San Martin Island Anchorage. Its name reads as
  **220 fathoms**, about 1,320 ft, the deepest claim in the zone, and its
  namesake the Double 220 is **91.6 nm** away in
  [Baja - 270 / Double 220 / 295](baja-270-double-220-295.md). Coordinates only.
- **The Tuna Hole (179)** — the southern attachment, **21.2 nm** from the pair's
  midpoint, the **southernmost charted position in the spot library**, the only
  spot name on the numbered grid that states a target species, and the second
  mark in the zone whose name reads as **179 fathoms**. Its nearest charted
  neighbour of all is the **304**, **12.5 nm** northeast in the San Quintín
  zone. Coordinates only.

Charted positions for all three are in
[the spot library](../sources/spot-lists.md). No spot here carries corpus detail
beyond its position, so each spot page is coordinates plus its parent zone until
sources arrive.
