# Cortez South

**The character rung.** The Sea of Cortez below 28°N — Loreto, La Paz and the
East Cape ([regions & waters](regions.md)). It is the **billfish-and-tuna end
of the Gulf**, reached by air and fished off a resort or a local fleet rather
than driven to and fished from a hotel panga the way
[Cortez North](cortez-north.md) is. Blue and striped marlin, yellowfin to
**72 lb** (`GptrotE0x5M`), dorado, wahoo and sailfish are all on the table
here, and the shallow-rock program that carries the north —
[cabrilla](../species/cabrilla.md), pargo,
[yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md) — runs underneath them in the same
water. The paperwork rung is [Mexican waters](mexican-waters.md).

The corpus reaches this region three ways, and they are three different trips:
an annual resort tournament week on the **East Cape** shore
(`mG8ZZLFGlT8`, `tcso7Lpm_Xs`, `84XPJAeH0Rw`, `APsnsunT4gM`), a **La Paz**
fleet fishing island rock and bank high spots (`ORC1A68cEeM`, `qM7iOO7fOBw`,
`92y14x33etQ`), and a fly-in to **Loreto** for an island fishery
([Loreto & Puerto Escondido](loreto.md)). Roosterfish are the plainest
statement of the difference from the north: at Bahía de los Ángeles they are a
matter of luck, described as migratory and **more reliable further south in
the Sea of Cortez** (`P36VGPPf120`), and here they are a named target fished
from the beach and from a panga.

## The fishery

**Two fisheries are stacked in the same water, and the boat picks one in the
morning.** Above is an offshore troll for tuna, dorado, wahoo and marlin;
below is a structure program on shallow rock and bank high spots for
yellowtail, cabrilla and pargo. The East Cape material is almost entirely the
first, the La Paz material almost entirely the second, and
[Loreto](loreto.md) runs both in a day.

**The tuna grade is the region's headline, and it is bigger than anything in
the [Bight](socal-bight.md).** A tournament weigh-in at the East Cape recorded
[yellowfin](../species/yellowfin-tuna.md) at **37 lb**, **39.4 lb** and a fish
corrected on stage from **41 lb to 46 lb** (`mG8ZZLFGlT8`). Later years of the
same event weighed **72 lb, 63 lb and 63 lb** (`GptrotE0x5M`), with the day's
catch across the boat running **50–70 lb class** (`_c6UI3lGBVg`) and another
day of the same trip recapped as mostly **60s–70s lb** (`84XPJAeH0Rw`).

**Billfish are a routine part of the offshore day here, not a rarity.** An
estimated **400–500 lb blue marlin** was hooked roughly **five miles
offshore**, lost, hooked again on a second pass through the same water, lost
again, and connected on a third pass **about 40 minutes** later — that fish
was landed on a **team relay** between the captain, who brought it to leader
**four times** in roughly the first hour, and a second angler who finished it,
about **an hour and a half** of combined fight time (`mG8ZZLFGlT8`).
A young angler landed his first
[striped marlin](../species/striped-marlin.md) on that same trip
(`mG8ZZLFGlT8`), a marlin was released on camera on the opening day of a later
year's event (`BQ2U1PqxWi8`), and one marlin hookup **spooled** the angler
outright (`APsnsunT4gM`). Sailfish are actively targeted out of
[Loreto](loreto.md), where a Spanish-language segment describes fish "up to
300 lb" and fights of **four to five hours** (`Re56xUHOu6A`).

**The tackle these fish are fought on is lighter than the grade suggests, and
the fights are long.** A **30 lb mono top shot over spectra** fished at
**30 lb** of drag produced a **40-minute** fight on an estimated **60 lb** fish
that broke the gaff boatside, went back in, and was landed only after the
angler pushed the lever drag down and thumbed the spool by hand; the rod in use
was called "a little light" for the **60–80 lb** grade being caught that day
(`GptrotE0x5M`). A fish circling boatside is treated here as the normal
end-of-fight pattern — walk it through **two more circles**, with the cue to
call for the gaff being that no more line is going out (`84XPJAeH0Rw`).
Circle hooks carry the region: credited on camera for a solid hookup on a long
free-spooled run (`6j7V34GYzzw`), on a personal-best wahoo (`APsnsunT4gM`), and
on a **#2 circle** with a live sardine (`APsnsunT4gM`). See
[fighting a big bluefin](../techniques/fighting-big-bluefin.md) for the fight
mechanics and [hooks](../tackle/hooks.md) for the hookset timing.

**Volume, not just grade, is what the offshore troll produces.** One morning
session put roughly **15–16 [dorado](../species/dorado.md)** (most released),
roughly **15–20 [skipjack](../species/skipjack-tuna.md)**, tuna and a marlin
hookup on the boat, and a second morning's spread produced multiple
[wahoo](../species/wahoo.md) — most likely **three** boated with a fourth
hooked when the count was given, ASR-garbled (`tcso7Lpm_Xs`). The school-holding
tactic that goes with it is stated the same way twice: leave a hooked dorado
**in the water** and get a second bait out before boating the first, because
the hooked fish holds the school and draws followers (`tcso7Lpm_Xs`,
corroborated at La Paz, `qM7iOO7fOBw`).

**Inshore here is a real fishery, not a fallback.** Tuna were caught **less
than 2 mi off shore in 52 ft** of water (`_c6UI3lGBVg`). A **~20 lb dorado**
ate a live sardine only **~100 yards off the beach in ~30 ft**, on **50 lb
braid** to a **30 lb fluorocarbon** top shot and a **#2 circle hook** sized for
the sardine (`APsnsunT4gM`). Lighthouse Point is a beach spot with a drop very
close to shore holding sharks, roosterfish, jacks, dorado and even yellowfin,
all fishable by surf-casting (`mL4Ph7t0WcQ`); the surf program is a short
**~30 ft** cast held stationary and thumbed on the spool, with the coaching to
give a taking fish a **10 count** before winding tight rather than swinging
(`mL4Ph7t0WcQ`). Roosterfish are also worked from a panga on a **slow-trolled
pink skirt** (`6j7V34GYzzw`). Jack crevalle turn up in the same inshore mix
([Pacific crevalle jack](../species/pacific-crevalle-jack.md)).

**The La Paz end is the structure fishery, and it is shallow.** A live-bait
slow-troll for *jurel* ran in water called out at just **12 ft** deep against
visible rock, on **40 lb** line and circle hooks, with the crew's stated
hookset a counted **3-second pause** before putting the reel in gear; hookups
came as doubles **close to 30 lb** (`ORC1A68cEeM`;
[trolling](../techniques/trolling.md)). Flylined live mackerel at a crowded
shared high spot produced yellowtail, cabrilla, dorado and a large pargo, with
the crew deliberately fishing **outside the pack** rather than anchoring in it
(`qM7iOO7fOBw`; [flyline](../techniques/flyline.md)). A named bank the crew
call **"El Bajo"** — shallowest point roughly **50 ft on low tide, no more
than 80 ft on high** — was fished first with baits dropped to the bottom and
then by slow-trolling the same mackerel so the troll speed lifted the bait off
bottom into the high column, on **65 lb braid** to a **60 lb fluorocarbon** top
shot (`92y14x33etQ`).

**High spots are how the region's structure is found.** Off La Ventana a local
captain ran to a spot rising from roughly **200 ft to 80 ft**, described as
holding a lot of fish; a bait let swim freely for about **seven seconds** got
bit on a **30 lb fluorocarbon** top shot and a **3/0 circle hook**, producing a
large amberjack against a stated target list of tuna, dorado, wahoo and marlin
(`1nK7vSPl2sg`). Off Loreto the same read runs shallower — a boat working birds
sat in **30 ft**, and cabrilla came from **5–10 ft** (`n4nMCLLW3tY`,
`ikiXx9sUsoA`) — and the search sign is birds, worked the same way as the SoCal
program ([bird reading](../conditions/bird-reading.md)). Surface fish, when
they are up, want speed: spooks, swimbaits, [surface
iron](../techniques/surface-iron.md) and bass gear on a burn retrieve — "you
got to burn it, can't let them look at it" — with "20 followers on every cast"
(`s5PaKJX3VDo`).

**The species mix is warm-water pelagic over a structure fishery:**

| water | what this region offers |
| --- | --- |
| open-ocean | [yellowfin tuna](../species/yellowfin-tuna.md), [dorado](../species/dorado.md), [wahoo](../species/wahoo.md), [striped marlin](../species/striped-marlin.md), [skipjack](../species/skipjack-tuna.md), blue marlin, sailfish |
| bank | [yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md), amberjack, pargo/snapper, [cabrilla](../species/cabrilla.md) |
| island, nearshore-coast | [cabrilla](../species/cabrilla.md), [yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md), pargo/snapper, roosterfish, [Pacific crevalle jack](../species/pacific-crevalle-jack.md), sierra, triggerfish |

Roosterfish, sailfish, amberjack, pargo/snapper and African pompano are all
named in the corpus for this region but carry no router note of their own —
recorded here as context rather than linked. More than one cabrilla species
turns up, one fish called out on camera as "a different type of cabrilla"
(`n4nMCLLW3tY`).

## Season shape

Priors, not current intel, and the `seasonal/` calendar they normally rest on
is written for the [Bight](socal-bight.md). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: a
month-by-month prior for the southern Sea of Cortez. The rows below are dated
trips, not a calendar, and `season_peak` is `unknown` for that reason.

| window | what is dated here |
| --- | --- |
| Winter (Dec–Feb) | The East Cape resort's **first annual** tournament week, fished **2016-12-16** and **2016-12-22** — beach roosterfish, offshore yellowfin weighed at 37–46 lb, and the blue marlin above (`mL4Ph7t0WcQ`, `mG8ZZLFGlT8`). The same operation fished **2019-02-10**, **2019-02-13** and **2019-02-18**: tuna at 50–70 lb class, the 72/63/63 lb weigh-in, and casts made at surface activity spotted off the run (`84XPJAeH0Rw`, `GptrotE0x5M`, `_c6UI3lGBVg`) |
| Spring (Mar–May) | **2018-03-09** and **2018-03-12** — the offshore troll's biggest documented mixed bag (15–16 dorado, 15–20 skipjack, multiple wahoo) plus the panga rooster troll (`tcso7Lpm_Xs`, `6j7V34GYzzw`). The **fifth annual** tournament week ran **2020-04-01** and **2020-04-08**: "solid grade A" yellowfin, a marlin release, and a personal-best wahoo called the **first wahoo of the season** for the resort's fleet — ASR-garbled and not confidently resolved beyond that (`BQ2U1PqxWi8`, `APsnsunT4gM`) |
| Summer (Jun–Aug) | One Loreto trip dated on camera **2023-07-31** — birds worked in 30 ft, cabrilla in 5–10 ft, roosterfish on the troll (`n4nMCLLW3tY`) |
| Fall (Sep–Nov) | A **September** Loreto shoreline session (`0b0PDLVh-T4`); a La Ventana charter **2019-10-25** on the 200-to-80 ft high spot (`1nK7vSPl2sg`); a La Paz two-week stay documented **2023-10-25**, **2023-11-03** and **2023-11-09**, with jurel, yellowtail, cabrilla, dorado and pargo through it (`ORC1A68cEeM`, `qM7iOO7fOBw`, `92y14x33etQ`) |

**The one claim the corpus makes about this region's year is that it does not
have a season.** Along the East Cape coast the tuna bite was called
*"consistent all year round... along the coast,"* with fish moving between
locations through the year (`_c6UI3lGBVg`). The dated rows above are consistent
with it: they put fish on the boat in December, February, March, April, July,
September, October and November.

**Wind and current are what close a day here, not the month.** A La Paz trip's
first day was windy with strong current and slow fishing, and the fleet simply
moved to a different spot for day two (`qM7iOO7fOBw`); a later morning's
bait-making ran in **15–20 mph wind out of the north** (`92y14x33etQ`). See
[sea state](../conditions/sea-state.md) and
[current diagnostics](../conditions/current-diagnostics.md).

**Bait supply is its own season variable.** One trip gave a whole day to
gathering bait because the offshore bait supply had "been kind of poor lately"
(`APsnsunT4gM`); at La Paz the guide's target was **20–40 mackerel per boat**
jigged around floating structure, with the crew landing roughly a dozen and
buying sardines from local vendors to make up the difference (`ORC1A68cEeM`;
[making bait](../bait/making-bait.md)).

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: whether the East Cape's year shape applies at
Loreto or La Paz. [Loreto](loreto.md) explicitly warns against borrowing the
Bahía de los Ángeles calendar, and nothing in the corpus supplies one for
either place.

## Zones

**Three zones, and none of them come from coordinates.** The charted sections
of [the spot library](../sources/spot-lists.md) stop at San Quintín on the
Pacific side, so every zone on this page is derived from corpus depth rather
than from a position list. The consequence is concrete: this region publishes
no coordinates at all, so a day plan cannot pull per-spot BightSST conditions
here the way it can in the [Bight](socal-bight.md). Zone pages land with the
zone phase, so unlinked names below have no page yet.

**Regulatory — Mexico. Jurisdiction: CONAPESCA for fishing, INM for entry.
Checked as of 2026-08. CDFW rules do not apply on this side of the line, and a
Mexican limit differing from a California one is two jurisdictions, not a
conflict. Verify current requirements before you fish
([Mexican waters](mexican-waters.md)).** ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source:
Mexican closures, protected areas or depth restrictions anywhere in this
region, including at any of the named grounds below.

| zone | spots | character |
| --- | --- | --- |
| East Cape | corpus zone, no charted spots in the library | The resort-and-tournament end: an offshore troll for 50–70 lb yellowfin, dorado, wahoo and marlin run out of the same trip as beach roosterfish and a panga inshore program (`tcso7Lpm_Xs`, `mL4Ph7t0WcQ`) |
| La Paz | corpus zone, no charted spots in the library | The structure end: *jurel* in 12 ft against rock, flylined mackerel at shared high spots, and bank high spots like "El Bajo" at 50–80 ft, fished off a local fleet with guides (`ORC1A68cEeM`, `qM7iOO7fOBw`, `92y14x33etQ`) |
| [Loreto & Puerto Escondido](loreto.md) | corpus zone, no charted spots in the library | The island end: Isla Monserrat, Isla Carmen, Honeymoon Bay and Six Mile Reef worked for yellowtail, cabrilla, roosterfish, sierra, dorado and sailfish, fly-in access through CBX (`n4nMCLLW3tY`, `ikiXx9sUsoA`) |

**Named grounds the census derives no zone for** — logged rather than omitted,
because silence would read as "nothing there":

- **El Bajo (La Paz)** — ⚠ Flagged stub. Called on camera the area's most
  famous high spot: shallowest point roughly **50 ft on low tide, no more than
  80 ft on high**, fished for amberjack and yellowtail with dropped and
  slow-trolled mackerel (`92y14x33etQ`). The name is also heard as "El
  Elito"/"El Bajito" and is not confidently resolved to one spelling.
- **Isla Espíritu Santo** — ⚠ Flagged stub. Rock structure off the island
  fished as a snapper/cabrilla bottom stop, abandoned when it went quiet —
  *"they just were not chewing... no current whatever"* (`92y14x33etQ`).
- **La Ventana** — ⚠ Flagged stub. A six-pack charter base, working a high spot
  rising from roughly **200 ft to 80 ft** (`1nK7vSPl2sg`).
- **Lighthouse Point** — ⚠ Flagged stub. The East Cape's documented beach
  spot: a drop very close to shore holding a mixed inshore assemblage
  fishable by surf-casting (`mL4Ph7t0WcQ`).
- **Cabo Pulmo** — ⚠ Flagged stub, and the place name itself is unverified:
  the panga rooster-fish grounds south of the East Cape resort are named on
  camera only as something the captions render "Kabul Eidos", read as Cabo
  Pulmo from context and not confidently resolved (`6j7V34GYzzw`).

## Access & range

**This region is flown to, not driven to.** [Loreto](loreto.md) is reached
through **CBX**, the San Diego–Tijuana airport bridge (`ikiXx9sUsoA`), and the
La Ventana-area resort is a **~2.5 hour** flight from
LAX (`1nK7vSPl2sg`). That is the practical break from
[Cortez North](cortez-north.md), which is an ~8 hour drive and has no
documented air access at all.

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: driving into this region. Nothing in the
corpus covers the road south of Bahía de los Ángeles, the run times, fuel, or
whether the drive-in discipline that governs the north
([Cortez North](cortez-north.md)) applies here.

**Every documented trip fishes someone else's boat**, and there are four
patterns:

| access mode | what it is |
| --- | --- |
| Resort week | The East Cape's Buena Vista Beach Resort, framed on camera as offering offshore trolling and inshore/beach fishing out of the same trip, structured around an annual tournament (`tcso7Lpm_Xs`) |
| Local fleet | La Paz's Baja Rancheros, with a fleet captain setting the day's plan and local guides running each boat; a documented stay ran **two weeks** (`ORC1A68cEeM`, `92y14x33etQ`) |
| Six-pack charter | A **35 ft** Everglades out of a La Ventana-area resort (`1nK7vSPl2sg`) |
| Panga / mothership | Loreto's local pangas, and one operation running a mothership with an **18 ft** Grady White towed behind to fish from (`ikiXx9sUsoA`) |

**The runs are short.** The East Cape's blue marlin was hooked roughly **five
miles offshore** (`mG8ZZLFGlT8`), tuna were caught **less than 2 mi off in
52 ft** (`_c6UI3lGBVg`), and the beach dorado ate **~100 yards** off the sand
(`APsnsunT4gM`) — a different proposition from the multi-day run distances of
[Baja Pacific — South](baja-pacific-south.md) at the same latitude on the
other coast.

**Fish handling is part of the shore-side service.** The La Paz dock runs a
clean/fillet/vacuum-seal/freeze service, and the crew stopped keeping fish and
released the rest of the session's yellowtail once three bags' worth had gone
through it (`92y14x33etQ`;
[dorado & general care](../fish-care/dorado-and-general.md)).

**The paperwork gate applies to every trip here.** FMM, CONAPESCA licence and
passport ([Mexican waters](mexican-waters.md)).

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: ports, ramps, fuel, haul-out and marina

boat day rate or tip figure appears for any of the three zones.

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: running your own boat into this region, and
