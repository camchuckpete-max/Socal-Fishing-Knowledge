# Baja Pacific — North

**The character rung.** The Pacific side from the US border down to 28°N: the
Coronado Islands, Rosarito and Descanso, Ensenada, Punta Banda and Santo Tomás,
Colonet, San Quintín, Cedros, the San Benitos and Guadalupe
([regions & waters](regions.md)). It is the same species list as the
[Bight](socal-bight.md) at a heavier grade, reached across a border — the
paperwork rung is [Mexican waters](mexican-waters.md), and it applies from the
13 nm Coronados day trip (cameron) to a 235 mi run at Guadalupe
(`_r08B4bItAY`). The region's north end
is a day-boat fishery off San Diego; its south end is long-range and fly-in
water.

## The fishery

**Grade improves southward, and it changes how the fish bite, not just what
they weigh.** The Coronados run 10–12 lb schoolie
[yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md) that hit a surface iron "like a freight
train" (`4pT0efBD1j0`); Cedros runs 30–45 lb fish that take the same jig slowly
(`IdMJnlg_rZk`), with a lighter 19–33 lb grade recorded on another trip to the
same island (`9kGpcEHqIUc`). A Coronados hookset habit costs fish at Cedros.
The same gradient shows in [calico bass](../species/calico-bass.md) — personal
bests of 7, 8, 9 and 9.5 lb came out of one three-hour skiff rotation down the
coast from San Martin Island (`L3tkGVu516A`) — and in
[yellowfin](../species/yellowfin-tuna.md), which grade 50–150 lb at Guadalupe
against a 15–25 lb SoCal average (`ETw_3AFxEcM`).

**Coastal structure is points and high spots, and the high spots crest hard.**
A high spot 7–8 mi off the Ensenada marina comes up to about **25 ft from
roughly 200 ft** (`CKq0Z6ExVs4`); a short run down-coast the bottom drops to
about **300 ft** over rock (`0dIwWiOc1NY`); the Colonet spots in
[the spot library](../sources/spot-lists.md) are charted at **28 and 41
fathoms**. Fathom-named spots — the 15, 33, 38, 47 and 48 fathom spots between
Punta Banda and Santo Tomás — carry their depth in the name the way the Bight's
banks do ([zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md)).

**Deep water is the region's signature, and it sets the jig.** Colonet and
Guadalupe are named in the deepest of three yo-yo depth tiers, where the pick
is a Salas 7X heavy rather than the 6X or 6X Jr fished at 60–150 ft — more time
in the strike zone on a straight up-and-down (`hteLeDIy9Qs`;
[yo-yo iron](../techniques/yo-yo-iron.md)).

**Hard bottom taxes terminal tackle.** At Guadalupe roughly **eight of ten**
dropper-loop hookups are lost to rock cutoffs even on heavy leader
(`RgtkbmBFUXI`); a Cedros crew lost **about 10 jigs in two days** to fish that
rocked them (`IdMJnlg_rZk`); over the jagged 200 ft bottom at the San Benitos
sinkers and iron have to come up off the bottom immediately after touchdown or
they stay there (`ilINTeknKB4`). Budget terminal tackle before the trip, not
during it.

**Bait is made, not bought.** Mackerel are made just outside the Ensenada
marina — two local types are fished, a slower but tough "Spanish jack" and a
flashier, faster "greenback" (`0dIwWiOc1NY`;
[making bait](../bait/making-bait.md)). The Cedros lodge operation runs its own
bait barge and bait boat for large mackerel and large "Spanish"
([Cedros Island](cedros-island.md)).

**Offshore, the region is a bank grid and a temperature-break search.** From
Ensenada the offshore day plan is a run of **20–30 mi due west** to a
temperature break, then north or west off the break by sea conditions, with a
fallback search 20–30 mi north (`0bcDBGzQnGw`) — chart-first work
([water temperature](../conditions/water-temperature.md),
[current structure](../conditions/current-structure.md)). Bluefin were observed
arriving into the Ensenada zone "out of the west on the moon" (`Blh2BA-7Ono`;
[moon](../conditions/moon.md)).

**The species mix runs coastal bass to big tuna:**

Barracuda, bonito and yellowtail are the standing inshore mix named off
Ensenada (`0dIwWiOc1NY`). [White seabass](../species/white-seabass.md) carries
this region in its gate but ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: where or when
white seabass are taken on this coast.

## Season shape

Priors, not current intel, and the `seasonal/` calendar they normally rest on
is written for the [Bight](socal-bight.md). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source:
a month-by-month prior for the Baja Pacific north — the rows below are dated
observations, not a calendar.

| window | what is dated here |
| --- | --- |
| Winter (Dec–Feb) | Bluefin schools were sighted near San Martin Island in early **January 2021** in **63–65 °F** water (`shZCjX2-fkI`) — presence outside the SoCal slide, not a fishable pattern. ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: what this region offers in winter beyond that sighting |
| Spring (Apr–Jun) | The [Coronados](coronado-islands.md) are the springtime yellowtail draw (`D5DR7Kx42_A`), and they run seeing-not-biting cycles with one short daily bite window rather than an all-day grind (`YZtX1MiT0y8`; [April](../seasonal/april.md)). Mackerel spawn close to shore and spread out off Ensenada in late spring / early summer, which is when bait is easy, and the same trip fished the down-coast points for yellowtail and the ~300 ft rock bottom for lingcod and vermilion (`0dIwWiOc1NY`) |
| Summer (Jul–Sep) | The offshore season. A bank **42–43 mi** off Ensenada held breezing and kite-raised bluefin across an **August 2024** tournament, with ~40 boats landing **4 fish on day one and 7 on day two** — sign without a bite (`Ix0gG0-l3v0`, `U1AgwmlY5bI`). September runs west of Ensenada work temperature breaks for yellowfin, skipjack and marlin (`0bcDBGzQnGw`); Guadalupe is the September five/six-day destination (`_r08B4bItAY`) |
| Fall (Oct–Nov) | Big coastal calico came off the stretch down-coast from San Martin Island in **November 2014**, three personal bests in one three-hour rotation (`L3tkGVu516A`). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: any other fall pattern with a stated fishing date — the Cedros and San Benitos trip footage carries upload dates, not fishing dates |

**Two patterns run across the whole year rather than inside one window.** Baja
coastal structure from Ensenada southward holds yellowtail more or less
year-round with the grade improving as you go (cameron), and Cedros holds
resident "home guard" fish of 40+ lb in any month (`WE643Fue1_A`) — the 30–40 lb
grade concentrating at North Point (`wdbqTio1SQU`). Presence is not
catchability in either case: the Coronados can be full of visible fish that do
not eat (`YZtX1MiT0y8`).

**Where the bluefin are is a fleet-position question, not a calendar one.**
Three areas were being worked as of **2021-05** — one west of San Diego and two
west of Ensenada — with nothing running to Colonet or San Quintín because the
closer grounds were producing; the older structure down there is expected to
still hold fish, just unfished (`fgTmUq78ofQ`). The reading that these are a
resident stock ranging to Cedros Island is single-source and flagged on
[bluefin tuna](../species/bluefin-tuna.md).

## Zones

Twenty-eight zones, derived from the coordinates in
[the spot library](../sources/spot-lists.md) rather than from mention counts.
Zone pages land with the zone phase, so unlinked names below have no page yet.

**Regulatory — Mexico. Jurisdiction: CONAPESCA for fishing, INM for entry.
Checked as of 2026-08. CDFW rules do not apply on this side of the line, and a
Mexican limit differing from a California one is two jurisdictions, not a
conflict. Verify current requirements before you fish
([Mexican waters](mexican-waters.md)).** ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source:
Mexican closures, protected areas or depth restrictions anywhere in this
region.

## Access & range

**Three access modes, and the zone decides which one.** The north end is a
US-port day trip: the Coronados sit about **13 nm** from the San Diego bay
entrance (cameron). Ensenada is a drive-to port, worked out of the Hotel Coral
marina, with the high spots 7–8 mi out (`CKq0Z6ExVs4`), the
offshore grounds 20–50 mi west (`0bcDBGzQnGw`) and the bluefin bank 42–43 mi
out (`Ix0gG0-l3v0`); one tournament day totalled roughly **180 mi** run
(`U1AgwmlY5bI`). The south end is long-range or fly-in.

**Guadalupe is a single day's run from San Diego and nothing less.** **215 mi**
to the north end, **235 mi** to the south end: leave around 11:00, fish by
9–10:00 the next morning at the north end or around noon at the south end
(`_r08B4bItAY`). It is fished on five- and six-day trips, which can add Cedros
or the San Benitos on the same run (`_r08B4bItAY`, `ETw_3AFxEcM`). Chester's
Rock, in the Cedros complex, sits about **300 mi** from San Diego
(`_r_qKX_7080`).

**Cedros is reached by air, and the rods travel with you.** Drive to CBX, walk
your own rod bundle across the pedestrian bridge into the Tijuana airport, then
a roughly **2-hour** charter flight; a 10 ft jig stick fits down the aisle. An
earlier charter route flew out of **Ensenada** with the Mexican permits handled
in Tijuana, so the departure point and who handles paperwork are per-operator
questions (`xIUKmH9ccgQ`). Full chain: [Cedros Island](cedros-island.md).

**The paperwork is the gate on every one of these.** FMM, CONAPESCA licence,
passport and separate Mexican vehicle insurance for a drive
([Mexican waters](mexican-waters.md)) — and the corpus does not answer what a
private boat running from San Diego to the Coronados for the day must carry,
which is the single most common Mexican-water trip in the KB.
