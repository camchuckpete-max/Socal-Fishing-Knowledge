# International Artificial Reef

The southernmost built-structure zone on the US side of the Bight: **seven
charted waypoints** in the spot library (cameron) — International Reef A–F plus
the International Reef Missile Tower — packed into about a quarter of a minute
of latitude off the Imperial Beach stretch of coast. It is one of the three
artificial-reef zones of the [Bight](socal-bight.md), and the program that
applies to it is the SoCal artificial-reef program: anchor up, build a chum
line, and fish light line over the structure
([chunking](../techniques/chunking.md)).

**No corpus source names this reef on camera.** Everything below the
coordinates is the reef-system doctrine applied to a zone the corpus does not
otherwise describe, which is why the confidence here is `medium` and why the
zone-specific gaps are flagged rather than filled.

## Getting there

Artificial reefs generally sit in **shallow water close to the harbors**, which
is what makes them a short run and also why they have been fished hard for
years (`f4qYtHACGyk`).

**Where it sits in the library** (cameron). The zone's section falls between
Imperial Beach and the Coronado Islands, and the coordinates place it offshore
of the Imperial Beach structure rather than beyond it: the cluster spans
**32°32.495'N to 32°32.717'N**, inside the latitude span of the Imperial Beach
waypoints (32°32.300'N to 32°34.500'N), but at **117°14.783'W to
117°14.900'W** — west of every Imperial Beach waypoint, which run
117°09.000'W to 117°12.000'W. The northernmost Coronado Islands waypoint,
Pukey Point, lies about **6'** of latitude south at 32°26.850'N.

**The border is the open question here.** The region page calls this cluster a
sub-pixel cluster on the border (cameron), and the zone's name says the same,
but no corpus source states where the maritime boundary runs relative to these
seven positions. A day worked here is a day worked next to a jurisdiction line:
south of it, seasons, limits and licensing are CONAPESCA/INM and the paperwork
is [Mexican waters](mexican-waters.md), not CDFW. **Boundary compliance is
positional, not depth-based** — load the line into a chart app and check
position against it in real time rather than trusting the sounder or dead
reckoning ([US waters](us-waters.md), `iAr6sbRC384`).

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: the launch ramp or harbor this reef is run

**Regulatory — United States. Jurisdiction: California / CDFW for licences,
seasons, limits and closures. Checked as of 2026-08. Verify current rules,
closure boundaries and the position of the international boundary before
fishing; for anything south of the line see
[Mexican waters](mexican-waters.md).**

## Structure & bathymetry

**Built structure, not natural bottom.** The SoCal artificial-reef system was
built specifically for sport fishermen in the **1950s–70s**, funded by
sportfishing money rather than by a commercial-fishing program
(`bM2vUS1B-yQ`). What that buys is fish-holding relief in shallow water within
reach of the harbors, and because the reefs have been fished for years the fish
still on them are the ones most anglers fail to catch rather than the ones that
are not there (`f4qYtHACGyk`).

**The cluster** (cameron). Six lettered drops and the missile tower:

- **International Reef A–F** — six waypoints (cameron) inside **0.150'** of
  latitude (32°32.567'N at F to 32°32.717'N at E) and **0.117'** of longitude
  (117°14.900'W at B in to 117°14.783'W at F). B sits about **0.015'** of
  longitude west of A at essentially the same latitude, so the letters are
  drops on one piece of built structure rather than separate reefs.
- **International Reef Missile Tower** — **32°32.495'N 117°14.790'W**, about
  **0.072'** of latitude south of F and the southernmost position in the zone.

**One water cell, many drops.** The library names **International Reef A–F** as
a **sub-pixel cluster**: collapse it to about one MUR pixel for modelling
(SST/chlorophyll lookups) and keep every waypoint distinct for navigation
([zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md), cameron). The cluster line names A–F only;
the missile tower is charted in the same section and sits within the same
fraction of a minute, so a day plan pulling BightSST conditions for this zone
is pulling one cell, not seven.

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: reef material, relief height, footprint or
charted depth here, how the lettered drops differ from one another, and what
the International Reef Missile Tower actually is — a standing structure, a
wreck, or a charted obstruction.

## How it fishes

**Anchor, chum, fish light.** The zone's program is the anchored-chum program,
and the execution lives in [chunking](../techniques/chunking.md):

- **Build prey density, do not just feed.** Periodically add another handful of
  cut chunks to raise the prey-density level; past a certain density even fish
  that were not feeding come over and eat because they see the others doing it,
  which is what turns a slow pick into a frenzy (`f4qYtHACGyk`), stated as a
  general SoCal principle rather than a reef-only trick (`BdRX4b8Fo5w`). A
  private boat that cannot carry sportboat-scale bait gets the same effect from
  a chum tube (`JK2-cpaXkI8`).
- **Never chum off the stern.** At anchor the current runs down the *sides* of
  the boat, so hand chum goes as far up the side as you can throw it
  (`yKaHcxX46l4`), and a chum bucket is tied off at the bow by the anchor for
  the same reason (`xI9tPJFXbUM`, `bM2vUS1B-yQ`). Cut squid heads, tentacles
  and guts are the cheap chum source (`yKaHcxX46l4`).
- **Fish far lighter than instinct says.** The stated #1 mistake at artificial
  reefs is fishing way too heavy. A **No. 4–No. 6** hook (`bM2vUS1B-yQ`) tied
  straight to mono, a **1/16 oz** split shot 2–3 in above it by default,
  **1/8 oz** only if current demands it, **never heavier than 1/4 oz**, and a
  spaghetti-thin strip of bait rather than a wad (`f4qYtHACGyk`). Heavier puts
  the bait on the bottom, which draws shovelnose guitarfish and bat rays
  instead of the target species (`f4qYtHACGyk`). Gear class is light spinning
  or a small level-wind ([gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md)).
- For sheephead specifically the terminal detail is a
  [sliding sinker](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md) kept off the bottom — see
  the [sheephead](../species/sheephead.md) router.

**Anchoring is the zone-specific problem.** The program is an anchored one and
the zone is a jurisdiction line away, so where the boat ends up after it swings
is a legal question as well as a fishing one — set the anchor with the swing
circle checked against the boundary in the chart app
([US waters](us-waters.md)).

## Spots

Seven charted waypoints in one tight group (cameron). A lettered waypoint in an
artificial-reef series carries no fishing identity of its own and the library
already treats A–F as a single water cell, so the series — not the individual
letter — is the unit a day plan works with:

- **International Reef A–F** — the six lettered drops on the built structure.
- **International Reef Missile Tower** — a named feature rather than a letter,
  and the southernmost position in the zone.

Charted positions for all seven are in
[the spot library](../sources/spot-lists.md); no waypoint carries corpus detail
beyond its position. Whether a numbered/lettered reef series publishes as one
page per series or one page per waypoint is an open question awaiting Cameron
([escalations](../sources/escalations.md)), so the child list below stays
whatever the gazetteer phase builds.
