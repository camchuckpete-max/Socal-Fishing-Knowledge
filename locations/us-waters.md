# US waters

**The paperwork rung.** Not a fishing page — this is what you must carry, buy
or know to fish legally on the US side of the line, and what happens when you
are checked. One region sits under it, so in
practice US waters here means California: CDFW owns anything with a fish in it,
the USCG anything with a hull in it. How to fish belongs to the region and
zones below.

Note this rung is deliberately NOT region-gated: it applies to every region on
its side of the border by definition.

**Regulatory — United States. Jurisdiction: CDFW for licences, seasons, limits
and closures; USCG for vessel inspection and distress. Checked as of 2026-08.
Verify current requirements with CDFW before you fish — licence classes, bag
limits, closure boundaries and depth lines all move year to year.**

## Papers you need

1. **California fishing licence (CDFW).** Carried on your person, and checked
   before you ever reach the water — a sportboat crew asks for it at the
   gangway and an angler without one does not board (`LsFMBCa9DOQ`). Buy it
   ahead of the season rather than on the dock; a December tackle list for the
   season ahead puts the licence in the same breath as the jigs
   (`E9YtH56Dngo`).
2. **Your GO ID lives on that licence.** A **10-digit** number printed one line
   below the barcode (`Qs9oEsh3b_w`). It is the number that identifies gear you
   leave in the water — every hoop-net buoy must carry your name and GO ID
   (`b19_AJjYCok`), so the licence is a prerequisite for legal gear marking, not
   just for angling.
3. **Second documents stack on top of the licence.** Hoop-netting for lobster
   needs a **lobster report card** as well as the licence, filled out before you
   fish rather than after; the card fields, gear codes and the rest are on
   [California spiny lobster](../species/california-spiny-lobster.md).

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: licence classes and cost — annual versus
short-term, resident versus non-resident, the ocean-enhancement validation, and
where a licence is bought.

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: whether shore, pier and kayak fishing carry
different licence requirements from a boat.

## On the water

**Know the rules of the area before you leave the dock.** How many hooks you
may fish, how many rods, which species may be taken, which are regulated by
season, and the bag limit for each — a game warden writes the ticket whether or
not you knew the rule (`EGKesj7V64M`).

**Boundary compliance is positional, not depth-based.** A closure line runs at
different depths in different places along the same stretch of coast, so a boat
can be legal at one position and over the line at a similar depth a short
distance away; private boats have been boarded and cited for fishing the wrong
side of one. The fix is to load the boundary into a chart app and check
position against it in real time rather than trusting the sounder
(`iAr6sbRC384`). The depth figures themselves are rockfish-scoped and live with
their as-of dates on [rockfish & lingcod](../species/rockfish-lingcod.md).

**Hook counts are capped.** A jig fished in US waters runs **two hooks** — a
single assist on the nose and a single on the tail, so an assist *pair* on the
front alone already reaches the limit
([knife jigs](../lures/knife-jigs.md),
[slow-pitch jigging](../techniques/slow-pitch-jigging.md)).
⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: the issuing authority and as-of date behind
that two-hook cap, and whether it is a statewide rule or a fishery-specific
one.

**Distress response belongs to the Coast Guard.** Channel 16 is for hailing and
distress, and monitoring it all day hoping to be the boat that runs to a rescue
is not a private boat's job; the exception is an emergency visible in front of
you, which you help with directly (`9hEa3sGTh40`). Radio practice in general is
[fleet intelligence](../planning/fleet-intelligence.md).

**The boat you ride is inspected too.** A San Diego sportboat's annual
maintenance period is also its regulatory period — Coast Guard, American Bureau
of Shipping, FCC and fire-system checks (`shZCjX2-fkI`).

**Facts about one island or bank belong to that zone page, not here.** Navy
closure zones at San Clemente Island, MPA boundaries at Catalina and La Jolla,
and the Cow Cod Conservation Area are each a fact about a place. Several MPA
edges are carried in the spot labels themselves — `stay W of MPA at 118 29.300`
at Ship Rock, `stay north of MPA at 32 49.573` at Windansea
([spot lists](../sources/spot-lists.md)) — so the boundary travels with the
waypoint rather than with this page.

## Bringing fish home

Fish caught in US waters and landed at a US port cross no border, so there is
nothing to declare. Coming back the other way — fish caught
in Mexican water and carried north — is [Mexican waters](mexican-waters.md).

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: transport rules for fish caught in US waters
— whether a fish may be filleted at sea, what must stay attached for species
and size identification, and how a limit is counted in transit.

Bag limits, size minimums and seasons are set per species by CDFW, not by this
page: they are recorded on each species note with jurisdiction and an as-of
date, and indexed in
[regulatory claims](../sources/regulatory-claims.md).
