# Evidence — Cabo San Lucas

The observation layer behind [Cabo San Lucas](../cabo-san-lucas.md). One line
per observation. Observations never change the note's stated doctrine. Dates
are upload dates unless a trip date is stated on camera.

Two source groups carry this zone. Capt. Dave Hansen (Your Saltwater Guide) is
in `sources/source-registry.md` and films in Cabo regularly; the presenter is
not named on camera in these clips and is read from the channel's own
self-references and the registry's Cabo-charter continuity, and each clip
carries a subscription-site pitch that is capped `low`. StokedOnFishing is an
unregistered channel and its two-part Cabo trip is a sponsored production
(electronics, reels, the charter operator and a tackle shop all read as
sponsors), which caps its product claims `low` and the zone at `medium`.

## Getting there

- `Mwx5AAXNMvE` (StokedOnFishing, 2014-12-03, Cabo San Lucas): the trip reached
  the zone on a two-hour flight from John Wayne Airport, Orange County,
  California, with a cliffside hotel base and the fishing done off a chartered
  50 ft Hatteras; the closing tips segment told viewers to get to the bait
  boats early enough to get live bait, and to tip the captain and crew.
- `XH-Hrfet6To` (StokedOnFishing, 2014-12-02, Cabo San Lucas): the boat cleared
  the harbor, passed El Arco, and reached the fishing grounds shortly after —
  no distance or running time stated; the charter operator's own ad read in the
  same episode offered full-day, three-quarter-day, half-day and private
  multi-day packages (sponsor segment, `low`).

## Structure & bathymetry

- `dgauGbNxP84` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2021-01-28, Cabo San Lucas): the current
  break showed as a little whitewater ahead of the boat against a slick spot on
  the water, kicking out offshore; the crew of three watched both sides of it
  continuously and the fish kept popping up on one side or the other, wherever
  the bait was.
- `XH-Hrfet6To` (StokedOnFishing, 2014-12-02, offshore Cabo San Lucas): the
  water was called at about 85 °F with about 90 °F air, blue and "crystal," on
  a long swell with "no wind to speak of," read out mid-session; the same
  episode's pre-trip tackle-shop segment (Melton Tackle, Anaheim, California)
  gave the general Cabo pattern as flat in the morning and choppy in the
  afternoon as heat off the land mass drives wind. Both sit in one video and
  nothing else in the corpus checks either.

## What's there

- `ll7r4A6atno` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2021-01-19, Cabo San Lucas surf line):
  the beach troll for sierra produced no bite on the beach itself; the boat
  swung outside to working birds and an angler took a first-ever dorado on the
  same small hoochie. A whale and a calf rolled directly in front of the boat
  during the session. The presenter's running joke was that the wind comes up
  every time he unties the boat.
- `pX6mV3O0L_E` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2019-12-19 upload, dated November 2019
  on camera, Cabo San Lucas): dead ballyhoo trolled behind the boat produced
  multiple dorado hookups; the rigging narration was not captured in the
  transcript, so no leader, hook size or troll speed was logged.
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): only the **month** is on camera — "it's
  the middle of November" (@00:00:14). The **year comes from the title and the
  upload date**, not from the video, so "dated November 2019 on camera"
  overstates what the source anchors. The rigging note is right for a different
  reason than stated: the rigging demo is promised on camera ("we're gonna show
  you about the Riggin and the hook tie-in", @00:00:24) but falls inside a blank
  stretch of the manual captions, @00:00:42–@00:04:09.
- `XH-Hrfet6To` (StokedOnFishing, 2014-12-02, offshore Cabo San Lucas): a
  "little stripe marlin" rose to the trolled lures and was landed after a
  dropback; a sailfish was confirmed the same morning with no method shown; the
  crew reported being "four for four" on hookups by that point and were running
  toward open water hoping for tuna or blue marlin to make a five-species day,
  which is not confirmed on camera.
- `Mwx5AAXNMvE` (StokedOnFishing, 2014-12-03, offshore Cabo San Lucas): a
  striped marlin was fought to leader and released, the presenter stating the
  boat's practice is to "primarily release all the bill fish"; a big dorado came
  aboard on the preceding day of the same trip; two big blue marlin struck
  trolled lures and neither stayed hooked, the lures named as one heard as
  "Mean Joe" in green/black (asr-uncertain) and a red/black.
- `dgauGbNxP84` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2021-01-28, Cabo San Lucas): a school
  showed as a breezer on the break and the boat trolled straight through the
  middle of it; the charter angler hooked up on it, and the presenter's own
  fish that session was a skipjack, released.

## How it fishes

- `ll7r4A6atno` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2021-01-19, Cabo San Lucas surf line):
  the troll started at the beach in front of the petroglyph, hoochies dragged
  along the surf line at about 6 knots with rubber bands on the stern cleats
  holding them down in the water; white hoochies ran a single hook, the
  orange/pink ones a double, a per-color choice by the rigger rather than a
  stated rule; braided wire was used because single-strand was not available
  locally.
- `dgauGbNxP84` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2021-01-28, Cabo San Lucas): the stated
  rule on camera was to figure out where the break is and stay with it as much
  as possible — "once you find them don't leave them to go find them" — with
  the boat trolling back and forth, inside and outside, all day.
- `XH-Hrfet6To` (StokedOnFishing, 2014-12-02, offshore Cabo San Lucas): the
  program was described on camera as trolling with the sonar working and eyes
  out for birds and porpoise, on the reasoning that marlin, wahoo or tuna can
  all pop up; the pre-trip shop segment steered the trip to medium-to-larger
  lure heads rigged with a single big hook, on the tournament-scoring
  reasoning that a 400 lb fish can score as much as a 600-pounder (promotional
  segment, `low`).
- `Mwx5AAXNMvE` (StokedOnFishing, 2014-12-03, offshore Cabo San Lucas): the
  closing tips segment named the drop-back as "definitely the best bet," rigged
  with 80 lb "Soft Steel" Ultra monofilament (sponsor-named line) to a #9
  circle hook; a family group aboard fought their striped marlin from a fishing
  chair, which the presenter framed as help for kids and less-experienced
  anglers in rough seas.

## Spots

- `XH-Hrfet6To` (StokedOnFishing, 2014-12-02, Cabo San Lucas): El Arco, "the
  beautiful golden arch," named as the landmark passed on the way out of the
  harbor.
- `ll7r4A6atno` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2021-01-19, Cabo San Lucas): the beach
  fished is placed "right in front of the petroglyph" — the only named position
  in the zone, and it resolves to no charted place in the KB.

## Not this zone
