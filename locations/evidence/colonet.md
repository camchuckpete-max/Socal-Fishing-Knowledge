# Evidence — Colonet

The observation layer behind [Colonet](../colonet.md). One line per
observation. Observations never change the note's stated doctrine.

## Getting there

- `PRNMGpLj7Pw` (BDOutdoors, Steve Carson with Nate Winnicki, 2026-01-13,
  "Slow Pitch Explained | Long Range Report Ep.12", filmed dockside): the
  all-around SoCal/Baja slow-pitch rig is framed as what to carry "on an
  overnight boat out of the Channel Islands or a Colonet trip or even a longer
  trip where they take a little bit of a break and fish for rockfish" — the
  grouping that puts Colonet in the long-range travel tier rather than the
  overnight one; registered voice.
- `fgTmUq78ofQ` (Art Taylor and Mike, SearcherSportfishing via Turner's
  Outdoorsman, 2021-05-14): with the fleet working one area west of San Diego
  and two west of Ensenada, no boat had been down outside Colonet or San
  Quintín "in a while… because they haven't had to go that far"; registered
  channel.

## Structure & bathymetry

- `hteLeDIy9Qs` (SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 121 — Yo-yo
  fishing", 2020-10-15): three yo-yo jig sizes by depth — 6X Jr at 60/80/100
  ft, regular 6X (5–6 oz) at 120–150 ft, and a 7X heavy "if you're fishing real
  deep, which we do a lot of times in areas like Colonet, the ridge or Alijos,
  even Guadalupe," on the reasoning that more time in the zone on a straight
  up-and-down improves the odds; registered channel; asr-uncertain(Alijos
  rendered "alejos", Salas rendered "stylus/salis").
- `PRNMGpLj7Pw` (BDOutdoors, 2026-01-13): the slow-pitch leader stack — 25 ft
  of 50 lb braid plus 3 ft of 80 lb — is justified by "the pinnacles and rocks
  and whatnot that are going to be down there," with thin main braid breaking
  on a pinnacle, on a rig explicitly built for Colonet and Channel Islands
  bottom.

## What's there

- `YAKOv9bXKO0` (BDOutdoors, Steve Carson, 2026-02-05, "Yo-Yo Tips For Winter
  Yellows | Long Range Report Ep.13", filmed aboard the *Vagabond*): the whole
  episode is pitched at anglers going to Colonet the month after the taping,
  i.e. March 2026; most Colonet spots holding yellowtail are 120–180 ft,
  "sometimes as deep as 220, but almost always within that range"; the fish are
  very commonly about 30 ft off the bottom, "about five cranks off the bottom
  on almost all reels"; registered voice.
- `YAKOv9bXKO0` (BDOutdoors, 2026-02-05, Colonet): intermittent red-crab
  blooms — "specifically for Colonet, sometimes, and you kind of have to wait
  till you get there, you'll find a bloom of red crabs, and the yellowtail are
  just filling up on the red crabs," making red-crab jig colours the pick when
  it happens.
- `PRNMGpLj7Pw` (BDOutdoors, 2026-01-13, Colonet and the Channel Islands):
  "we are blessed with unbelievable productive rockfish grounds… if you drop a
  jig down, the very first fish that sees it is going to bite it," with big
  reds and big lingcod named on the tail hook; cow cod are present enough to be
  worth a regulatory warning.
- `fgTmUq78ofQ` (SearcherSportfishing, 2021-05-14): "there used to be a pretty
  good structure of water down there that, in the beginning when this all
  started, that's where that fish was at, and if that water structure is still
  there then I'm sure there's bluefin down there too — we just haven't had to
  go that far," said of the water outside Colonet and San Quintín; the same
  seminar's wider resident-stock reading is flagged single-source on
  [bluefin tuna](../../species/bluefin-tuna.md); registered channel.

## How it fishes

- `YAKOv9bXKO0` (BDOutdoors, 2026-02-05): the retrieve — drop to the bottom,
  rod under the left armpit with the tip down, wind back as fast as you can,
  and on the bite "don't set the hook like a TV fisherman… just wind, wind,
  keep winding" until drag actually pays out in the other direction, because
  "the fish is much more able to set the hook than you could possibly be";
  winding only 75–100 ft off the bottom before flipping out of gear and
  dropping back keeps the jig in the strike zone and is less tiring than
  winding to the top.
- `YAKOv9bXKO0` (BDOutdoors, 2026-02-05): on an anchored boat with the current
  running the same way past both rails, either side fishes — toss a few feet up
  current so the jig sinks down in front of you; yo-yo fishing tolerates an
  angled line, unlike bluefin jigging where the jig "has to be straight up and
  down… and if it's not, you're not fishing."
- `YAKOv9bXKO0` (BDOutdoors, 2026-02-05): jig and tackle picks for Colonet —
  Tady 4/0 in scrambled egg as the favourite, blue-and-white the other common
  colour, colour mattering little as long as the wind-back is fast; chrome
  avoided where bonito or barracuda are present because "they may get them
  first"; Salas 6X Junior when the fish are shallower or smaller; Salas 7X or a
  brass JRI when it is deep, windy or running hard, with the trade-off that
  heavier jigs are more tiring to wind fast; treble hooks unless tuna are
  around; Penn Fathom 40 Narrow Gen 2 two-speed (6:1 high, 50 in per turn,
  against 34 in per turn on the old yellowtail specials) on a 6'6" 30–80 lb
  Penn Carnage III West Coast rod, 80 lb braid with a 50 or 60 lb mono top
  shot, 50 lb the minimum for long range and 80 lb declined because it does not
  sink as well; sponsor-heavy (Penn) — mechanism and parameters qualify, model
  endorsements do not.
- `PRNMGpLj7Pw` (BDOutdoors, 2026-01-13): the 6 in grub tail on the rear hook
  turns an 8 in jig into a 14 in presentation so that small fish hesitate long
  enough for a better fish to come in or push them off it — purists call the
  tails cheating, and the counter-argument is exactly the productivity of the
  Colonet and Channel Islands grounds; a teaser above the jig instead is
  rejected because "the little teaser catches small fish"; the rig is 20 lb
  Berkley X9 braid (measured breaking 35–38 lb) on a Penn Fathom 15 extra
  narrow, thin diameter chosen so 300–500 g jigs sink straight to 500–600 ft,
  on a 350 g-max Penn Carnage III slow-pitch rod; a 280 g Williamson Kensaki is
  the favourite jig, rated by its user to about 500 ft and fished deeper than
  600 ft; sponsor-heavy (Penn, Berkley).
- `PRNMGpLj7Pw` (BDOutdoors, 2026-01-13, regulatory): cow cod is legal to catch
  in Mexican waters but cannot be brought back into California — "you're better
  off using your descending device, send that cow cod back down" — checked
  against the regulations shortly before the 2026-01-13 taping; and anglers
  fishing down at Colonet can use more hooks than the US two-hook maximum that
  applies once the California rockfish season opens in late spring, with no
  Mexican figure given.
