# Evidence — Coronados - 230 / 302 (Kidney Bank) / 226

Provenance for
[Coronados - 230 / 302 (Kidney Bank) / 226](../coronados-230-302-226.md).
**Every observation below names the 302, the 371, or the water this zone sits
in — none names the 226 or Coronados - 230.** Those two marks have no corpus
mention anywhere outside the coordinate library.

## Getting there

- `HzE4FHHcvTk` (BDOutdoors, 2022-08-18, SoCal Bight report): with paddy fish
  getting pressured close in, the boats fishing furthest from the dock did best
  — day-and-a-half boats down around Bonda Bank were consistent, while "most of
  the full day fleet is fishing the standard banks outside the Coronados and
  it's hit and miss, sometimes you get good scores other times they don't."
  Advice for private boaters running south was to expect a longer run and to
  watch for west wind coming home, which makes for a miserable ride back up the
  line. asr-uncertain(the islands are rendered "coronadoes"; "Bonda Bank" is
  the caption's form). No bank in this zone is named.

## Structure & bathymetry

- `Rf1HKJG-SDg` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2020-12-15, SoCal outer banks): the
  bank-naming convention used to read all four names in this zone — a bank is an
  isolated high spot rising out of deep water and takes the fathom depth of its
  shallowest point as its name, at six feet to a fathom. The segment's worked
  example is the 182; no mark in this zone is named in it.

## What's there

- `49joKHD7Umc` (SearcherSportfishing, 2020-06-03, San Diego offshore report
  segment): with the fleet still tied up, "there's still a ton of bluefin tuna
  out there and there are some 30 pound yellowfin tuna in the mix as well," the
  yellowfin read as the indicator that you are in the right area; the water was
  "still pretty close to home to San Diego — anywhere from the 302 bank down to
  the hidden bank has been a productive area that the fish have been now for a
  few months," with private boats getting bites on Yummy Flyers and fish up to
  300 lb. Registered channel; the Yummy Flyer is a branded product and the
  claim is a report rather than an endorsement.
  asr-uncertain("yummy fliers" for Yummee/Yummy Flyer; "hidden bank" is lower
  case and does not distinguish Hidden Bank from Upper Hidden Bank; "private
  voters" for private boaters).
- `yMiBtZ7k8-w` (BDOutdoors, 2021-02-04, deep-drop swordfishing seminar): the
  302 listed among the banks the presenters usually fish for daytime swordfish —
  "the ridge or the 9 or the 178 or the 302 or whatever, or even the 43" — with
  a down-and-in drift preferred along the contour edges the fish dwell and feed
  at. Sponsor-presented seminar (Costa); the mechanism and the spot list are
  fishery content, not a product claim.
  asr-uncertain(the caption renders 302 as "three or two").

## How it fishes

- `Blh2BA-7Ono` (BDOutdoors, 2022-07-21, SoCal Bight report): bluefin below San
  Clemente were framed as one search area — "that's a big area if you don't know
  where they are, it's not like running to the 302 or 371, or even running up to
  the stuff off the west end of Cat" — the contrast that makes the 302 and the
  371 the KB's named examples of a compact spot read in 20–30 minutes, against a
  ~50–60 square-mile box that costs hours of looking.
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): neither time figure is in the
  transcript. "minute" and "hours" (of searching) appear nowhere in it; the only
  time guidance is "you should expect to spend quite a bit of time driving
  around" (@00:08:50). The area figure is the hedged, self-inconsistent caption
  "you get probably 50 miles 60 square miles to look" (@00:08:46) against "a 60
  mile square area" (@00:09:06). The quoted clause also names a third
  alternative the line drops — "or even running up to uh the stuff off the west
  end of cat" — so 302/371 is not a closed pair in the source.
