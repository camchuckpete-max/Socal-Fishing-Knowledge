# Evidence — 81 / 381

Provenance for [81 / 381](../81-381.md). **Exactly one observation in the corpus
names a mark in this zone, and it names the 381.** No source names the 81 or the
267 (The Condom); both are carried on their charted positions alone.

## What's there

- `OYOda6T3f-8` (BDOutdoors, 2022-10-20, SoCal Bight report): the bluefin that had
  been hard to find the previous week had slid out to Tanner and Cortez, and boats
  were working the water in between — "from the 381 off the east end back side of
  Clementi out to Tanner and Cortez" — all of it a slightly cooler zone, called
  more ideal for those bluefin. The Tanner end read as the lighter orange band on
  the temperature chart, 60-something degrees, the same shade as the back side of
  Santa Cruz. In the same segment the yellowfin were placed the other way, in the
  warmest water on the coast from the West End of San Clemente down through
  Mackerel Bank to the 181/182 Ridge, so the report separates the two species by
  water rather than by area. The 499 material from the same report is carried at
  [172 / 125](../172-125.md).

## How it fishes

- `OYOda6T3f-8` (BDOutdoors, 2022-10-20, SoCal Bight report): the fall regime is
  to look 100% at the temperature chart and the chlorophyll chart — bluefin on the
  cool side of a break when the water is warm and the warm side when it is cold —
  and across a big area with no differentiation in temperature the fish are most
  likely associating with structure like the banks, though an open-water school is
  possible anywhere in that water. For yellowfin the read is inverted: warmest
  water crossed against the cleanest chlorophyll.
- `Blh2BA-7Ono` (BDOutdoors, 2022-07-21, SoCal Bight report): the Bight's two
  search types are stated as a complex — one ~50–60 square-mile box worked with
  hours of looking — versus a compact spot that is idled onto, graded on the meter
  and either committed to or left. Used here only to size the corridor the October
  report describes; no mark in this zone is named on either side of that split.

## Structure & bathymetry

- `Rf1HKJG-SDg` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2020-12-15, SoCal outer banks): a bank is
  an isolated high spot rising out of otherwise deep water and is named for the
  fathom depth of its shallowest point, at six feet to a fathom. Used here to read
  the three numeric spot names, none of which the source itself names.
- `m-M0iwX8DjA` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2024-01-27, Bart Hall show seminar): the
  209, the 181 and the 267 are named together as high spots in the middle of the
  ocean lying at 267, 181 and 209 fathoms, in a passage about the sounder being a
  fathometer. The grouping is the San Diego local banks, so it does not attach to
  the 267 (The Condom) in this zone.
- `-bw1KDfDjv4` (StokedOnFishing, 2015-09-27, offshore San Diego): a pocket of
  warm water was read on the plotter "between the 14 M Bank, the 209, the 267,"
  with the surface temperature about 66–67 °F on the bank and shooting to 68 just
  off it. Same collision — this 267 is the San Diego mark, charted in the spot
  library as 279 / 267.

## Getting there

- `XyKF34C5iB4` (Time On The Water, 2025-04-19, saltwater bass seminar): San
  Clemente Island is a U.S. Navy training range rather than open coastline, with
  closures rotating on the Navy's schedule, and the crossing is described as
  roughly 60 miles straight off the beach. Carried here because the 81 is charted
  2.4 nm off the island's back side; the closure rule itself belongs to the
  island pages.
- `LTCFjqtSI8g` (Dirty Hookers, 2023-03-29, yellowtail arsenal): San Clemente
  Island sits at overnight range rather than being a full-day run, unlike the
  Coronado Islands, Channel Islands and Catalina.

## Adjacent water — not scoped to a mark in this zone

- `Z3rZqy2Pi8E` (BDOutdoors, 2022-11-24, SoCal Bight report for 2022-11-23): about
  8 mi off San Clemente Island a 63 → 61.5 °F break inside roughly 1 mi carried
  sign on the edge, with the fish deep and sonar-dependent. No direction off the
  island and no charted mark is given, so it brackets this zone by distance only.
- `HzE4FHHcvTk` (BDOutdoors, 2022-08-18, SoCal Bight report): the best kelp-paddy
  water in the Bight is south of San Clemente Island and Catalina and along the
  coastal strip from Dana Point to Oceanside and southward, thinning north of Dana
  Point. This zone is charted inside that named band; the source names no paddy
  over any of its three marks.
