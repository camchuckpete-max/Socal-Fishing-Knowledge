# Evidence — Santa Barbara Island

The observation layer behind
[Santa Barbara Island](../santa-barbara-island.md). One line per observation.
Observations never change the note's stated doctrine.

**Naming note.** The auto-captions render Osborn Bank as "the osor" and "the oor
bank" (`HueC1KHrcVw`, `i3qIAHW-SJc`) and as "the Osborne" in the episode summary
(`i3qIAHW-SJc`); the spot library's spelling — Osborn Bank — is the one carried
on the main note. Both readings point at the same bank "up off Santa Barbara
Island."

## Getting there

- `PKf7G3uL4io` (StokedOnFishing, 2014-11-11 upload, Santa Barbara Island): the
  tournament boat that left Two Harbors, Catalina "got out here in about an hour
  and 15, 20 minutes," arriving while it was still dark to start on the beach;
  the same check-in called the day's weather beautiful with the swell "really
  laid down from yesterday."
- `SGbynqaiHdY` (StokedOnFishing, 2017-07-18 upload, Santa Barbara Island): the
  85 ft El Dorado out of Long Beach Sport Fishing ran out on a report of "some
  yellows out there," with the stated plan to "get there in the dark, get
  anchored up and be sitting on the numbers"; the captain's reason for not
  trusting the reported mid-morning bite — "the boat that tells me it's been a
  mid-morning bite is the boat that gets there mid-morning."
- `IATPg9110CE` (StokedOnFishing, 2014-11-11 upload, Santa Barbara Island): the
  island was "all blown out" on day one of the tournament and fished on day two
  "with the swell coming down."
- `CMQkHQMxbXM` (BDOutdoors, 2022-08-03, Bight): a forecast wind event was
  called to blow out the Channel Islands, San Clemente, San Nicolas, Santa
  Barbara Island and the tuna zone off Catalina together.
- `mDmbGdQAy-4` (StokedOnFishing, 2025-10-06 upload, NE of Santa Barbara
  Island): leaving the bait stop, the run to the zone "just northeast of Santa
  Barbara Island" was called a 45-minute ride on triple 350s cruising at 40 kt,
  with the plan to work south from there because "that's where they were
  yesterday"; the port was not named on camera.
- `LTCFjqtSI8g` (jig-stick top-shot rule): Santa Barbara Island is grouped with
  San Clemente Island as an overnight-range destination taking 50 lb top shot,
  against 40 lb for the full-day-accessible Coronado, Channel and Catalina
  islands.

## Structure & bathymetry

- `SGbynqaiHdY` (StokedOnFishing, 2017-07-18 upload, Santa Barbara Island): the
  anchoring spot had "102 ft underneath," "a little kelp on and a ridge it comes
  off the island," with red crab up the starboard side; mid-session the boat
  repositioned to the back — weather — side of the island, "out here on the
  outer edge with our anchor down and 20 fathoms," with meter marks behind the
  boat, because conditions had changed and the lines were no longer laying into
  the kelp the way they had for boats the day before.
- `EU_Dod4wfYw` (StokedOnFishing w/ Capt. Benny Florentino, 2012-03-22, Santa
  Barbara Island): kelp present at the spot, with the crew remarking there had
  been noticeably more kelp on a prior visit; audio quality is poor and no
  further structure detail could be reliably extracted.
- `IATPg9110CE` (StokedOnFishing, 2014-11-11 upload, Santa Barbara Island): fish
  worked "all the way up against that rock" and through a narrow gap between two
  rocks; day one at the island had produced only "a little stretch of beach that
  was biting."
- `PKf7G3uL4io` (StokedOnFishing, 2014-11-11 upload, Santa Barbara Island): day
  two fished right up into skinny water — fish "in the foam," in inches of water
  off the beach — on long casts down the beach.
- `i3qIAHW-SJc` (StokedOnFishing, 2016-08-08, Osborn Bank): "we're on the [Osborn]
  bank, believe it or not, up off Santa Barbara Island"; 70 °F right at the bank
  edge about a mile and a half off, 72 °F where the boat sat, against 74–75 °F
  at Cortez Bank the day before, with yellowtail and yellowfin southeast of the
  bank "where the water warms up a little bit."
- cameron (spot library): the seven island spots sit inside a box roughly
  1.9 nm east–west by 1.4 nm north–south computed from the library's charted
  positions; Osborn Bank, catalogued under the library's offshore-banks section,
  is 6.1 nm from the zone centre and 7.0 nm from the island group's own centre;
  Arch Reef is logged "caution — boilers in big swell," Three Sisters "caution —
  boilers," and the Cave Canyon WSB zone entry carries "stay N of MPA at
  33 28.500."

## What's there

- `EU_Dod4wfYw` (StokedOnFishing w/ Capt. Benny Florentino, 2012-03-22, Santa
  Barbara Island): a wide-open calico bite — multiple quality fish landed in
  quick succession, including one estimated at 4½–5 lb; no current, water-colour
  or tide detail given beyond the kelp presence; unregistered channel.
- `IATPg9110CE` (StokedOnFishing, 2014-11-11 upload, event ~early November 2014,
  Santa Barbara Island): day two of the fourth annual California Offshore
  Challenge (weigh-ins at Two Harbors, Catalina) — the crew that fished the
  island took a five-fish limit worth 24 lb, for a 47 lb two-day total that won
  the tournament; their day one had run to San Nicolas Island, fished about
  three hours until the bite died, then run back to Santa Barbara Island, where
  only a small beach bite was found because the island was blown out;
  unregistered channel.
- `PKf7G3uL4io` (StokedOnFishing, 2014-11-11 upload, Santa Barbara Island): the
  same tournament, day two — one team ran to the island while the other stayed
  at Catalina; both teams hooked calico early after settling into their morning
  spots; unregistered channel.
- `SGbynqaiHdY` (StokedOnFishing, 2017-07-18 upload, Santa Barbara Island): the
  charter ran on a report of yellows, with the captain noting yellowtail "tend
  to swim around the corner here every once in a while" and rockfish named
  pre-trip as the fallback plan; the session produced a heavy red/vermilion
  rockfish bite on live squid; a late fish the crew rated above the rockfish
  came on a captioned "squish jig" — read as a possible squid-pattern jig, but
  the species was never named, asr-uncertain(jig name); the pre-trip segment
  named the destination "St Clemente Island," at odds with the SBI location
  shown for the rest of the trip — flagged, not reconciled; unregistered
  channel.
- `5p6gu14ZC4w` (BDOutdoors, 2022-10-26, San Nicolas / Santa Barbara Island):
  "really not a lot of coverage — Nick, SBI, that area up there"; "I'm sure it's
  good rock fishing," a standing yellowtail chance, and squid around at several
  islands and on the Long Beach barge — "you take a squid [to] any of these
  islands you'll probably have a good chance to catching the yellowtail," with
  the advice to call the barge ahead.
- `5to3Q5P7w90` (BDOutdoors, 2022-09-14, Bight): the volume of bluefin sat in
  the square drawn between Santa Barbara Island, San Nicolas, San Clemente and
  Catalina, "where a lot of the bigger ones are."
- `XLVUhV8DW64` (BDOutdoors, 2022-10-12, Bight): the bluefin that had been off
  the West End of Catalina, west into Clemente, "at the Nick and SBI" dried up
  and moved down off the east end back side of Clemente.
- `OYOda6T3f-8` (BDOutdoors, 2022-10-20, Bight): the bluefin zone "tracks down
  by Santa Barbara Island," with a boat picking one up there while fishing
  yellows, and the warm water running on down the back side of Catalina and
  hooking around to Clemente and the 499.
- `HueC1KHrcVw` (StokedOnFishing, 2016-08-08, Top Gun 80, part 1): the plan for
  the following days was Cortez Bank and then possibly "up above Santa Barbara
  Island" at Osborn, because the bluefin were moving up with the colder water
  and wanted to stay in 70–72 °F water — against 81 °F water offshore the day
  before.
- `i3qIAHW-SJc` (StokedOnFishing, 2016-08-08, Osborn Bank): early-graylight
  bluefin starting about 4:30–5:00 a.m. and building, with yellowtail and
  yellowfin southeast of the bank in the warmer water.
- `usHl-4SfqDA` (StokedOnFishing, 2024-11-13, NE of Santa Barbara Island): the
  zone worked was "just northeast of Santa Barbara Island," then south from
  there; spreader bars were pulled to cover water while flying fish and the kite
  were rigged.
- `mDmbGdQAy-4` (StokedOnFishing, 2025-10-06 upload, NE of Santa Barbara
  Island): foamers all around the boat did not convert — a trolled spreader-bar
  pass and cast plugs both went scoreless — with the crew's read that the fish
  were locked onto saury, anchovy and microbait; going back to a dead flying
  fish under the kite is what converted; unregistered channel.

## How it fishes

- `SGbynqaiHdY` (StokedOnFishing, 2017-07-18 upload, Santa Barbara Island):
  pre-trip rig call for the rockfish fallback — half-pound slider, 30 lb test
  (25 lb "if you want to go a little lighter"), 2/0 to 5/0 hooks; on the water,
  live squid on deliberately light sinkers "letting them sink down in the
  current," with the captain hoping for "a little bit of tide."
- `IATPg9110CE` (StokedOnFishing, 2014-11-11 upload, Santa Barbara Island): the
  plastic was cast in, allowed to sink close to bottom, then wound on a real
  slow roll right along the bottom with periodic stops to let it fall — bites
  came as it fell after the pause; anglers discussed 8 to 8½ ft rods casting
  farther into structure but wearing you down, against a 7 ft rod with "tons of
  leverage" for pulling fish out of rock and kelp.
- `PKf7G3uL4io` (StokedOnFishing, 2014-11-11 upload, Santa Barbara Island): day
  two's retrieve was called out as "just like yesterday" — a slow crawl,
  "creeping it across the bottom," while working to stay out of the structure.
- `i3qIAHW-SJc` (StokedOnFishing, 2016-08-08, Osborn Bank): one morning fish
  came on a sardine with no sinker at all — thrown out, held away from the boat
  with few anglers on the rail and left to "wiggle until one climbs on"; the
  dropper loop produced repeatedly on the same stop; sardine fished down 60–70
  ft "on the sink" produced bluefin; later in the morning the fish were "coming
  down a little bit deeper underneath the boat."
- `mDmbGdQAy-4`, `usHl-4SfqDA` (StokedOnFishing, NE of Santa Barbara Island):
  the fight style on this ground is the beltless, rail-locked method this
  operation names "railroading," with the kite hookup's slack closed by punching
  the throttle on the bite.
