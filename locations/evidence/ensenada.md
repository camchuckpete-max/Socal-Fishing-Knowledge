# Evidence — Ensenada

The observation layer behind [Ensenada](../ensenada.md). One line per
observation. Observations never change the note's stated doctrine.

## Getting there

- `xIUKmH9ccgQ` (StokedOnFishing, 2012-08-10, Cedros Outdoor Adventures
  charter): an earlier Cedros access route ran a small-plane charter out of
  Ensenada with the Mexican fishing permits obtained in Tijuana; unregistered
  channel.
- `U1AgwmlY5bI` (StokedOnFishing, upload 2024-08-14, Baja Blue Fin Tuna
  Tournament out of the Ensenada area): the day's total run was called out as
  roughly 180 mi with about 40 mi still to get in; the crew stood 4th among
  roughly 35 boats afterward; unregistered channel.

## Structure & bathymetry

- `CKq0Z6ExVs4` (StokedOnFishing, upload 2017-11-25, "Navico Weekend Getaway,"
  a high spot roughly 7–8 mi from the Hotel Coral marina, Ensenada; same annual
  Navico supplier-conference trip series as `0bcDBGzQnGw`, an earlier year): the
  spot was described on camera as coming up to about 25 ft from roughly 200 ft;
  unregistered channel.
- `0dIwWiOc1NY` (StokedOnFishing, upload 2026-02-17, "What It's Like Fishing in
  Ensenada, Mexico," guide Alejandro, a local full-time commercial fisherman):
  after the yellowtail stop went quiet the crew slid out to where "the bottom
  drops off to about 300 ft" over rocky bottom as read on the sounder;
  unregistered channel.

## What's there

- `0dIwWiOc1NY`: barracuda, bonito and yellowtail were named as the standing
  inshore mix; the down-coast point/reef stop ran a couple of hours for no
  yellowtail and produced one calico bass — kept, and called "one of the best
  eating fish in the ocean" — and a small bonito; the 300 ft stop produced
  vermilion (called "red snapper" on camera as a local nickname, then
  self-corrected to rockfish), a bocaccio ("salmon grouper") and multiple
  lingcod, with one angler noting lingcod colour varies fish to fish, plus two
  more bonito on slow-pitch jigs; a "regular size" sand bass came incidentally
  at the bait stop just outside the marina; the guide relayed that yellowfin had
  been showing roughly 30–40 mi out that morning, and the crew chose the closer
  structure over the run — local intel, not a landed-fish report; unregistered
  channel.
- `CKq0Z6ExVs4`: the high-spot stop produced multiple lingcod (handled carefully
  for their teeth), a fish the crew called a "rock bass" and then nicknamed a
  "tree fish," a whitefish as the first fish of the day, a sheephead a crew
  member identified on camera as a female, and at least one yellowtail — "nice
  yellow," "a bigger one" — on 20 lb line, no weight given; barracuda and
  yellowtail were named as the expected species for the spot; unregistered
  channel.
- `Ix0gG0-l3v0` (StokedOnFishing, upload 2024-08-21, 2nd annual Baja Blue Fin
  Tuna Tournament, Ensenada): a tough two-day bite on a bank roughly 42–43 mi
  from the dock — across an estimated ~40 boats, 3 boats landed fish on day one
  at roughly 30 lb class, with tournament director Manuel Hernandez reporting 4
  fish across the fleet on day one and 7 on day two; unregistered channel.
- `Blh2BA-7Ono` (BDOutdoors, 2022-07-21, report): new waves of bluefin were
  described as arriving "out of the west on the moon" into the Ensenada zone.
- `fgTmUq78ofQ` (Art Taylor and Mike, SearcherSportfishing via Turner's
  Outdoorsman, 2021-05-14): as of the seminar the fleet was working three
  areas — one west of San Diego and two west of Ensenada — with nothing running
  to Colonet or San Quintín because the closer grounds were producing;
  registered channel.
- `0bcDBGzQnGw` (StokedOnFishing, upload 2018-12-17, "Fishing In Baja with
  Navico & Tito Ortiz, Part 1"; footage from the annual September Navico
  supplier-conference trip out of Hotel Coral, Ensenada — the fishing year is
  not stated on camera beyond "every September"): the offshore search produced a
  floating object/school marker (captioned "Cal Pal" — asr-uncertain, not
  carried as a term) about 35 mi from Ensenada with skipjack and yellowfin sign
  nearby and a multi-angler yellowtail bite, a large dolphin/porpoise school
  that broke into a full foamer, and a striped marlin seen jumping right at the
  boat — whether the hookup that followed was the marlin or a separate fish is
  not confirmed on camera; unregistered channel.

## How it fishes

- `0dIwWiOc1NY`: mackerel were made just outside the marina during what is
  described on camera as late-spring/early-summer mackerel spawning, when the
  bait runs close to shore and spread out, giving two local types — a slower but
  tough "Spanish jack" and a smaller, mini-tuna-shaped, flashier and faster
  "greenback"; at the down-coast point the crew ran two presentations at once,
  slow-trolling a couple of live mackerel from the stern while casting jigs from
  the bow at the same structure, the stated logic being that the surface splashes
  pull fish into the area alongside the trolled bait; the crew attributed the
  blank on yellowtail to timing — the fish feed hardest in the gray light early
  in the morning, this stop was fished after that window, and the tide was called
  "off"; at the 300 ft stop the boat split between slow-pitch jigging and a
  classic dead-bait dropper rig (a couple of hooks above a weight, reeled up off
  the rocks and left to soak), both producing steadily; unregistered channel.
- `CKq0Z6ExVs4`: working the high spot on the drift, the captain repositioned
  mid-session after the boat's drift direction reversed from what the first pass
  had shown, resetting up-drift of the structure "to have a good window to...
  drop down" onto it again; unregistered channel.
- `0bcDBGzQnGw`: the stated day plan was to run roughly 20–30 miles due west from
  Ensenada to a temperature break, then continue north or west from the break
  based on sea conditions, with a fallback search 20–30 miles north; the crew
  deliberately worked away from the rest of the supplier-conference fleet, which
  they placed 45–50 miles out from the same base ("if we could find fish away
  from them, that's our goal"); trolling was used to locate — cedar plugs around
  a large dolphin/porpoise school, "the name of the game is finding life," after
  "a couple hours of desert" — and when the school broke into a full foamer the
  crew stopped trolling and worked flylined sardine plus chunked cut bait into
  it, losing "about three or four" before gaffing one; unregistered channel.
- `U1AgwmlY5bI`: with about 20 mi still to run to the start zone the crew trolled
  two Mad Macs staggered so they would not cross — one at roughly an 80 count,
  the shorter at roughly a 50 count — while glassing from the tower with
  gyro-stabilized binoculars; one zone was abandoned after about 45 minutes with
  no marks or surface sign; a 3-bar spread went out for the first time that trip
  once bait finally showed scattered rather than bunched; an unattributed
  on-camera comment framed the day's uncertainty as partly tide-driven
  ("sometimes they show up, sometimes they don't... has to do with the tide"),
  no mechanism given, logged as an attributed framing rather than a tested rule;
  the plan for the final day was to fly the kite and hold on the best sign;
  unregistered channel.
- `Ix0gG0-l3v0`: on day two the crew spotted a school breezing on the surface and
  ran a long spreader bar across them, but as they closed the fish showed again
  roughly 200 yd to the left of the trailing bar and did not connect; a kited
  flying fish later drew a swirl that would not commit; the boat landed no fish
  across the tournament despite kite and spreader-bar effort in a zone with
  confirmed sign; unregistered channel.

## Not this zone — a place claim that was retracted

- `Ob45hK4HSWc` (Jared Saaib, Strictly Irons, 2019-02-07): the offshore tuna-pen
  yellowtail session an earlier KB version filed under Ensenada is **not**
  established as being here. The transcript never names Ensenada, Punta Banda or
  the Coronado Islands; it gives only a garbled landing name, "two hours only
  from border," and yellowtail dropping straight down about 120 ft at the pens.
  The Ensenada attribution was an inference from a travel time and was retracted
  (cameron, 2026-08-17); both readings stay live on
  [yellowtail](../../species/yellowtail.md). Region gating is unaffected — every
  candidate is `baja-pacific-north` — so only the place-level attribution is
  open.
