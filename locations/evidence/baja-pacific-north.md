# Evidence — Baja Pacific — North

The observation layer behind
[Baja Pacific — North](../baja-pacific-north.md). One line per observation.
Observations never change the note's stated doctrine.

## The fishery

- `4pT0efBD1j0` (Strictly Irons, 2020-08-18, Coronado Islands): the crew's
  Coronados sessions ran 10–12 lb schoolie yellowtail that hit the surface
  iron "like a freight train," the program built around chumming a metered
  school up before casting; unregistered channel.
- `IdMJnlg_rZk` (Strictly Irons, upload 2024-10-08, Cedros Island; a four-day
  trip whose day 3 is dated on camera 2024-06-08, narrated by Jared Saaib —
  registry row `jared-saaib`): 30–45 lb yellowtail taken on the cast on
  surface iron in 60–68 ft over rock, with the crew losing roughly 10 jigs in
  two days to fish that rocked them; the take is slow rather than a freight
  train.
- `9kGpcEHqIUc` (Tackle Express, upload 2026-05-06, Cedros Island): a lighter
  Cedros grade — 19–33 lb, biggest weighed 33 lb — kept as a separate data
  point beside the 30–45 lb trip, not merged; unregistered channel.
- `L3tkGVu516A` (StokedOnFishing, 2014-11-13, Isla San Martin then Dono and
  Sacramento Reef): the crew checked San Martin first thing and passed on it
  because conditions "didn't look all that great," ran down the coast hunting
  bigger calico, and the first 3-hour skiff rotation produced personal bests
  of 7, 8 and 9 lb plus a 9.5 lb fish on the scale — a "10.2 lb" restatement
  in the same debrief is asr-uncertain; unregistered channel.
- `CKq0Z6ExVs4` (StokedOnFishing, upload 2017-11-25, a high spot ~7–8 mi from
  the Hotel Coral marina, Ensenada): the spot comes up to about 25 ft from
  roughly 200 ft; the captain reset up-drift after the drift direction
  reversed, and the stop produced lingcod, whitefish, sheephead and yellowtail
  on 20 lb; unregistered channel.
- `0dIwWiOc1NY` (StokedOnFishing, upload 2026-02-17, Ensenada, guide Alejandro,
  a local commercial fisherman): mackerel made just outside the marina during
  late-spring/early-summer spawning gave two local types — a slower but tough
  "Spanish jack" and a smaller, faster "greenback"; a down-coast point/reef
  stop worked slow-trolled mackerel and cast jigs together for yellowtail
  without one, and the boat then slid out to where the bottom drops to about
  300 ft over rock for slow-pitch and dropper work producing vermilion,
  bocaccio, lingcod and two bonito; unregistered channel.
- `RgtkbmBFUXI` (StokedOnFishing, 2012-03-27, Guadalupe Island): dropper-loop
  fish nicknamed "water buffalo" on a drift across a rock drop-off, with the
  angler putting his own loss rate at about eight of ten hookups lost to rock
  cutoffs even on heavy leader; unregistered channel.
- `ilINTeknKB4` (StokedOnFishing, upload 2015-10-29, San Benito Island, part 3
  of an 8-day Intrepid charter): a drift over 200 ft of jagged rock, the
  captain warning the rail to get sinkers and iron up immediately after
  touchdown or lose them; sponsor-heavy, unregistered channel.
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the place label is wrong — the drift
  is a lingcod/rockfish stop along the coast on the run north, after the
  Benitos yellowtail segment ("we're stopping along the coast to let them do
  some link cod fishing before we head back out and do some tuna fishing",
  06:22). Depth, "real Jagged" and the instruction are verbatim (07:47).
- `hteLeDIy9Qs` (Fred, Secret Fishing Rods, via SearcherSportfishing,
  2020-10-15, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 121"): three yo-yo jig sizes by depth
  band, with Colonet, "the ridge," Alijos and Guadalupe named as the deep tier
  calling for a 7X heavy — stated reasoning is more time in the strike zone on
  the straight up-and-down; registered channel.
- `ETw_3AFxEcM` (Steve Carson, SearcherSportfishing, 2022-07-14, Guadalupe
  Island): yellowfin graded 50–150 lb on the six-day trips, fished on the same
  flyline live-bait rig used for SoCal yellowtail work; unregistered-channel
  content type, medium confidence.

## Season shape

- `shZCjX2-fkI` (SearcherSportfishing, Art and Armando, 2021-01-14/15, "Tackle
  Talk Live: Bluefin Tuna"): bluefin framed as a cold-water fish and "around
  right now" in January 2021, referencing schools sighted near San Martin
  Island about ten days earlier in 63–65 °F water, used to justify starting the
  spring trip season earlier; registered channel.
- `Ix0gG0-l3v0` (StokedOnFishing, upload 2024-08-21, 2nd annual Baja Bluefin
  Tuna Tournament, Ensenada): a tough two-day bite on a bank roughly 42–43 mi
  from the dock — across an estimated ~40 boats, 3 boats landed fish on day
  one at roughly 30 lb class, the tournament director reporting 4 fish day one
  and 7 day two; a long spreader bar run at breezing fish and a kited flying
  fish both failed to connect; unregistered channel.
- `U1AgwmlY5bI` (StokedOnFishing, upload 2024-08-14, same Ensenada tournament):
  two Mad Macs staggered on the ~20 mi run to the start zone while glassing
  from the tower, a zone abandoned after ~45 minutes with no marks, a 3-bar
  spread deployed once bait showed scattered, and the day's total run called
  out as roughly 180 mi; unregistered channel.
- `0bcDBGzQnGw` (StokedOnFishing, upload 2018-12-17, offshore of Ensenada;
  footage from the annual September Navico supplier-conference trip out of
  Hotel Coral): the stated day plan was to run 20–30 mi due west to a
  temperature break then continue north or west by sea conditions, with a
  20–30 mi north fallback, working away from the conference fleet placed
  45–50 mi out; the search produced a floating object about 35 mi from
  Ensenada with skipjack and yellowfin sign, a dolphin school that broke into
  a full foamer, and a striped marlin jumping at the boat; unregistered
  channel.
- `Blh2BA-7Ono` (BDOutdoors, 2022-07-21, report): new waves of bluefin
  described as arriving "out of the west on the moon" into the Ensenada zone.
- `fgTmUq78ofQ` (Art Taylor and Mike, SearcherSportfishing via Turner's
  Outdoorsman, 2021-05-14): as of the seminar the fleet was working one area
  west of San Diego and two west of Ensenada, with nothing running to Colonet
  or San Quintín because the closer grounds were producing, and both captains
  expecting the historically productive structure down there to still hold
  fish; their resident-stock reading (Point Conception to Cedros, out to
  200–300 mi) is their own stated belief and is flagged single-source on the
  bluefin note; registered channel.
- `JaKSGkZ6CAc` (StokedOnFishing, aired 2015-01-18, San Benito Island): the
  yellowtail were out on squid sitting 30–40 fathoms deep and feeding off the
  bottom, the squid having moved out of the shallows when small mackerel took
  over, with fresh squid in nearly every fish boated; fish ran 30 lb and
  better on dropper loop and yo-yo iron; unregistered channel.
- `97clKtVsEOs` (StokedOnFishing, upload 2015-10-29, San Benito Island, part 2
  of the same 8-day charter): a surface-iron bite went quiet on the anchor
  while fish still marked, and getting above the marks and drifting over them
  brought it back; sponsor-heavy, unregistered channel.
- `WE643Fue1_A` (Dirty Hookers, 2022-09-14, Cedros Island): resident "home
  guard" yellowtail of 40+ lb, sometimes 30 lb, described as available in any
  month; unregistered channel.
- `wdbqTio1SQU` (StokedOnFishing, 2017-03-27, Cedros Island): North Point named
  as the spot to target for the bigger 30–40 lb grade; unregistered channel.
- `D5DR7Kx42_A` (BDOutdoors, 2023-01-12, Coronado Islands): the chain framed as
  the springtime yellowtail destination and called synonymous with yellowtail,
  its west coast exposed to colder Pacific currents and tidal swings and the
  water staying filled with bait.
- `YZtX1MiT0y8` (Erik Landesfeind, BDOutdoors, 2022-04-21, report, Coronado
  Islands): the Coronados back into a cycle where the boats down there daily
  are "seeing fish and they're not really biting," with a short weather or
  bite window each day when the yellowtail actually eat; registered channel.

## Zones

- `9tIp4n7q850` (StokedOnFishing, upload 2023-05-17, Cedros Island): a Cedros
  trip whose mixed bag ran halibut, dorado and bonito alongside the yellowtail
  program, with bonito incidental on an offshore troll; unregistered channel.
- `_r08B4bItAY` (Capt. Art, SearcherSportfishing, 2009-08-25, "Fall, 2009 on
  Searcher"): Guadalupe yellowtail sign placed at the island's north end ahead
  of the September five/six-day trips, with "always a chance to go to Cedros or
  San Benitos" on the same run.
- `Sx40JvCXFuA` (Capt. Art, SearcherSportfishing, 2009-08-25, season recap —
  read as the same interview as `_r08B4bItAY`, not an independent sighting):
  the operator's first-ever Guadalupe trip in 2009 produced yellowfin graded
  40–70 lb and yellowtail to 25 lb.

## Access & range

- `_r08B4bItAY` (Capt. Art, SearcherSportfishing, 2009-08-25): the run from San
  Diego given as 215 mi to Guadalupe's north end and 235 mi to the south end —
  a single day's run leaving ~11:00 AM and fishing by 9–10:00 AM at the north
  end or around noon at the south end.
- `_r_qKX_7080` (StokedOnFishing, 2014-11-13, "MC Swimbaits Skiff Trip part
  2"): Chester's Rock described as historically a good big-yellowtail spot at
  40–50 lb and about 300 mi from San Diego; unregistered channel.
- `xIUKmH9ccgQ` (StokedOnFishing, 2012-08-10, Cedros Outdoor Adventures
  charter): an earlier Cedros access route — small-plane charter out of
  Ensenada, with the Mexican fishing permits obtained in Tijuana; unregistered
  channel.
