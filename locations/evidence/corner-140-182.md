# Evidence — The Corner / 140 / 182

Provenance for [The Corner / 140 / 182](../corner-140-182.md). **Every
observation below names the 182 and nothing else in the zone.** No source in the
corpus names the 140 or the 166; The Corner has one probable mention, flagged
uncertain where it is used.

## Structure & bathymetry

- `Rf1HKJG-SDg` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2020-12-15, SoCal outer banks): the 182
  used as the worked example of the bank-naming convention — water 2,000–3,000 ft
  deep, then a spot that comes up to 182 fathoms and levels off again on the far
  side, that shallowest point being what earns the name; the same segment notes
  that places all over the ocean read 182 fathoms without being banks, lists
  "209, 181, 182, the 43" as banks, and separates Nine Mile Bank as
  distance-named (9 miles off the coast) with many closely spaced high spots of
  similar depth rather than one dominant peak.

## What's there

- `Blh2BA-7Ono` (BDOutdoors, 2022-07-21, SoCal Bight report): bluefin below San
  Clemente were not getting coverage, so the area was framed as one search — "the
  43, the 181, 182, 289, the entire Clemente Ridge, all the way up to Mackerel
  Bank" treated as one section of water, roughly 50–60 square miles to look at,
  explicitly contrasted with running to the 302, the 371 or the stuff off the
  West End of Catalina.
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): two hardenings in this line. The report
  says the fish below Clemente are "not getting **a lot of** coverage"
  (@00:08:15), not "not getting coverage"; and the area figure is a hedged,
  self-inconsistent caption — "you get **probably** 50 miles 60 square miles to
  look" (@00:08:46) against "a 60 mile square area" (@00:09:06) — so "roughly
  50–60 square miles" is a reconstruction. The quote itself also stops before the
  speaker's own qualifier that Mackerel Bank "is not below clementi but that's
  all one section of water the way i see it".
- `OYOda6T3f-8` (BDOutdoors, 2022-10-20, SoCal Bight report): from the West End
  of San Clemente down through Mackerel Bank to the 181/182 Ridge "down there
  towards the corner" was called the warmest water on the coast and where the
  yellowfin were, with bigger fish (50 lb class) coming on the front side of
  Clemente. asr-uncertain("the corner" is lowercase and unpunctuated while
  the proper nouns around it in the same sentence are capitalised; it may name
  the charted The Corner at 32°37.600'N 117°49.400'W or a generic corner of the
  Bight). The same sentence is carried
  without this clause in the yellowfin router and the San Clemente front-side
  zone page.
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the capitalisation evidence is weaker
  than stated — the same sentence renders Mackerel Bank as "**macrobank**", all
  lowercase and one word, between the two capitalised examples cited. The
  lowercase-and-unpunctuated reading of *the corner* stands; "the proper nouns
  around it in the same sentence are capitalised" does not. Separately, "bigger
  fish (50 lb class) coming on the front side of Clemente" is a grade claim about
  the front side, not about the warm band the yellowfin sentence describes.
- `yMiBtZ7k8-w` (BDOutdoors, 2021-02-04, deep-drop swordfishing seminar): working
  an SST/chlorophyll plot on camera, the presenter picked the 138 to fish and
  named the 182 next because it showed mixing water and heavy current flow, with
  the stated reason being that converging waters form at any edge, temperature or
  chlorophyll. This is the source behind the "138/182 area on break days" entry in
  the swordfish router.
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): two speakers, not one. Capt. Duane
  Diego picks the 138 ("yeah i'm going to fish right there on the the 138",
  @00:52:07); the 182 and the "mixing water / heavy current flow" read are host
  **Ali Hussaini's** line, asked as "that looks like a good place to check out
  wouldn't you say" (@00:52:14), which Diego then agrees with. The converging-waters-at-any-edge
  sentence is Diego's general answer, not his stated reason for the 182. The image
  is introduced only as an **SST** image, and the format is a Costa webinar
  discussing static screenshots, not a chart worked live.
