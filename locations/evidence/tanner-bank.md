# Evidence — Tanner Bank

Provenance for [Tanner Bank](../tanner-bank.md). **One source of the fifteen is
a registered channel** (`shZCjX2-fkI`, SearcherSportfishing); the rest are
unregistered, and **most of them are the same boat**: the 85 ft *El Dorado* out
of Long Beach Sportfishing, Captain TJ Will, runs an annual August charter that
accounts for `oXunQKSbc2g`, `fDSd9kqwYW0`, `c3NFkQbdDy0`, `947solNfiPw`,
`nQvJnfb5jQ4`, `eL1Qm33-Mj0` and `Y1xeieQI3B4`. Repeated figures across those
are **one boat's program, not independent corroboration.**

## Getting there

- `UfuiWFVvz2E` (StokedOnFishing, 2026-08-11 upload, trip framed as June, year
  not stated, Cortez Bank aboard the 80–85 ft *Constitution* out of Fisherman's
  Landing, San Diego, Captain Cameron — not this KB's `cameron`): 2.5-day
  limited-load plan stated as an overnight run to Cortez Bank ~110–115 mi from
  San Diego, anchor in 15–25 fathoms, yellowtail from sunup to ~11:00 a.m. or
  noon, then deeper water searching bluefin through the second night, boat
  turning south into Mexican waters ~3:00 a.m. on day two for Baja kelp paddies.
- `QSvzVHW9UMk` (StokedOnFishing, 2025-02-12 upload, part 2 of a 3-day Ranger 85
  charter, Cortez Bank): position given on camera as 96 mi SW of San Pedro,
  111 mi west of Point Loma/San Diego and 47 mi SW of San Clemente Island.
- `nQvJnfb5jQ4` (StokedOnFishing, 2025-04-30 upload, August 2024, Cortez Bank,
  85 ft *El Dorado*, Captain TJ Will, Long Beach): ~90 mi off Long Beach on a
  2.5-day charter for 25 people, anchoring among a crowded fleet — "northern
  boats… southern boats… middle boats," private and commercial sportboats
  together.
- `ntQXxcH5sjI` (StokedOnFishing, 2024-08-29 upload, mid-August 2024, Tanner
  Bank): multi-day trip out of Long Beach anchored roughly 90–100 mi west of
  Long Beach; the on-camera framing gives the figure for "Tanner and Cortez
  banks" together rather than for Tanner alone.
- `fDSd9kqwYW0` (Cortez Bank, El Dorado out of Long Beach): the run stated twice
  on camera as 110 mi — against the ~90 mi given for the same port-to-bank run
  on two other El Dorado trips (`nQvJnfb5jQ4`, `c3NFkQbdDy0`). Unresolved,
  queued in the fact-check ledger.
- `vCskOx6N-XM` (StokedOnFishing, 2022-03-23 upload, trip resolved to late
  October 2021, Tanner Bank): a day charter on Captain Andrew's own
  triple-outboard boat, distinct from the 85 ft sportboats — a group new to
  bluefin ran 2.5–3 hours out, the crew calling the return leg alone over 100 mi.
- `oXunQKSbc2g` (StokedOnFishing, 2019-12-11 upload, trip framed as October 2019,
  Cortez Bank, limited-load 2-day charter, 85 ft *El Dorado*): Cortez was the
  fallback destination, chosen on inside intel from a couple of boats that had
  fished it a few days prior. The earliest-dated El Dorado Cortez trip in the
  corpus.
- `947solNfiPw` (StokedOnFishing, 2023-09-20 upload, El Dorado series, Tanner
  Bank): a two-fish-per-person daily bluefin limit stated on camera.

## Structure & bathymetry

- `UfuiWFVvz2E` (June trip, Cortez Bank): anchored in 15–25 fathoms; later, with
  the tide coming in, the crew worked the bank's high spot and marked yellowtail
  at 60–80 ft in 120 ft of water.
- `ntQXxcH5sjI` (mid-August 2024, Tanner Bank): anchored in ~120 ft, bluefin
  taken off bait marks sitting around 60 ft while the boat chummed sardines.
- `947solNfiPw` (mid-August 2023, Tanner Bank): anchored on the Eastern side of
  the high spot.
- `nQvJnfb5jQ4` (August 2024, Cortez Bank): mid-trip the crew repositioned by
  reading where other boats sat relative to a high spot and a drop-off, then
  worked the 50–60 fathom edge of that drop-off, which produced "right out the
  gate."
- `fDSd9kqwYW0` (StokedOnFishing, 2023-02-22 upload, Cortez Bank, day-and-a-half
  charter, 85 ft *El Dorado*, Captain TJ, Long Beach Sportfishing): a morning
  drift up on a ridge on the edge of the bank in roughly 150 ft; later the boat
  anchored on the bank's far end near a buoy in the shallows. The buoy is
  unnamed.
- `zBd1mayUt_I` (StokedOnFishing, 2025-02-19 upload, part 3 of the Ranger 85
  charter, Cortez Bank): both a good drifting job and an anchor job produced
  that day, in water around 50 fathoms.
- `LsFMBCa9DOQ` (StokedOnFishing, 2025-02-05 upload, mid-August 2024, Cortez and
  Tanner Bank, 3-day charter out of H&M Landing, San Diego, Captain Jake
  Hensley): an overnight jig session read marks 360° around the boat from
  50 fathoms down to 80 fathoms, "even below."

## What's there

- `OYOda6T3f-8` (BDOutdoors, 2022-10-20, SoCal Bight report): bluefin that had
  been hard to find the previous week had slid out to Tanner and Cortez, boats
  working the corridor "from the 381 off the east end back side of Clementi out
  to Tanner and Cortez," all of it slightly cooler and called more ideal for
  those bluefin; the Tanner end read as the lighter orange band on the chart,
  60-something degrees, the same shade as the back side of Santa Cruz.
- `i3qIAHW-SJc` (2016-08-08, "Top Gun 80 Epic 5 Day, part 2," Cortez Bank →
  Osborn Bank; unregistered channel): day one at Cortez Bank fished 74–75 °F
  water for a mixed yellowtail / yellowfin / skipjack bite; the boat then ran to
  Osborn Bank near Santa Barbara Island "chasing some cooler water," finding
  70 °F at the bank edge about a mile and a half off and 72 °F where it sat,
  where the ~4:30–5:00 a.m. graylight bite was bluefin.
- `c3NFkQbdDy0` (StokedOnFishing, 2023-09-27 upload, trip landed mid-August 2023,
  ~90 mi off the California coastline, at anchor, 85 ft *El Dorado*, Captain TJ,
  Long Beach Sportfishing): a mixed bluefin-and-yellowtail bite at anchor,
  yellowtail called "Home Guard" fish, at least one bluefin called out at 40 lb,
  and one hooked fish identified live on camera as a "Tanner Bank yellow" on a
  trip otherwise logged as Cortez — the naming tension the zone page carries,
  flagged and unresolved.
- `947solNfiPw` (Tanner Bank): sardine bite from first light; crew reported
  bluefin up to 41 lb the previous day; day-one grade opened around 30 lb, one
  fish fought on 50 lb.
- `eL1Qm33-Mj0` (StokedOnFishing, 2025-03-31 upload, part 1 of the Cortez Bank
  series, El Dorado out of Long Beach): the school held outside the bank for
  several days, close to a week, with a further 10 to 14 days passing before the
  fish pushed in close enough for the boat to anchor directly on the bank. The
  opening bite was a "fly line day" — yellowtail, a 20–30 lb bluefin and an
  incidental sheephead — then a shift to iron throwing.
- `LsFMBCa9DOQ` (mid-August 2024, Cortez and Tanner Bank): the school's arrival
  was "a sudden switch the week before the trip" after fish had been scattered
  and sparse; distribution unusually wide — "not very common to just drive
  through 20 to 30 miles of fish," with a boat 15 miles away seeing the same
  thing rather than the typical isolated 4–5 sq mi patch; 50 to 100 fish days,
  anchor and drift both producing.
- `Y1xeieQI3B4` (StokedOnFishing, 2025-05-10, Cortez Bank, 85 ft *El Dorado*):
  on day two of a 2.5-day trip, after limits of bluefin for 23 passengers and a
  good stack of yellowtail, Captain TJ switched the whole boat to rockfishing to
  fill the bags — lingcod and a large ("absolutely Jurassic") grade of ocean
  whitefish, on gear described as 90–95% braid; crew stated the boat's daily
  allowance as 20 fish per angler, not stamped as current regulation.
- `fDSd9kqwYW0` (Cortez Bank): the ~150 ft ridge drift produced reds/vermilion
  rockfish alongside California sheephead and ocean whitefish; the crew reported
  filling a stated 10-fish rockfish allowance before moving into the shallows for
  more sheephead and whitefish.
- `oXunQKSbc2g` (October 2019, Cortez Bank): quality yellowtail on both yo-yo
  iron and surface iron, with large ("giant") bonito heavy as bycatch. No depth,
  current or specific-location detail survived the auto-captions.

## How it fishes

- `fDSd9kqwYW0` (Cortez Bank): the pre-trip call was to fish the bank as a
  flyline with the current — hooked sardine, 25 lb test, No. 2 hook, sardines
  running 6–8 in. The morning was slow on exotics with slack current — "we're
  missing the current… we need a little bit of current to kind of get our day
  here for the Exotics" — so the crew scratched rockfish while waiting, and the
  current finally moving produced a five-hour yellowtail bite that closed the
  charter. Yo-yo iron and surface iron named as working options.
- `UfuiWFVvz2E` (June trip, Cortez Bank): yellowtail worked on yo-yo jigs,
  surface irons and sinker rigs for a "larger grade Cortez yellowtail," fish
  graded 20–40 lb; 40 lb flyline, droppable to 30 lb if the fish are line-shy,
  with the caveat that Cortez yellowtail "swim straight down" into the rocks on
  lighter line; J hooks for yellowtail, circle hooks for bluefin. Night jig
  floor of 300 g, 250 g only for anglers savvy enough to fish it, over 80 lb
  braid and 130 lb mono or fluorocarbon; jig colour called close to irrelevant,
  white with polka dots if pressed.
- `nQvJnfb5jQ4` (August 2024, Cortez Bank): bluefin and yellowtail bit together
  on flylined sardines, 25 lb mono or fluorocarbon leader to a #2 circle hook; a
  kite-fished double-trouble two-sardine rig landed a big yellowtail — "they buy
  double trouble all the time"; 100 lb line was called easier for hooking the
  bigger fish being seen than 30 lb. During an evening "Sundowner" bite on the
  bigger grade the crew estimated they hooked about six and landed one on 40 lb
  with a tiny circle hook — "really tough," one loss after a long fight with the
  hook area looking like "a firecracker went off."
- `QSvzVHW9UMk` (Cortez Bank): a hot morning bluefin bite while adrift with the
  kite flown alongside bait rods, tally into the 20s; mid-morning the captain
  moved onto anchor after hearing a nearby boat was stacking fish "1 to two at a
  time or 5 to 10 at a time" on an established chum line, judging his own boat
  was picking off only "a little piece" of that school from outside the spot.
  With the current pulling down the left-hand side, the deck call was to spread
  along that side including from the bow rather than stacking the stern, and a
  yo-yo jig for yellowtail toward the bottom. Day's bluefin grade 20–30 lb,
  ~25 lb average, on 20 lb fluorocarbon to No. 4 and No. 2 hooks, with a caution
  that some No. 4s run light wire. Evening wrap-up ~6:00 p.m. on anchor: weather
  up, uphill current against a light wind — chop, but fishable; cumulative count
  about 96 after day one.
- `zBd1mayUt_I` (Cortez Bank, days two–three): a full-speed yo-yo iron bite at
  anchor with fish fighting "mean" on 60 lb and 50 lb line, one angler rocked on
  60 lb; when the anchor spot slowed the crew moved off to search rather than
  sit. A large flat oceanic slick with fish breaking "everywhere" shut off
  abruptly — "like a light switch" — and could not be relocated. An intense
  sundown bite then produced multiple bluefin over 100 lb on both the kite and a
  flyline in the same window, one angler crediting a break in the clouds sending
  a shaft of sunlight onto the water.
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): "over 100 lb" is the promo intro
  [00:00:23]; the deck recap of that window says only "a couple kite bites
  couple fly line on that better grade" [00:17:08], and the window falls after
  the boat leaves Cortez at an unnamed position.
- `LsFMBCa9DOQ` (Cortez and Tanner Bank, mid-August 2024): running out overnight,
  the boat started yo-yo and jig fishing around 1:00 a.m., that first session
  putting roughly half a dozen fish aboard in the 30 lb class. On a windy,
  hard-drifting night the standard 250–350 g jigs would not hold the zone and the
  boat stepped up to 500 g. One captain's read of the night bite ran roughly
  9:00 p.m. to 11:00 p.m. or midnight, a lull, then 3:30 a.m. to sunrise —
  single-source, one season, queued in the fact-check ledger. Asked circles vs.
  Js for the finicky bite fished on No. 4/No. 2 hooks, a crew member stated a
  personal preference for J hooks; a crew member also reinforced the freshness
  rule — "the guys that change baits often are the ones that are going to catch
  more fish."
- `947solNfiPw` (Tanner Bank): the bank's mixed grade was the trip's core tackle
  problem — "10 pounders all the way up to 80 pounders, maybe even 100" moving
  through on "one size line" — and matching hook, leader and weight without
  going under-gunned was called "a total game." One bluefin flylined on a No. 4
  circle hook with 40 lb line and leader plus a short piece of fluorocarbon
  (captions self-correct from "30 pound" — asr-uncertain(line class)).
- `ntQXxcH5sjI` (Tanner Bank): bluefin marked shallow around the boat while
  chumming, with no surface foamer showing, were answered with a yo-yo drop
  rather than waiting for a visual bite — a Salas 6X Jr in blue-and-white, 40 lb
  fluorocarbon leader to 65 lb braid on a uni-to-uni, dropped to about 80 ft with
  the bait marks sitting around 60 ft. The bite read as the rod suddenly going
  heavy, "almost like it got stuck on the bottom."
- `vCskOx6N-XM` (late October 2021, Tanner Bank): the program was to troll
  spreader bars to cover ground until the sounder marked good numbers, then stop
  and fish a dead flying fish under the kite on the drift — the captain's stated
  preference once fish are found, not a claim that trolling under-performs.
  Balloon-to-kite rigging: balloon twisted onto a dedicated clip with no knot,
  clip rubber-banded to the kite, with a taped length of line as a backup tether
  about three per kite. With a heavier-than-usual swell running, anglers new to
  bluefin were coached to kneel with the rod butt under the left armpit and time
  the crank to the swell, and to pace themselves. The triple outboards were
  trimmed up once a fish was fought up-and-down near the boat.
- `QSvzVHW9UMk` (Cortez Bank): a lure on a short ~3 ft 40 lb pink fluorocarbon
  top shot was cast out rather than dropped and worked back with a stop-and-go
  retrieve through the column at roughly 45°, called "sneaky."
- `eL1Qm33-Mj0` (Cortez Bank): anglers were coached to watch the direction they
  cast on a crowded bow — not toward or on top of a rail-mate already bent on a
  fish, since a lure landing in that fish's water costs the hooked angler his
  fish.
- `shZCjX2-fkI` (Captain Armando, SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Talk Live:
  Bluefin Tuna," 2021-01-14; registered channel): the **Tanner Bank data
  point** — referenced on camera as "last year," resolved to the 2020 season — a
  season with 40–60 lb bluefin at Tanner Bank where a smaller hook size got bit
  better than a larger one regardless of bait size. Stated as part of the
  general rule to go down in hook size rather than line class, because line too
  light to land the fish wastes the trip: "you're gonna flag a fish for two
  hours."
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the quotation is spliced — the audio
  runs "you're just not gonna land a fish that size" [00:18:40] into the
  fragment "flag a fish for two hours yeah" [00:18:51], and "you're gonna"
  prefixes neither. The attribution to Captain Armando is also unverifiable:
  the panel is host plus Captains Mike Todder and Armando Marquez [00:03:27]
  and the auto-captions carry no speaker labels.
- `Y1xeieQI3B4` (Cortez Bank, 2025-05-10): the boat ran an RSW fish hold at about
  36 °F that fish went into and stayed in until filleted, rather than being iced
  and pulled.
- `vCskOx6N-XM` (Tanner Bank): on the trip's last big bluefin the gaff shot
  contacted the fishing line during the stick — "I hit the line with the gaff, it
  came off" — but the fish was secured; once boated the line had not parted, the
  hook had pulled free, so the gaff connection was what kept the fish attached.
