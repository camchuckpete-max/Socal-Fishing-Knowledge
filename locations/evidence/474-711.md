# Evidence — 474 / 711

Provenance for [474 / 711](../474-711.md). **One source in the corpus names
anything in this zone, and it names the 711 — as a corner of a search block, not
as a mark anyone fished.** Nothing names the 474; it is carried on its charted
position alone. The remaining entries are the region-scale doctrine the zone page
reads against these two positions, not observations made on them.

## Lead / Structure & bathymetry

- `Blh2BA-7Ono` (BDOutdoors, 2022-07-21, SoCal Bight report): the water above San
  Clemente was drawn as a block "from cat to the osborne bank to nick to that
  7-eleven outside of clementi outside of the western clemente," called "another
  big area." The corner name is spoken, never tied to a charted position; the
  zone page identifies it as the 711 on the spot library's numbering and flags
  the identification as unstated. asr-uncertain(the second "clemente" renders as
  "climate" in the captions).
- `Rf1HKJG-SDg` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2020-12-15, SoCal outer banks): a bank is
  an isolated high spot rising out of otherwise deep water and is named for the
  fathom depth of its shallowest point, at six feet to a fathom; the exception is
  a mark named for its distance off the beach. The inshore mainland water from
  Dana Point through Newport and Marina del Rey out to Catalina is already
  2,000–3,000 ft deep, which is the featureless plain banks are contrasted
  against. Used here to read the names 474 and 711, neither of which the source
  names.
- `Blh2BA-7Ono` (BDOutdoors, 2022-07-21, SoCal Bight report): the split between a
  ~50–60 square-mile complex you plan hours of looking across and the compact
  spot you idle onto, grade and leave inside 20–30 minutes, with "the stuff off
  the west end of cat" given as a compact example. Neither mark in this zone is
  named in that source.

## What's there

- `Blh2BA-7Ono` (BDOutdoors, 2022-07-21, SoCal Bight report): bluefin that had
  been outside San Clemente the previous week slid in between San Clemente and
  Catalina, joined — on a theory the reporter credits to a friend, Jonathan — by
  fish that had been on the beach inside Catalina and slid up off the West End; a
  ton of boats including long-range boats were up there over the weekend "doing
  really well." Those fish then scattered or moved off the coverage, and the
  block above was drawn as where to look for them again.
- `Blh2BA-7Ono` (BDOutdoors, 2022-07-21, SoCal Bight report): boats fishing sea
  bass at San Nicolas or yellows at San Clemente could slide off the islands and
  add bluefin to the day; one trip took bluefin, sea bass, halibut and yellowtail
  on the same charter. The mixed bag is reported for the islands, not for these
  two marks.
- `5to3Q5P7w90` (BDOutdoors, 2022-09-15 upload, Bight report of 2022-09-14, SoCal
  Bight): the volume of fish was "from the santa barbara island to nick to
  clementi to kat[alina] … if you make draw a square in there that's where a lot
  of the bigger ones are," and the fish had been "pretty much the same place
  they've been for three weeks now … in between the islands on the [fathom]
  curves on the edges of the banks and ridges up there." No named mark is given
  inside the square. asr-uncertain("thalam curves" in the captions, read as
  fathom curves).
- `5to3Q5P7w90` (BDOutdoors, 2022-09-15 upload, Bight report of 2022-09-14, SoCal
  Bight): the September dorado were in open-water schools below Catalina, and the
  spread of dorado and bluefin ran from the east end of Catalina out to the east
  end of San Clemente and down Clemente Ridge to the 289, with more yellowfin the
  further south you went — the east side of the islands, away from this zone.
- `XLVUhV8DW64` (BDOutdoors, 2022-10-13 upload, Bight report of 2022-10-12, SoCal
  Bight): by mid-October the bluefin had left San Nicolas for the east end and
  back side of San Clemente, with Nick named as water they could already be back
  on without
  the coverage to know. Carried in full at
  [San Nicolas Island](../san-nicolas-island.md); the zone page uses it only to
  note that the straight line between those two islands passes through this
  water.
- `HzE4FHHcvTk` (BDOutdoors, 2022-08-18 upload, Bight report of 2022-08-17, SoCal
  Bight): the paddy-water split — best paddy water south of San Clemente Island
  and Catalina and along the coastal strip from Dana Point to Oceanside and
  southward, thinning north of Dana Point. Carried in full at
  [bight geography](../bight-geography.md); used here only to place these marks
  outside the named band, with no paddy observed over either.

## How it fishes

- `5to3Q5P7w90` (BDOutdoors, 2022-09-15 upload, Bight report of 2022-09-14, SoCal
  Bight): the bigger tuna that week were being caught at night, by four-pack
  boats, or by boats flying kites and trolling spreader bars and Mad Macs — "same
  stuff, same areas" as the volume fishing.
- `5to3Q5P7w90` (BDOutdoors, 2022-09-15 upload, Bight report of 2022-09-14, SoCal
  Bight): a charter out of Catalina's back side found early bluefin sign off the
  fathom curve, did not want to wait for the slack tide, moved off and finished
  on dorado in open water below the island. A slack-tide cost example, on
  Catalina rather than in this zone.
- `5to3Q5P7w90` (BDOutdoors, 2022-09-15 upload, Bight report of 2022-09-14, SoCal
  Bight): bluefin were seen foaming on the surface in roughly 76 °F water, the
  warmest the reporter had seen them do it, flagged on camera as unusual.
  Carried as doctrine in [September](../../seasonal/september.md).
- `Blh2BA-7Ono` (BDOutdoors, 2022-07-21, SoCal Bight report): the big-area search
  method — run bank to bank looking for colour changes, temperature changes and
  bird life, anything that looks different than it did before, then be back in
  the fishiest-looking area on the slack tide, specifically a late-morning slack
  after 09:30. Stated for the below-Clemente complex; the report calls the block
  this zone sits in "another big area" with fish that move 10–15 miles overnight,
  but never restates the method for it.
- `OYOda6T3f-8` (BDOutdoors, 2022-10-20, SoCal Bight report): the fall warm-band
  arc — backside Santa Cruz/Rosa to Santa Barbara Island to backside Catalina,
  hooking to the 499 and San Clemente, with the bluefin condensing outward toward
  Tanner and Cortez as the coast cools. Carried in full at
  [bight geography](../bight-geography.md); no mark in this zone is named on the
  arc.
