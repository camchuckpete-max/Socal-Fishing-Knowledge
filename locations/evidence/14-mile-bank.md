# Evidence — 14 Mile Bank

Provenance for [14 Mile Bank](../14-mile-bank.md). **Two dated days in the
corpus reach this bank on camera or in report**, one source uses it as the
worked example for approaching a bank, one names it in a swordfish rotation, and
one supplies the naming reading behind the bank's number.

## Structure & bathymetry

- `Rf1HKJG-SDg` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2020-12-15, SoCal outer banks): a bank is
  an isolated high spot rising out of otherwise deep water, named for the fathom
  depth of its shallowest point at six feet to a fathom — with the stated
  exceptions being distance-named. Nine Mile Bank is "nine miles off the beach"
  rather than nine fathoms deep, and "14 is 14 miles off the beach." This is the
  source of this bank having no depth claim at all.

## What's there

- `-bw1KDfDjv4` (StokedOnFishing, "Simrad Summer, part 1" — a summer trip, month
  not stated on camera; 2015-09-27 upload, running to "the 14 M bank" and later
  reporting from "offshore on the 209 area"; unregistered channel, sponsor-heavy
  Simrad/Okuma episode): the crew ran out looking for yellowtail, kelp paddies,
  bird activity, puddling fish and breezers. With the plotter's sea-surface-
  temperature readout in text mode over the chart they read about **66–67 °F** at
  the 14 Mile Bank, shooting to **68 °F** a little off it, and called the whole
  section "between the 14 M Bank, the 209, the 267" a pocket of nice warm water.
  Boiling bluefin showed ahead of the boat — explicitly **not** the 100-pounders
  being talked about in 2015, "that 30 to 40 lb fish," with a first grade out of
  the water at **40–60 lb** and smaller fish coming up behind. Later in the day
  they reported a kelp paddy, "probably the best one we've seen all day," and "a
  nice jig stop with some Bonita up to about 8 lb"; bluefin were spotted
  repeatedly and never cooperated. Yellowtail came off the paddy on light tackle,
  mixed in with the bonito. Same-day material is carried in
  [bonito](../../species/bonito.md), [flyline](../../techniques/flyline.md),
  [kelp paddies](../../conditions/kelp-paddies.md),
  [electronics and sounder](../../planning/electronics-and-sounder.md) and
  [fleet intelligence](../../planning/fleet-intelligence.md).
- `CMQkHQMxbXM` (BDOutdoors — Erik Landesfeind, SoCal Bight report, 2022-08-03):
  a private-boat day running Mackerel Bank → the 289 → "the 14" that found
  yellowfin and 60–80 lb bluefin in two areas; small spots of yellowfin up
  chasing bait and boiling late in the day refused surface lures, and wind chop
  then shut down spotting. Carried in
  [yellowfin tuna](../../species/yellowfin-tuna.md) as the parent's
  presence-is-not-catchability example.
- `yMiBtZ7k8-w` (BDOutdoors, 2021-02-04, San Diego offshore — Costa-sponsored
  deep-drop webinar): the swordfish rotation named on camera is the Ridge, the 9
  (9 Mile Bank), **the 14** (14 Mile Bank), the 43, the 178 and the 302, all
  fished on a preferred down-and-in drift along the contour edges; on a break day
  the 138 and the 182 are picked off the SST/chlorophyll plot for the mixing
  water and heavy current on them. No drift, depth or date is attached to this
  bank specifically. Full provenance in
  [swordfish evidence](../../species/evidence/swordfish.md).

## How it fishes

- `DnSqw4r7A1s` (BDOutdoors, 2022-07-14 SoCal Bight report, Erik Landesfeind on
  glassing): the worked example for approaching a bank is a stated plan to run
  out to the **14 mile bank**. Arriving, stop somewhere within about **two miles**
  of the **highest part** of the bank and stop the boat entirely if it is calm;
  if it is not, angle the boat for the calmest possible ride and barely leave it
  in gear at **one to one and a half miles an hour**. Then glass for an extended
  period, moving the binoculars a couple of inches at a time — **three or four
  minutes** to work all the way around the boat — looking for more than foamers.
  Pulled up on the **inside edge** of the 14 Mile Bank, the majority of the
  looking goes at the bank rather than at open water, because the chance of
  finding fish out in open water is "a little bit slimmer" than right on the
  bank. Running in open water with no relation to structure, any deviation from
  what he has been seeing — birds, bait, a scum line off a current break, a
  couple of paddies in a row, a temperature or colour break — stops the boat for
  a look around the immediate area. The general protocol built from this report
  is [search and glassing](../../planning/search-and-glassing.md).
- `-bw1KDfDjv4` (StokedOnFishing, 2015-09-27, this bank complex): the radar was
  run while searching so the crew could see where the other boats were — the
  stated payoff was finding a boat sitting on a kelp paddy "before he knows we're
  even looking at him." The same episode supplies the stated read that **not every
  school of fish you find is going to bite** — stay positive, move on and look for
  new fish or kelps — and the re-work rule that a paddy which already gave up a
  yellowtail is worth circling back to rather than abandoning after one fish. The
  paddy build was **25 lb** fluorocarbon to a **No. 2 or No. 3** J hook with
  nose-hooked sardines.

## Adjacent water — not scoped to this mark

- `HzE4FHHcvTk` (BDOutdoors, 2022-08-18 SoCal Bight report): the best kelp-paddy
  water in the Bight is south of San Clemente Island and Catalina and along the
  coastal strip from **Dana Point to Oceanside and southward**, thinning north of
  Dana Point. This bank is charted 3.5 minutes of latitude south of the Dana Point
  kelp buoy, and is not named in the source.
