# Evidence — The Slide / 152 / 277

Provenance for [The Slide / 152 / 277](../slide-152-277.md). **Two dated trips
in the corpus fish this ridge**, one on the 277 and the tanker lanes above it,
one on the 152. **No source in the corpus names the offshore Slide** — that mark
is carried on its charted position alone.

## Getting there

- `3qSY328fFYo` (StokedOnFishing, 2014-11-08, SoCal — Catalina tanker
  lanes/277 bank): the plotted track ran 19.2 miles at 193° from the home port
  to the bottom of the tanker lanes, about 17 miles out, where the jigs went in,
  then a further ~35-minute run toward the 277 "at the bottom of the ridge that
  runs off the East End of Catalina," hunting 68 °F water before dropping in.
  The port is never named on camera. Unregistered channel; sponsor-heavy
  (Simrad); presenter/angler Jimmy Decker.
- `HzE4FHHcvTk` (BDOutdoors, 2022-08-17, SoCal Bight report): a Balboa Angling
  Club lady-anglers tournament trip launched at Newport intending the back side
  of the 152 ridge or a run out to Mackerel Bank, aborted the outside run in a
  morning west wind, fished its way up to the 152 ridge instead, and ran back
  out toward Mackerel Bank once the wind laid down about 11:00.
  asr-uncertain(the destination bank is transcribed "macro bank"; read as
  Mackerel Bank from the surrounding report).

## Structure & bathymetry

- `3qSY328fFYo` (StokedOnFishing, 2014-11-08, SoCal — Catalina east end): the
  277 is described on camera as sitting at the bottom of the ridge that runs off
  the East End of Catalina — the only corpus statement of what the feature is.
  Unregistered channel.
- `HzE4FHHcvTk` (BDOutdoors, 2022-08-17, SoCal Bight report): the 152 is called
  "the 152 ridge" and its "back side" is named as a destination, which is the
  only indication the feature has sides; no side is defined.
- `Rf1HKJG-SDg` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2020-12-15, SoCal outer banks): a bank is
  an isolated high spot rising out of otherwise deep water, named for the fathom
  depth of its shallowest point at six feet to a fathom, and the mainland water
  from Dana Point through Newport and Marina del Rey out to Catalina is already
  2,000–3,000 ft deep. Used here to read the two numeric names in the zone; the
  source names neither mark.

## What's there

- `3qSY328fFYo` (StokedOnFishing, 2014-11-08, SoCal — tanker lanes/277 bank): a
  half-day trolling stop hooked and released a striped marlin estimated on
  camera at roughly 160 lb, buried in the corner of the mouth on a
  flying-fish-pattern swimmer and radioed in as released "on a flying fish"; the
  live-mackerel dropback was rigged and explained on camera as the answer to a
  fish that shows without eating a jig, not as what took this one. A large cloud
  of bait, identified as mackerel, was metered under the boat before the first
  drop. Unregistered channel.
- `HzE4FHHcvTk` (BDOutdoors, 2022-08-17, SoCal Bight report): a Mad Mac pulled
  at the 152 ridge drew two bites in about five minutes, neither of which stuck
  for more than a few seconds; the back hook had not been swapped out, and the
  fish were never identified beyond a guess at smaller tuna. Running back out
  toward Mackerel Bank a few miles past the 152 ridge, the boat found a big
  bluefin foamer.
- `HzE4FHHcvTk` (BDOutdoors, 2022-08-17, SoCal Bight report): there was a lot of
  bait on the 152 and toward the end of the shipping lanes, and the bait dried up
  on the way north-northwest to Avalon Bank.

## How it fishes

- `3qSY328fFYo` (StokedOnFishing, 2014-11-08, SoCal — tanker lanes/277 bank): a
  friend on another boat put this boat onto a good temp break in an area it had
  not been working; crossing it the boat started metering bait, trolled past a
  kelp paddy with the graph lit up with bait, and was bit within moments.
  Unregistered channel.
- `HzE4FHHcvTk` (BDOutdoors, 2022-08-17, SoCal Bight report): running from the
  152 toward Avalon Bank the marlin jigs went out on the edge where bait was
  still showing, which was the dirtier
  water — "not super dirty, just not that blue offshore water you would
  expect" — and trolling from there into the clear water where marlin would live,
  the bait disappeared; the presenter's read was that the bait was hanging in the
  off-colour water.
- `3qSY328fFYo` (StokedOnFishing, 2014-11-08, SoCal — tanker lanes/277 bank):
  target water for the stop was 68 °F, hunted before the jigs went in;
  mid-morning the boat crossed a break of about two-tenths of a degree and a
  nearby boat reported the bite on the colder side, which this boat then worked.
  The console sounder was set to display down to 120 ft so bait could be spotted
  under way. Unregistered channel.
- `3qSY328fFYo` (StokedOnFishing, 2014-11-08, SoCal — tanker lanes/277 bank):
  jig strikes were said to land about an hour before or after the tide change;
  the day's high was given as 7:30 and its low first as roughly 11:30–12:00 and
  later restated as 11:15. asr-uncertain(the first low-tide figure is garbled
  and is kept beside the restated one rather than reconciled).
