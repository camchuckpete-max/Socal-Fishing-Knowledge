# Evidence — 172 / 125

Provenance for [172 / 125](../172-125.md). **Every observation below names the
499 and nothing else in the zone.** No source in the corpus names the 172 or the
125; both are carried on their charted positions alone.

## Structure & bathymetry

- `Rf1HKJG-SDg` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2020-12-15, SoCal outer banks): a bank is
  an isolated high spot rising out of otherwise deep water and is named for the
  fathom depth of its shallowest point, at six feet to a fathom; the inshore
  mainland water from Dana Point through Newport and Marina del Rey out to
  Catalina is already 2,000–3,000 ft deep, which is the featureless bottom banks
  are contrasted against. Used here to read the three numeric spot names, none of
  which the source itself names.
- `Blh2BA-7Ono` (BDOutdoors, 2022-07-21, SoCal Bight report): the water above San
  Clemente was drawn as a block "from Cat to the Osborn Bank to Nick to that
  7-Eleven outside of Clemente," called another big area; the fish there were not
  holding hard to structure and could move 10–15 miles overnight, which is why
  boats that had been on them lost them. The block is carried in full on the
  [San Nicolas Island](../san-nicolas-island.md) page; this zone's three marks
  sit on its Catalina-to-Osborn and Catalina-to-711 legs.

## What's there

- `Blh2BA-7Ono` (BDOutdoors, 2022-07-21, SoCal Bight report): bluefin that had
  been outside San Clemente the previous week slid in between San Clemente and
  Catalina, joined — per a friend's read the presenter endorsed — by fish that
  had been on the beach inside Catalina and slid up off the West End. The
  movement was called predictable, and the 499 was named as the logical place
  for them to end up; boats went and looked there and caught them, with long-range
  boats on the fish over the weekend. In the same segment, boats fishing white
  seabass or yellowtail at San Nicolas and San Clemente could slide off the island
  and add bluefin, one trip reported taking bluefin, white seabass, halibut and
  yellowtail in a day. By the time of the report the bite had scattered again.
- `OYOda6T3f-8` (BDOutdoors, 2022-10-20, SoCal Bight report): the bluefin zone
  tracked down past Santa Barbara Island, with the warmer water running down the
  back side of Catalina and hooking around to San Clemente — "and that's at 499
  Zone." Of the other areas that had held bluefin in the past, it did not sound
  like there was a lot there in that week; the volume had slid out to Tanner and
  Cortez, working the water between the 381 off the east end back side of San
  Clemente and those banks, all of it slightly cooler water. The same report sets
  the fall regime: bluefin on the cool side of a break when the water is warm, the
  warm side when it is cold.

## How it fishes

- `Blh2BA-7Ono` (BDOutdoors, 2022-07-21, SoCal Bight report): the below-Clemente
  complex was framed as one 50–60 square-mile search box, explicitly contrasted
  with "running to the 302 or 371 or even running up to the stuff off the West End
  of Cat" — the compact, checkable end of the split. No charted mark is named for
  that last phrase, so whether it covers the 172 and the 125 (4.6 and 11.3 nm off
  the charted West End Humps) is unstated.
