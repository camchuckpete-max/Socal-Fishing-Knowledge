# Evidence — 209 / 312

Provenance for [209 / 312](../209-312.md). **Every observation below names the
209.** No source in the corpus names the 312, in any context — the mark carries a
charted position and nothing else.

Six of the twelve sources are Capt. Dave Hansen / Your Saltwater Guide, a
registered voice, and the 209 recurs across them as a teaching example rather
than as a trip report.

## Getting there

- `9hEa3sGTh40` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2019-08-20, tutorial): the worked scenario
  is leaving Dana Point Harbor at five in the morning with a couple of scoops of
  bait from the bait barge and running out to the 209 — "it's 200 degrees out of
  Dana Point." The same scenario names channel 72 and channel 65 as the two
  channels recreational boats default to, and has the boat later turning "180
  degrees down the coast to the 181." Also the source of the ch 16 hailing rule
  carried in [US waters](../us-waters.md).
- `ztpj1Xll0-U` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2023-03-02, PCS Show live seminar): the
  same day set up from Dana Point — bait barge at five in the morning, "not six…
  because that's when everybody goes" — with the day's target given as dorado
  "biting just inside of the 209 a little bit," numbers supplied. The
  bait-barge-clock half of this already feeds [Dana Point](../dana-point.md) and
  [bait tanks](../../bait/bait-tanks.md).

## Structure & bathymetry

- `Rf1HKJG-SDg` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2020-12-15, SoCal outer banks): after
  defining a bank on the 182 — water 2,000–3,000 ft deep coming up to a high
  spot and levelling off again, that shallowest point earning the name, with
  places all over the ocean at 182 fathoms that are not banks — the 209 is given
  as "another mountain in the middle of what you would call a desert." Bait
  congregates around the mountain for safety, "and then the chain reaction just
  occurs, and then all of a sudden here's the big fish"; current, tide and water
  temperature all matter but structure is what they act on. "The 209 is 209
  fathoms deep" is then used to define the fathom and the fathometer, and the 209
  is listed again with "181, 182, the 43" as the banks a sounder sees fish on.
  The same segment is the naming-convention source behind
  [zone lexicon](../zone-lexicon.md) and several sibling bank zones.
- `9hEa3sGTh40` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2019-08-20): "the 181 is 181 fathoms, the
  209 209 fathoms," used to explain that the fish finder is properly a
  fathometer because ocean depth is measured in fathoms.
- `m-M0iwX8DjA` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2024-01-27, Bart Hall Show live seminar):
  the 209, the 181 and the 267 named together as high spots in the middle of the
  ocean lying at 209, 181 and 267 fathoms, in a passage about the machine being a
  fathometer. Also cited on
  [279 / 267 / 14 Mile Bank](../279-267-14-mile-bank.md) for the 267 half of the
  same trio.
- `BskCsKaTiZw` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2025-03-08, PCS Show day two): "this whole
  fishy thing revolves around spots. Have you ever heard of the 181? The 209? The
  267? … They're high spots in the ocean where the bait gathers up." The ocean is
  framed as "a giant desert with these little tiny oases in it called spots."

## What's there

- `-bw1KDfDjv4` (StokedOnFishing, "Simrad Summer, part 1" — a summer trip, month
  not stated on camera; 2015-09-27 upload, the 14 Mile Bank / 209 / 267 complex
  off San Diego; unregistered channel, sponsor-heavy Simrad/Okuma episode): the
  on-water segment filmed "hanging out offshore on the 209 area" has the best kelp
  paddy the crew had seen all day behind the boat, bluefin spotted but "they just
  have not cooperated at all," and "a nice jig stop with some Bonita up to about
  8 lb," with yellowtail off the paddy the stated next objective. Surface
  temperature read 66–67 °F on the 14 Mile Bank climbing to 68 °F a little off it,
  and the whole area "between the 14 M Bank, the 209, the 267" was called one
  pocket of comparatively warm water. The same day is cited on
  [279 / 267 / 14 Mile Bank](../279-267-14-mile-bank.md),
  [kelp paddies](../../conditions/kelp-paddies.md),
  [electronics & sounder](../../planning/electronics-and-sounder.md),
  [fleet intelligence](../../planning/fleet-intelligence.md) and
  [bonito](../../species/bonito.md).
- `SczdZIq3UmE` (StokedOnFishing, 2019-10-02, offshore SoCal; unregistered
  channel): the run opens "we're heading over the 209 and we're gonna be racing
  all over the place today," then stops short — "we had to stop here because the
  marks were unbelievable" — on white terns over 72 °F water. The presenter's
  coaching for anglers new to tuna fishing is to look for the white terns, "not
  pelicans and not seagulls… they do help, but it's the white terns." Yellowfin
  boated that day graded 10–15 lb, ~25 lb and ~20 lb. Already cited in
  [bird reading](../../conditions/bird-reading.md) and
  [yellowfin tuna](../../species/yellowfin-tuna.md).
- `XLVUhV8DW64` (BDOutdoors, report dated 2022-10-12): a named private-boat angler
  fished offshore the day before, "ran out to like the 209 area, got some dorado,"
  then went looking for yellowfin that had been in that general zone and found
  them within ten miles of Newport Harbor. Framed inside the broader point that
  fish will be in the reasonable places they have been before, as set by current,
  water temperature and water colour.
- `AIHvJj-paoo` (StokedOnFishing, 2016-11-29; unregistered channel, sponsor-heavy
  Simrad/Okuma episode): "we just made a jump, went from the 209 to out San
  Clemente area" — about six miles off the island outside Desperation Point, in
  1,200–1,300 ft of water, with the sportboat Oceanside 95 alongside and hooked
  up, fish boiling around the boat. The 209 was the leg the boat left, not the
  place it fished; the same trip's light-fluorocarbon detail is logged in
  [yellowfin tuna](../../species/yellowfin-tuna.md).
- `HzE4FHHcvTk` (BDOutdoors, report dated 2022-08-17): the Bight-wide paddy split
  — best paddy water south of San Clemente Island and Catalina and along the
  coastal strip from Dana Point to Oceanside and southward, scarcer north of Dana
  Point. Does not name this zone; applied to it by position. Carried in full in
  [bight geography](../bight-geography.md).
- `OYOda6T3f-8` (BDOutdoors, report dated 2022-10-20): the fall warm-band arc —
  the band running backside Santa Cruz / Rosa, down the backside of Catalina and
  hooking around to Clemente and the 499 zone, with the bluefin sliding out to
  Tanner and Cortez as the warm water condenses. Does not name this zone; applied
  to it by position. Carried in full in
  [bight geography](../bight-geography.md).

## How it fishes

- `9hEa3sGTh40` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2019-08-20): the manual-range sounder rule
  stated on this bank — "you get offshore, you're out on the 209, it's 209
  fathoms deep; if you have your machine on auto it's looking for the bottom" —
  with the reasoning that a private boat lacks the bait budget to hold and chum a
  school up from 30 fathoms the way a sportboat can, and the working range given
  as 0–120 ft on older machines, 0–150 ft on newer. Also the radio-misinformation
  scenario in full: "anybody out there on the 209?" as the standard call, the
  escalating fake bite on the 181 (running out of bait, the boat sinking), the
  course change from 200° to 180°, and the admission that the caller was himself
  running to the 209 — closing on "turn your VHF radio off." Both halves are
  carried in [electronics & sounder](../../planning/electronics-and-sounder.md)
  and [fleet intelligence](../../planning/fleet-intelligence.md); this file
  records that the 209 is the bank they were taught on.
- `ztpj1Xll0-U` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2023-03-02): the same tactic told against
  himself — "we'd be out there on the 209 and I'd send out the numbers for the
  dump, wide open Yellowfin, five boats would take off." Stated conclusion:
  "getting the radio is not your friend."
- `sIoNELGlxmk` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2023-03-04): the counterpart to the fake
  numbers — "the Dorado are biting wide open three miles inside the 209; I even
  gave you the GPS numbers to where the kelp paddy is, because that's what I do on
  my website." A paid, attributable numbers source rather than an anonymous
  broadcast; the subscription pitch around it is promotional and rates `low` per
  the sponsored-claim rule, while the spot detail itself is what is carried.
- `-bw1KDfDjv4` (StokedOnFishing, 2015-09-27): re-working a paddy already
  produced — catch a yellowtail, drift off, circle back and give the same piece
  another try, for a few more fish; the light paddy build of 25 lb fluorocarbon to
  a No. 2 or No. 3 J hook with nose-hooked sardines; and radar run while searching
  doubling as fleet scouting, spotting a boat parked on a paddy on the screen
  before that boat noticed the approach.
