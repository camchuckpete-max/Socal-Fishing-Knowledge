# Evidence — 279 / 267 / 14 Mile Bank

Provenance for [279 / 267 / 14 Mile Bank](../279-267-14-mile-bank.md). **One
dated trip in the corpus fishes this water on camera**, and it names the 14 Mile
Bank directly; a second source describes how to approach this specific bank, and
two more supply the naming and depth readings behind the spot names. No source
names the 279.

## What's there

- `-bw1KDfDjv4` (StokedOnFishing, "Simrad Summer, part 1" — a summer trip, month
  not stated on camera; 2015-09-27 upload, running to "the 14 M bank" and later
  reported from "offshore on the 209 area"; unregistered channel, sponsor-heavy
  Simrad/Okuma episode): the crew ran out looking for yellowtail, kelp paddies,
  bird activity, puddling fish and breezers. With the plotter's sea-surface-
  temperature readout in text mode over the chart they read about **66–67 °F** at
  the 14 Mile Bank, shooting to **68 °F** a little off it, and called the whole
  section "between the 14 M Bank, the 209, the 267" a pocket of nice warm water.
  Boiling bluefin showed ahead of the boat — explicitly **not** the 100-pounders
  being talked about in 2015, "that 30 to 40 lb fish," with a first grade out of
  the water at **40–60 lb** and smaller fish coming up behind them. Later in the
  day they reported a kelp paddy, "probably the best one we've seen all day,"
  and "a nice jig stop with some Bonita up to about 8 lb"; bluefin were spotted
  repeatedly and never cooperated. Yellowtail came off the paddy on light tackle
  and were called mixed in with the bonito. Same-day material is carried in
  [bonito](../../species/bonito.md), [flyline](../../techniques/flyline.md),
  [kelp paddies](../../conditions/kelp-paddies.md),
  [electronics and sounder](../../planning/electronics-and-sounder.md) and
  [fleet intelligence](../../planning/fleet-intelligence.md).

## How it fishes

- `DnSqw4r7A1s` (BDOutdoors, 2022-07-14 SoCal Bight report, Erik Landesfeind on
  glassing): the worked example for approaching a bank is a stated plan to run out
  to the **14 mile bank**. Arriving, stop somewhere within about **two miles** of
  the **highest part** of the bank and stop the boat entirely if it is calm; if it
  is not, angle the boat for the calmest possible ride and barely leave it in gear
  at **one to one and a half miles an hour**. Then glass for an extended period,
  moving the binoculars a couple of inches at a time — **three or four minutes**
  to work all the way around the boat — looking for more than foamers. Pulled up
  on the **inside edge** of the 14 Mile Bank, the majority of the looking goes at
  the bank rather than at open water, because the chance of finding fish out in
  open water is "a little bit slimmer" than right on the bank. Running in open
  water with no relation to structure, any deviation from what he has been seeing
  — birds, bait, a scum line off a current break, a couple of paddies in a row, a
  temperature or colour break — stops the boat for a look around the immediate
  area. The general protocol built from this report is
  [search and glassing](../../planning/search-and-glassing.md).
- `-bw1KDfDjv4` (StokedOnFishing, 2015-09-27, this bank complex): the radar was
  run while searching so the crew could see where the other boats were — the
  stated payoff was finding a boat sitting on a kelp paddy "before he knows we're
  even looking at him." Carried as fleet-scouting doctrine in
  [fleet intelligence](../../planning/fleet-intelligence.md). The same episode
  supplies the stated read that **not every school of fish you find is going to
  bite** — stay positive, move on and look for new fish or kelps, because it is
  about putting in the time — and the re-work rule that a paddy which already gave
  up a yellowtail is worth circling back to rather than abandoning after one fish.
- `Blh2BA-7Ono` (BDOutdoors, 2022-07-21 SoCal Bight report): the Bight's two
  search types are stated as a complex — a roughly 50–60 square-mile box worked
  with hours of looking — versus a compact spot that is idled onto, graded on the
  meter and either committed to or left inside 20–30 minutes. Used on the zone page
  only to size the choice; no mark in this zone is named on either side of it.

## Structure & bathymetry

- `Rf1HKJG-SDg` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2020-12-15, SoCal outer banks): a bank is an
  isolated high spot rising out of otherwise deep water, named for the fathom depth
  of its shallowest point, at six feet to a fathom — with the stated exceptions
  being distance-named. Nine Mile Bank is "nine miles off the beach" rather than
  nine fathoms deep, carries many closely spaced high spots of similar depth rather
  than one dominant peak, and in places comes up to roughly 600 ft; and "14 is 14
  miles off the beach." This is the source of the zone's name reading and of the
  14 Mile Bank having no depth claim.
- `m-M0iwX8DjA` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2024-01-27, Bart Hall show seminar): the
  **209, 181 and 267** are named together as high spots in the middle of the ocean
  lying at **209, 181 and 267 fathoms**, in a passage about the machine being a
  fathometer rather than a fish finder. The grouping is the San Diego-side local
  banks, which attaches the 267-fathom reading to this zone's 267 rather than to
  the 267 (The Condom) charted in [81 / 381](../81-381.md).

## Adjacent water — not scoped to a mark in this zone

- `HzE4FHHcvTk` (BDOutdoors, 2022-08-18 SoCal Bight report): the best kelp-paddy
  water in the Bight is south of San Clemente Island and Catalina and along the
  coastal strip from **Dana Point to Oceanside and southward**, thinning north of
  Dana Point; the fall warm band tracks backside Santa Cruz / Rosa → Santa Barbara
  Island → backside Catalina → the 499 and Clemente, with bluefin condensing out to
  Tanner and Cortez as the coast cools; and a big NW event rolls the northern
  Channel Islands and the LA / Ventura shelf 5–8 °F while water south of Newport is
  less prone to those rollovers. Both marks in this zone are charted south of the
  Dana Point latitude, and neither is named in the source.
