# Evidence — San Nicolas Island

The observation layer behind
[San Nicolas Island](../san-nicolas-island.md). One line per observation.
Observations never change the note's stated doctrine.

**Naming note.** The BD reports treat the island and the ground beside it as one
map segment, named on camera as "San Nick and the Dirt Clod" — the auto-captions
render it "the dirk claude" (`Blh2BA-7Ono`) and "the dirt clawed"
(`CMQkHQMxbXM`), so the second half of the name is asr-uncertain(place) and is
not carried onto the main note as a spot.

## Getting there

- `zKmZ4zql2ws` (BDOutdoors, 2023-02-04, forecast curriculum, worked at the West
  End of San Nicolas Island): the island's West End is the click-point for the
  episode's forecast demo — locally "fairly calm Thursday Friday," a little
  windier into the weekend, but swell climbing from 4 ft Thursday afternoon to
  over 12 ft Monday, traced on the synoptic map to a system running up to
  Oregon; the point forecast alone would not have shown it.
- `Blh2BA-7Ono` (BDOutdoors, 2022-07-21, San Nicolas Island): with 15–20 kt
  gusting 25 forecast for the weekend, an in-progress seabass bite at Nick was
  called unreachable — "I don't really see any boats making that run out there
  in that weather" — with the advice to wait for the wind to die and jump on a
  trip.
- `CMQkHQMxbXM` (BDOutdoors, 2022-08-03, outside Bight): wind was forecast to
  blow out the Channel Islands, San Clemente, Nick, Santa Barbara Island and the
  tuna zone off Catalina in the same event, straightening back out the following
  week.
- `ouBrIdO7d4k` (SD Fish and Sips, 2025-03-08, filmed at the Pacific Coast
  Sportfishing Show, recapping the 2024 season on a 39 ft Contender running
  charters): San Nicolas was the season's headline run — "a lot of it was by San
  Nick" — and the furthest point, a little past the island, was called roughly
  115 mi offshore from the launch, with "80 to 90" stated separately in the same
  exchange and never reconciled; the crew was described as totally exposed out
  there, sleeping-bag overnights on an open boat. Unregistered channel, boat
  sponsored by Okuma.
- `IATPg9110CE` (StokedOnFishing, event dated to roughly early November 2014 per
  the 2014-11-11 upload — see the dating caveat in
  [calico bass](../../species/calico-bass.md); two-day calico tournament with
  weigh-ins at Two Harbors, Catalina): team Killer Whale's day one ran all the
  way to San Nicolas, put fish on the boat, and left after about 3 hours when
  the bite died, running back to Santa Barbara Island the same day; the day-one
  bag weighed 23 lb and change for second place. Unregistered channel.
- `O5aQkex0qGg` (BDOutdoors, 2023-03-24, rockfish tackle episode): a sportboat
  trip out to San Nick is named alongside private-boat Channel Islands and Santa
  Barbara Island trips as the setting where the 7'10"-heavy bass-style rod stays
  in hand — "if I'm fishing less than 300 feet of water this could be my go-to
  setup" — rather than dedicated deep rockfish gear.

## What's there

- `Blh2BA-7Ono` (BDOutdoors, 2022-07-21, San Nicolas Island): boats that made
  the run found "really good sea bass fishing" over the weekend and through the
  week, at a time when the northern Channel Islands seabass bite had dropped off
  after the full moon and had gone hit-and-miss; the Mirage, out of a Channel
  Islands landing, picked up a bluefin on the way back from Nick, placing fish
  between the island and Ventura Harbor.
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the landing is hedged on camera — "the
  mirage out of uh i think they're out of channel islands i'm not exactly sure
  which landing they're out of" — and the seabass line is "over the weekend and
  through the week here", the week in progress at a mid-week recording.
- `Blh2BA-7Ono` (BDOutdoors, 2022-07-21, Bight): boats fishing seabass at Nick
  or yellows at Clemente could slide off the islands and add bluefin to the
  mix — one unidentified boat weighed bluefin, sea bass, halibut and yellowtail
  on a single trip.
- `CMQkHQMxbXM` (BDOutdoors, 2022-08-03, San Nicolas Island): the San Nick
  ground was where "most of the bluefin tuna action" was happening, with "a huge
  volume of fish out there"; a first-timer's first fish taped at about 250 lb on
  a spinning rod after an extended fight; a Monday charter went two for three —
  a smaller yellowfin, a 70–75 lb bluefin, and a hooked fish estimated at
  100–150 lb on a 10 ft heavy crankbait rod; a separate boat took three fish
  including a 200 lb-plus one trolling Mad Macs. Fish were scattered from
  Catalina to San Nick and above it all the way to Ventura, and were in their
  normal spots.
- `5to3Q5P7w90` (BDOutdoors, 2022-09-14, Bight): the volume of bluefin sat in
  the square drawn between Santa Barbara Island, San Nicolas, San Clemente and
  Catalina, where "a lot of the bigger ones are."
- `XLVUhV8DW64` (BDOutdoors, 2022-10-12, Bight): the bluefin that had been off
  the West End of Catalina, west into Clemente, "at the Nick and SBI" dried up
  and moved to the east end and back side of Clemente; after the October full
  moon the fleet declared them gone, and the read was that they could be "up by
  San Nick again but there's not enough coverage to relocate these fish."
- `5p6gu14ZC4w` (BDOutdoors, 2022-10-26, San Nicolas / Santa Barbara Island):
  "really not a lot of coverage — Nick, SBI, that area up there"; the week's
  read was that the rockfishing was probably good, a yellowtail chance was live,
  and with squid around the islands, taking squid to any of them was rated a
  good shot at yellowtail.

## How it fishes

- `CMQkHQMxbXM` (BDOutdoors, 2022-08-03, San Nicolas Island): most boats on the
  ground were trolling Mad Macs, spreader bars or flying-fish rigs — "but you
  don't need to do it," the same week's fish having come on a spinning rod and a
  crankbait rod. For a mixed-grade day the stated preference was a Penn Fathom
  25-narrow two-speed with 80 lb braid to an 80 or 100 lb leader on an 8 ft
  graphite-composite rod, keeping cast stability while leaving a chance of
  landing a big fish.
- `XLVUhV8DW64` (BDOutdoors, 2022-10-12, Bight): the days leading up to the full
  moon were named the best bite windows, with the post-moon drop-off read as
  fish relocating to uncovered water rather than shutting off — long-range and
  multi-day boats with the time to look were expected to find them again.
- `Blh2BA-7Ono` (BDOutdoors, 2022-07-21, Bight): the offshore search block was
  drawn from Catalina to Osborn Bank to Nick to the "7-Eleven" outside Clemente;
  the fish were not holding hard to structure and could move 10–15 miles
  overnight, which is why boats that had been on them lost them.
- `ouBrIdO7d4k` (SD Fish and Sips, 2025-03-08, recapping the 2024 season at San
  Nicolas): asked the most effective way to get bit, the skipper ranked
  kite-fished frozen dead flying fish as producing the majority of the boat's
  bites, ahead of a "California flyer" artificial under the same kite, with a
  helium balloon named as a secondary kite-adjacent method — logged in full in
  [evidence — bluefin tuna](../../species/evidence/bluefin-tuna.md).
  Unregistered channel, sponsored boat.
