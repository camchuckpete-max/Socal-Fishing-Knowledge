# Evidence — Bahía de los Ángeles

The observation layer behind
[Bahía de los Ángeles](../bahia-de-los-angeles.md). One line per observation.
Observations never change the note's stated doctrine. Dates are upload dates
unless a trip date is stated on camera.

## Getting there

- `P36VGPPf120` (Strictly Irons, 2026-08-06, drive from San Diego to BOLA):
  trip-planning walkthrough filmed on the road, answering viewer questions —
  route, the pothole stretch past Gonzaga, money exchanged just past the
  crossing, fuel discipline, no cell service in the desert, group of 13 splitting
  a 4-day trip. The hosts are explicit that they cannot give "a hard fast answer"
  on timing.
- `T3cEQUqiH8E` (BDOutdoors / Nate Winnicki, filmed 13 May, uploaded 2026-05-22,
  Mexicali → BOLA): left at 06:30 and reached Costa del Sol at 17:31 — an
  11-hour travel day with lunch in San Felipe and "a couple of checkpoints, but
  no big deal"; the drive from Mexicali was expected to be "about six-ish hours".
  Evening plan on arrival was to "sneak across the street" and shore fish.
- `odiIWmut6gM` (Time On The Water / Capt. Luke, Artemis Charters, uploaded
  2026-07-02, BOLA): 8-hour drive "give or take with the stops", arriving about
  16:00 at Hotel Villa Vita. On-camera dates are day-of-month only ("Wednesday
  the 24th"), so the trip month is not established.

## Lodging, operators and cost

- `odiIWmut6gM` (Time On The Water, uploaded 2026-07-02, BOLA): the crew's
  pre-booked operator never showed for a 14:00–15:00 meet with a 15:00 launch
  planned; a captain they knew from an earlier trip, Jorge, came off the water,
  dropped his own clients and had them fishing by 17:45. The captain fishing them
  the following days gives his name as "Hmed" (rendered "Hamed" in `6P34jI9q1tE`)
  of Fish Arms, contactable as Capt. Morales on Instagram and Facebook —
  ASR-uncertain on both the given name and the handle.
- `P36VGPPf120` (Strictly Irons, 2026-08-06, Costa del Sol, BOLA): walkthrough of
  the hotel — two-bed and four-bed rooms at 1,800 and 2,000 pesos, AC, ceiling
  fans, Wi-Fi, soap and shampoo, owner "Roco" on site, mercado a 2-minute walk
  and the ramp 5–7 minutes, with Fish Arms collecting the crew in front of the
  rooms at whatever time they set. Baja Bound vehicle insurance quoted at $90 for
  4 days on a 2021 car and $130 on a new Tacoma.
- `8c07sN9wxGM` (Airstream Expeditions, 2025-10-02, Campo Archelón, BOLA): owner
  Antonio Resendes describes the camp on camera — palapas at $24, cabins at
  $120–160 with AC, Wi-Fi and kitchens, a café-restaurant open 07:00–20:00 and
  closed Wednesdays, and the family's move to BOLA in 1979 for sea-turtle
  conservation. The crew books pangas through Fish Arms and uses a local
  filleting/vacuum-sealing service before the drive home. The channel monetises
  gear links in its description, so its equipment recommendations are
  promotional context.

## Structure & bathymetry

- `wX7jUycIJ-U` (Bryan Timm, 2021-12-08, BOLA, day one of a multi-day trip):
  ran about 20 miles to an island high spot; it "got quite nautical" there, the
  crew nearly abandoned filming, and one angler was seasick — but the fish were
  "full speed", with triples on the first drop. They then went "inside" to one of
  the islands for a half-morning of cabrilla, returned to the high spot when the
  weather laid down, and planned day two at "the big island" for bigger fish.
- `odiIWmut6gM` (Time On The Water, uploaded 2026-07-02, BOLA): fishing named
  on camera as "Cardonosa" and, in the next sentence, "Isla Cartito" — described
  as a rising shelf coming up to 60 ft, "probably the south side of it". One of
  the two names is ASR garble.
- `vqsD0qpwcJA` (BDOutdoors / Jordan, 2022-04-06, BOLA): "legendary yellowtail
  grounds… yellowtail everywhere on this high spot"; the trip expected to fish
  150–300 ft and fished 150–180 ft, with 130–250 g jigs matched to each rod's jig
  rating. The narrator self-declares as new to slow-pitch ("admittedly it's
  totally new to me"), and the re-upload `Jtf-bU4aM-c` is the same recording —
  one source, not two. Full status in the
  [yellowtail evidence file](../../species/evidence/yellowtail.md).

## What's there

- `ojE5H6AdGGo` (Dirty Hookers, 2024-08-15, just outside the bay, BOLA): four or
  five yellowtail in the box by 07:55 chasing bird schools with windows of "maybe
  a couple seconds"; fish of 24 and 25 lb weighed on camera and released, a
  13-pounder released casually, the boat's first golden cabrilla of the trip at
  about 5 lb, and a small Mexican hogfish seen on the rock. The crew beached the
  panga at 12:40 for a swim and fresh sashimi.
- `6P34jI9q1tE` (BDOutdoors, 2026-05-30, BOLA, Puro Fierro tournament days 2–3):
  weighed an 18 lb yellowtail and a 7.5 lb cabrilla, gilled and gutted, under a
  single-fish-per-team-per-day-per-species format. Day three struck out on
  yellows and worked every protected cove and island lee for cabrilla on the way
  in, in 25 kt gusting 40. Guide named as Hamed of Fish Arms.
- `XRIjUgpuyZ8` (Fired Up Fishing, 2023-05-07, BOLA, aboard Bola Sport Fishing's
  *Guardian*): first spot in about 600 ft on single high dropper loops with live
  mackerel, "slow and steady"; the day's bottom fishing ran 600–700 ft with 700
  and 800 ft called on camera, a snowy grouper estimated at maybe 50 lb, and a
  170 g knife jig plus a Colt Sniper 150 g fished alongside the bait. Conditions
  were low 80s and light wind.
- `8c07sN9wxGM` (Airstream Expeditions, 2025-10-02, BOLA): day one about five
  yellowtail and a couple of leopard grouper, called a slow day; day two targeted
  baqueta/snowy grouper at over 600 ft, one fight running past the 25-minute
  mark. The narrator states that in the warmer months yellowtail are up on the
  surface and take a flylined live bait or a cast jig.
- `GJrNG2zmdB0` (Haggard Pirate, 2021-08-25, BOLA): a friends' trip, mostly camp
  and hotel footage; on the water the crew got "on the board" with both leopard
  grouper and yellowtail, with no depths, spots or conditions stated.

## How it fishes

- `T3cEQUqiH8E` (BDOutdoors, filmed 13 May, uploaded 2026-05-22, BOLA): first
  stop of day one produced seven yellowtail in the boat plus releases in about 20
  minutes; the crew then ran to deeper water for slow-pitching with 180–200 g
  knife jigs for baqueta and snowy grouper. Jerkbaits were cast at the rocks and
  the shoreline and wound "as fast as we possibly can", and on high spots in
  under 100 ft the fish came up for a surface bait even when deep. Gear shown:
  a Tranx 300 with a power handle on a G. Loomis IMX Pro, and a Fathom 400.
- `odiIWmut6gM` (Time On The Water, uploaded 2026-07-02, BOLA): the whole fleet
  stopped in one place to make bait for the dropper-loop program while this crew
  ran straight to the cabrilla; the captain's stated day-order was cabrilla
  first, then a move after roughly two hours for yellowtail and snapper. Later in
  the trip the crew tied the slow periods to a particular tide and asked to go
  looking in deeper water, which produced a 28–29 lb yellowtail on a 200 g jig
  from a Calstar 600-based jig stick and a Penn Torque; birds "bombing" nearby
  moved the boat.
- `wX7jUycIJ-U` (Bryan Timm, 2021-12-08, BOLA): every angler aboard helped make
  bait each morning, which the visitor noted as unlike SoCal. Shimano Shimmer
  Fall jigs at 170 g or 220 g were the top producers and the long skinny shapes
  outproduced the flatter falling jigs; the fish "wanted it really fast" and a
  slow presentation drew little. Vertical jigging with an 8 ft rod proved hard
  enough that he burned the jig like a yo-yo instead, and cabrilla broke him off
  on 40–50 lb — "definitely need that heavier line". Two lures were also lost to
  unexplained snaps in old fluorocarbon out of the boat's box.

## Scope — trips not in this zone

- `K22a8Ui8tWg` (Dirty Hookers, 2023-03-21, Sea of Cortez aboard the Tony Reyes
  mothership out of San Felipe): filed in the corpus's "BOLA" playlist and
  previously cited on this page, but the trip is the San Felipe mothership
  fishery, with "islands 360 degrees around us" — not this zone. Its content —
  winding faster so "the little guys can't catch them", and reading the back side
  of a point because "it swells a lot less" there — belongs to
  [Cortez North](../cortez-north.md) and [Sea of Cortez](../sea-of-cortez.md),
  under the naming caution recorded there. Retained here so the cite is not lost;
  it supports no claim on this page.
