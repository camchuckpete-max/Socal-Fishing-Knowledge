# Evidence — Cortez North

The observation layer behind [Cortez North](../cortez-north.md). One line per
observation. Observations never change the note's stated doctrine.

## The fishery

- `8IRONtdkceM` (Dirty Hookers, upload 2023-03-09, San Felipe mothership,
  trip filmed 2022; "Ep. 2: Big Shallow GROUPER vs BASS RODS!"): the fishery
  summarised on camera as "literally calico fishing on steroids" and the
  search read as "find the structure" on boiling rocks; cabrilla taken in six
  feet of water, which the angler gave as the reason for fishing 80 lb — "I
  think we would have lost a lot of them already"; grade ran 12–15 lb with
  six or seven fish inside the first hour; unregistered channel.
- `ZYAILGyrkRk` (Dirty Hookers, upload 2023-03-13, San Felipe mothership;
  "Ep. 3"): surface iron produced cabrilla with the SoCal hookset doctrine
  holding — "don't swing, just wind" — but the angler switched off it because
  "surface iron recovery is so bad" on a fast, high-volume shallow bite; a
  Wax Wing "has gotten bit every cast"; yellowtail follow rather than commit;
  unregistered channel.
- `ts9rJgxjmSg` (Dirty Hookers, upload 2023-04-27, San Felipe mothership;
  "Ep. 8"): the jerkbait trigger stated as a half-second pause placed right
  next to a rock rather than a steady retrieve — "on that pause they just
  launch out of the water for it"; replacing split rings mid-session comes up
  repeatedly; unregistered channel.
- `81YHbZQFzgk` (Dirty Hookers, upload 2023-03-03, San Felipe mothership;
  "Ep.1 GROUPER on BASS GEAR!"): bass gear as the framing for the whole
  program, with spare split rings and assist/stinger hooks called out as
  consumables the rocks and volume chew through; unregistered channel.
- `4Dn7yChmhk0` (Dirty Hookers, upload 2023-11-22): two golden cabrilla — a

  "Bahía de Los Ángeles" while its own narration says "aboard the Tony Reyes

- `EmZO8QiOfik` (Cesar, via Dirty Hookers, 2025-03-04, "CABRILLA | How, When,

  a surface jerkbait early, drop to 60–80 ft by roughly 09:00–10:00, and the
  bigger fish push to 80–150 ft at midday — plus the "dark spot" read for
  submerged rock once the sun is up, and the 7'10"–8 ft ~50 lb jerkbait
  outfit on a 6.5:1–7:1+ level-wind at 2–3 casts per minute; registered
  voice, single cabrilla-specific appearance — medium, model picks low.
- `M8hOYQ_6rSg` (Cesar, via Dirty Hookers, 2025-05-20, "Sea of Cortez &
  EVERYTHING to Fish!"): the trip quiver — a 50–60 lb 6.5–7 ft all-rounder, a

## Season shape

- `P36VGPPf120` (Strictly Irons, 2026-08-06, "Planning a BOLA Fishing Trip?"):
  the whole season table — before the wind season through April, "Mayo es
  malo" for the April–May wind, end of April to early June as the good window,
  May–June prime, "everything starts to go down" from July as the water heats
  and fish drop out of the surface program, August–October for dorado, marlin,
  sailfish and occasional tuna, winter normally slow; the stated mechanism for
  the summer drop-off is that fish sound, moving the program to dropper loops,
  live bait, yo-yo iron and knife jigs, "anything heavy on the bottom"; the
  2026 season ran against the pattern, biting through winter from November
  with yellowtail limits "every other day," and the hosts' own advice is to
  plan 2–3 months out then follow the operators; roosterfish present but a
  matter of luck and more reliable further south; the hosts are explicit they
  cannot give "a hard fast answer"; unregistered channel.
- cameron (September program, Bahía de los Ángeles): the yellowtail are on the
  bottom in September, worked with dropper loops and yo-yo iron — an
  independent arrival at the same heat-and-sound mechanism `P36VGPPf120`
  states, logged in [September](../../seasonal/september.md).
- `T3cEQUqiH8E` (Nate Winnicki, BDOutdoors, 2026-05-22, Puro Fierro tournament
  part 1, Bahía de los Ángeles): the wind read — "this area is weird, because
  you go 5 miles up the beach and then it's totally calm" — so a blown-out
  morning at the bay is not a blown-out day; the tournament format is one fish
  per team per day per species with only yellowtail and cabrilla counting;
  military checkpoints on the drive reported as routine; one crew's 06:30
  departure reached the hotel at 17:31, an 11-hour travel day.
- `6P34jI9q1tE` (BDOutdoors, 2026-05-30, same tournament): crews starting
  earlier on tournament days specifically to take the morning window before
  the wind builds.
- `aT-fL7vaavY` (Dirty Hookers, upload 2023-04-12, San Felipe mothership;
  "Ep. 6"): an overnight relocation of 8–9 hours to get back to the grounds
  fished on day one, "closer to everything we need to be at for the rest of
  the week," run through 12–15 ft rollers with "some of them coming up to the
  roof"; when the pangas cannot launch the fallback is fishing the mothership
  sport-boat style, "everyone's on the bow slinging jigs"; unregistered
  channel.
- `BSNGeduOHYM` (Dirty Hookers, upload 2023-04-20, San Felipe mothership;
  "Ep.7"): pangas held on deck on arrival — "it's been a little too snotty to
  take the pangas out" — and on a marginal morning only three launched,
  running three anglers per panga in two-hour rotations until the wind died:
  "two-hour sessions is pretty quick, but that just means we gotta grind
  really hard"; unregistered channel.
- `0dNfsle54Wo` (Dirty Hookers, upload 2023-06-23, San Felipe mothership,
  "Ep. 9"): the trip's last morning spent deep-dropping for pargo and grouper
  at Gonzaga Bay with sierra showing beside the boat; unregistered channel.

## Access & range

- `P36VGPPf120` (Strictly Irons, 2026-08-06): the route as the 8 east to
  Calexico, cross at Mexicali, then south — San Diego to Calexico 1.5–2 h,
  Calexico to San Felipe 2 h, San Felipe to Gonzaga 2 h, Gonzaga to Bahía 2 h,
  about 8 h without stops and 9–10 h relaxed; exchange money about 3 minutes
  past the crossing, $300–400 for a 4-day trip; top off fuel before crossing
  and again in San Felipe because stations are "few and far between" beyond
  it; no cell service through the desert, at Gonzaga or at Bahía, with
  Starlink, Roam and iPhone satellite messaging all named as working; travel
  in a minimum of two vehicles; a 4-day trip with 2 fishing days runs
  $800–1,000 per person all in, roughly $250/day, panga $550/day plus about a
  $100 tip.
- `odiIWmut6gM` (Capt. Luke, Artemis Charters, upload 2026-07-02): "not the
  trip you want to pack light for" — everything bought before crossing,
  because supply beyond the border is limited and renting gear locally needs
  arranging in advance.
- `aT-fL7vaavY`, `BSNGeduOHYM` (Dirty Hookers, 2023 uploads, trip filmed
  2022): the mothership as a week-to-ten-day live-aboard carrying pangas on
  deck and relocating overnight between zones, out of San Felipe.
