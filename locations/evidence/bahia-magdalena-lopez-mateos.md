# Evidence — Magdalena Bay (Mag Bay / Lopez Mateos)

The observation layer behind
[Magdalena Bay](../bahia-magdalena-lopez-mateos.md). One line per
observation. Observations never change the note's stated doctrine. Dates are
upload dates unless a show or trip date is stated on camera.

**Name normalization.** The auto-captions render the town as "Lopez Matos",
"Lopez Mattel", "Lopez tails" and "L Tails". Normalized to **Lopez Mateos**
on four independent geographic anchors that all agree in `EcQK5elTPJE`: a bay
"a little bit north of San Carlos", an extensive mangrove system, a gray-whale
nursery arriving mid-January, and access by flying into Loreto and driving
through Ciudad Constitución — not an inference from travel time or species
range.

**Source standing.** `EcQK5elTPJE` is a trip-selling seminar: Capt. Dave
Hansen (Your Saltwater Guide, `dave-hansen` registered) with Larry Hansen (no
relation — Pacific Sport Fishing Alliance), 2024 Bart Hall Show, 2024-01-27.
Larry Hansen's voice is unregistered, which caps the pair at medium regardless
of Dave Hansen's registered status, and the promotional context is why the
four-fishery parameters carry a fact-check row. `gimFbgN5Jjk` is a 2025 PCS
Show "Slow Pitch to Speed Jigging" panel (James Kikkawa's channel, uploaded
2025-03-14) — the Mag Bay passage is Benny Ortiz, unregistered, medium.
`4t_Z75shK_E`, `isXJONlpUP4`, `OVwqUKim9Pc`, `3tQ1_xiqwVU` and `UuyqTE21-kc`
are StokedOnFishing, unregistered — medium. The mangrove series
(`dlxA22FVNGc`, `vyX5FGoDH0A`, `frX09YMQxKE`, `FxgLol_IHa0`, `-5kooyIyavs`) is
Your Saltwater Guide, one January–February 2022 Lopez Mateos program with
local guide Reuben.

## Getting there

- `EcQK5elTPJE` (Your Saltwater Guide / PSA, 2024 Bart Hall Show, 2024-01-27,
  Lopez Mateos): access given as a flight into Loreto and a drive through
  Ciudad Constitución with a dirt-road final stretch; boca five minutes from
  the dock; mid-range tuna 10–12 mi out fished as a half-day, back at the dock
  by 10:00; live shrimp arranged with the guide the day before because no
  dockside receiver stocks it; San Diego long-range boats *Independence*,
  *Success* and *Constitution* named fishing the same offshore banks. Trip
  logistics, lodging, pricing and how-to-book content from the same seminar
  are promotional and generic — skipped per the curation bar.
- `3tQ1_xiqwVU` (StokedOnFishing, upload 2012-05-01, aboard the *Intrepid*):
  the transit from near Magdalena Bay toward Alijos Rocks narrated as 160 mi
  and about 16 hours; the destination is captioned "Leo's Rocks"/"Allejo's
  Rocks", read as Alijos Rocks on the stated distance and long-range context.

## Structure & bathymetry

- `EcQK5elTPJE` (2024-01-27, Lopez Mateos): four fisheries described in one
  bay — mangrove interior at 12–15 ft with the snook program down to 2 ft on
  oyster-covered roots, the boca at 40–50 ft with grouper to 100 lb and snook
  to 42 lb, a mid-range tuna ground 10–12 mi out over 200–300 ft holding 20–50
  lb fish, and offshore banks named only as "the 23 and the 38"; the mangrove
  system described as big enough to run without repeating water; forage named
  as sardines, mullet, clams, oysters and scallops.
- `isXJONlpUP4` (StokedOnFishing, upload 2020-10-07, Mag Bay estuary): guide
  Enrique called the channel depth near the mangroves at roughly 25–30 ft,
  cued by "already it's so shallow" as the boat closed to within roughly
  50–100 m of the mangrove edge.
- `OVwqUKim9Pc` (StokedOnFishing, upload 2020-10-14): the day's bank picked
  off the wave state outside the estuary mouth — the longer run to "the Ridge"
  if it looked manageable, the closer "Modesto Main" if not. The Ridge's depth
  is captioned "23 fetus", most likely ~23 fathoms but not confidently
  resolved — carried as a fact-check row rather than a stated depth.
- `dlxA22FVNGc` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2022-01-23, Lopez Mateos): casts made
  into water sometimes under 12 in deep, tight to the roots.

## What's there

- `gimFbgN5Jjk` (Benny Ortiz, 2025 PCS Show panel, upload 2025-03-14, Mag
  Bay): roughly 30 years fishing this bay; personal best day of 72 marlin with
  the boat capable of "over a hundred" the same day, October–November framed
  as the main draw; the bottom/jig fishery called "virtually untapped" because
  the visitors in that window are there for the marlin, with one day of jig
  fishing producing 14–15 species — Colorado snappers, "broom tail" and gulf
  groupers, and yellows — plus a trigger fish taken incidentally on a dropped
  live bait rather than a jig.
- `4t_Z75shK_E` (StokedOnFishing, upload 2020-09-25): the episode intro framed
  the bay itself, not that day's catch, as loaded with striped marlin
  ("everywhere") plus a healthy wahoo and dorado population "come fall time";
  in the dockside interview Toby gave cedar plugs as the crew's standard go-to
  once into tuna, and a bait-to-lure-size heuristic — both mackerel and sardine
  bait balls present, marlin feeding on the smaller sardines taking smaller
  trolled lures, bigger lures kept for blue/black marlin (none landed on
  camera).
- `OVwqUKim9Pc` (upload 2020-10-14): an offshore day run targeting wahoo
  turned into a "full speed" dorado bite instead; the bank fished that day was
  not named on camera. The trip's final day returned to the estuary for a
  multi-fish snook bite kept for the table.
- `isXJONlpUP4` (upload 2020-10-07): guide-named inshore targets going in were
  halibut, spotted bass, snook, pargo/snapper and grouper; trolling small
  lures tight to the mangrove edge produced fast repeated action on fish the
  guide called palometa, described as a pompano-family colour variant that
  grows bigger than a pompano; a larger fish that broke off running for the
  mangroves was guessed as snook but not confirmed.
- `-5kooyIyavs` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2022-02-24, Lopez Mateos): snook,
  corvina, pargo, triggerfish and baby halibut all taken casting bait into the
  same mangrove cove, plus a juvenile broomtail grouper on a separate cast; a
  hedged aside that live bait worked off the boat's opposite side would
  "probably do pretty good" on corvina was never tried.
- `vyX5FGoDH0A` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2022-01-26, Lopez Mateos): a wide-open
  snook bite on the sardina fly line with multiple fish landed in quick
  succession; a fish first called "grouper" was corrected on camera to spotted
  bay bass; the captions render a fish name as "ravallo"/"rubble", likely
  robalo (snook) but not confidently resolved.
- `dlxA22FVNGc` (2022-01-23): anchored chumming with live sardines produced a
  fast multi-species bite along the root edge and the mouth of a dead-tree
  cove — snook, a small broomtail grouper and jack, the jack released as
  bycatch.
- `3tQ1_xiqwVU` (StokedOnFishing, upload 2012-05-01, the Ridge near Mag Bay,
  aboard the *Intrepid*): a yellowtail (called on camera as "yellow tail, I
  think") hooked yo-yoing iron at 180 ft, 10 ft off the bottom, working back up
  on the Ridge after the crew left Thetis Bank overnight on opposite current;
  angler named as Ray; no jig weight or colour given. The crew also narrated a
  plan to fish the kite, helium balloon rigs and the flyline on 50 lb for
  yellowfin graded 60–90 lb, possibly a few over 100 lb, at the next stop — a
  stated plan, not shown executed.
- `UuyqTE21-kc` (StokedOnFishing, upload 2015-01-18, "lower banks" near
  Magdalena Bay): giant yellowfin of 150 to 300-plus lb worked on flying-fish
  baits floated downwind under a helium balloon; the ground is narrated as the
  area running from Mag Bay down toward Cabo San Lucas, which is carried as a
  region-level stub rather than as part of this zone.

## How it fishes

- `EcQK5elTPJE` (2024-01-27): the inside of the bay stays flat when it blows
  outside — wind chop only, no swell — with multiple consecutive fishable days
  through bad outside weather described as routine by moving between the four
  fisheries; wahoo framed as a strong fishery whose stated key is getting out
  early in the morning.
- `4t_Z75shK_E` (upload 2020-09-25): roughly three days of wind had just
  settled and blew up again the night before the shoot, with no boats out of
  Mag Bay for a couple of days beforehand — a fleet stand-down after wind. Same
  episode: a dead bait's mouth tied closed before slow-trolling so it would not
  take on water and swim poorly; a second dead bait dropped back roughly 50 ft
  while another fish was being fought, to try for a double — it did not
  connect. A lure rigged with a magnet holding the hook to the body so it
  "runs true" to 20 kt, so it can stay in the water at transit speed, is the
  lure-company owner describing his own product — capped at low confidence and
  not carried on the zone page.
- `isXJONlpUP4` (upload 2020-10-07): the crew had fished offshore for striped
  marlin the prior day and "got a little beat up" by wind and chop, so the
  lodge switched the plan to the inshore estuary rather than running offshore
  again. Guide Enrique's tide framing: the falling/low tide had produced a lot
  of fish earlier, and once the tide started flooding the open-estuary bite
  would normally slow — but the fish move inside the mangroves rather than
  stopping, so the crew went back to trolling tight along the edge. Later in
  the session a school "looks like peppers" showing on top had the trolling
  rods put down for light spinning tackle and a 3/4 oz casting spoon.
- `dlxA22FVNGc` (2022-01-23): the ebb from peak high toward low given as the
  best window ("that's when they really get excited and start biting"), near
  peak high as secondary, and the flood from low toward high as the poor one
  for lack of water in the root zone; tide direction read on the water as
  drifting leaves and debris. Anchoring stated as crucial in a small cove
  because a drifting boat is constantly off the strike zone. Stealth build for
  gin-clear skinny water: a size 4 thin-wire bronze hook (brand heard as "rot
  mustang", unresolved) on 25 lb fluorocarbon, with anything under 35 lb given
  zero chance of coming out of the bushes once snagged; spare hooks and spools
  carried. A level-wind conventional was used for punching casts into tight
  pockets, with a spinning outfit stated to work just as well. The presenter
  broke off on the roots mid-session and re-tied; the tide fell from a
  super-high mark through the session.
- `FxgLol_IHa0` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2022-02-22, Lopez Mateos, same crew as
  `-5kooyIyavs` two days later): read the tide through the lagoon before
  dropping the anchor and predict the swing so the casting side of the boat
  lays along the target mangrove edge; the boat keeps swinging until the
  anchor comes tight, so the lay cannot be judged early. The first bait cast
  after the boat settled was eaten instantly — a ~5 lb snook called "perfect
  eating size", on a Monterey sardine cast-netted en route.
- `-5kooyIyavs` (2022-02-24): the rig is a thin-wire hook straight to
  fluorocarbon straight to braid — 60 lb braid to 35 lb fluorocarbon, zero
  weight, no swivel; placement in a narrow zone on the branch edge is what
  gets bit, and an off-target bait is wound in and recast rather than left to
  soak; on a fast outgoing tide even a well-placed bait is in the zone for
  about a second; a live bait that stops swimming well is replaced before the
  next cast.
- `frX09YMQxKE` (2022-01-28, Lopez Mateos): the presenter free-dove the spot
  to see the structure before fishing, then flylined bait into one specific
  mangrove-edge slot identified from that look — casts landing in the slot
  drew fast repeated bites; a small grouper was landed, and small pargo plus a
  larger snook were seen holding in the same slot on the underwater look.
- `gimFbgN5Jjk` (upload 2025-03-14): the operational takeaway is to bring
  dedicated jigging gear on a Mag Bay trip even when marlin is the headline
  target, rather than treating the jig fishery as a bonus activity.
