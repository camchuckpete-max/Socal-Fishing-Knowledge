# Evidence — 43 / 91 / 300

Provenance for [43 / 91 / 300](../43-91-300.md). **Every observation below names
the 43.** No source in the corpus names the 91, and no corpus mention of "the
300" refers to this bank — the KB's other 300s are jig weights, reel sizes and
depths in feet.

## Getting there

- `A6DJoXbID4c` (StokedOnFishing, 2016-10-06, offshore San Diego): the day's
  plan stated on camera after loading fresh mackerel from a receiver off Shelter
  Island — go out towards the 43 and probably end up looking at San Clemente by
  the end of the day — a rare case of a departure point paired with an outbound
  mark for a numbered Bight bank. No distance, time, fuel figure
  or fishing position is given, and the bluefin that were caught came off a kelp
  paddy whose position is never stated.

## Structure & bathymetry

- `Rf1HKJG-SDg` (Your Saltwater Guide, Capt. Dave Hansen, 2020-12-15, SoCal
  outer banks): the 43 given as 43 fathoms deep, with 43 fathoms named as the
  highest it comes to — a crest depth stated outright, which the other numbered
  marks in this grid do not get. The surrounding water is framed as a desert
  with nothing around,
  then a little mountain comes up under the water; bait congregates around it
  for safety, a chain reaction occurs and the big fish follow, with current, tide
  and water temperature all mattering but structure being what they act on. The
  same segment lists the 43 with "209, 181, 182" as banks, defines a fathom as
  the unit ocean depth is measured in, notes that a sounder does not see fish
  spread everywhere but on the high spots, and separates Nine Mile Bank as
  distance-named (nine miles off the coast, many closely spaced high spots of
  similar depth) rather than depth-named.

## What's there

- `Rf1HKJG-SDg` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2020-12-15): the 43 described as the
  rockfish destination the fleet ran to through the 1940s, 50s, 60s, 70s, 80s
  and 90s; with the rockfish area now closed, everyone goes to the 43 for giant
  bluefin instead, on the same high spot, because the bait hangs out around the
  edges of the mountain. California / CDFW, as of 2020-12; carried in
  [regulatory claims](../../sources/regulatory-claims.md) and in the
  [bluefin](../../species/bluefin-tuna.md) and
  [rockfish & lingcod](../../species/rockfish-lingcod.md) routers.
- `Blh2BA-7Ono` (BDOutdoors, 2022-07-21, SoCal Bight report): bluefin below San
  Clemente were not getting coverage, so the area was framed as one search — "the
  43, the 181, 182, 289, the entire Clemente Ridge, all the way up to Mackerel
  Bank" treated as one section of water, roughly 50–60 square miles to look at,
  explicitly contrasted with running to the 302, the 371 or the stuff off the
  West End of Catalina. Finding a 23-foot patch of fish in a 60-square-mile area
  was named as the problem; four-pack and charter boats were running that
  direction. The 43 is the first mark named in the enumeration.
- `A6DJoXbID4c` (StokedOnFishing, 2016-10-06, offshore San Diego, run toward the
  43 with San Clemente as the far end): bluefin taken off a kelp paddy, keyed on
  anchovies, with white terns glassed to locate the school. Position not stated,
  so the fish are not attributed to a mark in this zone. The trip's tactical
  material is carried in [kelp paddies](../../conditions/kelp-paddies.md)
  (stand ~100 yd off the paddy; watch all 360° around the boat),
  [bird reading](../../conditions/bird-reading.md) (watch the individual bird —
  diving birds mean the school is feeding at the surface, birds off the fish
  mean it is still there but puddling),
  [foamer casting](../../techniques/foamer-casting.md) (swing out and around a
  travelling school, shut down ahead of it, match the hatch),
  [tuna poppers & stickbaits](../../lures/tuna-poppers-and-stickbaits.md) (a
  Savage Gear Squish jig picked for its anchovy profile) and
  [making bait](../../bait/making-bait.md) (Lucky Joe rig baited with mackerel
  pieces at the Shelter Island receiver).

  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source), `A6DJoXbID4c`: the bluefin were not taken off the
  paddy. The paddy stop (@00:05:08) is worked for yellowtail and dorado —
  "there's a lot of patties out here with three Pounders on it yellows… but we're
  hoping for a a Dorado" — and the tuna sequence starts after they leave it, on
  fish "flopping around outside this boat" (@00:06:01). "Keyed on anchovies" and
  "white terns glassed to locate the school" both come from the closing tips
  voiceover (@00:19:15, @00:19:26), not from the paddy scene. Same finding
  ledgered on the main note.
- `OYOda6T3f-8` (BDOutdoors, 2022-10-20, SoCal Bight report): the October
  warmest-water yellowfin path ran from the West End of San Clemente down
  through Mackerel Bank to the 181/182 Ridge "down there towards the corner".
  Recorded here because its nearest named marks — The Corner and the 182 — are
  7.6 nm and 14.1 nm east of this zone's centre, so the path brackets this zone
  without entering it. The sentence is carried in
  [The Corner / 140 / 182](../corner-140-182.md) and the yellowfin router.

## How it fishes

- `Blh2BA-7Ono` (BDOutdoors, 2022-07-21): the stated method for the
  below-Clemente box is to run bank to bank looking for colour changes,
  temperature changes and bird life — anything that looks different than it did
  before — then, if a piece of water looks fishier than the rest, to be back in
  that area on a late morning slack tide after 09:30, named as the best shot at
  finding fish. Boats should expect to spend a lot of time driving around rather
  than driving to a spot.
- `yMiBtZ7k8-w` (BDOutdoors, 2021-02-04, deep-drop swordfishing seminar
  presented by Costa): the 43 named twice. First in the set of banks "where we
  usually fish" — the Ridge, the 9, the 178, the 302 "or whatever have you… or
  even the 43" — with a down-and-in drift preferred along the contour edges
  these fish dwell and feed at, fished up to about 11–13 kt of wind with 10 lb
  of weight before scope becomes a problem and on a drift of one to two and a
  half knots. Second, in the surface-sign discussion: rolling out to a spot,
  "whether that's the 9 mile bank or the 14 or the 43", and finding whales,
  dolphins and birds is a good sign even though that life sits a thousand feet
  above where the bait will be presented, because the reason it is there is
  food. Sponsor-presented seminar (Costa); the habitual framing is what
  distinguishes this mention from the live chart picks the presenter made for
  the 138 and the 182 in the same session.
