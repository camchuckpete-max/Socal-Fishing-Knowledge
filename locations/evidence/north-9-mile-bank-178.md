# Evidence — North 9 Mile Bank / 178

Provenance for [North 9 Mile Bank / 178](../north-9-mile-bank-178.md). **Five
corpus sources name something in this zone**, which is more than most bank zones
get: the naming-convention source that uses Nine Mile Bank as its worked
exception, the deep-drop swordfish seminar that names the 178 and "the 9" in one
list, a jig seminar that names the bank as a rockfish destination, a show-floor
recollection of a yellowfin day on it, and one cameron bird sighting east of it.
The remaining entries are general doctrine the zone page reads against these two
positions, not observations made on them.

## Structure & bathymetry

- `Rf1HKJG-SDg` (Your Saltwater Guide, 2020-12-15, SoCal outer banks): a bank is
  an isolated high spot rising out of otherwise deep water and is named for the
  fathom depth of its shallowest point, at six feet to a fathom — 182, 209, 43.
  Nine Mile Bank is given as the exception: "that's not nine miles deep, that's
  nine miles off the coast," and "it has so many high spots all over this thing,
  and a lot of them are the same depth, so there's not really one high spot,"
  coming up "almost as high as 600 feet" in places. "The 14" is named the same
  way, at 14 miles off the beach. asr-uncertain(the 600 ft figure is hedged in
  the audio — "maybe four on, but 600 is no"). The zone page carries the
  exception, the ~600 ft crown and the distance naming; the same source is the
  origin of the bank-naming convention used to read the 178.

## What's there

- `yMiBtZ7k8-w` (BDOutdoors, 2021-02-04, deep-drop swordfishing seminar, San
  Diego banks): the banks "where we usually fish" for daytime swordfish are
  listed as "the ridge or the 9 or the 178 or the 302 … or even the 43" — the
  only corpus list naming two marks that fall in one zone on this ladder. No
  depth, drop, bait or date is attached to either of them individually.
- `yn1suHH6b1Y` (FISHING SYNDICATE, 2026-05-13, Day at the Docks jig seminar with
  Dan Wade and Oliver Solis): a run "to the 9-mile bank fishing cods" is given as
  the reason to carry a jig-stick-class rod — "you don't know what's going to
  jump out of the water." The bank is named as a routine rockfish destination; no
  season, depth, drift or catch is stated. The rod-material detail this sits
  inside is carried in [surface iron](../../techniques/surface-iron.md); the
  channel is a rod manufacturer's stage, so the product framing stays there and
  only the destination-and-class point is used here.
- `ouBrIdO7d4k` (SD Fish and Sips, 2025-03-08, Pacific Coast Sportfishing Show
  floor, San Diego): two anglers recall a yellowfin day together "a couple years
  ago" — about 2023 by the show's date — at "a 9 Mile," with "25 on the upper
  nine." Fish came on live bait, with a popper worked for one angler while
  another drove; the routine was to throw one piece of chum from the bait tank,
  then the hooked bait, for an immediate bite, off a private pilothouse boat run
  through a boat club. unregistered channel; asr-uncertain(the count "25," the
  lure names, and whether "the upper nine" is the library's North 9 Mile Bank —
  no source makes that mapping). Flagged single-source in
  `sources/fact-check-ledger.md`.

## How it fishes

- `yMiBtZ7k8-w` (BDOutdoors, 2021-02-04, deep-drop swordfishing seminar, San
  Diego banks): the drift preference on those banks is "down and in … along the
  contour edges that these fish dwell out and feed at," fished on a traditional
  straight NW wind up to "10 to 12, 13" kt as the maximum, with 10 lb of weight.
  asr-uncertain(the weight phrase is cut off mid-sentence in the captions — "11,
  13 knots with 10 pounds of …" — so it is kept here and not in the main note).
  Separately, on arrival: "if you roll out to your spot, whether that's the 9
  mile bank or the 14 or the 43, and you got whales and dolphins and birds …
  even though that's a thousand feet above where you're going to present your
  bait, there's a reason why those fish are there" — food. And on where the fish
  sit: "they're gonna go where the food is … whether that water moves in over the
  structure or it's outside of it, that's where they're gonna be." Carried as
  doctrine in [swordfish](../../species/swordfish.md) and
  [deep-drop swordfishing](../../techniques/deep-drop-swordfishing.md); used here
  because the zone's own marks are the ones named.
- `Blh2BA-7Ono` (BDOutdoors, 2022-07-21, SoCal Bight report): the split between a
  50–60 square-mile complex you plan hours of looking across and the compact spot
  you idle onto, grade and leave inside 20–30 minutes. Neither mark here is named
  in that source; the zone page reads the bank's many-high-spots description
  against the search type and the 178 against the compact type, and flags the
  placement as unstated.

- cameron (2026-08, east of the 9 Mile Bank, San Diego): a probable
  **black-footed albatross**, called unusually close to shore. Carried in
  [bird reading](../../conditions/bird-reading.md); it is the only field note in
  the corpus that places anybody on this water, and it sits inshore of both
  marks rather than on either of them, with no fish attached.

## Getting there

- `HzE4FHHcvTk` (BDOutdoors, 2022-08-17, SoCal Bight report): the paddy-water
  split — best paddy water south of San Clemente Island and Catalina and along
  the coastal strip from Dana Point to Oceanside and southward, thinning north of
  Dana Point — and the wind-rollover contrast in which the northern Channel
  Islands and the LA/Ventura shelf get crushed while south of Newport is less
  prone. Carried in full at
  [bight geography](../../locations/bight-geography.md); the zone page uses it
  only to place these two marks on the abundant side of the paddy line and the
  resilient side of the rollover map, with nothing observed over either mark.
