# Cortez North

**The character rung.** The Sea of Cortez above 28°N — San Felipe, Gonzaga,
Bahía de los Ángeles and the Midriff islands
([regions & waters](regions.md)). It is a **panga fishery reached by road**,
not a sportboat fishery reached by water: you drive roughly **8 hours** from
San Diego (`P36VGPPf120`) and fish out of an open boat, or you board a
mothership at San Felipe that carries its pangas on deck (`aT-fL7vaavY`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): `aT-fL7vaavY` never names San Felipe —
it shows the pangas being loaded and the run out; the departure port is stated
only in `81YHbZQFzgk` ("we're going to San Felipe… the Tony Reyes"). Two
species carry the region —
[cabrilla](../species/cabrilla.md) and
[yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md) — over shallow rock that sets the
tackle. The paperwork rung is [Mexican waters](mexican-waters.md).

This is a different ocean from the Pacific side at the same latitude. Cabrilla
and pargo are Cortez fish; white seabass and calico run the
[Baja Pacific](baja-pacific-north.md), which is why the vocabulary splits the
two rather than lumping them as "Baja" ([regions & waters](regions.md)).

## The fishery

**Shallow rock, worked like calico fishing scaled up.** One angler's summary
after an hour on the rocks is *"this is literally calico fishing on
steroids"* (`8IRONtdkceM`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the line is spoken *inside* the first
hour, not after one — "we've been on the ponga for less than an hour"
immediately precedes it (`8IRONtdkceM`). The quote itself is verbatim.
The read is boiler rocks and visible structure —
*"we're just looking for nice boiling rocks, good structure — find the
structure"* (`8IRONtdkceM`) — with bottom-colour changes used to pick out
submerged rock, and the "dark spot" off the visible shoreline rock holding
the bigger fish once the sun is up (`EmZO8QiOfik`;
[cabrilla](../species/cabrilla.md)).

**The rocks set the line class, not the fish.** Cabrilla were taken in **six
feet of water**, which is the stated reason for **80 lb**: *"we're in six
feet of water… I see now why we fish 80 [lb]. I think we would have lost a lot
of them already"* (`8IRONtdkceM`). Big yellowtail bury you in the same
structure (`6P34jI9q1tE`), and at Gonzaga Bay one boat steps all the way to a
**wire leader on 65 lb spectra** for cabrilla and barred pargo cast tight to
the rock, on the reasoning that these reef fish run to **15+ lb** and the
structure holds toothy fish (`_Wb4z4ammoM`;
[line & leader](../tackle/line-and-leader.md)).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): 65 lb is the stated *floor*, not what
the boat ran — the same trip's tackle segment says "65 pounds spectra was
mandatory but myself and most of the guys were using 80 pound spectra tied to a
three to four foot 80 pound mono and or fluorocarbon top shot" (`A6s-A1NARuA`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): "run to 15+ lb" hardens a hedged
secondhand recollection about the biggest fish — "they get pretty big um I
think Landon and Daniel gotten some big ones I don't know like 15 plus some of
them" (`_Wb4z4ammoM`). The "old rocks and toothy critters" reason is verbatim,
but the only bite-offs shown on camera are sierra, not cabrilla or pargo.

**Cabrilla and yellowtail are not in the same water, so you pick a target for
the day** rather than expecting to cover both (`odiIWmut6gM`). One captain's
stated day-order is cabrilla first thing, then a move after roughly two hours
to try for yellowtail and snapper (`odiIWmut6gM`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): "not in the same water, so you pick a
target for the day" is not what the cited transcript says, and the day-order
line contradicts it — the captain plans both in one day ("go for [cabrilla] and
yellow tail too. Maybe try for white [seabass] and after… at the end these
programs uh catch a maybe snow[y] grouper at the 700 ft"). The crew's later move
to deeper water for yellows is described as their own read on a slow tide, not
a rule (`odiIWmut6gM`).

**Cabrilla drop through the day, and the technique follows them down.** Fish
rise **30–40 ft** to eat a jerkbait fished on the surface early; by roughly
09:00–10:00 they have dropped to **60–80 ft**; by midday sun-high the bigger
fish push to **80–150 ft** (`EmZO8QiOfik`). That ladder runs
[jerkbaits](../lures/jerkbaits.md) into
[yo-yo iron](../techniques/yo-yo-iron.md), and it is the region's core
program. Full decision logic: [cabrilla](../species/cabrilla.md).

**There is a genuinely deep program in the same water.** Out of Bahía de los
Ángeles, big grouper and pargo are taken at **600–700 ft** on a single high
[dropper loop](../techniques/dropper-loop.md) with live mackerel, worked
"slow and steady" (`XRIjUgpuyZ8`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): three drifts in one sentence
(`XRIjUgpuyZ8`). The named target is grouper only — "those big groupers those
paquetas [baquetas]" and repeated "snowy grouper"; no pargo or snapper appears
anywhere in the transcript. The rig is not only the single high dropper —
"we're getting them on cut bait on single and double dropper loops," with live
mackerel stated for the first spot only. And the day's headline grouper came
deeper than the band given: "800 feet."
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-internal): [Bahía de los Ángeles](bahia-de-los-angeles.md)
states the same deep program as **600–800 ft** and names 700 and 800 ft
soundings; this page caps it at 700 ft.
At Gonzaga the grouper drop starts around
**80–90 ft** on wire, biggest bait available, drag run button-down with the
crew expecting to be broken off (`A6s-A1NARuA`). Weights are heavier than
anything SoCal asks for: **16 oz** held bottom for the whole of the grouper
program on a November 2022 panga trip, stepping to **24 oz** for bigger baits
or a ripping current (`dEPuDrhoClM`), and a purpose-built slider rig carries
**8 oz minimum, 16 oz standard** (`M8hOYQ_6rSg`;
[sliding sinker](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md)).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the two numbers are attached to the
wrong things. "At least 8 oz and go to minimum of 16 or if not heavier" is the
general what-to-bring sinker range, not a slider spec; the slider itself is
"an 8 oz slider. Sometimes you put two or three of these things on there," and
16–20 oz is the *torpedo* alternative on a ~6 in breakaway (`M8hOYQ_6rSg`).
Nothing is called a 16 oz standard.
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-internal):
[sliding sinker](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md) carries the same source
correctly — 8 oz egg sliders stacked two or three, with a 16–20 oz torpedo as
the alternative — so the two notes disagree on what the 16 oz figure describes.

**Vertical speed jigging is the growth technique here.** It is called out as
gaining popularity with skippers in the Bay of LA, fished on a short
**5.5–6 ft, 150–350 g** rod with **50–65 lb braid**, and described as
devastating on yellowtail and amberjack (`M8hOYQ_6rSg`;
[knife jigging](../techniques/knife-jigging.md),
[rod & reel selection](../tackle/rod-and-reel-selection.md)). Sea-of-Cortez
yellowtail sizing starts around **150 g** and works up to about **350 g**;
anything heavier than 350 g is a lot of work (`M8hOYQ_6rSg`).

**The panga is a small platform, and it shortens the rods.** The stated pick
is an **8 ft, 20–50 lb** jig rod over the 9 ft jig-stick standard, because a
9–10 ft rod makes it hard to work a big fish close enough to gaff with the
short **4–5 ft** gaffs typically carried aboard (`M8hOYQ_6rSg`). The jerkbait
outfit is an oversized, super-stiff **7'10"–8 ft, ~50 lb class** rod on a
low-profile level-wind at **6.5:1–7:1+**, because the program runs **2–3
casts per minute** and a reel you hand-guide fatigues the angler into
backlashes (`EmZO8QiOfik`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the ratio range is closed in the source,
not open-ended — "we're fishing 6 and 1/2 to one to 7 to 1 reels… anything
slower than the 6 and 1/2 you have to work a lot harder" (`EmZO8QiOfik`). The
"+" on 7:1 is not stated. Rod, cast rate and the backlash-fatigue reason all
verify verbatim.

**Bait is made, and it is made every night.** The sabiki rig runs heavier
than the SoCal mackerel program — **25–30 lb** main line, **20 lb**
hooks/leaders, **4–6 oz** sinker for mackerel and sardine, up to **8 oz**
when yellowtail mix in, because a hooked string of 2–4 yellowtail snaps a
light sabiki instantly (`dEPuDrhoClM`;
[making bait](../bait/making-bait.md)). Leftover bait carries into the next
day's bottom fishing.
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): two separate statements are merged. The
"next day" belongs to the nightly bait-making line ("making bait every single
night whether you use it or not… for the next day and for the rest of the
trip"); the leftover-bait destination is the grouper day specifically — "if you
don't use it while you're out fishing cabrilla and yellowtail you're gonna use
it for grouper fishing whenever that day might be for us it was the last day"
(`dEPuDrhoClM`).
On the BOLA day boats the whole fleet stops and makes
bait first, for the dropper-loop program; a crew fishing lures can skip it
and run straight to the rock (`odiIWmut6gM`).

**The species mix is structure fish plus a late-season pelagic window:**

| water | what this region offers |
| --- | --- |
| nearshore-coast, island | [cabrilla](../species/cabrilla.md), [yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md), grouper, pargo/snapper, sierra, triggerfish, [Pacific crevalle jack](../species/pacific-crevalle-jack.md) |
| bank | [yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md), grouper, pargo/snapper |
| open-ocean | [dorado](../species/dorado.md), [striped marlin](../species/striped-marlin.md), sailfish, occasional tuna — the August–October window only (`P36VGPPf120`) |

The `open-ocean` term is on this page for that late-season window alone; the
year-round fishery is rock and bottom.
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): "only" / "alone" is a hardening. The
source frames August–October as where to look *if you want to target that
stuff* — "if you want to target that stuff too, then I would look towards going
August, September, October" — and conditions it on temperature ("it depends how
hot the water gets"), with a forward hedge about the coming year. It states no
exclusive window (`P36VGPPf120`).
**Golden cabrilla**, a distinct colour
morph, turn up in the same catch (`4Dn7yChmhk0`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the cited transcript says only "we got
two goldens on board within the first hour of fishing" — it never says "golden
cabrilla" and never characterises them as a colour morph. That framing comes
from elsewhere in the corpus ("that was a golden grouper dude… it looked
different", `A6s-A1NARuA`; the Dirty Hookers "rare grouper mutation" title,
`K22a8Ui8tWg`).
[White seabass](../species/white-seabass.md) turn up randomly (`odiIWmut6gM`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the only white-seabass mention in the
cited transcript is the captain's hedged optional target for the day ("maybe
try for white [seabass] and after…"); none is caught or discussed, and nothing
says they turn up randomly (`odiIWmut6gM`).
Roosterfish are present but a matter of luck, described as migratory and more
reliable further south (`P36VGPPf120`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): "more reliable further south" reverses
the source, which points the other way — "we've seen them down there randomly
um that far and it's higher up in the Sea of Cortez as well" (`P36VGPPf120`,
ASR-garbled). Luck and migration-on-conditions verify.
[Barracuda](../species/barracuda.md) are caught from the beach at Bahía de los
Ángeles — one angler counted a seventh in a day (`HC64Tzc_aTo`) — though
Sea-of-Cortez barracuda are not confirmed as the same species as the
California fish.
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the cited transcript never names Bahía
de los Ángeles — it places the trip only as "Baja", "Sea of Cortez" and
"camping… miles and miles away from civilization" — and the seventh fish is
called off a rocky point ("now we're on that point right there"), not the
beach. The count is verbatim ("it's like my seventh barracuda today") and the
session is shore-based throughout.
Barracuda also serve as **live bait** here, rigged with trap hooks toward the
middle or tail because a grouper of 100 lb or less grabs the tail of a long
bait rather than swallowing it (`M8hOYQ_6rSg`).

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: any sonar signature for cabrilla. The

([cabrilla](../species/cabrilla.md) carries the same stub).

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: bay and estuary fishing anywhere in this

## Season shape

Priors, not current intel, and the `seasonal/` calendar they normally rest on
is written for the [Bight](socal-bight.md). Everything below is sourced at
Bahía de los Ángeles, and the region's other grounds are undated.

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: whether the BOLA season shape extends north
to San Felipe and Gonzaga. The northern end of this region is 4 hours further
up the coast and nothing in the corpus dates its year.

| window | what the corpus says |
| --- | --- |
| Winter (Dec–Feb) | Normally the slow season — "it tapers off, all the bonitos get to rest" (`P36VGPPf120`) |
| Before April | Get in **before** the wind season if you want surface fish (`P36VGPPf120`) |
| April–May | Hit or miss, **wind the biggest factor**. The local saying is *"Mayo es malo"* — May is bad, because of wind. Expect days on the beach waiting for it to lie down, then relocating the fish (`P36VGPPf120`). The Puro Fierro tournament is fished in May (`T3cEQUqiH8E`) |
| End of April → early June | The good window, wind permitting (`P36VGPPf120`) |
| May–June | **The prime months** (`P36VGPPf120`) |
| July on | "Everything starts to go down." The water heats and **fish drop out of the surface program** (`P36VGPPf120`) |
| August–October | The late-season play for dorado, marlin, sailfish and occasionally tuna — how hot the water gets decides it (`P36VGPPf120`) |
| Fall into winter | Sea of Cortez baitfish shrink to about **1–1.5 in** and cabrilla key hard on that micro-bait; carry downsized lures for the window (`EmZO8QiOfik`) |

**The mechanism behind the summer drop-off is depth, not absence.** As the
water heats the fish sound: you lose the surface bite and move to
[dropper loop](../techniques/dropper-loop.md), more live bait, some
[yo-yo iron](../techniques/yo-yo-iron.md) and
[knife jigs](../lures/knife-jigs.md) — "anything heavy on the bottom"
(`P36VGPPf120`). Cameron's own September program at BOLA is the same read
arrived at independently: the yellowtail are on the bottom, worked with
dropper loops and yo-yo iron (cameron;
[September](../seasonal/september.md)).

**Wind, not the calendar, is what the season actually turns on, and it is
intensely local.** *"This area is weird, because you go 5 miles up the beach
and then it's totally calm"* (`T3cEQUqiH8E`) — a blown-out morning at the bay
is not a blown-out day, because the operators move. Crews also simply start
earlier to take the morning window before it builds (`6P34jI9q1tE`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): this is one crew on one windy tournament
day, not a general practice, and the stated reason is the opposite of "before
it builds" — the morning was *already* windy at departure and the wind was not
going to let up at all: "Windy this morning. We're getting an early start
because the wind is not going to be letting up anytime for the next like two
days. So, the earlier we get out, the better shot we get at that morning
window that we had yesterday" (`6P34jI9q1tE`).
At the
San Felipe end the same weather governs whether anyone fishes at all: one
overnight crossing ran **12–15 ft rollers**, "some of them coming up to the
roof," and the pangas stayed on deck the next morning — *"it's been a little
too snotty to take the pangas out"* (`aT-fL7vaavY`, `BSNGeduOHYM`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): two defects. The run is never called an
overnight crossing — it is the daytime relocation that departs after the pangas
are loaded and arrives "five hours later"; the 12–15 ft figure is the
narrator's own estimate ("so it's 12 15 foot rollers", `aT-fL7vaavY`). And the
pangas did **not** stay on deck the next morning: the "too snotty" line is
Wednesday's, from `aT-fL7vaavY`, replayed inside `BSNGeduOHYM`'s opening recap
montage; `BSNGeduOHYM`'s actual next morning launched three pangas ("this
morning it sounds like only three are going out").

**Treat the table as the prior, not the forecast.** The 2026 season ran
against the pattern, biting through the winter from November with consistent
yellowtail limits posted "every other day"; the sourced advice is to plan 2–3
months out, then follow the operators and talk to the captains close to the
date (`P36VGPPf120`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the year label mis-resolves the source's
relative time. The speaker says "this year has been kind of different… since
November, it's been biting in the winter"; the video uploaded 2026-08-06, so
the run began **November 2025** and carried through the 2025–26 winter.
"The 2026 season… biting through the winter from November" reads as November
2026. The "every other day" yellowtail-limit cadence verifies verbatim
(`P36VGPPf120`).

## Zones

**One zone, and no coordinates anywhere in the region.** The charted sections
of [the spot library](../sources/spot-lists.md) stop at San Quintín on the
Pacific side, so the census derives this region's single zone from corpus
depth rather than from a position list. The consequence is concrete: a day
plan cannot pull per-spot BightSST conditions here the way it can in the
[Bight](socal-bight.md). Zone pages land with the zone phase, so the name
below has no page yet.
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-internal): the zone page already exists — the table
below links [Bahía de los Ángeles](bahia-de-los-angeles.md), and this note's own
children block lists it alongside [Gonzaga Bay](gonzaga-bay.md) and
[San Felipe](san-felipe.md). "No page yet" is stale.

**Regulatory — Mexico. Jurisdiction: CONAPESCA for fishing, INM for entry.
Checked as of 2026-08. CDFW rules do not apply on this side of the line, and a
Mexican limit differing from a California one is two jurisdictions, not a
conflict. Verify current requirements before you fish
([Mexican waters](mexican-waters.md)).** ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source:
whether the Midriff-islands biosphere bracelet applies to a trip in this
region, and Mexican closures, protected areas or depth restrictions anywhere
in it.
⚠ Fact-check (unverifiable): the agency split — CONAPESCA for the fishing

## Access & range

**The region is reached by road, and the road is the trip.** From San Diego
the route is the 8 east to **Calexico**, cross at Mexicali, then south down
the Cortez coast, hitting each of the region's grounds in turn
(`P36VGPPf120`):

| leg | time |
| --- | --- |
| San Diego → Calexico | 1.5–2 h |
| Calexico → San Felipe | 2 h |
| San Felipe → Gonzaga Bay | 2 h |
| Gonzaga → Bahía de los Ángeles | 2 h |
| **total** | **~8 h without stops; 9–10 h at a relaxed pace** |

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-internal): the Calexico → San Felipe leg is not
settled at 2 h. The cited source gives 2 h in its planning summary but ~1–1.25 h
on the road ("we'll be in San Felipe in 1 hour", "an hour 15"), and
[San Felipe](san-felipe.md) records a second crew at **2.5 h** Mexicali → San
Felipe and **~3 h** San Diego → border (`_Wb4z4ammoM`), against this table's
1.5–2 h. The total, the two southern legs and the 9–10 h relaxed figure all
verify verbatim.

Door-to-door reality is longer than the leg times: one crew left at 06:30 and
reached the hotel at 17:31, an **11-hour travel day** with a lunch stop
(`T3cEQUqiH8E`). It is all paved — "no dirt roads down there" — and past San
Felipe the road runs along the coast (`wX7jUycIJ-U`). Military checkpoints on
the route are reported as routine (`T3cEQUqiH8E`).

**Everything you need, you carry across the border.** Tackle and food further
from the border run up to roughly **twice** the price, and *"there's no
tackle shop to really speak of… everything is really limited, so you pack up
extra. Not the trip you want to pack light for"* (`odiIWmut6gM`,
`P36VGPPf120`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the paired cite conflates two sources
that each carry only half the sentence. The quote is `odiIWmut6gM` alone —
`P36VGPPf120` never says "no tackle shop", "limited" or "pack light". The price
claim is `P36VGPPf120` alone, and it is hedged there ("almost twice as much
sometimes… for the further away you get from the border"); `odiIWmut6gM`
contains no price or cost statement at all.
The same discipline applies to fuel — top off before
crossing and again in San Felipe, because stations are "few and far between"
past San Felipe city — and to money: exchange right after the crossing,
**$300–400** covering a 4-day trip (`P36VGPPf120`).

**You lose comms for the second half of the drive.** Coverage holds around
Calexico and San Felipe, then there is **no cell service** through the
desert, at Gonzaga, or at Bahía; Starlink, Roam and iPhone satellite
messaging were all named as working, and the standing advice is to travel in
a group of **minimum two vehicles**, because a tyre problem in the desert
puts you a long way from help (`P36VGPPf120`).

**The San Felipe mothership is the region's other access mode, and it moves
while you sleep.** A week to ten days aboard, with the boat relocating
overnight between zones — one relocation was an **8–9 hour run** to get back
to the grounds fished on day one (`aT-fL7vaavY`). When the pangas cannot
launch the fallbacks are to fish the mothership sport-boat style off the bow,
or to rotate the pangas that can go — one marginal morning launched three
boats running **three anglers per panga in two-hour sessions** until the wind
died (`aT-fL7vaavY`, `BSNGeduOHYM`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the heading claim "it moves while you
sleep" and "relocating overnight between zones" are not in the corpus — every
documented relocation runs in daylight (Ep. 6 departs after the pangas are
loaded and arrives "five hours later"; Ep. 3 moves zones just after lunch;
Ep. 8 calls the next one "a full day's drive"). "A week to ten days" has no
ten-day source; the trips are called "a week" and reach day four on camera.
And **8–9 hours was the announced plan, not the run** — "it sounds like we're
gonna make it eight to nine hour drive back to where we fished the first day",
followed at 03:04 by "we're almost at our destination five hours later"
(`aT-fL7vaavY`). The pangas-on-deck fallback verifies verbatim; the
rotate-the-pangas fallback is `BSNGeduOHYM` only.

**Cost, for the drive-in version.** A 4-day trip — 4 days of travel, 3
nights, **2 fishing days** — split across a group runs **$800–1,000 per
person all in**, roughly **$250/day**, with the panga at **$550/day** plus
about a **$100** tip; food and drink are what push the number toward the top
(`P36VGPPf120`). Detail on lodging, operators and how a fishing day runs is
at [Bahía de los Ángeles](bahia-de-los-angeles.md).

**The paperwork gate applies to every trip here.** FMM, CONAPESCA licence,
passport and separate Mexican vehicle insurance for a drive
([Mexican waters](mexican-waters.md)).

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: running your own boat into this region.
Every documented trip fishes a local panga or a mothership, and nothing in
the corpus covers trailering a boat down, ramps and haul-out beyond the one
launch ramp noted at the BOLA hotel strip, fuel on the water, or the range a
private boat needs to cover these grounds.
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-internal): the corpus does cover it, at the other
end of the region — [Gonzaga Bay](gonzaga-bay.md) documents crews who "trailer
their own center-consoles down and camp on the beach, fishing the local reefs
and offshore islands with no other fishing boat around" (`_Wb4z4ammoM`,
`A6s-A1NARuA`), and that page's `distance_nm` records a 20-statute-mile run
from the launch. "Every documented trip fishes a local panga or a mothership"
is not true of this region.

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: sea-state and weather geography beyond the
local-wind note (`T3cEQUqiH8E`) and the one 12–15 ft crossing
(`aT-fL7vaavY`). Which grounds a blow shuts down, and the prevailing wind
direction and season for this coast, are unrecorded.

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: air access. Nothing in the corpus covers
flying into this region, so the ~8 hour drive is the only documented way in.
