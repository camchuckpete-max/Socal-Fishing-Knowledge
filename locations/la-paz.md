# La Paz

**[Baja only]** — The **structure end** of
[Cortez South](cortez-south.md), and the zone where the region's shallow-rock
program is the whole day rather than the thing that runs under an offshore
troll. La Paz is the capital of Baja California Sur, about three-quarters of
the way down the peninsula, and the water in front of it is fished for
*jurel* — [yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md) — [cabrilla](../species/cabrilla.md),
pargo and [dorado](../species/dorado.md) on live bait against rock
(`ORC1A68cEeM`, `qM7iOO7fOBw`, `92y14x33etQ`).

**Everything documented here happens shallow.** A live-bait slow-troll ran
against visible rock in **12 ft** of water, and the zone's named bank high spot
tops out at roughly **50 ft** on low tide (`ORC1A68cEeM`, `92y14x33etQ`). No
offshore troll, no billfish and no tuna are recorded in this zone — the water
that produces those at the [East Cape](cortez-south.md) end of the region is
not what the corpus fishes here.

The whole record is one **two-week** stay by one crew fishing one local fleet,
filmed as three days (`92y14x33etQ`). Read the page as one operation's program,
not as the zone's full fishery. Paperwork for every Mexican trip is
[Mexican waters](mexican-waters.md).

## Getting there

**You fly in and fish off a local fleet.** The documented approach lands at the
**San José del Cabo** airport, the same gateway used for Cabo San Lucas and the
East Cape, and continues to La Paz by road — the fleet meets the party at the
airport, runs them to the hotel, and has them fishing the next morning
(`ORC1A68cEeM`, `qM7iOO7fOBw`).

**The base is the town waterfront, not a marina compound.** The hotel is
beachfront and within walking distance of the *malecón* (`ORC1A68cEeM`,
`92y14x33etQ`).

**The boats and the plan are the fleet's.** Baja Rancheros supplies local
captains who run each boat, with a fleet captain setting the day's plan the
night before or at the dock; the captains are described as speaking English and
Spanish, and the operation runs **365 days a year** (`ORC1A68cEeM`,
`92y14x33etQ`). The day's opening move is stated as a plan, not a choice —
make bait first, then run, with the bite expected to be **"on and rolling by
10:00"** (`ORC1A68cEeM`).

**Fish come home through the dock, not your cooler work.** The fleet cleans,
fillets, vacuum-seals and freezes the catch at its own office; the stated
angler obligation is to bring coolers (`92y14x33etQ`;
[bleeding & general care](../fish-care/dorado-and-general.md)).

**Regulatory — Mexico. Jurisdiction: CONAPESCA for fishing, INM for entry.
Checked as of 2026-08. CDFW rules do not apply on this side of the line, and a
Mexican limit differing from a California one is two jurisdictions, not a
conflict. Verify current requirements before you fish
([Mexican waters](mexican-waters.md)).**

## Structure & bathymetry

**Three grounds are documented, and they are three different depths.**

- **Shallow rock, in 12 ft.** The day-one slow-troll started in water the crew
  called out at **12 ft**, against rock close enough that hooked fish reached it
  immediately and cut off — *"it's really really shallow, we started in 12 ft of
  water"* (`ORC1A68cEeM`). Grass and weed grow on it; fish were worked out of
  the weed on the same session (`ORC1A68cEeM`).
- **Island rock at Isla Espíritu Santo.** Rock worked **close to the island**
  for snapper, with mullet snapper and cabrilla the stated targets; it is cast-
  and bait-fished rather than trolled (`92y14x33etQ`).
- **"El Bajo", the bank high spot.** Called on camera the most famous high spot
  in La Paz: shallowest point **roughly 50 ft on low tide, no more than 80 ft on
  high**, fished by working around the rock (`92y14x33etQ`). ⚠ Fact-check
  (unverifiable): the name is heard across one transcript as "El Bajo", "El
  Elito" and "El Bajito" and resolves to no charted place in the KB.

**A fourth ground is a crowded shared high spot with no name.** Day two moved
to *"this little high spot"* with the rest of the fleet's boats on it, fished
in open water rather than against a shoreline; the bottom there is called sand
(`qM7iOO7fOBw`).

**The bait ground is floating structure, not bottom.** Mackerel are jigged
around containers near the port before the run out (`ORC1A68cEeM`).

**Deeper water sits immediately outside the rock.** When the shallow-rock bite
moved off, the crew followed it into water they called deeper and clean of
structure — *"deeper water here"*, with *"no rocks nothing"* under the boat —
and the fish there ran bigger and were fought without cutoff risk
(`ORC1A68cEeM`). No depth is given for it.

2023-10-25, `qM7iOO7fOBw` 2023-11-03, `92y14x33etQ` 2023-11-09), so the season
column is a record of one fall trip and nothing more.

**Green mackerel and sardines are the forage the program imitates**, and they
are also the only baitfish the corpus names in this zone — mackerel jigged
around floating structure, sardines bought from local vendors (`ORC1A68cEeM`).

**Frigatebirds work the surface here and will hit a jig.** A cast
[surface iron](../techniques/surface-iron.md) drew frigatebirds instead of
yellowtail during a boil (`ORC1A68cEeM`) — the bird that marks fish elsewhere
in the corpus ([bird reading](../conditions/bird-reading.md)) is a hazard on a
retrieve here.

## How it fishes

**The day is bait first, then a run to a guide's spot.** The stated bait target
is **20–40 green mackerel per boat**, jigged around floating structure, topped
up with sardines bought from local vendors for chum and for live bait; on the
documented morning the crew got about **a dozen** mackerel and made the
difference up with purchased sardines (`ORC1A68cEeM`;
[making bait](../bait/making-bait.md#la-paz-baja-california-sur--mackerel-and-sardine-bait)).
Bait-making runs regardless of weather — one morning's was worked in **15–20
mph wind out of the north** (`92y14x33etQ`).

**Test the drag with the captain before the first pass.** The crew's stated
ritual on this fishery is to ask the captain to set and check the drag before
fishing — *"number one, test your drags"* (`ORC1A68cEeM`).

**Program one — slow-troll live bait against shallow rock.** Live mackerel and
sardines pulled in **12 ft** on **40 lb** line and circle hooks, with the stated
hookset a counted **three-second pause** before putting the reel in gear;
hookups came as doubles close to **30 lb** (`ORC1A68cEeM`;
[slow-trolling bait](../techniques/slow-trolling-bait.md),
[full execution](../techniques/evidence/trolling.md#la-paz-baja-california-sur--live-bait-slow-troll-for-yellowtailjurel)).
Sardines are thrown at a boil that pops up beside the boat to hold it
(`ORC1A68cEeM`).

**Program two — flyline mackerel at the shared high spot, from outside the
pack.** With the fleet's boats stacked on one small high spot, the crew's
deliberate positioning was to fish **outside everybody else** rather than
anchor in the crowd, and the bite went from nothing to instant when fish moved
through (`qM7iOO7fOBw`;
[flyline](../techniques/flyline.md#la-paz-baja-california-sur-sea-of-cortez-side--open-water-mackerel-flyline-at-a-shared-high-spot)).

**Program three — drop the bait to the bottom, then let the troll lift it.**
At El Bajo the mackerel goes down on a weighted rig until it **hits bottom**,
and the same slow troll that is already running lifts it back up into the high
column; a sardine is fished simultaneously well back in the high column on a
separate line (`92y14x33etQ`;
[full execution](../techniques/evidence/trolling.md#la-paz-baja-california-sur--live-bait-slow-troll-for-yellowtailjurel)).

**Line class runs heavier than the shallow water suggests, because the rock is
the opponent.** El Bajo was fished **65 lb braid** to a **60 lb fluorocarbon**
top shot (`92y14x33etQ`); the shallow-rock troll ran **40 lb** (`ORC1A68cEeM`);
the shared high spot produced two different setups called out in the same boat,
roughly 65 lb braid to 60 lb with a 40 lb top shot, and 40 lb to 40 lb
fluorocarbon (`qM7iOO7fOBw`).

**The fight is a race off the bottom, and the captain drives it.** Hooked fish
run straight for rock and cut off — a boat alongside was **0 for 3** early in
the same session — and the recorded save is the captain motoring the boat away
from the structure to pull the fish off it while the angler holds on
(`ORC1A68cEeM`, `92y14x33etQ`). Once a fish is clear of the rock it is played
out normally.

**Casting gear is a real part of the program, not a novelty.** Stickbaits, a
[swimbait](../techniques/swimbaits.md) and
[surface iron](../techniques/surface-iron.md) were all thrown at boils and
breaking schools alongside the bait rods (`qM7iOO7fOBw`, `ORC1A68cEeM`). The
warning that goes with it: **braid will straighten the factory trebles on a
cast hardbait** here — hooks came back bent open after a big fish, and the
stated fix is heavier trebles before you fish it, not after (`92y14x33etQ`).

**Current is the switch, and no current means move.** Day one of the documented
trip was windy with **strong current** and slow fishing, and the fleet simply
fished a different spot the next day (`qM7iOO7fOBw`). The Espíritu Santo island
stop was abandoned mid-morning on the same read — *"they just were not chewing
as simple as that, maybe there no current whatever"* — and the boat ran to El
Bajo, where it caught (`92y14x33etQ`). See
[current diagnostics](../conditions/current-diagnostics.md) and
[sea state](../conditions/sea-state.md).

**Moving is the default response to a slow spot, within the day and between
days.** Both a mid-day move on day one (`ORC1A68cEeM`) and the island-to-El-Bajo
move on the last day (`92y14x33etQ`) were made on the guide's call, with the
anglers deferring to local knowledge of where to go next.

**Stop keeping fish when you have enough.** The crew released the rest of a
wide-open yellowtail bite once **three bags** had gone through the dock's
processing — *"when you got enough you got enough"* (`92y14x33etQ`).

## Spots

**No place in this zone has a public name plus a charted position in the KB, so
no spot pages hang below it.** The named grounds on record are:

- **"El Bajo"** — the bank high spot, called the most famous in La Paz;
  shallowest point roughly **50 ft on low tide, no more than 80 ft on high**,
  fished by dropping and slow-trolling mackerel around the rock for yellowtail
  and amberjack (`92y14x33etQ`). The name is heard three ways in one transcript
  and is not confidently resolved.
- **Isla Espíritu Santo** — island rock fished close in for mullet snapper and
  cabrilla; abandoned on the documented visit when the current died
  (`92y14x33etQ`).
- **The shared high spot** — day two's ground, unnamed on camera, small enough
  that the fleet's boats stacked on it (`qM7iOO7fOBw`).
- **The roosterfish beach** — a white-sand beach the fleet runs to and works
  laps off; unnamed, and it produced no bite on the one documented attempt
  (`92y14x33etQ`).
- **The bait containers** — floating structure near the port where the mackerel
  are jigged; a stop rather than a ground (`ORC1A68cEeM`).

All five are logged in [the spot harvest](../sources/spot-harvest.md) for the
gazetteer rather than minted as pages, because none carries a position.
