# Gonzaga Bay

**[Baja only]** — the **midpoint of the drive** down the Sea of Cortez coast,
**2 hours** south of San Felipe and **2 hours** north of
[Bahía de los Ángeles](bahia-de-los-angeles.md) (`P36VGPPf120`), and a fishery
in its own right. The corpus documents it two ways: crews who **trailer their
own center-consoles down and camp on the beach**, fishing the local reefs and
offshore islands with no other fishing boat around (`_Wb4z4ammoM`,
`A6s-A1NARuA`), and as a **half-day stop on the San Felipe mothership
itinerary**, reached by an overnight relocation and fished until noon
(`ts9rJgxjmSg`, `0dNfsle54Wo`). The targets are the region's:
[cabrilla](../species/cabrilla.md), grouper, barred pargo and sierra, worked
with the [Cortez North](cortez-north.md) cast-at-the-rock program and one
bait-dropping program off the same grounds.

## Getting there

**It is a drive, and the drive is most of the trip.** From San Diego the
region route runs the 8 east to Calexico and crosses at Mexicali
(`P36VGPPf120`); one crew driving it called roughly **3 hours to the border**,
**2.5 hours** from Mexicali to San Felipe and **2 hours** more to the
destination (`_Wb4z4ammoM`), against the region's leg table of **2 hours**
San Felipe → Gonzaga (`P36VGPPf120`;
[Cortez North](cortez-north.md#access--range)). The last landmark before it is
Puertecitos, about **60 miles** south of San Felipe, with Gonzaga a further
**40 miles** south of that (`_Wb4z4ammoM`) — the captions render Puertecitos
"PTO cedos" ⚠ Fact-check (unverifiable). A **military checkpoint** sits about
30 minutes short of San Felipe and passing it is routine (`_Wb4z4ammoM`).

**Fuel, tackle and food come with you.** San Felipe is where the gas cans get
topped off before the last legs (`_Wb4z4ammoM`), and there is **no cell
service** at Gonzaga (`P36VGPPf120`) — the region page carries the
two-vehicle-minimum and satellite-comms advice that follows from that
([Cortez North](cortez-north.md#access--range)).

**You sleep on the beach or in a room, and both are on the bay.** The
documented trailer-boat trip camps at a **campo**, picking a cabaña and
setting up before fishing (`_Wb4z4ammoM`); the alternative on the other side
is **Papa Fernández** and a second camp with a hotel, bar, Wi-Fi and air
conditioning (`_Wb4z4ammoM`) — the captions render the second name "Alano's"
⚠ Fact-check (unverifiable). Landmarks named from the road are **Isla
Willard** and the bay itself (`_Wb4z4ammoM`).

**The launch is tide-gated, and that is the day's first decision.** On a
lower-than-normal tide one crew scouted a second spot before committing, then
worked a trailer boat down a small ramp — *"a little launch ramp here… it's
just a little tricky"*, taking a couple of tries, *"and once you're in, you're
in"* (`A6s-A1NARuA`). The standing advice for a first trip is to go with
someone experienced, because the experience is in knowing the right turns, the
safe camping spots, and **which launch ramps are accessible at high and low
tide** (`_Wb4z4ammoM`); the general layer is
[tide & slack](../conditions/tide-and-slack.md).

**The mothership arrives here instead of driving.** Gonzaga is a scheduled
stop on the San Felipe live-aboard itinerary, reached by a full day's
relocation and fished as a **half day with the boat off the water by noon**,
with the anglers choosing how to split the morning between cabrilla and the
bottom program (`ts9rJgxjmSg`, `0dNfsle54Wo`;
[Sea of Cortez](sea-of-cortez.md#getting-there)).

**Regulatory — Mexico. Jurisdiction: CONAPESCA for fishing, INM for entry.
Checked as of 2026-08. A Mexican limit differing from a California one is two
jurisdictions, not a conflict. Verify current requirements before you fish**
([Mexican waters](mexican-waters.md)).

wind envelope for the bay — the region's wind material is all sourced at
Bahía de los Ángeles and San Felipe
([Cortez North](cortez-north.md#season-shape)).

## Structure & bathymetry

**The grounds are islands and rocky shoreline, and the rock is what the day is
built on.** The country is described as *"the enchanted islands in the Sea of
Cortez"*, fished inshore for calico-bass-style targets, grouper and pargo
(`A6s-A1NARuA`). Working shoreline, one stretch reads as **granite**: *"this
looks like granite or something like that"*, with **bait everywhere** along
it and the fish holding **toward the outer edges** of the stretch
(`A6s-A1NARuA`). Sign on the water includes **boiler spots** — visible rock
with sierra and mackerel bait on it (`0dNfsle54Wo`) — which is the region's
standard read ([Cortez North](cortez-north.md#the-fishery)).

**The casting ground is genuinely shallow.** One island stop was fished in
**10–12 ft**, which the crew called hard fishing and expected to cost tackle
all day (`_Wb4z4ammoM`) — the same shallow-rock trap that sets the region's
line class.

**The bottom program starts at 80–90 ft**, stepped into from the shallow
program on the same day (`A6s-A1NARuA`), and the bottom under it is hard
enough that a hooked fish reaches structure immediately (`0dNfsle54Wo`).

**One run figure exists and it is in statute miles.** Twenty miles out from
the launch put a crew on ground they had never fished — *"20 miles, so this is
all new territory… and it looks excellent"* (`A6s-A1NARuA`). The islands
themselves are close: birds and fish were found by coming *"offshore"* from
camp and running north to the islands visible from the road (`_Wb4z4ammoM`).

⚠ Fact-check (unverifiable): the name of the island fished on day one of that
trip. Asked what they were fishing, the crew answer is rendered by the
auto-captions as *"St Louise Island"* (`_Wb4z4ammoM`), and that rendering is
the only one the corpus offers — this page carries it rather than correcting
it to a guess. It resolves to no charted position in the KB. The same trip
names two outer points, rendered "Puna finel" and "Puna buo", as the far
limits of the range. Queued in
[the fact-check ledger](../sources/fact-check-ledger.md).

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: any coordinate, sounding, chart or sounder

composition anywhere here

## How it fishes

**The whole shallow program is calico fishing with heavier hardware.** The
description on the water is *"real similar to Calico fishing… except you're
using wire"* (`_Wb4z4ammoM`), and the fish behave the part: wolf packs follow
a lure in, and a big one *"came right out of that shadow"* on a stretch of
rock the crew was working with the **sun on the shadows** (`A6s-A1NARuA`).
Fish are found **toward the outer edges** of a rock stretch rather than tight
in it (`A6s-A1NARuA`). The mechanics are in
[surface iron casting](../techniques/surface-iron-casting.md) and
[jerkbaits](../lures/jerkbaits.md); the decision logic is
[cabrilla](../species/cabrilla.md#situations--techniques).

**Wire is the leader answer here, and it is a structure decision.** One boat
runs a **wire leader on 65 lb spectra** for cabrilla and barred pargo cast
tight to the rock, on the reasoning that the structure holds toothy fish and
these reef fish run to **15+ lb** (`_Wb4z4ammoM`;
[line & leader](../tackle/line-and-leader.md#line--leader-matching--the-actual-combo-logic)).
No wire gauge or leader length is stated. The parallel build from the same bay
runs **7–8 ft heavy/extra-heavy rods**, **6:1** reels, **65 lb spectra as the
mandatory minimum** with most of the crew on **80 lb** to a **3–4 ft, 80 lb**
mono or fluorocarbon top shot (`A6s-A1NARuA`;
[rod & reel selection](../tackle/rod-and-reel-selection.md)).

**The baits that produced are a 6 in suspending jerkbait in smelt and a solid
7X surface iron in mint**, worked both near and far around the rocky points
and edges (`A6s-A1NARuA`). The island ground added a homemade Alabama rig and
a chrome jig, both of which got bit (`_Wb4z4ammoM`), and a **Colt Sniper**
took sierra on the mothership day (`0dNfsle54Wo`;
[iron jigs](../lures/iron-jigs.md)).

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): **"near and far" is not a presentation
note.** In `A6s-A1NARuA` those words belong to the Simrad radar spot running
beside the tackle rundown (*"see near and far, leave nothing unseen"*); the
transcript's own presentation line is that the crew *"fish for the
hard-fighting grouper in and around the beautiful rocky points and edges"*.
The jerkbait and surface-iron specs themselves verify verbatim.

⚠ Fact-check (unverifiable): the transcript says only *"sniper"* — the brand
and model "Colt Sniper" are never spoken (`0dNfsle54Wo`).

**Foamers here beat the rock, and they are jerkbait water.** Roughly **30
cabrilla** swirled on the surface *"like they were a school of carp"*, every
one of them over 5 lb, under boobies crashing sierra schools; the first bite
was a 7–8 lb fish that **launched clear of the water** on a jerkbait, and a
[surface iron](../techniques/surface-iron.md) thrown into the same boil was
refused — *"they wanted the jerk bait on that stop"* (`0dNfsle54Wo`). Two more
foam stops later in the morning produced the 17 lb fish. The bird layer is
[bird reading](../conditions/bird-reading.md); the general trailer-boat search
is pelicans, boobies, cormorants and gulls working bait just off the beach
(`_Wb4z4ammoM`).

**The bottom program is a break-off program by design.** Step deeper, start in
**80–90 ft**, drop the **biggest baits you have** on a wire leader with the
**drag buttoned down**, and fish expecting to lose fish: *"we're gonna try to
get some big old grouper, hopefully we get broken off"* (`A6s-A1NARuA`).
On hard bottom that is exactly what happens — one angler left the reel in
**free spool until he felt the fish kick**, pulled it out for a second, got
three turns on the handle and broke off (`0dNfsle54Wo`). Live yellowtail is
one of the baits that goes down (`0dNfsle54Wo`).

**The mothership half day runs deep first, then hunts.** The morning opens on
the deep drop, moves at about **08:40** onto a couple of sierra spots, and
spends its last **three hours** running bird schools for foaming cabrilla
before the noon cutoff (`0dNfsle54Wo`). A **sea lion** worked the deep-drop
stop (`0dNfsle54Wo`), and whale sharks turn up on the surface twice in this
corpus, one of them coming straight at a boat (`_Wb4z4ammoM`, `A6s-A1NARuA`).

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the 08:40 move runs the other way — at
that clock time the sierra spots had already been fished and the boat was
leaving them for cabrilla: *"it's about 8 40. change of pace change of scenery
stopped on a … couple spots of Sierras … but now we're looking for cabria we
got about three hours left"* (`0dNfsle54Wo`). The three-hour figure and the
noon cutoff (*"just a little half day gotta be off the water at 12"*) verify.
