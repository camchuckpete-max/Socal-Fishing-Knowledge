# 385 / 238 / 475

**A zone is a run grouping.** 385 / 238 / 475 is the inshore corner of the
numbered offshore-bank grid southwest of Ensenada — four charted positions
(cameron) inside **9 minutes of latitude** (31°33.000'N to 31°42.000'N) and
**13 minutes of longitude** (117°05.000'W to 117°18.000'W). Three of them —
the **385**, the **238** and the **475** — sit inside 9 nm of each other and are
the cluster the zone is named for; the **480** to the northwest is a lone bank
the census attached here as the nearest grouping it could be fished with.

**Nothing in the corpus describes fishing any of these four marks.** The
[region page](baja-pacific-north.md) carries the same gap across the whole
numbered-bank grid — fifty-one charted positions over seventeen bank zones with
no depth, no target species and no doctrine attached to one of them. This is
therefore a **position-and-ladder page**: it says where the zone is, what the
spot names claim, and what is missing.

**What separates it from the rest of the grid is reach.** Every other numbered
zone but one sits further off the Ensenada coast than this one, and the corpus's
own offshore distance ladder out of that port brackets these four positions
rather than falling short of them. That does not make this zone documented — it
makes it the one place in the grid where the documented Ensenada offshore day
and the charted marks occupy the same water.

## Structure & bathymetry

**The three-bank core is what makes this one zone.** The 385 at **31°36.600'N
117°15.000'W**, the 238 at **31°33.000'N 117°11.000'W** and the 475 at
**31°36.000'N 117°05.000'W** (cameron) sit **5.0 nm** (385 to 238), **5.9 nm**
(238 to 475) and **8.5 nm** (385 to 475) apart — every pair well inside the
census's **12 nm** zone-diameter cap, which is the clustering rule that grouped
them. This is the tightest bank core in the Baja grid the review has written so
far: the whole triangle spans **8.5 nm**, well under half the **20.0 nm** across
[1010 Trench / 378 / 213](1010-trench-378-213.md).

**The 480 was attached, not clustered, and only just.** It lies at
**31°42.000'N 117°18.000'W** (cameron), **7.1 nm** from the zone centre and
**6.0 nm** from the 385 — closer to a core mark than the 385 and the 475 are to
each other — but **12.6 nm** from the 475, which is what put the four-mark
diameter past the 12 nm cap and left it a lone bank for the clustering to
attach. It is carried here as queued rather than re-parented, because the zone
list is set by the census and not by this page.
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-internal): nothing about the 480 is queued — there is
no row for it in [the relocation queue](../sources/relocation-queue.md), and the
census attach threshold is 25.0 nm, which its 9.4 nm from the three-bank core
clears outright (cameron).

**One charted bank outside the zone sits closer to the three-bank core's centre
than the 480 does.** The 450 at **31°27.200'N 117°05.250'W** is **9.1 nm** off
that centre against the 480's **9.4 nm**, and **7.6 nm** from the 238 and
**8.8 nm** from the 475 (cameron), yet it is parented to
1140 Finger / 450 to the south. That is the complete-linkage rule working as
designed — the 450 is 18.4 nm from the 480, so joining the two zones would
breach the cap — but it means the water between this zone's southern edge and
that one is continuous bank ground split across two pages. ⚠ Flagged gap — no
corpus source: whether the 450 is fished with the 238 and the 475 or with the
1140 Finger.

**The names are the only depth claims in the zone, and every one of them reads
deep.** A bank takes the **fathom depth of its shallowest point** as its name,
at six feet to a fathom ([zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md), `Rf1HKJG-SDg`).

same "Most", and this page states it bare under four-figure derived depths.
Read
that way the 238 tops out at **238 fathoms — about 1,428 ft**, the 385 at
**385 fathoms — about 2,310 ft**, the 475 at **475 fathoms — about 2,850 ft**

([deep-drop swordfishing](../techniques/deep-drop-swordfishing.md)).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-internal): that note fishes **structure at 150–350
fathoms**, so the 238's name sits inside its working band, not below the KB's
deepest working depth; the bait depth is not the depth of the ground fished.
Nothing
here reads as structure fished on the bottom the way the

## How it fishes

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: the zone's program in full. Nothing states

**The only program the corpus scopes to this water is the port's, and it is
chart-first rather than spot-first.** The stated Ensenada offshore plan is to run
**20–30 mi due west** to a temperature break, then continue north or west off it
by sea conditions, with a fallback search **20–30 mi north**; trolling cedar
plugs locates fish and the crew deliberately worked away from the fleet
(`0bcDBGzQnGw`; [Ensenada](ensenada.md),
[water temperature](../conditions/water-temperature.md),
[current structure](../conditions/current-structure.md)).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the plan is hedged — "probably... head

fishing away from the fleet is stated as a goal, "if we could find fish away from
them that's our goal" (`0bcDBGzQnGw`).

makes the four charted positions **things you would pass through on a break

## Spots

Four charted spots (cameron). They group as a core plus one near-miss
attachment:

- **The core three — the 385, the 238 and the 475**, spanning 5.0 to 8.5 nm and
  the only part of this zone that clustered on its own. The 385 and the 238 are
  the tightest pair at 5.0 nm; the 475 is the easternmost mark in the zone and
  the closest of the four to the coast, 24.9 nm from Punta San Miguel (cameron).
- **The 480** — the northwestern mark, 7.1 nm off the zone centre and 6.0 nm
  from the 385, but 12.6 nm from the 475, which is what kept it out of the core
  cluster. It is also the deepest-reading name here and the furthest of the four
  from the 450 to the south.

Charted positions for all four are in
[the spot library](../sources/spot-lists.md). No spot here carries corpus detail
beyond its position, so each spot page is coordinates plus its parent zone until
sources arrive.
