# The 43

A charted offshore bank at **32°39.333'N 117°58.417'W** (cameron), the northern
mark of the [43 / 91 / 300](43-91-300.md) zone, sitting on the numbered
offshore-bank grid west of San Diego and inshore of San Clemente Island. It is
named for the fathom depth of its shallowest point, and at **43 fathoms** it is
the shallowest numbered bank in the [Southern California Bight](socal-bight.md)
grid (`Rf1HKJG-SDg`, cameron). It is also the only mark of the three in its zone
the corpus names at all — as the worked example of what a bank is, as the
first-named member of the June–July below-Clemente search box, as a named
daytime swordfish ground, as the stated heading of one on-camera run, and as the
one numbered bank in the KB whose fishery a closure moved from one species to
another.

## Getting there

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: run distance, compass bearing, run time, fuel
figure or sea-state envelope to this bank from any port.

**One corpus trip names this mark as its outbound heading, and it is the rare
case of a stated departure point paired with a destination.** After loading
fresh mackerel off a receiver at **Shelter Island** in San Diego, the plan given
on camera was to go out towards the 43 and probably end up looking at San
Clemente by the end of the day (`A6DJoXbID4c`; bait mechanics in
[making bait](../bait/making-bait.md)). That gives the port, the mark and the
day's shape — but no distance, no time, and no position for where the boat
actually fished.

**Charted, that is a long run, and the island end of it is the near end.**
**Pyramid Head** at the southeast tip of
[San Clemente Island's front side](san-clemente-island-front-side.md) is
**21.5 nm** from this position, against **34.9 nm** back to the **Point Loma
Pipe** and **31.2 nm** to **Widow Rock** off [La Jolla](la-jolla.md) (cameron) —
which is what makes the stated "out to the 43, then San Clemente" day a run that
keeps going west rather than a turn for home.

**The bank sits outside both numbered groupings between it and the beach.**
[The Corner / 140 / 182](corner-140-182.md) and [51 / 181 / 138](51-181-138.md)
are the inshore neighbours on this line, and their nearest members lie
**7.8 nm** and **12.6 nm** east and northeast of this mark (cameron), so
anything closer to the coast than the 43 is a fallback rather than a stop on the
way. For scale on the other axis, the [Coronado Islands](coronado-islands.md)
run **~13 nm from the San Diego bay entrance** (cameron) — this is roughly two
and a half times that, on open water.
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-internal): the nearest member of 51 / 181 / 138 is
the **138** at **12.1 nm** by the coordinates in `sources/spot-lists.md`, not the
51 at 12.6 nm.

**Regulatory — United States. Jurisdiction: California / CDFW. Checked as of
2026-08. Verify current rules before you fish** ([US waters](us-waters.md)).

**A rockfish closure covers this bank — the only closure the corpus names on any
numbered mark.** The 43 was the destination rockfish ground the fleet ran to
from the **1940s through the 1990s**; it now sits inside a closed rockfish area,
so the effort that used to stop here for rockfish targets the same high spot for
giant bluefin instead (`Rf1HKJG-SDg`, California / CDFW, as of 2020-12;
registered in [regulatory claims](../sources/regulatory-claims.md)). The
structure and the bait draw did not change — the target species did, because the
bottom-fishing option was regulated out. **Rockfish depth limits and the
year-end closure change by year and by area, and the KB does not encode
current-year regulations: confirm the boundary and the season with CDFW before
fishing here** ([rockfish & lingcod](../species/rockfish-lingcod.md)).
⚠ Fact-check (single-source): the closure over this bank, and the 1940s–1990s
rockfish era it ended, rest on one 2020-12 transcript; the transcript's own
wording is "everyone goes to the 43 because rockfish area is closed" — no
boundary, statute or agency is named.
⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: the closed area's boundary, or whether the
closure is still in force after 2020-12.

## Structure & bathymetry

**The name is the crest depth, and here a source states it outright.** A bank is
an isolated high spot rising out of otherwise deep water, named for the **fathom
depth of its shallowest point** at six feet to a fathom — places all over the
ocean read the same figure without being banks
([zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md#structure--bathymetry), `Rf1HKJG-SDg`). The 43
is given as **43 fathoms deep**, with 43 fathoms named as the highest it comes
to (`Rf1HKJG-SDg`) — about **258 ft**. That is a stated
crest rather than one read off the naming convention, which the other numbered
marks in this grid do not get.

**It is the shallowest numbered bank in the Bight grid.** No other bare-numbered
mark in the [Southern California Bight](socal-bight.md) reads shallower; the
next is the **51** at **51 fathoms** in [51 / 181 / 138](51-181-138.md)
(cameron). Its own zone partners read far deeper by the same convention — the
**91** at 91 fathoms and the **300** at 300 fathoms, roughly **546 ft** and
**1,800 ft** ([43 / 91 / 300](43-91-300.md)).

**Why it holds fish is stated as a bottom fact, not a conditions one.** The
surrounding water is "a desert — there's just nothing around", then a little
mountain comes up under the water; bait congregates around it **for safety**,
the chain reaction occurs, and the big fish arrive. Current, tide and water
temperature all matter, but structure is what they act on (`Rf1HKJG-SDg`;
[current & structure](../conditions/current-structure.md)). The same segment
lists the 43 among banks with "209, 181, 182" and notes that a sounder does not
show fish spread everywhere — it shows them **on the high spots**
(`Rf1HKJG-SDg`, [electronics & sounder](../planning/electronics-and-sounder.md)).

**Charted neighbours, nearest first** (cameron): the **91** at **3.1 nm** and
the **300** at **4.0 nm**, its own zone partners; **The Corner** at **7.8 nm**
and the **140** at **10.2 nm** in [The Corner / 140 / 182](corner-140-182.md);
the **166** at **12.4 nm**; the **51** at **12.6 nm** in
[51 / 181 / 138](51-181-138.md); the **182** at **13.6 nm**; the **181** at
**15.4 nm**; and
the **284** and **289** at **16.2 nm** and **17.8 nm** north in the unbuilt
[289 / 284](289-284.md) grouping. Further out on the sword rotation, the
**178** lies **24.7 nm** east in
[North 9 Mile Bank / 178](north-9-mile-bank-178.md) and the **302** **23.8 nm**
southeast in [Coronados 230 / 302 / 226](coronados-230-302-226.md).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-internal): the **138** at **12.1 nm** is missing from
this nearest-first list, which otherwise runs out to 17.8 nm; by
`sources/spot-lists.md` it sits between the 166 (12.4 nm) and the 51 (12.6 nm).

**Nothing here is attached to land, which sets how it would be worked.**
Standalone structure rising off a plain fishes in **any** direction, but each
individual bank still has its own **preferred directions** learned over repeat
trips ([island structure](island-structure.md)), and the working bias on a bank
is the **up-current face** ([current & structure](../conditions/current-structure.md)).
⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: relief, high-spot shape, footprint, bottom
composition, contour orientation, the surrounding depth the bank rises out of,
or the preferred directions and drift behaviour at this mark.

**The bluefin case here is the structure case restated.** The fish are there
because it is a high spot in the middle of a giant desert where the mountain
comes up under the water and **all the bait hangs out around the edges of it**
(`Rf1HKJG-SDg`). That is a bank-scale reason to look, not a reason to expect
fish on a given day — the July report that names the 43 first is explicit that
those fish were **not getting coverage** and had to be found across roughly
**50–60 square miles**, with a 23-foot patch of fish in that area named as the
problem (`Blh2BA-7Ono`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the captions read "trying to find a you
know **23 foot park** and a 60 mile square area", with four-pack and charter
boats already out there and the upshot "you're gonna need to go out and find
your own fish" — ASR garble that most plainly reads as a 2–3 boat pack, not a
23-foot patch of fish. The same sentence also hardens the report's "not getting
**a lot of** coverage" (@00:08:15) into "not getting coverage".

**The one on-the-water trip aimed at this mark caught its bluefin on a paddy,
not on the bank.** Running out toward the 43 with San Clemente as the far end,
the crew worked a **kelp paddy** for bluefin keyed on **anchovies**, glassing
white terns to find the school (`A6DJoXbID4c`, 2016-10-06;
[kelp paddies](../conditions/kelp-paddies.md),
[bird reading](../conditions/bird-reading.md)). **No position is given for the
paddy**, so none of that catch is attributed to this bank — it is evidence that
this water gets fished on that heading, not that the fish were on the 43.

**The swordfish mention is habitual rather than a chart pick.** The 43 is named
inside the set of banks "where we usually fish" for daytime swordfish, and again
as a spot you roll out to alongside the 9 Mile Bank and the
[14](14-mile-bank.md) (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`) — unlike the 138 and the 182 in the same
session, which the presenter picked live off an SST/chlorophyll plot.

## How it fishes

**This mark sits on the complex side of the search split, which is what decides
the day's budget.** You do not "check the 43" — the below-Clemente banks are
worked as one search box, planning **hours of looking**, running contour and
glassing between features and treating the named marks inside it as reference
points in a single hunting zone. The opposite type is the compact spot — the
302, the 371, the ground off the West End of Catalina — a small definable piece
of structure you idle up to, grade on the meter, and either commit to or leave
(`Blh2BA-7Ono`, [zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md),
[search and glassing](../planning/search-and-glassing.md)). The 43 is the first
mark named inside that box, so it reads as where the looking starts, not as a
stop.

**The stated search method inside that box is a bank-to-bank sweep with a timing
rule attached.** Run bank to bank looking for **colour changes, temperature
changes and bird life — anything that looks different than it did before** — and
if a piece of water reads fishier than the rest, be back in it on a **late
morning slack tide after 09:30**, named as the best shot at finding fish
(`Blh2BA-7Ono`; [water colour](../conditions/water-color.md),
[water temperature](../conditions/water-temperature.md),
[bird reading](../conditions/bird-reading.md),
[tide & slack](../conditions/tide-and-slack.md)). Expect to spend the day
driving around rather than driving to a spot (`Blh2BA-7Ono`).

**The June–July read pairs that box with the moon.** Full moons drive the night
bite in that window ([bluefin tuna](../species/bluefin-tuna.md),
[June–July](../seasonal/june-july.md), [moon](../conditions/moon.md)).

**If a paddy turns up on this heading, the trip that ran it fished from off the
edge.** You do not have to fish tight against the kelp — stand roughly
**100 yards off**, because the tuna sit outside the piece rather than directly
under it — and keep watch through the full **360°** around the boat rather than
fixating on the paddy (`A6DJoXbID4c`;
[kelp paddies](../conditions/kelp-paddies.md)). On a travelling school the same
crew read the terns for direction, swung **out and around** so the boat came to
rest ahead of the school, shut down, let the fish come into casting range, and
matched the hatch of the bait they were on (`A6DJoXbID4c`;
[foamer casting](../techniques/foamer-casting.md),
[tuna poppers & stickbaits](../lures/tuna-poppers-and-stickbaits.md)).

**The swordfish program here is a contour drift, and the crest is not where the
bait goes.** The preferred set-up on the banks in that rotation is a **"down and
in" drift along the contour edges** these fish dwell and feed at, fished up to
about **11–13 kt** of wind with **10 lb** of weight before scope becomes a
problem, on a drift of **1 to 2.5 kt** (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`;
[deep-drop swordfishing](../techniques/deep-drop-swordfishing.md),
[sea state](../conditions/sea-state.md)). The fishery works **150–350 fathoms**
with baits averaging **800–1,100 ft** ([swordfish](../species/swordfish.md)),
which this bank's **258 ft** crest sits far above — so the drift is set on the
edges dropping off the high spot, not over it. The same seminar makes the
confidence read from the surface: rolling out to a spot like the 43 and finding
whales, dolphins and birds is a good sign even though that life sits **a
thousand feet above** where the bait will be presented, because the reason it is
there is food (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the transcript gives 1–2.5 kt as the
drift the presenter does NOT want, not the drift fished — "anywhere from a one
to two two and a half knot drift so i seem to find when i'm drifting that fast
i'm not getting the presentation i like". The 11–13 kt and 10 lb figures are
verbatim.

**This is not a BightSST eval target**, so conditions here are pulled per
charted position rather than read off a scored spot
([eval targets](bightsst-eval-targets.md)), and the break-aging and advection
rules in
[report reading & forecasting](../planning/report-reading-and-forecasting.md)
decide whether the sweep is worth starting on this mark on a given day.

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: how long to give this bank before moving off,
which face or edge to start on, or what tide, light and current do here
specifically. The zone-level program is on
