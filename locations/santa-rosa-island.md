# Santa Rosa Island

**One of the northwest outer [Channel Islands](channel-islands.md), and the only
island in that group the corpus places in two different programs.** It is named
as one half of the "Rosa/Miguel" pair that boats run to out of Santa Barbara
Landing for shallow rockfish and lingcod when a Santa Ana lays the water down
(`Z3rZqy2Pi8E`, `OYOda6T3f-8`, `5D1vx29LVpI`, `5p6gu14ZC4w`), and it is also one
of the three islands the routers scope the Ventura/Oxnard **100–250 ft** squid-
and-variety drift to, alongside [Santa Cruz](santa-cruz-island.md) and
[Santa Barbara Island](santa-barbara-island.md) (`6-8KfjEg0x8`,
[rockfish & lingcod](../species/rockfish-lingcod.md),
[ocean whitefish](../species/ocean-whitefish.md)). Its backside is the **first
station on the fall warm-band arc** that carries the bluefin south — backside
Santa Cruz / Rosa → Santa Barbara Island → backside Catalina → the 499 and
Clemente (`OYOda6T3f-8`, [bight geography](bight-geography.md),
[October](../seasonal/october.md)).

**Within the group it is the island that drops out of the forecast first.** Both
dated wind reads that rank the northern islands put Rosa on the wrong side of the
line: with wind up the northern Channel Islands for the weekend after 2022-07-21,
Santa Cruz and [Anacapa](anacapa-island.md) were called "plenty fishable" while
Santa Rosa "might be an issue for private boats" (`Blh2BA-7Ono`), and on the
windy weekend of 2022-10-26 Santa Cruz "will probably be fine both days" for
private boaters while Santa Rosa was only a Sunday option (`5p6gu14ZC4w`). The
April 2022 northwest event put the worst of it on
[San Miguel](san-miguel-island.md), Santa Rosa and the front side of Santa Cruz
(`YZtX1MiT0y8`). Two dated instances and one event are not a standing rule, but
they run the same direction: access, not presence, is what gates this island.

**The zone rung above this page is missing, so it hangs off the island group.**
The northern Channel Islands carry no coordinates in
[the spot library](../sources/spot-lists.md) and therefore mint no zone, a gap
the [region page](socal-bight.md) already flags (cameron); `parent` points at the
group page the same way [San Miguel Island](san-miguel-island.md) and
[Santa Cruz Island](santa-cruz-island.md) do. The nearest built ladder pages are
the [12 Mile Reef](12-mile-reef.md), the one charted mark in the Santa Barbara
Channel, and
[Davis Knoll / San Miguel Gap / Rodriguez Seamount](davis-knoll-san-miguel-gap-rodriguez-seamount.md)
to the northwest — neither carries a bearing or distance to this island.

## Getting there

**Santa Barbara Landing is the stated jump-off for the bottom program, and the
island is picked at the dock.** The plan described on camera is to trailer up to
Santa Barbara Landing and then go to Santa Rosa or San Miguel "depending on sea
conditions and weather" — the choice between the two is made on the day, not
before (`Z3rZqy2Pi8E`). The same reporter frames a mid-week window as a drive up
to Santa Barbara and a run to Rosa, Miguel "or up towards Conception"
(`5p6gu14ZC4w`), and sport boats out of that landing work the same water
(`Z3rZqy2Pi8E`). That is a different port framing from the **Ventura and
Oxnard** squid-and-variety day that covers the rest of the group
(`6-8KfjEg0x8`, `nRIAgz5G_Bc`, [Channel Islands](channel-islands.md)); this
island sits in both and no source says which port reaches it more easily.

**It is a long run, and the weather has to hold for days rather than hours.**
The December 2022 read calls light winds Saturday and Sunday — Saturday the
better of the two, Friday a little offshore — "a great opportunity to make a
long run" out to Santa Rosa or San Miguel for shallow-water rockfish
(`5D1vx29LVpI`). The go/no-go rule is stricter than a single forecast day: west
and northwest wind blows around Point Conception at **night** and quits in the
morning, so a calm dawn reading is a trap and the crossing wants **two or more
consecutive days** of no wind or Santa Ana conditions before it is committed to
(`Z3rZqy2Pi8E`, [rockfish & lingcod](../species/rockfish-lingcod.md)). That is
why a **12–24 h wind hindcast** for the route belongs in the conditions pull
beside the forward forecast
([sea state](../conditions/sea-state.md#wind-history-not-just-wind-forecast-1224-h-hindcast)).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the transcript's threshold is "a couple
days of continuous no wind or Santa anas before I plan a trip up there" — days,
loosely counted, not a "two or more consecutive days" rule (`Z3rZqy2Pi8E`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): `Z3rZqy2Pi8E` states no hour window at
all, so the 12–24 h hindcast cannot be chained to it by "that is why"; the figure
is the KB's own operationalization and carries a standing flag on
[sea state](../conditions/sea-state.md).

**A Santa Ana is the window, not merely a nice day.** Santa Ana events flatten
the northwest outer islands and are the stated trigger to run Rosa and Miguel
for shallow rockfish and lings in late fall (`OYOda6T3f-8`, `Z3rZqy2Pi8E`,
[November–December](../seasonal/november-december.md)). The fish are reported as
reliable and the crossing is what gates the trip
([sea state](../conditions/sea-state.md)).

**A northwest blow is the first gate, and it lands on this end of the region
hardest.** A strong NW event rolls surface temperature down **5–8 °F** across the
northern Channel Islands and the LA/Ventura shelf; after the April 2022 event San
Miguel, Santa Rosa and the front side of Santa Cruz sat in the low 50s °F while
backside Santa Cruz, San Clemente, Catalina and the Coronados held their water
(`YZtX1MiT0y8`, [bight geography](bight-geography.md),
[upwelling and turnover](../conditions/upwelling-and-turnover.md)).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): `YZtX1MiT0y8` states no 5 °F low end
anywhere. Its only delta is a hedged "seven maybe eight degrees" read off one
boat's gauge along the Long Beach beach; the northern-island drop is given as an
absolute — "hardest hit was the northern channel islands miguel rosa the front
side of santa cruz island … they're in the low 50s **in some of those areas**" —
so both the 5–8 °F range and its attachment to two whole geographies are the
KB's, not the source's. Same finding already ledgered on
[Channel Islands](channel-islands.md), [April](../seasonal/april.md),
[Hancock Bank](hancock-bank.md) and [Sverdrup Bank](sverdrup-bank-126.md); the
NW-event map itself was adjudicated by Cameron 2026-08-26 and stands.
⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source states this island's own wind threshold, or a
sea-state envelope for the crossing beyond the two-day rule.

**On a full-day island trip the line class is the local one.** The
schoolie-grade [surface iron](../techniques/surface-iron.md) build runs a
**40 lb** monofilament top shot for the local, full-day-accessible islands — the
Coronados, the Channel Islands and Catalina — stepping to **50 lb** only for the
overnight-range destinations, San Clemente and Santa Barbara Island
(`LTCFjqtSI8g`, [line and leader](../tackle/line-and-leader.md)).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the word "only" is wrong — the transcript
puts a third member in the 50 lb group, "if I'm fishing on overnight Island like
San Clemente Island or Santa Barbara island **or going down anywhere in Baja**"
(`LTCFjqtSI8g`). The 40 lb grouping and the full-day/overnight framing verify
verbatim. ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source
reconciles that grouping with the Santa Barbara Landing "long run" framing
(`5D1vx29LVpI`); they are two different trips to the same island and neither
states a range figure.

**Regulatory — United States. Jurisdiction: California / CDFW. Checked as of
2026-08. Verify current rules before you fish** ([US waters](us-waters.md)).
The season, depth and bag rules for the bottom targets that carry the late-fall
trip here move every year and are registered in
[regulatory claims](../sources/regulatory-claims.md) and in
[rockfish & lingcod](../species/rockfish-lingcod.md) — the recreational rockfish
season closing at year-end is what makes the late-fall Santa Ana window the last
of the bottom season ([November–December](../seasonal/november-december.md)).
⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: closures, MPA boundaries, landing or anchoring
restrictions stated for Santa Rosa Island itself.

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: a distance, heading, run time or fuel figure
from Santa Barbara Landing, Ventura or Oxnard to this island, or which end of it
a boat arrives on.

## Structure & bathymetry

**Three depth bands are stated and only the shallowest is described.** The inside
water is "a lot of stuff up there you can fish in 20 or 30 feet," clear enough
that a lingcod can be watched rolling on the bottom after it eats
(`Z3rZqy2Pi8E`). Above the bottom program, white seabass sit along kelp edges,
hard structure and squid nests and beds in **60–100+ ft** at these islands
(`Eo_bA8IWvyU`, `Xr4nURK-Z48`), and halibut sit on the structure *beside* those
nests in the same band and stay on it after the squid leaves (`OpcKQPA3vAI`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the 60–100+ ft figure rests on
`OpcKQPA3vAI` alone — "60 to 100 plus feet of water and there's squid nests out
there and you catch se bass or big h[alibut]". Neither white-seabass cite
supports it: `Eo_bA8IWvyU` gives "typically between 60 to about 90 ft" for the
dropper-loop band and reaches 100 ft only inside a hypothetical ("if we're
fishing 60 to 100 ft of water and the skipper says…"), with no "+" and no island
named anywhere in that video, and `Xr4nURK-Z48` gives no depth at all and is shot
at Catalina ("we're here at Catalina Island"). Same finding already ledgered on
[Channel Islands](channel-islands.md).
The deepest stated band is the group's **100–250 ft** island high spots, where a
private boat gets reds, blue bass, bocaccio and short lingcod off a single drift
over one rock — with the one stop given a number that day a rock at roughly
**175 ft** (`6-8KfjEg0x8`, [rockfish & lingcod](../species/rockfish-lingcod.md)).
⚠ Fact-check (scope): the routers scope that 100–250 ft band to Santa Cruz, Santa
Rosa and Santa Barbara Island, but the day it comes from names "the Channel
Islands" on camera and never an island — which rock sits on which island is not
stated (`6-8KfjEg0x8`, [Channel Islands](channel-islands.md)).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the band itself is not in that source
either. `6-8KfjEg0x8` states one depth — "we ventured out to a little shallow
spot. It's about 175 ft deep" — and calls it *shallow*, so "100–250 ft" is the
routers' band, not this day's, and calling it "the deepest stated band" inverts
how the source frames the stop.

**Hard bottom and hard-to-sand seams are the stated reason fish are there.**
Those seams give [ocean whitefish](../species/ocean-whitefish.md) and
[sheephead](../species/sheephead.md) off the same shallow island stops that
produce the rockfish variety bag (`6-8KfjEg0x8`), and the halibut version of the
same idea is sandy bottom lying right beside a hard edge (`OpcKQPA3vAI`,
[California halibut](../species/california-halibut.md)).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): "hard bottom", "hard-to-sand seams" and
any bottom-composition reasoning are absent from `6-8KfjEg0x8` — it states only
co-occurrence in the shallows ("we just finished up in the shallows… We got some
whitefish. We lost a couple sheephead"), so no *stated reason* attaches to that
cite. The halibut half verifies verbatim from `OpcKQPA3vAI` ("basically sandy
bottom near structure and when I say near structure even 5 ft away from it 10 ft
away from it").

**One sub-feature is named and it carries no position.** The "Santa Rosa flats"
appears once, in a one-breath list of island squid-bed halibut water alongside
backside Catalina, San Clemente and the Coronados in the 60–100+ ft band
(`OpcKQPA3vAI`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the list membership is wrong in both
directions. The spoken list is "that backside East End of Catalina, uh Santa Rosa
Flats, I know there's stuff on the backside of Clemente, I'm sure there's stuff at
**Santa Cruz** and every other island" — Santa Cruz is dropped and the Coronados
are added. The Coronados come from a different, earlier passage about halibut on
a sandy beach "right up against the beach", not the squid beds. The phrase "Santa
Rosa Flats" does occur once, but "Santa Rosa" is spoken twice in the transcript.
Same correction applies to the halibut row in
[What's there](#whats-there) below. No source gives it a lat/lon, bearing, depth or structure of its
own beyond that band, and none exists in
[the spot library](../sources/spot-lists.md), so it stays a name on this page
rather than a spot page of its own (cameron).

**A backside is named and never described.** The backside is the head of the fall
warm-band arc (`OYOda6T3f-8`, `XLVUhV8DW64`) and the April 2022 event treats this
island as a single unit rather than splitting it by face the way it splits Santa
Cruz (`YZtX1MiT0y8`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): `XLVUhV8DW64` does not carry the arc and
never says "Santa Rosa". Its only movement narrative is "that stuff was up off
the West End of cat, west into Clementi at the Nick and SBI for a while… it kind
of moved down off the east end back side of Clementi" — different stations, no
backside Santa Cruz/Rosa, no backside Catalina as a station, and no "499" (its
only numbered marks are the 209 and macro bank 289). The arc rests on
`OYOda6T3f-8` alone, which also ends "it hooks around to Clementi and that's at
499 zone" — Clemente then the 499, not "the 499 and Clemente" — and carries an
ASR-unresolved station between backside Rosa and Santa Barbara Island. The same
`XLVUhV8DW64` co-cite repeats in the bluefin row and in
[How it fishes](#how-it-fishes).
⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source says what either face
looks like underwater or where the divide between them runs.

**The general island read is what a plan has to work from.** Which face of an
island is worth running to is a wind-and-swell decision made per island and per
spot, and each mark has its own preferred conditions learned over repeat trips
([island structure](island-structure.md),
[current structure](../conditions/current-structure.md),
[zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md)).

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: any charted position, sounding, kelp line,
boiler rock, pinnacle, bottom composition or shoreline orientation at Santa Rosa.
No island in this group carries a coordinate in
[the spot library](../sources/spot-lists.md) (cameron), which is why this page
publishes none.

## How it fishes

**The bottom day is two halves: deep for reds, then inside for lings.** Load up
on reds in deeper water, then head inside and fish artificial lures in the
20–30 ft clear water (`Z3rZqy2Pi8E`). The deep half is the standard gangion or
heavy-jig program in
[rockfish deep-dropping](../techniques/rockfish-deep-dropping.md) and
[dropper loop](../techniques/dropper-loop.md); the inside half is what makes the
crossing worth the weather window.

**Inside, the eat is visible and the hookset waits a beat.** A slug on the bottom
is the only presentation the source has actually taken lings on at this island —
the fish rolls on the bottom as it eats, so wait about a second before setting
the hook (`Z3rZqy2Pi8E`,
[lead-head swimbait retrieve](../techniques/leadhead-swimbait-retrieve.md),
[soft-plastic swimbaits](../lures/soft-plastic-swimbaits.md)). An
[inshore crankbait](../techniques/inshore-crankbaits.md) and a
[spinnerbait](../techniques/spinnerbaits.md) are named as something to try on the
same trip, not as a proven eat (`Z3rZqy2Pi8E`).

**The gear class is named for this island by name.** Shallow-water rockfish "up
at San Miguel or Santa Rosa" is one of the stated jobs for a **300-size**
baitcaster — the faster gear ratio and heavier line against the 200-size
workhorse — loaded with **50 lb braid**, the same outfit used for a slug on a
lead head for suspended calicos (`ONH1K2MOp7Q`,
[rod and reel selection](../tackle/rod-and-reel-selection.md)). The heavier
bottom half of the day runs the heavy rockfish class in
[rockfish & lingcod](../species/rockfish-lingcod.md).

**The squid day is a different trip to the same island, fished top to bottom.**
Make squid on the way out of Ventura, then cover the column with a
[dropper loop](../techniques/dropper-loop.md) and a
[sliding sinker](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md) — the slider described as the
way to fish "every level of the water column" from surface to bottom and
especially good with squid at the Channel Islands (`6-8KfjEg0x8`). Weights, hook
sizes and the leadhead and fluke variants for this water are on the
[group page](channel-islands.md) (`nRIAgz5G_Bc`), the squid itself is in
[making bait](../bait/making-bait.md) and [bait tanks](../bait/bait-tanks.md),
and the nest presentation — head pinned through the mantle to mimic a spawn — is
in [leadhead mods & rigging](../rigging/leadhead-mods.md) (`Xr4nURK-Z48`).

**Current is the gate, and the meter alone will fool you.** A spot with squid but
no current meters fish that do not bite: at the islands current is "just as
important as the bait you're fishing," and the conditions make or break the day
out there (`6-8KfjEg0x8`,
[current structure](../conditions/current-structure.md)).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the two quoted fragments verify — "we did
not have much current, which at the islands is just as important as the bait
you're fishing… the conditions make or break it out here" — but the causal
sentence in front of them does not. On that low-current day the boat still caught
("we got some whitefish, we lost a couple sheephead"), and the only marks
language ("some kind of scratchy marks coming through throughout the column")
belongs to the other stop, so "a spot with squid but no current meters fish that
do not bite" is not a claim `6-8KfjEg0x8` makes. Grade the water moving
over the structure before grading the marks on the screen
([electronics and sounder](../planning/electronics-and-sounder.md)).

**This island is the head of the fall arc, which means the pattern moves away
from it.** As the coast cools and the warm band fragments the water tracks a
repeatable path — backside Santa Cruz / Rosa → Santa Barbara Island → backside
Catalina → hooking to the 499 and Clemente (`OYOda6T3f-8`, `XLVUhV8DW64`,
[bight geography](bight-geography.md)). Every later station is further south, so
the fall pattern moves the pelagic fishery off this island while the Santa Ana
opens the bottom fishery on it — which is why the corpus treats it as a late-fall
bottom destination rather than a tuna one
([water regimes](../conditions/water-regimes.md)).

**Coverage up here is thin, and thin coverage is not a dead island.** October
2022 reports repeatedly note "really not a lot of coverage" across the outer
islands and the coast, and the August pattern is the same — with the fleet
offshore the islands stop being reported (`5p6gu14ZC4w`,
[report reading & forecasting](../planning/report-reading-and-forecasting.md),
[August](../seasonal/august.md)).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the quote is verbatim but its geography is
not the northern islands. Both instances in `5p6gu14ZC4w` attach to San Nicolas /
Santa Barbara Island ("really not a lot of coverage uh Nick, SBI, that area up
there") and to the coast ("on the coast really not a lot of coverage either") —
the northern-island segment of that same report is actively reported, including
this island's Sunday-only call.

**Conditions come from a per-position pull, and there is no position.** Santa
Rosa is not a scored spot ([eval targets](bightsst-eval-targets.md)) and has no
charted coordinate in this repo, so a day plan cannot pull SST or chlorophyll for
it — a real loss on an island whose whole bottom program turns on a temperature-
and-wind window ([water temperature](../conditions/water-temperature.md)).
⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: a public position for Santa Rosa Island; one
coordinate would make this island plannable and would mint the northern Channel
Islands zone the [region page](socal-bight.md) is waiting on.

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: a tide, slack, light or current window for
Santa Rosa, or an anchoring versus drifting call at any depth here.
