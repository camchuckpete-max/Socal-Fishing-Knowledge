# Cedros Island

**[Baja only]** — Isla de Cedros sits off the Pacific coast of Baja
California at roughly **28°N**, just inside the northern half of the
[Baja Pacific](regions.md) line. It is a **fly-in island fishery**: no drive
access, no trailer boats, all fishing done from lodge pangas and skiffs. It is
the corpus's benchmark **big-yellowtail-on-the-surface-iron** destination —
30–45 lb fish on the cast, in shallow water, over rock that eats jigs.

Trip-envelope doctrine below is a single 2024 four-day trip filmed across three
videos (Strictly Irons, `IdMJnlg_rZk` / `2cxeg_AE418` / `_MygUHw19q8`; **day 3
dated on camera 2024-06-08**), narrated by **Jared Saaib** — `jared-saaib` is
a registered voice ([source registry](../sources/source-registry.md)). One
operator, **one trip**, so the trip envelope stays `medium` under the
repeated-doctrine rule rather than under any channel cap. The **lodge and
operator specifics are promotional** and sit at `low` per the sponsored-claim
rule. The species detail that pre-dates this batch is
in the species notes and is not restated here.

## Getting there

The access chain is unusual enough to be the first thing a planner needs:

1. **Drive to CBX** (Cross Border Xpress, Otay Mesa, San Diego) — the pedestrian
   bridge into the Tijuana airport.
2. **Bundle rods and gear and walk them across the bridge yourself.** On the
   Mexican side the operator's driver meets you and takes over — you stop
   handling gear from that point.
3. **Shuttle to a private terminal** at the Tijuana airport, ~30 min wait while
   the charter is loaded.
4. **~2-hour charter flight** to Cedros in a small aircraft — roughly **12
   people** (possibly 16), described as genuinely tight, **no restroom**; use
   one before boarding.
5. **~10-minute truck ride** from the airstrip to the lodge; the dock is a
   ~3-minute drive from the lodge.

**An earlier corpus trip used a different route** (StokedOnFishing,
`xIUKmH9ccgQ`, 2012-08-10, a Cedros Outdoor Adventures charter, already logged
in [calico bass](../species/calico-bass.md)): small-plane charter **out of
Ensenada**, with Mexican fishing permits obtained in **Tijuana**. Both routes
are charter flights; the departure point and the paperwork step differ by
operator, so **confirm the route and who handles permits with your operator**
rather than assuming either one.

**Rod length is the practical constraint, and it is generous:** rods travel
laid down the centre aisle of the aircraft. A **10 ft** jig stick fits; a rod
about **6 in longer** than that will still go in. That matters because the
fishery is a full-size-jig-stick fishery — see
[gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md).

## The trip shape

- **Arrival day is a half day.** Land, ~1 hour to rig, fish the afternoon. On
  the trip filmed, that half day produced the first yellowtail inside the first
  session.
- **Middle days are full days**, and the boats will **run south** to hunt the
  bigger grade (40s, with 50s reported on the same trip).
- **Departure day is a short window.** With a **10:30 flight out**, boats leave
  around **5:20 am** and are back at the dock by **8:00–8:30**. That is roughly
  **1.5–2 hours of fishing**, and it is worth taking: the big-fish grounds are
  about **20 minutes** from the dock, and the filmed departure morning produced
  four fish in that window.

## When the fish bite

**The morning bite is the event.** On the trip filmed, the crew moved their
start earlier specifically because the day before they had watched another boat
already hooked up on a 43 lb fish at around 07:00–08:00 as they pulled up. The
adjusted start (05:30 off the dock) had them with **about five fish hanging by
06:30**.

There is also a **late-afternoon push**: the arrival day's last yellowtail
school came up at **17:40**, with the boat back at the dock by 18:30.

**The midday lull has a filler, not a stop.** When the yellowtail shut off
mid-day, the program was to **go in tight on the beach for
[calico bass](../species/calico-bass.md)** on a downsized iron (a 5.5 in
mini-iron) and wait for the yellows to reappear. Calicos are described as
abundant here — *"it's raining calicos out here."*

## Fishing it — what is specific to Cedros

- **The fight is a rock fight, not an open-water fight.** These fish are hooked
  in **~60 ft** over structure and go straight for it. Doctrine from the trip:
  **lock the drag down and pull as hard as you can** — *"these are too big for
  all that noise, just use the leverage"* — and **chase the fish with the
  boat**, backing down when one is headed into the rocks. Expect to lose gear
  anyway: the crew lost **roughly 10 jigs in two days** to fish that rocked
  them. Budget jigs accordingly.
- **The take is soft, not a freight train.** Repeatedly noted on camera:
  *"they're not slamming like a freight train, it's like slow."* Anglers used
  to a Coronados-style surface-iron strike (see below) should expect to feel
  weight rather than a hit, and to wind through it rather than swing — the
  standard [surface iron](../techniques/surface-iron.md) hookset applies.
- **It is not easy fishing, and the video record flatters it.** Stated plainly
  on camera: *"don't look at the videos and think it's easy — the
  opportunities are there, you still have to make the cast, you have to be
  ready."* The last-day session was *"a couple of stops and goes"* producing
  four fish.
- **Line:** **50 lb mono** is fished straight off the blank on these fish. That
  sits alongside, not in place of, the 40 lb floor / "50 lb for most
  everything" Cedros breakdown already recorded in
  [line & leader](../tackle/line-and-leader.md).
- **Jig sizing is fought over in real time.** With fish up and not eating, the
  filmed crew argued the jig was **too small**, went **bigger**, and switched to
  a **flat** profile — *"try a flat, flat might work."* Jig-shape selection
  logic (flat vs concave, hips, nose shape) is in
  [iron jigs](../lures/iron-jigs.md#reading-a-jig--how-to-pick-one-that-swims).

## Doctrine & conflicts

- **Cedros vs. the Coronados — the same species, two different fisheries.**
  The same crew's Coronado Islands sessions (`4pT0efBD1j0`, 2020-08-18)
  ran **10–12 lb** schoolies that *"hit it like a freight train,"* with the
  program built around chumming a metered school up before casting. Cedros ran
  **30–45 lb** fish taking the jig slowly, cast at visible fish and boils.
  Recorded as a grade-and-behaviour contrast between grounds, not as
  conflicting doctrine — and it is the reason a Coronados retrieve/hookset
  habit can cost fish at Cedros.
- **The lodge content is promotional.** Open bar, laundry service, tomahawk
  steaks, massage/jacuzzi in a premium package, a 15-person dock crew that
  handles all gear — all stated on camera by a creator hosted by the operator.
  Recorded as `low` confidence and as trip-comfort context, not as fishery
  knowledge, and deliberately **not** attached to an operator recommendation.

## Planning notes

  general Mexico requirements (FMM, CONAPESCA sport-fishing licence, and the
  fact that they are regulations with jurisdictions and expiry) are recorded

  with CDFW-equivalent Mexican authorities before travelling**; the KB's
  Mexico entries are stamped as of 2026-08.
