# Cedros / San Benitos

**[Baja only]** — the island complex at the south end of
[Baja Pacific — North](baja-pacific-north.md): Cedros Island, the San Benito
Islands, Geronimo Island and Chester's Rock, with Punta Eugenia as the mainland
corner of what one crew calls the "Yellow Tail triangle" (`_r_qKX_7080`). It is
the corpus's benchmark big-yellowtail zone. Resident "home guard" fish of 40+ lb
can turn up anywhere around the island (`WE643Fue1_A`), taken on the cast with a
[surface iron](../techniques/surface-iron.md) in **60–68 ft** over rock that eats
jigs (`IdMJnlg_rZk`); [calico bass](../species/calico-bass.md) are the mid-day
filler and are fished catch-and-release by operator agreement (`ldVj0BoB-kE`).

Access is a charter flight to a lodge running pangas, or a long-range boat out of
San Diego that adds the island to a Guadalupe run (`_r08B4bItAY`). The zone
carries **no charted positions in [the spot library](../sources/spot-lists.md)** —
it exists here because thirty-eight notes carry it, and the named grounds below
have no coordinates in the KB.

## Getting there

**The fly-in chain is the first thing a planner needs, because it constrains the
tackle** (`IdMJnlg_rZk`, `_C7TJoGT0nw`):

1. **Drive to CBX** — Cross Border Xpress, Otay Mesa, San Diego — the pedestrian
   bridge into the Tijuana airport.
2. **Bundle rods and gear and walk them across the bridge yourself.** On the
   Mexican side the operator's driver takes over and you stop handling gear.
3. **Shuttle to a private terminal** at the Tijuana airport, roughly 30 minutes
   waiting while the charter loads.
4. **A ~2-hour charter flight** in a small aircraft, roughly **12 people**
   (possibly 16), genuinely tight and with **no restroom** — use one before
   boarding.
5. **A ~10-minute truck ride** from the airstrip to the lodge; the dock is about
   a 3-minute drive from the lodge.

**Rod length is the practical constraint, and it is generous.** Rods travel laid
down the centre aisle of the aircraft: a **10 ft** jig stick fits, and a rod
about **6 in longer** than that will still go in (`IdMJnlg_rZk`). That matters
because this is a full-size-jig-stick fishery — see
[gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md).

**Weight is the other constraint.** Anglers are allowed **35–40 lb combined**
between tackle and clothes on the charter flight; one angler weighed his own bag
at **28 lb** the night before and called it "in that nice sweet spot," reasoning
he would rather pack light and bring home more fish (`_C7TJoGT0nw`). The lodge
does laundry, so packed clothing volume can run low. Reels stay spooled and
ready with covers on, and **rod straps** keep each bundle tight — with 10–12
people each bringing a bundle, the aircraft stacks them and any one bundle can
end up at the bottom of the stack. Load last and your rods come off first
(`_C7TJoGT0nw`). The specific rods this is written around are in
[Tackle Express — Cedros four-rod quiver](../tackle/tackle-express-cedros-four-rod-quiver.md).

**An earlier charter route flew out of Ensenada**, with Mexican fishing permits
obtained in Tijuana (`xIUKmH9ccgQ`). Both routes are charter flights; the
departure point and who handles the paperwork differ by operator, so confirm both
with the operator rather than assuming either.

**The second access mode is a boat, not a plane.** Cedros and the San Benitos
come up as a bonus stop on San Diego five- and six-day trips whose primary
destination is Guadalupe — "there's always a chance to go to Cedros or San
Benitos" (`_r08B4bItAY`). On one 8-day charter the boat fished bluefin and
yellowfin roughly **170 mi** south of the border on day one, then ran a further
**~150 mi** south to San Benito for day two (`JaKSGkZ6CAc`). Chester's Rock, at
the south end of the complex, sits roughly **300 mi** from San Diego — a
**188 mi** leg plus a further **112 mi** (`_r_qKX_7080`). No trip-envelope detail
(day count at the island, technique, grade) is given for the long-range route;
it is a different operator and access mode to the same water, kept beside the
fly-in trip shape rather than merged into it.

**Fog is a standing navigation fact here.** A Cedros-based captain described
running the island's yellowtail grounds in fog **5–6 months out of the year**, in
zero visibility, navigating out and back on radar alone while the sounder does
the separate job of finding fish — "I'm gonna live or die by my radar, I'm gonna
make a living by my sonar" (`d0yGBQDeY_4`). See
[electronics & sounder](../planning/electronics-and-sounder.md).

**Getting the catch home is part of the lodge program.** Each boat's fish go into
colour-taped bins — red, blue, green tape by boat — so the dock crew can sort by
boat at day's end; the resort has an indoor cutting station and the fish are
vacuum-sealed and frozen ready to travel (`_C7TJoGT0nw`, `iHwvUl0dhxk`). One
angler's trip total was **35–40 lb** of fish taken home frozen, mostly
yellowtail plus calico bycatch and a 20 lb halibut — the same speaker frames
packing light on the way in as the trade for fish weight on the way out.
⚠ Fact-check (unverifiable): that figure rests on a garbled caption digit
string ("3540 lbs") that both cuts of the interview render identically; read as
"35, 40 lbs" it matches the trip's own 35–40 lb baggage allowance far better
than 354 lb of fish would, but it is not caption-confirmed.

**Regulatory — Cedros Island voluntary catch-and-release.** Local fishing
companies at Cedros collectively adopted **100% catch-and-release** for
[calico bass](../species/calico-bass.md), grouper (cabrilla) and black seabass,
on the stated rationale that catches had dropped from routinely "by the hundred"
to "none or very few" under pressure on the **summer spawning aggregations**
(`ldVj0BoB-kE`, `Zo92MG459gQ`). This is an **operator agreement, not a Mexican
federal or state regulation** — compliance depends on the operator, not on
enforcement. Recorded on or before 2023-05-24; **verify current status with your
operator before fishing** ([regulatory claims](../sources/regulatory-claims.md)).

**Regulatory — Mexico, jurisdiction CONAPESCA for fishing and INM for entry,
checked as of 2026-08.** The general requirements (FMM, CONAPESCA sport-fishing
licence) sit on [Mexican waters](mexican-waters.md) and are also recorded under
[Bahía de los Ángeles](bahia-de-los-angeles.md). **Verify current requirements
before travelling.** ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: what a fly-in angler
specifically must carry, or which step of the chain the operator handles.

## Structure & bathymetry

**The zone is a triangle of island rock with a mainland corner.** Chester's Rock
is placed in a "Yellow Tail triangle" with Cedros Island, the Benitos Islands and
Punta Eugenia (`_r_qKX_7080`). Cedros itself is worked as distinct faces: a front
side, a **West End** the crews nickname "Jurassic Park" and reach only rarely
because of wind (`RPSRH0jwyw4`, `3yK3JYrKoZY`), a **North Point** that holds the
bigger grade (`wdbqTio1SQU`), and named stops the crews carry by their own names —
**San Augustine** (`u0scEBby7nA`) and "the Calico spot" (`Zo92MG459gQ`). The
south end runs from **Geronimo Island** to **Chester's Rock** (`3SATCeA3KaU`),
and a bank the crews call "macro banks" sits offshore of the island (`yjwIGFzWO8I`).

**The working depths are shallow, and they are the reason the zone loses tackle.**

| --- | --- | --- |

**Kelp is structure at every scale here.** A hooked yellowtail will use a full
kelp bed to cut you off (`9D3Oiy0ASzg`, `ILBl12Jm7-0`), but a strike also came
right next to a small isolated patch rather than a full paddy — small kelp holds
fish too (`u0scEBby7nA`). A named kelp bed off the Cedros/Gono islands is fished
at anchor with bombed casts allowed to fall (`NGxyOlPx3ug`).

**The bottom taxes terminal tackle on both islands.** A Cedros crew lost roughly
**10 jigs in two days** to fish that rocked them (`IdMJnlg_rZk`); over the jagged
San Benito bottom sinkers and iron have to come up off the bottom immediately
after touchdown or they stay there (`ilINTeknKB4`).

**October frames the zone as a multi-species draw** — resident big yellowtail,
yellowfin, dorado, calico, sheephead and big halibut in one trip (`A8SuzB5qiKE`).
**Grade tracks water state, not the calendar.** On an October 2024 trip the water
had cooled enough to leave yellowfin and dorado prospects uncertain while
yellowtail were "going berserk" (`6EDQtQHEwFE`), and cooler-than-normal water held
the tuna and dorado back until late in the month (`ILBl12Jm7-0`).

**Forage.** Micro red crab in the water and bait metered on the bottom in 183 ft
(`IdMJnlg_rZk`); large mackerel and large "Spanish" supplied off the operator's
own bait barge and bait boat, which goes out the day before to make bait
(`IdMJnlg_rZk`). At the San Benitos the fish key on **squid**, and the squid had
moved out of the shallows because small mackerel had taken over there — nearly
every fish boated had fresh squid in its stomach (`JaKSGkZ6CAc`). Bigger bait
selects bigger fish: crews spend parts of two days catching giant mackerel
specifically to slow-troll them (`Zo92MG459gQ`, `I-QBxuV2p7M`).

## How it fishes

**The trip shape on the fly-in program** (`IdMJnlg_rZk`, `2cxeg_AE418`,
`_MygUHw19q8`): arrival day is a half day — land, about an hour to rig, fish the
afternoon. Middle days are full days and the boats **run south** for the bigger
grade. Departure day is a short window, and it is worth taking: with a 10:30
flight out, boats leave around **05:20** and are back at the dock by
**08:00–08:30**, roughly 1.5–2 hours of fishing, with the big-fish grounds about
**20 minutes** from the dock.

**The morning bite is the event.** One crew moved their start earlier after
watching another boat hooked up on a 43 lb fish around 07:00–08:00 as they pulled
up; off the dock at 05:30, they had about **five fish hanging by 06:30**
(`IdMJnlg_rZk`). Day two of another trip opened running and gunning between fresh
boils, boats hooking up within minutes of each stop (`ILBl12Jm7-0`). There is
also a **late-afternoon push** — an arrival day's last yellowtail school came up
at **17:40** (`IdMJnlg_rZk`) — and a session written off as done reopened into a
roughly 30-minute blitz under gulls, including a triple hookup (`6EDQtQHEwFE`).

**The midday lull has a filler, not a stop.** When the yellowtail shut off, the
program is to go in tight on the beach for calico bass on a downsized iron — a
5.5 in mini-iron — and wait for the yellows to reappear; calicos are described as
abundant, "it's raining calicos out here" (`IdMJnlg_rZk`). Midday **wind** does
the same thing on its own, pushing crews off the morning grounds onto a calico
point (`ILBl12Jm7-0`, `I-QBxuV2p7M`).

**Three outfits work at once, and by the end of a good session all three
produce** — surface iron, [yo-yo iron](../techniques/yo-yo-iron.md) and
[dropper loop](../techniques/dropper-loop.md), "you name it, they're biting"
(`u0scEBby7nA`), with the same three-way mix running through the day at the San
Benitos (`SH7zOA9ZF3o`). Bait fishes alongside them: slow-trolled live mackerel
on circle hooks over the shallow rock (`QSmE3mdEL28`, `Zo92MG459gQ`), a flylined
bait (`RhULLaUsEDk`), and a **1/2 oz** [sliding sinker](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md)
with a nose-hooked sardine for suspended fish (`ilINTeknKB4`, `d0yGBQDeY_4`).
Cast at the **outside edge** of a bait ball rather than into the middle — the
yellowtail holding around it key on the larger livebait over the massed bait
(`d0yGBQDeY_4`).

**Fish are found by eye as much as by meter.** Repeated sight-casts to
individually spotted fish — "spotted him, pointed at him, casted on him" — under
working birds is the documented cast at Cedros (`6EDQtQHEwFE`), and holding the
iron until the yellows actually show under a bird push is the discipline
(`3SATCeA3KaU`). Working seabirds over a wide-open bite is also the stated
trigger for reaching for a jointed three-piece hard bait (`fnaGBGbrhqU`,
`9kGpcEHqIUc`). See
[search & glassing](../planning/search-and-glassing.md).

**The fight is a rock fight, not an open-water fight.** These fish are hooked
close to structure and go straight for it. Chase the fish with the boat and back
down when one heads for the rocks (`IdMJnlg_rZk`). Expect losses anyway — the
rock-cutoff pattern runs across the whole triangle (`_r_qKX_7080`), and at the
San Benitos deep-diving fish look for "anything that's structure, a piece of
weed, anything to rock you off on" (`SH7zOA9ZF3o`).

**The take is soft, not a freight train** — "they're not slamming like a freight
train, it's like slow" (`IdMJnlg_rZk`). Expect to feel weight rather than a hit,
and wind through it rather than swing; the standard
[surface iron](../techniques/surface-iron.md) hookset applies.

**It is not easy fishing, and the video record flatters it** — "don't look at the
videos and think it's easy — the opportunities are there, you still have to make
the cast, you have to be ready" (`IdMJnlg_rZk`). The same spot can go from
repeated hookups to zero bites the next day on heavier gear: "every single day is
different here" (`I-QBxuV2p7M`).

**Jig sizing is argued in real time.** With fish up and not eating, one crew
called the jig too small, went bigger, and switched to a flat profile — "try a
flat, flat might work" (`IdMJnlg_rZk`). Shape-selection logic is in
[iron jigs](../lures/iron-jigs.md#reading-a-jig--how-to-pick-one-that-swims);
downsizing to match small forage — red crabs, small sardines — is
[yo-yo iron](../techniques/yo-yo-iron.md) doctrine attributed to a Cedros angler.

**Seals and sea lions work hooked fish here.** Fish have been taken through the
gut boatside (`6EDQtQHEwFE`), grabbed off the rail while being bled
(`d0yGBQDeY_4`), and lost after a prolonged fight — one crew left a spot with the
bite still going rather than fish through repeated pressure (`XwwIvPFxRiQ`).
Counter-moves are in [fighting big fish](../techniques/fighting-big-bluefin.md).

**The San Benitos fish differently from Cedros.** They are squid-keyed and sit
**30–40 fathoms** down feeding off the bottom rather than up top, moving with the
squid rather than holding a fixed structure depth (`JaKSGkZ6CAc`) — so the rigs
are the dropper loop and the yo-yo, with a **100 lb line minimum** called on
camera for the day's 30 lb-and-better grade (`JaKSGkZ6CAc`). A surface-iron bite
that dies on the anchor can be revived by getting above the marks and drifting
over them instead (`97clKtVsEOs`). Fish can pass under the boat in small
scattered groups rather than schools, and one crew called a repeated **noon**
window at the same spot from the previous day's pattern (`ilINTeknKB4`).

**Offshore, October sequences tuna before yellowtail.** Day one of one 5-day trip
ran straight out **20–30 mi** for yellowfin and dorado rather than working the
resident yellowtail first: dock guidance framed this as standing October logic,
since the resident fish are already there and can be picked up afterwards
(`YUdbrIm9vrE`). The spread is trolled Rapala-class hardbaits run one short and
one long on a **60 lb** line class, and running the **same colour on both lines**
was called the reason doubles came aboard (`YUdbrIm9vrE`;
[trolling](../techniques/trolling.md)). Closer in, feathers pulled over the
outside rim on the run home for a planned **20–30 minutes** connect quickly
(`A8SuzB5qiKE`).

### Doctrine & conflicts

**Section order below is not a ranking.**

- **Drag: locked down, or left alone?** One crew's doctrine is to lock the drag
  and pull as hard as you can — "these are too big for all that noise, just use
  the leverage" (`IdMJnlg_rZk`). Another crew coached an angler mid-fight on a
  big fish to "keep that loose drag" (`RPSRH0jwyw4`), and a mid-fight drag
  increase of "a couple more clicks" was called "always the wrong thing to do"
  (`Zo92MG459gQ`). **What the choice turns on** is *when*: the locked-drag
  doctrine is a pre-hookup setting chosen for shallow rock, while both other
  calls are instructions against changing the setting once a big fish is on. The
  corpus does not record a crew that loosened a locked drag mid-fight, or one
  that fished loose from the cast.
- **Line class: 40/50, or 80/100?** One Cedros breakdown puts the trip's floor at
  **40 lb**, **50 lb** for most of it — short bait leaders and surface iron —
  with **80 lb** carried just in case for black seabass and bigger white seabass
  (`WE643Fue1_A`). Another crew fielded **80–100 lb** as their standing class
  over 28–30 ft rock (`QSmE3mdEL28`), and returned the next day on 65 lb braid to
  **80 lb** fluorocarbon reasoning that "big fish could care less what size line
  you're using" (`I-QBxuV2p7M`). **What the choice turns on** is the technique
  and how close the hookup is to the rock: the 40/50 breakdown is written around
  casting iron and short bait leaders, the 80–100 bracket around slow-trolling
  giant mackerel over structure a boat length away. At Chester's Rock anglers on
  **40–50 lb** landed their fish while several on lighter line lost them in the
  shallow rock (`_r_qKX_7080`), and a mono/fluoro top shot on the iron broke off
  in kelp twice on one trip (`ILBl12Jm7-0`). Full parameters in
  [line & leader](../tackle/line-and-leader.md). The 80–100 lb figure is queued
  in [the fact-check ledger](../sources/fact-check-ledger.md) as a single-crew
  bracket.
- **Straight braid vs a top shot.** One angler advises against straight braid on
  these fish: zero stretch means "you feel every single ounce" of the fight,
  enough to leave you sore the next morning (`ILBl12Jm7-0`). The cost of the top
  shot is a third connection and a casting-past-the-knot hazard, logged in
  [line & leader](../tackle/line-and-leader.md).
- **Cedros against the Coronados — the same species, two fisheries.** The same
  crew's [Coronado Islands](coronado-islands.md) sessions ran **10–12 lb**
  schoolies that "hit it like a freight train," with the program built on
  chumming a metered school up before casting (`4pT0efBD1j0`). Cedros runs
  30–45 lb fish taking the jig slowly, cast at visible fish and boils
  (`IdMJnlg_rZk`). This is a grade-and-behaviour contrast between grounds, not
  conflicting doctrine — and it is why a Coronados retrieve and hookset habit
  costs fish here.
- **The lodge content is promotional.** Open bar, laundry, tomahawk steaks,
  massage and jacuzzi in a premium package, a 15-person dock crew that handles
  all gear — all stated on camera by a creator hosted by the operator
  (`IdMJnlg_rZk`). Trip-comfort context at `low` confidence, deliberately not
  attached to an operator recommendation.

## Spots

**None of this zone's grounds carry a charted position in the spot library** —
they are named grounds from trip footage, and several of the names are
asr-uncertain. They group as the crews run them:

- **Cedros, front side and North Point** — the default water off the lodge dock,
  with the big-fish grounds about 20 minutes out (`IdMJnlg_rZk`) and North Point
  the call for the 30–40 lb grade (`wdbqTio1SQU`).
- **Cedros West End / backside, "Jurassic Park"** — rarely reached because of
  wind, and rarely fished when reached: one crew was among only two or three
  boats on the backside that day and called the calicos untouched, "haven't seen
  lures in months" (`RPSRH0jwyw4`, `3yK3JYrKoZY`).
- **San Augustine and "the Calico spot"** — crew-named stops on Cedros, the first
  a yo-yo spot fished to 130 ft (`u0scEBby7nA`), the second a calico ground that
  also gave up a shallow-water personal-best yellowtail (`Zo92MG459gQ`).
- **The south end — Geronimo Island to Chester's Rock** — "endless miles" of
  calico water that turns into a home-guard yellowtail brawl (`3SATCeA3KaU`);
  Chester's Rock itself is "historically a good big yellowtail spot" for
  40–50 lb fish (`_r_qKX_7080`). When wind blows boats off the rock the fallback
  is the lee of another island near Rose Island / Dondo Ridge — both names
  asr-uncertain — where a "puff of current" bite ran to 40 lb (`_r_qKX_7080`).
- **The San Benito Islands** — a separate island group fished on the same
  long-range routes, squid-keyed and deeper than Cedros (`JaKSGkZ6CAc`,
  `SH7zOA9ZF3o`, `97clKtVsEOs`, `ilINTeknKB4`, `9D3Oiy0ASzg`), and named beside
  Cedros as favourite surface-iron yellowtail ground (`lYyi_Yh3S5g`).
- **Offshore — "macro banks" and the tuna water 20–30 mi out** — a bank named
  only on camera, which gave a mixed yellowtail-and-dorado bite (`yjwIGFzWO8I`),
  and the offshore troll lane for yellowfin and dorado (`YUdbrIm9vrE`).
- **Punta Eugenia** — the mainland corner of the triangle, carried as a name only
  (`_r_qKX_7080`). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: anything about fishing it.

**Not in this zone:** the Dono and Sacramento Reef stops that share trip footage
with Chester's Rock sit roughly 112 mi up-coast of it, off San Martin
(`_r_qKX_7080`) — they belong to the San Quintín grouping, not here.
