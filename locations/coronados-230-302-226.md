# Coronados - 230 / 302 (Kidney Bank) / 226

**A zone is a run grouping.** This is a four-mark piece of the numbered
offshore-bank grid in open water west of the
[Coronado Islands](coronado-islands.md) — the **226**, the **302 (Kidney
Bank)**, **Coronados - 230** and the **371** (cameron) — spanning **13.5
minutes of latitude** (32°16.200'N to 32°29.667'N) and **9.6 minutes of
longitude** (117°32.800'W to 117°42.417'W).

**The 302 is the mark the corpus carries, and it carries three separate uses.**
It is one of the KB's two worked examples of the *compact spot* — the small
definable structure you idle up to and read in twenty minutes rather than hunt
through for half a day (`Blh2BA-7Ono`); it is a named daytime-swordfish working
spot (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`); and it is one of the eight original **BightSST** eval
targets, so the upwelling/turnover model is scored on this water
(cameron, [eval targets](bightsst-eval-targets.md)). The **371** is named once,
as the other half of that same compact-spot pair (`Blh2BA-7Ono`). The **226**
and **Coronados - 230** appear nowhere in the corpus but the coordinate library.
So this is a real zone with one well-documented mark, one type-example mark, and
two that are position-only.

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the **twenty minutes** / **half a day**
figures are not in `Blh2BA-7Ono` — the word "minute" never appears in it. The
whole of that transcript's 302/371 content is one clause, "that's a big area if
you don't know where they are it's not like running to the 302 or 371 or even
running up to uh the stuff off the west end of cat" (@00:08:38), whose only
time guidance is "you should expect to spend quite a bit of time driving
around". The timing is `locations/zone-lexicon.md`'s wording carried here under
a transcript cite.

## Getting there

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: any run distance, heading, timing or fuel

**This is full-day-fleet water rather than an overnight destination.** The
San Diego full-day fleet works "the standard banks outside the Coronados"
(`HzE4FHHcvTk`), and a stretch anchored on "the 302 bank" is described as
"still pretty close to home to San Diego" (`49joKHD7Umc`). ⚠ Flagged gap — no
corpus source names a bank in either sentence, so neither statement is pinned to
one of these four positions; both scope the water this zone sits in.

**The charted positions put the run in scale.** The nearest coastal position in
the spot library is the **Point Loma Pipe**, **20.5 nm** from the 302, **20.7
nm** from the 226, **27.6 nm** from the 371 and **28.6 nm** from Coronados - 230
(cameron). The [Coronado Islands](coronado-islands.md) lie between: every spot
in that zone sits **13.5–19.3 nm** east of the nearest mark here, and the chain
itself runs **~13 nm from the San Diego bay entrance** (cameron). This zone is
therefore roughly
half again to twice the island run, on the far side of the islands.

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-internal): this page states the island-to-zone
spread twice and differently — **13.5–19.3 nm** here against
**14.1–20.1 nm** in the front-matter `distance_nm`. Recomputing each of the
seven [Coronado Islands](coronado-islands.md) spots in
[the spot library](../sources/spot-lists.md) against its nearest of the four
marks gives **14.2–19.3 nm** (Pukey Point 14.15 to the 302; Rockpile 19.32 to
the 371), so the upper bound holds in both places and neither lower bound
reproduces. The front matter's 20.1 nm is a different measure entirely — it is

23.5 nm, outside the stated range. The four Point Loma Pipe distances, the
inter-mark distances and every neighbour figure on this page all reproduce

**Wind on the ride home is the stated transit risk on this line.** A boat
running south past this water should watch for **west wind coming home**,
because it makes for a miserable ride back up the line (`HzE4FHHcvTk`). See
[sea state](../conditions/sea-state.md).

**Regulatory — the jurisdiction is not uniform across this zone, and that is a
gate, not a detail.** Three of the four marks — the 226, the 302 and
Coronados - 230 — sit **8.7 nm, 4.2 nm and 2.0 nm** north of the maritime
boundary as the census approximates it (a line running southwest from the
coastal border at ~32°32.000'N), which is why the zone is gated
`socal-bight`. The **371** falls **6.7 nm on the Mexican side** of that same
approximation (cameron). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source states which side of
the border any of these four positions is on, and the boundary used here is an
approximation in the census code, not a charted line. **Treat a trip to the 371
as a possible Mexican-water trip and confirm the boundary off a current chart
before you fish it** — the paperwork differs completely between
[US waters](us-waters.md) (California / CDFW) and
[Mexican waters](mexican-waters.md) (CONAPESCA for fishing, INM for entry).
**Checked as of 2026-08. Verify current rules before you fish.**

⚠ Fact-check (unverifiable): all four boundary offsets reproduce exactly

## Structure & bathymetry

**The four marks and what separates them.** The 226 sits at **32°29.667'N
117°38.000'W**, the 302 (Kidney Bank) at **32°26.333'N 117°34.750'W**,
Coronados - 230 at **32°21.417'N 117°42.417'W** and the 371 at **32°16.200'N
117°32.800'W** (cameron). The tightest pair is **226 to 302 at 4.3 nm**; the
widest is **226 to 371 at 14.2 nm** (cameron).

**Three of the four clustered; the 371 was attached.** The 226, the 302 and
Coronados - 230 group inside a **9.1 nm** diameter, under the census's **12 nm**
zone-diameter cap, and that trio is the cluster the zone is named for. The
**371** exceeded the cap against the 226 and clustered alone, then joined this
zone under the lone-bank rule — it sits **8.0 nm** from the four-mark centre
and **10.7 nm** from the core trio's centre, inside the **25 nm** attach
radius (cameron). It is carried here as queued
rather than re-parented, because the zone list is set by the census and not by
this page.

**All four names read as depths, and none is confirmed.** A bank is an isolated
high spot rising out of otherwise deep water, named for the **fathom depth of
its shallowest point** as read on the sounder, at six feet to a fathom
([zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md), `Rf1HKJG-SDg`). Read that way the **226** tops
out at **226 fathoms — about 1,356 ft**, the **Coronados - 230** at **230
fathoms — about 1,380 ft**, the **302** at **302 fathoms — about 1,812 ft**, and
the **371** at **371 fathoms — about 2,226 ft**. ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus
source confirms any of the four as a charted depth, or describes relief,
high-spot shape or bottom composition at any of them.

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): `Rf1HKJG-SDg` states the naming rule
with a hedge and two named exceptions this page drops — "most of them are the
banks are called after the highest part of the bank on the fish finder
fedometer" (@00:06:09), and "the nine mile bank that's not nine miles deep
that's nine miles off the coast… the nine mile but it's nine miles off the
beach 14 is 14 miles off the beach" (@00:05:29). The exception class is not
hypothetical here: **North 9 Mile Bank** and **South 9 Mile Bank** are both on
this page's own neighbour list. The isolated-high-spot definition and the
six-feet-to-a-fathom conversion verify verbatim; the shallowest-point rule is
stated three times but never as universal.

**One independent cross-check supports the reading on the 302.** SoCal daytime
swordfishing works **150–350 fathoms** on contour lines, thumbs, notches and
humps, and the 302 is a named working spot in that fishery
([swordfish](../species/swordfish.md), `yMiBtZ7k8-w`). Its name reads inside
that band, as do the 226 and Coronados - 230; the **371** reads deeper than the
band's outer edge. This is consistency between two corpus statements, not a
charted confirmation.

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the cross-check does not hold, for two
reasons. The **150–350 fathom** figure is hedged on camera as one captain's
personal observation, not a band "the fishery works" — "these fish definitely
uh eat it in a preferred depth of water **it seems like**… the hot bites and
the best fishing **i've seen** have been… whether it's in 150 fathoms out to
you know 350 fathoms" (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`, @00:29:21). And it is a **bottom-depth**
band being compared here to bank **crest** depths read off the names — two
different measurements, so a name landing "inside the band" is not evidence of
anything. [The Corner / 140 / 182](corner-140-182.md) already carries this same
finding for its own marks. **ext-verify**: the depth band SoCal daytime
swordfish are actually worked in, and whether crest or surrounding bottom depth
is the operative measure.

**The 302's parenthetical name is not the same bank as Kidney Bank (63).** The
two are distinct spots with colloquially colliding names, and the coordinates
separate them by **99.3 nm** — Kidney Bank (63) is up off Catalina at
33°34.467'N 119°00.850'W (cameron), and the distinction is recorded in the
[zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source explains why
the 302 carries the second name at all.

**The "Coronados" prefix is the collision rule doing its job.** Naming
collisions are resolved by prefixing the closest landmass (cameron), a rule
recorded in the [zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md), and the library also carries a
**Baja - 230** at **31°16.800'N 116°50.800'W** — a different bank in
[Baja Pacific — North](baja-pacific-north.md), far south of this water. Read
"230" without its prefix and the two are indistinguishable.

**Charted neighbours, nearest first** (cameron): the **439** at **12.5 nm** from
the zone centre, the **425** at **14.4 nm**, **San Salvador Knoll** at **15.0
nm**, **South 9 Mile Bank** at **16.0 nm**, the **166** at **16.6 nm**, **North
9 Mile Bank** at **17.3 nm**, **The Corner** at **17.6 nm** and the **182** at
**18.5 nm**. Only one of those groupings has a page on this ladder —
[The Corner / 140 / 182](corner-140-182.md), which reciprocally lists the 226
and the 302 among its own neighbours. ⚠ Flagged stub — no corpus source yet:
the 439, the 425, San Salvador Knoll and the 9 Mile Bank groupings carry no
zone page, so there is little to hand a fallback plan off to.

## How it fishes

**This zone sits on the compact side of the search split, and that decides the
whole day's budget.** The 302 and the 371 are the KB's named examples of a
compact spot: a small definable piece of structure you idle up to, grade on the
meter, and either commit to or leave — **a 20–30 minute read**, not an
expedition. The opposite type is the below-Clemente complex, one ~50–60 square
mile box that costs hours of looking (`Blh2BA-7Ono`,
[zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md), [June–July](../seasonal/june-july.md)). The
planning consequence is that several marks here fit inside the time one search
box would consume, so this zone is worked as a string of reads rather than as a
hunting ground.

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the execution detail in that sentence is
note-authored and sits under a transcript cite. `Blh2BA-7Ono` contains no
**meter**, no **idling up to**, no **commit-or-leave** and no **20–30 minute**
language anywhere — its 302/371 content is the single clause at @00:08:38 and
its only method guidance is "you just gotta look around run bank to bank look
for color changes temperature changes bird life" (@00:09:12). The wording
belongs to [zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md), which is co-cited;
[The Corner / 140 / 182](corner-140-182.md) carries the identical finding. The
~50–60 sq mi figure is itself the hedged, self-inconsistent caption "you get
probably 50 miles 60 square miles to look" (@00:08:46) against "a 60 mile
square area" (@00:09:06), and is already flagged single-source in
[June–July](../seasonal/june-july.md).

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source places the 226 or
Coronados - 230 on either side of that split, and none states the order to work
the four in or which to start on.

**The 302 is a scored conditions spot, which is unusual on this ladder.** It is
one of the eight original BightSST upwelling/turnover eval targets
(cameron, [eval targets](bightsst-eval-targets.md)), so a plan for this zone can
read the model's **time-to-turnover** output on the mark itself rather than
pulling conditions per raw coordinate. The doctrine that output serves is that
fishing is best **right before turnover** — the sharp SST drop plus chlorophyll
spike ([upwelling and turnover](../conditions/upwelling-and-turnover.md),
cameron). The other three marks are not eval targets and are read per position.

**The swordfish read is a conditions read before it is a location read.** It is
a fair-weather fishery — **>15 kt is a no-go**, comfortable at **10–13 kt**,
drift **≤ ~2–2.5 kt** — and the water you want is an SST plus chlorophyll break
where masses converge (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`, [swordfish](../species/swordfish.md),
[sea state](../conditions/sea-state.md),
[water colour](../conditions/water-color.md),
[deep-drop swordfishing](../techniques/deep-drop-swordfishing.md)). Break aging
and advection decide whether the 302 is on the plan on a given day
([report reading & forecasting](../planning/report-reading-and-forecasting.md)).

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): all three gates re-frame `yMiBtZ7k8-w`.
**10–13 kt** is stated as the ceiling, not a comfort band, and only under one
wind direction — "if we have like our traditional just straight northwest wind
then i like 10 to 12 13 would be the **max** for us" (@00:50:25), with
"up until… 11 13 knots **with 10 pounds of weight**" (@00:50:52). **2–2.5 kt**
is the drift at which the presenter says presentation already fails — "anywhere
from a one to two two and a half knot drift so i seem to find when i'm drifting
that fast **i'm not getting the presentation i like**" (@00:51:01) — so writing
it as the acceptable ceiling admits the speed he complains about. **>15 kt** is
a preference with a stated workaround ("guys aren't really wanting to fish",
plus a drift sock), not a no-go. The break is also **disjunctive** in the
source — "temperature breaks chloro breaks" and "whether it's temperature the
chlorophyll" (@00:28:23, @00:52:22) — either edge qualifies, and what the
seminar calls the jackpot is water lined up with *structure*, not SST lined up
with chlorophyll. [Swordfish](../species/swordfish.md),
[The Corner / 140 / 182](corner-140-182.md) and [The 43](43.md) carry the same
finding.

**The one presentation named on this water is a trolled/kited flying-fish
lure.** Private boats working the 2020-06 stretch got their bites on Yummy

[flying-fish harness](../rigging/flying-fish-harness.md) and the presentation in

## Spots

Four charted spots (cameron), listed north to south. Three clustered on their
own coordinates and the fourth was attached on distance:

- **The 226** — the northern mark, **4.3 nm** from the 302 and the closest
  position in the zone to North 9 Mile Bank (**13.1 nm**); the 302 is the closer
  of the two to the 439 and to the southern half of the 9 Mile complex.
  Coordinates only; the corpus never names it.
- **The 302 (Kidney Bank)** — the eastern mark of the core trio and the only
  position in this zone the corpus carries. A named compact spot
  (`Blh2BA-7Ono`), a named
  daytime-swordfish working spot (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`), a BightSST eval target
  (cameron), and the northern anchor of the 2020-06 productive stretch
  (`49joKHD7Umc`). Its second name collides colloquially with Kidney Bank (63)
  off Catalina, 99.3 nm away (cameron).
- **Coronados - 230** — the western mark, and the one whose name needs its
  landmass prefix to stay distinct from Baja - 230 (cameron). Coordinates only;
  the corpus never names it.
- **The 371** — the southern mark, attached to this zone rather than clustered
  into it, the deepest-reading name here, and the one position that falls on the
  Mexican side of the approximated maritime boundary. Its single corpus mention
  pairs it with the 302 as a compact spot (`Blh2BA-7Ono`).

Charted positions for all four are in
[the spot library](../sources/spot-lists.md). No spot here carries corpus detail
beyond its position and, for the 302, the cites above, so each spot page is
coordinates plus its parent zone until more sources arrive.
