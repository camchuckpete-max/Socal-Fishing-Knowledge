# Bass Structure

How to break down the kelp, reef, and hard-bottom structure that
[calico bass](../species/calico-bass.md) and
[sand bass](../species/sand-bass.md) live on: reading it at isolated-spot vs.
superstructure scale, and setting up on the current edge that makes it fish.
This is universal, cross-zone knowledge — it applies wherever bass-holding
structure exists in the SoCal Bight; species behavior and technique routing
live in the species notes.

## Structure & bathymetry

Two scales of structure fish the same way outside the harbor as inside it
(`kwMIgkCtFUE`):

- **Isolated structure** — a single rock, a patch of hard bottom, or a wreck,
  surrounded by open sand. Small enough to fish as one spot.
- **Superstructure** — continuous structure running a few hundred yards to
  several miles, e.g. Point Loma's reef, which runs most of the way to the
  Coronados, or Long Rock (the Santa Ana River pipe off Newport Beach), a
  narrow multi-mile structure holding and feeding bass year-round
  (`kwMIgkCtFUE`). Too big to fish from one waypoint.

Both scales run roughly **20-120 ft** of water (`kwMIgkCtFUE`). ⚠ Fact-check
(single-source): verbatim in the transcript ("20 or 30 feet of water up to
120 feet of water") but corroborated nowhere else in this note's citations.

- **Kelp.** The stringers, the outer edge, the pockets and lanes inside the
  bed, and the deep-water side where the kelp grows off harder bottom. Calico
  relate to the kelp; the **edges and pinch-points** produce, not the dense
  middle (`0KQ--N5TjqE`).
- **Reef / hard bottom.** Often shale, frequently covered by shifting sand —
  invisible on a chart, but the fish finder reads it as a harder return; the
  harder bottom changes the current, which aggregates bait by association and
  in turn holds sand bass and calico (`kwMIgkCtFUE`). Rock, boiler rocks, high
  spots, and the **hard-to-soft transition** where reef meets sand also carry
  fish; sand bass hold on and around hard bottom and the deeper structure,
  calico work the reef edges too.
- **Natural and artificial structure fish the same way**, regardless of
  composition — rock ridges, wrecks, artificial reefs, and submerged pipes all
  hold bass on the same edge logic (`kwMIgkCtFUE`). Isaac's Reef is built from
  dumped concrete light poles and freeway rubble (`kwMIgkCtFUE`) ⚠
  asr-uncertain: the transcript names the beach it sits off as "Alpine to
  Beach," which does not resolve to a place name — not asserted here.
- **A reef that looks good on the sounder can be barren of bass** except where
  fish were actually holding; most of what marks over reef structure is perch,
  not bass (`kwMIgkCtFUE`). ⚠ Fact-check (single-source): verbatim ("pretty
  much everything you're metering for the most part is going to be perch")
  but corroborated nowhere else in this note's citations.
- **The edge is the spot.** Across all of it, the productive water is a
  **defined edge** — kelp-to-open, reef-to-sand, high-spot-to-flat — where
  current pins bait against structure.

## How it fishes

Bass structure only fishes when current sets up a working edge on it. The
**parallel-current rule** governs (`0KQ--N5TjqE`): current running
**parallel** to the structure (uphill or downhill along the coast) creates a
definable leading edge and is the better of the two options; **straight into**
the structure can still produce, less consistently; **straight off** the
structure is treated as unfishable — nowhere for bass to organize. The full
rule, the typology (which structures are two-directional), and the late-fall
into-the-island exception are in
[current structure](../conditions/current-structure.md).

**Read the current off the kelp itself** (`0KQ--N5TjqE`): kelp **pulled
under** = raging current (surface presentations dead); kelp **laid down** =
strong; kelp **resurfacing** = slacking, switch presentation back toward the
surface. ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): `0KQ--N5TjqE` does not state
this three-tier scheme — it ties kelp **laid down** to current direction
(running toward the beach, still fishable on the leading edge), not to a
"strong"-intensity tier, never shows kelp **pulled under**, and never uses
"raging"; only the **resurfacing** = slack = switch back to the surface lure
tier matches the transcript verbatim ("the kelp came back to the surface then
they got back onto the slug"). The full pulled-under/laid-down/resurfacing
table is stated in
[current diagnostics](../conditions/current-diagnostics.md), cited there to
`HnqiE05vdXs` (itself flagged single-source there for the laid-down/
resurfacing rows) — not to `0KQ--N5TjqE`. These on-water tells are collected
in [current diagnostics](../conditions/current-diagnostics.md). Track **water
temperature and water color spot to spot** while moving — relative warmth
against a neighboring spot matters more than an absolute number, and
clean-vs-dirty water at a bite spot is worth logging (`0KQ--N5TjqE`); see
[water temperature](../conditions/water-temperature.md) and
[water color](../conditions/water-color.md).

**Search method scales to spot size** (`kwMIgkCtFUE`). Stop and drop directly
on an isolated spot. On a superstructure spot, blind casting can't cover a
mile of structure — drive it with the fish finder hunting for bait balls
(solid, unbroken bait usually carries **no predators**; broken, harried,
patchy bait usually does), and treat a found bite as a coordinate to relocate
on rather than a spot to sit on. Grade the mark before committing — the same
principle that governs stopping on offshore marks; see
[electronics and sounder](../planning/electronics-and-sounder.md).

Species tell apart on the sounder by behavior more than shape (`kwMIgkCtFUE`):
**perch hold nearly still** under the boat, so the longer they sit there the
bigger their mark grows — easy to misread as a bigger fish like seabass or
yellowtail; **feeding gamefish show movement**, rising and falling through the
water column rather than holding a fixed line; **sand bass usually don't mark
at all** unless they've come up off the bottom to feed.

**Depth follows the bait, not a fixed number** (`kwMIgkCtFUE`). Fish relating
to structure change depth through the day — a spot that produced in 10 ft one
trip can produce in 100 ft the next, because gamefish track bait, bait tracks
zooplankton, and zooplankton tracks where current is upwelling across the
structure that day. A mid-winter bite at the Newport pipe came in 80 ft of
water on crankbaits running only 15 ft deep — the fish rose well off bottom to
reach bait rather than the reverse. ⚠ Fact-check (single-source): the 80 ft /
15 ft numbers are verbatim (`kwMIgkCtFUE`) but corroborated nowhere else in
this note's citations; the "rose off bottom" framing paraphrases the
surrounding point (fish track bait, not depth) rather than quoting a claim
made specifically about this catch.

**Fish orient into the current and sit with the bait, not staged above or
below it.** A feeding fish has to point into the current to hold position, so
it can't face backward to intercept bait behind it — if bait is stacked on the
up-current edge, the fish are there too, not up-current waiting for the bait
to arrive (`kwMIgkCtFUE`).

Work the **up-current edge** so your bait or jig sweeps down onto the
structure the way current-borne food does. On anchor, standoff scales to the
fish: bottom fish → on the spot; midwater/gamefish → up-current, standoff
scaled to current strength; break-off-prone yellows → further still (see the
anchoring geometry in [search and glassing](../planning/search-and-glassing.md)).
Once a presentation is producing, keep running it until conditions change
(kelp resurfacing, current slacking) — switching early loses a working
pattern, and a current direction that produces at one spot tends to repeat at
a different spot on the same current direction on a later trip, worth tracking
across trips (`0KQ--N5TjqE`).
