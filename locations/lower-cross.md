# Lower Cross

**A zone is a run grouping.** Lower Cross is three charted positions on the far
outside of the Baja Pacific bank grid (cameron) — **Lower Cross**, **Catchers
Mitt** and the **950** — spanning **11.0 minutes of latitude** (31°00.000'N down
to 30°49.000'N) and **30.5 minutes of longitude** (117°40.000'W to
118°10.500'W). Lower Cross itself is a genuinely isolated bank: nothing on the
grid clustered with it, and no existing zone centre sat inside the census's
**25 nm** attach radius, so it minted a zone of its own. Catchers Mitt and the
950 then joined it as the nearest grouping they could be fished with.

**No two marks in this zone are inside the clustering cap.** Lower Cross to
Catchers Mitt is **17.3 nm**, Lower Cross to the 950 **14.2 nm**, and Catchers
Mitt to the 950 **27.7 nm** (cameron) — every leg past the **12 nm** zone
diameter the census uses to decide that two banks are one run grouping. Of the
five zones in the library where no internal leg falls inside that cap, this is
the only one holding **three** spots; the other four are pairs. At **27.7 nm**
corner to corner it is also the **widest three-spot zone in the library** and
the widest of the nine isolated-bank zones (cameron). Read the page as a
container of three separate marks, not as a circuit.

**Nothing in the corpus describes fishing any of the three.** Neither Lower
Cross, Catchers Mitt nor the 950 appears in a transcript, a knowledge note or an
evidence file — the whole zone exists on coordinates from
[the spot library](../sources/spot-lists.md) (cameron). This is therefore a
**position-and-ladder page**: where the zone is, what its names claim, what it
sits between, and what is missing. The [region page](baja-pacific-north.md)
carries it as a flagged stub and this page does not lift that stub.

## Structure & bathymetry

**The three positions.** Lower Cross at **31°00.000'N 118°00.000'W**, Catchers
Mitt at **30°58.000'N 117°40.000'W** and the 950 at **30°49.000'N
118°10.500'W** (cameron). From Lower Cross, Catchers Mitt lies **17.3 nm** east
on **097°** and the 950 **14.2 nm** southwest on **219°**; the two outliers are
**27.7 nm** apart on **251°**, the span that sets the zone. Measured from the centre of the
three, the marks lie **5.1 nm** (Lower Cross), **13.5 nm** (the 950) and
**14.6 nm** (Catchers Mitt) out — Lower Cross sits
near the middle because the zone was built around it.

**Upper Cross and Lower Cross are a charted pair on one meridian.** Upper Cross
at **32°00.000'N 118°00.000'W** and Lower Cross at **31°00.000'N 118°00.000'W**
share a charted longitude and sit exactly **60 minutes of latitude — 60.0 nm —
apart**, Upper Cross due north (cameron), in
[Upper Cross / 421 / 390](upper-cross-421-390.md). The same upper/lower
convention runs elsewhere in the Baja grid on **Upper Hidden Bank** against
**Hidden Bank** and on the **Upper 500** against the **Lower 500**.

**These two are the only whole-degree intersections in the spot library.** Of
**391** charted positions, Upper Cross and Lower Cross are the only ones whose
latitude and longitude both land on an exact whole degree — 32°00.000'N and
31°00.000'N on 118°00.000'W (cameron). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source defines
what *Cross* denotes as a structure or naming term, or says whether the pair is
named for that grid crossing, for a feature on the bottom, or for something
else; the coordinates are the whole of the evidence, and a name is not a chart
reading.

**Only one name in the zone claims a depth, and it claims an implausible one.**
A bank is named for the **fathom depth of its shallowest point** as read on the
sounder, at six feet to a fathom ([zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md),
`Rf1HKJG-SDg`):

| spot | number in the name | read as fathoms |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 950 | 950 | about **5,700 ft** |
| Lower Cross | none | no claim |
| Catchers Mitt | none | no claim |

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): `Rf1HKJG-SDg` states the convention as a
tendency, not a rule — "most of them are the banks are called after the highest
part of the bank on the fish finder fedometer" — and names its exception class in
the same passage: "the nine mile bank that's not nine miles deep that's nine
miles off the coast" and "14 is 14 miles off the beach". Its worked examples are
the SoCal 181/182, 209 and 43, and [zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md) carries the
distance-naming exception under its own heading where this table does not —
leaving a third reading of *950* unconsidered beside the fathoms and feet
readings weighed below. "Six feet is in a fathom" is verbatim and the arithmetic
verifies. Sibling rows stand on [179 / 220](179-220.md) and
[Sniffer / West 400 / 300 (The Rampart)](sniffer-west-400-300.md).

Read that way the 950 tops out deeper than any depth the KB fishes: SoCal
daytime swordfish baits average **800–1,100 ft**
([deep-drop swordfishing](../techniques/deep-drop-swordfishing.md)), the
swordfish contour band is **150–350 fathoms** — about **900–2,100 ft** —
([swordfish](../species/swordfish.md)), and the deep rockfish game runs
**300–900 ft** ([rockfish & lingcod](../species/rockfish-lingcod.md)). Read
instead as **950 feet**, the number lands on the depth the deep scattering layer
prefers — **~950 ft / 150 fathoms**
([deep scattering layer](../conditions/deep-scattering-layer.md)) — and inside
the deep-drop bait band. The library does label a spot in feet when it means
feet, and marks it: *Upper Finger Bank rockfish 300ft* (cameron). ⚠ Flagged gap
— no corpus source says whether the fathom convention extends to numbers this
large, or gives the 950 a depth independently of its own name. The same question
is open on the four-digit names to the north
([1010 Trench / 378 / 213](1010-trench-378-213.md)).

**Catchers Mitt belongs to the library's shape-name family, not the numbered
grid.** Across the offshore-bank section the un-numbered names are shapes and
objects — The Pistol, The Worm, Mushroom, Elephant, Peanut Bank, the Sniffer,
The Bumps and Catchers Mitt among them (cameron). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus
source says what shape the name describes, or whether these names report bottom
contour on a sounder screen, a chart outline, or something else.

**Nothing here is attached to land, which sets how it would be worked.**
Standalone structure rising off the plain fishes in **any** direction, but each
individual bank still has its own **preferred directions** learned over repeat
trips ([island structure](island-structure.md)). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus
source: bottom composition, relief, contour, high-spot shape, surrounding depth,
preferred directions, drift or current behaviour at any of these three marks.
The `depth_band` is unknown for that reason, not because the marks are shallow.

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-internal): [island structure](island-structure.md)
says standalone banks "sit off the island with open water on every side and fish
in **any current direction**" — a current-direction typology whose named example
is Farnsworth Bank off Catalina. Dropping "current" turns a statement about
which way the current may run into one about approach direction generally, and
lifts an island-adjacent read onto open-ocean marks **57.6–83.9 nm** from the
nearest charted coastal position. The same row already stands on
[Sniffer / West 400 / 300 (The Rampart)](sniffer-west-400-300.md).

**This is far-offshore water by the library's own arithmetic.** The nearest
charted coastal or island position to any mark here is the **48 Fa. Spot** in
[Punta Banda / Santo Tomas](punta-banda-santo-tomas.md), **57.6 nm** from
Catchers Mitt, **70.3 nm** from Lower Cross and **83.9 nm** from the 950
(cameron). Only six zones in the library have their nearest coastal position
further off than that.

**Charted neighbours, nearest first** (cameron): **East 400** at **13.6 nm**
almost due north of Lower Cross — closer to Lower Cross than either of its own

same crossing from its side; the **Sniffer** **21.0 nm** northwest of the 950 and
**22.2 nm** west-northwest of Lower Cross; **West 400** **25.8 nm** from the 950;
the **250** **25.8 nm** and the **483/500** **28.2 nm** from Lower Cross; the
**300 (The Rampart)** **26.7 nm** from the 950. All of those belong to
[483/500 / 437](483-500-437.md),
[Sniffer / West 400 / 300 (The Rampart)](sniffer-west-400-300.md) or
[372 / 245 / 250](372-245-250.md), whose centres lie **29.8 nm**, **30.4 nm** and
**40.1 nm** from this zone's centre. Catchers Mitt looks the other way: its
nearest neighbours after East 400 are **The Bumps** **31.3 nm** southeast, the
**1140 Finger** **31.9 nm** northeast and the **Lower 500** **34.5 nm**
east-northeast — ⚠ Flagged stub — no corpus source yet: The Bumps has no page on

## How it fishes

**The geometry says this is three separate stops, not a box.** The Bight's
vocabulary separates the complex — a **~50–60 square-mile** box you plan hours of
looking across — from the compact spot you idle onto, grade on the meter, and
either commit to or leave (`Blh2BA-7Ono`, [zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md)). Every
leg here is a transit rather than a search box: the shortest, Lower Cross to the
950, is **14.2 nm**, and the longest is **27.7 nm** (cameron). ⚠ Flagged gap —
no corpus source places any mark here on either side of that split, and the
vocabulary is stated for the Bight rather than for this coast.

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): `Blh2BA-7Ono` carries only the complex

## Spots

Three charted spots (cameron), each of which stands alone at this zone's
spacing. Listed north to south:

- **Lower Cross** — the seed of the zone and the only mark in it that clustered
  with nothing: no bank on the grid falls inside the **12 nm** cap of it, and no
  zone centre inside **25 nm**. It is the southern half of the Upper/Lower Cross
  pair, **60.0 nm** due south of
  [Upper Cross](upper-cross-421-390.md), and one of the library's two
  whole-degree intersections. Its nearest charted neighbour of all is **East
  400**, **13.6 nm** north in [483/500 / 437](483-500-437.md) — nearer than
  either of its own zone-mates (cameron). Coordinates only; the corpus never
  names it.
- **Catchers Mitt** — the eastern attachment, **17.3 nm** east of Lower Cross and
  **14.6 nm** off the zone centre. It is the mark closest to the coast in this
  zone at **57.6 nm** from the Punta Banda spots, the only member whose name
  comes from the shape family rather than the grid, and the one whose other
  neighbours (The Bumps, the 1140 Finger, the Lower 500) all lie in different
  zones **31–35 nm** away. Coordinates only.
- **The 950** — the southwestern attachment, **14.2 nm** from Lower Cross and
  **27.7 nm** from Catchers Mitt, the westernmost and southernmost mark in the
  zone and the only one whose name makes a depth claim. Its nearest neighbour is
  the **Sniffer**, **21.0 nm** northwest in
  [Sniffer / West 400 / 300 (The Rampart)](sniffer-west-400-300.md), which
  records the same distance from its side. Coordinates only.

Charted positions for all three are in
[the spot library](../sources/spot-lists.md). No spot here carries corpus detail
beyond its position, so each spot page is coordinates plus its parent zone until
sources arrive.
