# Cedros West End ("Jurassic Park")

**[Baja only]** — the west end and backside of Cedros Island, in the
[Cedros / San Benitos](cedros-island.md) zone, nicknamed **"Jurassic Park"** by
the crews who fish it (`RPSRH0jwyw4`, `3yK3JYrKoZY`). It is a
[calico bass](../species/calico-bass.md) stretch, and its defining fact is how
rarely anyone gets to it: rounding the corner "does not happen uh practically
ever due to the wind," and the boat filming it had "never ever ever been around
this corner before" (`RPSRH0jwyw4`). The fish there were called untouched —
"these things have not been touched in a long time," calicos that "haven't seen
lures in months" (`RPSRH0jwyw4`).

**It carries no charted position in [the spot library](../sources/spot-lists.md).**
Both sources name it by the end of the island and by nickname only, so a
conditions pull has to be run against the zone rather than against this stretch.

## Getting there

**It is a corner you round, not a mark you run to.** Both documented sessions
reach it the same way — from the front-side lodge water, around the west end of
the island (`RPSRH0jwyw4`, `3yK3JYrKoZY`) — and the 2017 crew ran back around to
the front side afterwards to rejoin the rest of the group for
[yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md) (`RPSRH0jwyw4`). The zone's two access modes — the Cedros fly-in chain and the San Diego
long-range boat — are on [the zone page](cedros-island.md#getting-there); neither
is written around a run to this end of the island.

**Wind is the gate, and it is the reason the fishing is what it is.** Getting
around the West End is a rare event because of wind (`RPSRH0jwyw4`); the payoff
is fish nobody has cast at. The 2019 crew put numbers on the pressure — they were
"one of two boats maybe three that are fishing the backside," calling it a
**window** they had been given (`3yK3JYrKoZY`). Plan it as an opportunistic stop
taken when the wind allows, and judge the day by
[sea state](../conditions/sea-state.md) rather than by the schedule.

**A calm backside can follow a blown week.** The 2019 trip's own recap places a
**hurricane swell and wind** in the week before it, with the island still
producing quality yellowtail and calico around that time (`3yK3JYrKoZY`) —
conditions context, not a rule.

**Regulatory — Mexico.** Mexican entry and sport-fishing paperwork, and the
zone's operator-level **100% catch-and-release** agreement for calico bass,
grouper (cabrilla) and black seabass, are recorded on
[the zone page](cedros-island.md) and [Mexican waters](mexican-waters.md). That
agreement is an operator agreement, not a federal or state regulation. Recorded
on or before 2023-05-24; the Mexican paperwork requirements were checked as of
2026-08; **verify current status with your operator before fishing**
([regulatory claims](../sources/regulatory-claims.md)).

## Structure & bathymetry

**The corpus fishes this stretch and never sounds it.** No depth, bottom
composition or working depth is stated on either trip. The zone's recorded
soundings — the **12 ft** rock at Chester's Rock (`_r_qKX_7080`), the
**60–68 ft** surface-iron band (`IdMJnlg_rZk`), the **18–20 ft** calico reef
(`I-QBxuV2p7M`) — all attach to other Cedros grounds and are on
[the zone page](cedros-island.md#structure--bathymetry).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): `IdMJnlg_rZk` states no depth anywhere;
68 ft is `2cxeg_AE418` and 60 ft is `_MygUHw19q8` — the unflagged copy of a band
already ledgered on the zone page.
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): "the 12 ft rock" reframes a fishing
depth — `_r_qKX_7080` says "we're fishing 12 ft of water" — the unflagged copy
of a framing already ledgered on Chester's Rock.
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): only `_r_qKX_7080` states where its
ground is ("right off of south of sedus Island"); `IdMJnlg_rZk` and
`I-QBxuV2p7M` name no side of Cedros at all, so "all attach to other Cedros
grounds" is unsupported for two of the three.

**What is described is the face, and it is worked in tight.** The shoreline here
is cliff-backed (`3yK3JYrKoZY`; ⚠ asr-uncertain — the caption renders the
crew's remark on the cliffs too poorly to quote), and the 2017 boat was inside
the wave zone while fighting fish, with "watch wave, watch wave" called during a
hookup (`RPSRH0jwyw4`). Expect to be casting at the rock from close in, with
swell the thing that decides how close.
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): "grumpy watch Wave Watch Wave Watch
wave" (@00:03:48, three calls) is verbatim, but `RPSRH0jwyw4` nowhere states
the boat was inside the wave zone, near breakers or in the whitewater — that
is read off the footage, and it is the sole basis for casting-in-tight here.

**There is enough kelp to fool you.** On the 2019 stop a fish that ran like a
big yellowtail turned out to be "just kelp" on the retrieve (`3yK3JYrKoZY`); the
bait fished on that same stop was a weedless rig (see
[How it fishes](#how-it-fishes)).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): "weedless" is not stated of the bait in
use — see the flag in [How it fishes](#how-it-fishes).

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: soundings, bottom composition, relief, kelp
extent, how far the backside stretch runs, or where along it the boats set up.

## How it fishes

**The weedless plastic is the working bait.** Both stops are fished with soft
plastics, and the 2019 angler credits the weedless rig to Corey of MC Swimbaits
on camera — "props to you for showing me the weedless and all that fun stuff"
(`3yK3JYrKoZY`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): "weedless" is spoken exactly once in
`3yK3JYrKoZY` — inside that thank-you credit (@00:17:55) — and no weedless rig
is described or stated to be in use; the stop's swimbait lines (@00:11:32,
@00:12:25) never say weedless, so "the working bait" is read from adjacency.
The captions also spell the name "Corrie MC swimbaits", not "Corey".
The retrieve called out with a landed fish is a fast one, "as
fast as I could wind it" (`3yK3JYrKoZY`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): no landing, species or fish is stated
at either instance of that line, and the two are the same audio duplicated
(@00:12:46 and @00:16:54).
Rigging is in
[soft-plastic swimbaits](../lures/soft-plastic-swimbaits.md), retrieve and
hookset in [swimbaits](../techniques/swimbaits.md).

**Iron is in the mix too.** Comparing the 5 lb calico of the day against an
earlier fish, the 2017 crew called "the iron fish was bigger" (`RPSRH0jwyw4`) —
⚠ asr-uncertain, since
[evidence — calico bass](../species/evidence/calico-bass.md) reads that earlier
bigger fish as a swimbait fish. Either way both a plastic and an iron produced on
the same session; the method is
[calico bass — surface iron](../species/calico-bass-surface-iron.md).

**It bites on arrival — the first casts are the bite.** The 2019 crew was into
solid calico off its opening casts on getting around the corner
(`3yK3JYrKoZY`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the first-cast fish (@00:05:16, "whoa
first cast") is never identified by species; "solid calico bass" is called
~45 s later (@00:06:00). The 2017 half of the bullet is exact — 23 s from
"stop talking start casting" (@00:02:38) to the first hookup (@00:03:01).
The 2017 session went from "stop talking, start casting" to
hooked up inside the first minutes (`RPSRH0jwyw4`). Untouched fish are the stated
reason: the boat had essentially never fished the ground and the calicos there
had not seen lures in months (`RPSRH0jwyw4`).

**Doubles are the signature.** The 2017 stop ran repeated doubles — "we're
doubled up," one angler with two fish at once — through the session
(`RPSRH0jwyw4`), and the 2019 stop repeated it with the "double banger"
(`3yK3JYrKoZY`). Both sessions pair the doubles with the untouched-fish call
(`RPSRH0jwyw4`, `3yK3JYrKoZY`). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: what a slower
bite here would indicate, or how the stretch fishes once it has been pressured.

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: a tide, moon or current read for this stretch,
what depth is being fished, how long to give the stop, what wind speed closes the
