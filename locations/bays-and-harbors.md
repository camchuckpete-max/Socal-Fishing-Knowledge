# Bays and Harbors

How a SoCal bay or harbor is laid out for fishing — the universal structure a
new-to-SoCal angler should be able to name and read on any of them (San Diego
Bay, Mission Bay, Newport, Huntington Harbour, the LA/Long Beach complex).
This note is the *structure*; how each species uses it lives in the species
notes — [spotted bay bass](../species/spotted-bay-bass.md) and
[California halibut](../species/california-halibut.md) are the two you will
fish these features for most.

## Structure & bathymetry

A SoCal bay's structure inventory repeats across San Diego Bay, Mission Bay,
Newport, Huntington Harbour, and the LA/Long Beach complex; read a bay by
finding these features and the **edges** where two of them meet (`YgqXf9iICyg`):

- **Eelgrass flats.** The bay's primary cover and forage habitat — grass beds
  on the shallow flats hold bait, shrimp, and crabs. The **edge of the grass**
  (grass meeting sand or a channel drop), not the middle of the bed, is the
  ambush line.
- **Channels and channel edges.** Bays are cut by deeper, often dredged
  channels that carry the tidal flow; a flat running 0-12 ft drops into a
  channel that runs 20-30 ft in San Diego Bay, with a documented 12-14 ft
  sweet spot in Newport Harbor (`YgqXf9iICyg`). ⚠ Fact-check (single-source):
  three numerically precise depth figures (0-12 ft, 20-30 ft, 12-14 ft)
  resting on one source. Current runs slower in the
  deeper channel than over the flat, so the **drop-off from flat to channel**
  is a current-swept edge: a fish holds on the down-current side, exerts
  little energy to keep position, and lets the tide bring food to it
  (`YgqXf9iICyg`). Dredged, ship-cut channels create the same edge-into-a-gully
  profile at harbor scale — sand bass, halibut, and white seabass hang off
  that edge and take a bait sinking through the water column (`usHl-4SfqDA`).
- **Docks and pilings.** Vertical structure and shade; a piling lets a fish
  draft out of the current the same way a channel edge does, holding position
  with little effort while watching for bait (`YgqXf9iICyg`). Fish sit tight to
  the pilings to ambush bait rather than working the open water around them
  (`AxLlx2Ug-rs`, `usHl-4SfqDA`); older, weathered pilings carry more growth and crustaceans
  than new ones and hold fish better as a result (`usHl-4SfqDA`). Not every
  piling holds a fish — depth, current, bait, and shade at that specific
  piling decide it (`YgqXf9iICyg`).
- **Riprap edges.** The rock lining channels, jetty bases, and harbor mouths
  behaves like the outside riprap in miniature — see
  [break walls, jetties and riprap](breakwalls-jetties-riprap.md) for how the
  rock deviations concentrate current and fish, and how birds on the rocks
  mark them.

## How it fishes

A bay is a **tidal system first**: water pours in and drains out through the
mouth and channels on every tide, and that flow is what turns the structure
on. Tide amplitude tracks the moon — springs (bigger swings, more water moved)
fall on the full and new moon, neaps (smaller swings) on the quarters
(`YgqXf9iICyg`). Slack and the moving-water windows drive the bite; see
[tide and slack](../conditions/tide-and-slack.md).

On the **flood**, water and bait push **in** through the mouth and up the
channels; on the **ebb**, everything drains back out. Set up **up-current of
the structure** so a bait sweeps naturally down onto it, and re-read the
picture at the tide change — a spot that sat on the up-current side of a
channel edge on the flood is on the wrong side once the tide switches. The
current-over-structure mechanism is the same one that governs the outside
coast; see [current structure](../conditions/current-structure.md).

Spotted bay bass sit at the tide-driven extreme of this pattern: they are
**fully tide-driven**, unlike the current-driven, tide-indifferent calico
outside the harbor (`YgqXf9iICyg`).
