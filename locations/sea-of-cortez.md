# Sea of Cortez

**The Gulf side of Baja — a shallow-rock, structure-ambush fishery that plays
like SoCal calico fishing scaled up.** One angler's summary after an hour on
the rocks: *"this is literally calico fishing on steroids"* (`8IRONtdkceM`).
This page documents the **San Felipe mothership** program specifically;
[Bahía de los Ángeles](bahia-de-los-angeles.md) (drive-in, day-boat) and
[Loreto](loreto.md) (fly-in, island fishery) are different trips with their
own pages. Species decision logic lives in the routers —
[cabrilla](../species/cabrilla.md), [yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md);
Sea-of-Cortez tackle detail is in
[rod & reel selection](../tackle/rod-and-reel-selection.md#baja-sea-of-cortez-gear-quiver)
and [line & leader](../tackle/line-and-leader.md).

## Getting there

| Access | What it is | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bahía de los Ángeles** | Drive down, stay in a hotel, day-boat pangas | [BOLA](bahia-de-los-angeles.md) |
| **San Felipe mothership** | Multi-day live-aboard carrying its own pangas | this page |
| **Loreto / Puerto Escondido** | Fly in; island fishery, different species mix | [Loreto](loreto.md) |

A **week to ten days** aboard a mothership out of San Felipe that carries
pangas on deck and relocates overnight between zones, documented here through
a nine-episode series filmed in **2022** (sign-off states "Tony Reyes 2022"
despite 2023 uploads) (`81YHbZQFzgk`) plus a companion tackle breakdown
(`dEPuDrhoClM`).

- **The boat moves at night.** One relocation was an **8–9 hour run** to get
  back to the zone fished on day one, "closer to everything we need to be at
  for the rest of the week" (`aT-fL7vaavY`).
- **Weather governs everything.** That crossing ran **12–15 ft rollers** —
  "some of them coming up to the roof" — and on arrival the pangas stayed on
  deck: *"it's been a little too snotty to take the pangas out"*
  (`aT-fL7vaavY`, `BSNGeduOHYM`).
- **Two fallbacks when the pangas can't launch**, both worth knowing before
  you book:
  1. **Fish the mothership sport-boat style** — "everyone's on the bow
     slinging jigs" (`aT-fL7vaavY`).
  2. **Rotate the pangas that can go.** On a marginal morning only three
     launched, running **three anglers per panga in two-hour sessions** until
     the wind died — "two-hour sessions is pretty quick, but that just means
     we gotta grind really hard" (`BSNGeduOHYM`).
- **Gonzaga Bay is on the itinerary** — the documented trip's last morning
  was spent deep-dropping there (species and catch in
  [What's there](#whats-there) below).

**A naming caution.** One episode is titled "Wide Open Cabrilla Fishing! Bahía
de Los Ángeles" while its own narration says *"I was gone aboard the Tony
Reyes out of San Felipe"* — and the same narrator also calls the grounds "Bay
of LA" (`4Dn7yChmhk0`). Because the mothership relocates for hours at a time,
both labels may be partly true for a single trip. **Treat a San Felipe
mothership episode as San Felipe unless the grounds are named on camera** — do
not file its content under [BOLA](bahia-de-los-angeles.md), which is a
drive-in day-boat fishery with different logistics entirely.

## Structure & bathymetry

**Boiling rocks and visible structure are the read**: *"we're just looking
for nice boiling rocks, good structure — find the structure"* (`8IRONtdkceM`)
— the same boiler-rock and dark-spot doctrine as the
[cabrilla](../species/cabrilla.md) router.

**The rocks, not the fish, set the tackle.** Cabrilla were taken in **six feet
of water** on this program: *"we're in six feet of water… I see now why we
fish 80 [lb]. I think we would have lost a lot of them already"*
(`8IRONtdkceM`) — the shallow, snag-heavy rock forces the heavy line class,
not the fish's size.

## How it fishes

**Jerkbaits draw a reaction strike on a pause placed next to the rock**, not
on a steady retrieve: *"When I get really close to a rock with a jerkbait
I'll go, and then pause for a half second… and on that pause they just launch
out of the water for it."* (`ts9rJgxjmSg`) ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source:
mechanism (why the pause next to the rock draws the strike). This is the
*why* behind the [cabrilla](../species/cabrilla.md) router's
cast-tight-to-the-rock row.

**Surface iron works on cabrilla here too**, and the hookset doctrine holds
in Baja: *"don't swing, just wind — it's a surface iron"* (`ZYAILGyrkRk`) — an
independent Baja corroboration of the wind-through-not-swing rule in [surface
iron](../techniques/surface-iron.md). **Trade-off recorded:** the same angler
switched off surface iron because "surface iron recovery is so bad" — on a
fast, high-volume shallow bite, the slow retrieve costs casts. Kept beside the
surface-iron doctrine, not merged into it.

**Wax Wing** "has gotten bit every cast" on this program (`ZYAILGyrkRk`) — a
Baja data point on the lure, not the SoCal calico execution that [calico
bass](../species/calico-bass.md) and [inshore
crankbaits](../techniques/inshore-crankbaits.md) flag as a stub for; those
stubs stand.

**Yellowtail follow a lure rather than commit to it** — "you gotta follow,
yellowtail follows" (`ZYAILGyrkRk`). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source:
mechanism.

**80 lb line** is what the shallow rock demands (above). Gear is framed
around **bass rods** on this trip — episodes are titled around "grouper on
bass gear" and "big shallow grouper vs bass rods"; full quiver in [rod & reel
selection](../tackle/rod-and-reel-selection.md). **Carry spare split rings
and assist/stinger hooks** — replacing a split ring mid-session comes up
repeatedly (`ts9rJgxjmSg`, `81YHbZQFzgk`); the rock and the volume chew
hardware.
