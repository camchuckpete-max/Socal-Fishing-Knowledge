# South 9 Mile Bank / 439

**A zone is a run grouping.** This is two charted marks in the water off San
Diego — **South 9 Mile Bank** at **32°32.000'N 117°21.000'W** and the **439** at
**32°29.500'N 117°24.000'W** (cameron). They lie **3.6 nm** apart on a
northeast–southwest line — **2.5 minutes of latitude** and **3.0 minutes of
longitude** — the 439 bearing **225°** from South 9 Mile Bank and South 9 Mile
Bank **045°** back. Only four of the census's multi-spot zones span less water
than this one, and the whole of it sits far inside the **12 nm** diameter cap
(cameron).

**This is the south end of the distance-named Nine Mile complex.** Nine Mile
Bank is the corpus's stated exception to fathom naming: it carries many closely
spaced high spots of similar depth rather than one dominant peak, coming up
"almost as high as 600 feet" in places, so no single fathom number describes it,
and the name records that it sits **nine miles off the beach** (`Rf1HKJG-SDg`),
read as statute miles by the [zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md). **North 9 Mile Bank** is
**7.3 nm** at **325°** from South 9 Mile Bank
([North 9 Mile Bank / 178](north-9-mile-bank-178.md), cameron), so the two ends
of the same bank are two zones on this ladder.

**What separates this half from the northern one is the border.** These two
marks are the closest positions the spot library carries under
[US waters](us-waters.md) to the Coronado Islands chain — **5.7 nm** from Pukey
Point (cameron) — and the nearest charted position of any kind, **Lower 9 Mile
Bank** at **0.65 nm** from South 9 Mile Bank, is carried on the far side of the
region line in `baja-pacific-north` ([Coronado Islands](coronado-islands.md),
cameron). Which papers a day here needs is therefore a positional question, and
the KB cannot answer it.

**South 9 Mile Bank is a BightSST eval target** (cameron,
[eval targets](bightsst-eval-targets.md)) — the original "9 Mile Bank (San
Diego)" entry split into North + South — so conditions for half this zone come
off a scored spot rather than a bare position pull.

**No corpus source names either mark.** Neither "South 9 Mile Bank" nor the
"439" appears in a transcript, a knowledge note or an evidence file (cameron).
What this page has instead is inheritance: everything the corpus says about the
Nine Mile Bank as a whole, and everything the geometry says about what this zone
sits between. Each inherited line is marked as inherited, and everything a
source would have to supply is flagged and aggregates in
[the gap report](../sources/gap-report.md).

## Getting there

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: any run distance, heading, time or fuel figure
to this zone from any port, and no source names the port either mark is fished
out of.

**The bay entrance is the useful reference, and this zone is inside 14 nm of
it.** The **Zuniga Jetty** at the San Diego Bay entrance is **10.5 nm** at
**038°** from South 9 Mile Bank and **14.0 nm** at **040°** from the 439
(cameron). For scale, the [Coronado Islands](coronado-islands.md) run **~13 nm**
from that same entrance (cameron,
[Southern California Bight](socal-bight.md)) — this is the same order of run, on
a heading that starts in US water. ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source states a run
time, sea-state envelope or minimum boat range for it.

**The bank's own name is the corpus's only distance statement, and the south end
does not check it exactly.** Nine Mile Bank is named for sitting **nine miles**
off the coast (`Rf1HKJG-SDg`); the source does not say which mile, and the
[zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md) reads them as **statute**, which puts the name
at **7.8 nm** off the beach rather than 9.0. Measured to the nearest charted
positions the library carries, South 9 Mile Bank lies **7.6 nm** — **8.8 statute
miles** — at **224°** from the **Whistler Buoy**, **8.6 nm** — **9.9 statute
miles** — at **268°** from the **Imperial Beach Pipe**, and **9.0 nm** —
**10.4 statute miles** — at **202°** from the **Point Loma Pipe** (cameron —
each bearing is the one steered *from* the named mark *to* the bank). All three are
themselves offshore marks — a buoy and two pipes — rather than shoreline
positions, so they bracket the name without confirming it, and the 439 lies a
further **3.6 nm** out on the far side. ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: a
shoreline or headland position to measure either mark against.

**Where the region line runs through this water cannot be plotted from the KB.**
The library's **International Reef** cluster is described as a sub-pixel cluster
**on the border** (cameron,
[Southern California Bight](socal-bight.md)) and sits **6.7 nm** east-northeast
of the centre of the two, on the **32°32.5–32.7'N** parallels; South 9 Mile Bank is
on **32°32.000'N** and the 439 **2.5 minutes** of latitude south of it, while
**Lower 9 Mile Bank**, **0.65 nm** from South 9 Mile Bank, is carried as Mexican
water (cameron). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source gives the boundary position,
so the crossing point is unplottable here — verify it against a chart before
working the southwest end of this zone. Papers for the two sides are
[US waters](us-waters.md) and [Mexican waters](mexican-waters.md); what a *boat*
run into Mexican water actually requires is itself flagged as missing on
[Coronado Islands](coronado-islands.md).

**This is water an ugly outside forecast does not automatically close.** The
**Catalina Eddy** shields everything **east of Catalina and San Clemente**
(⚠ adjudicated, Cameron, 2026-08-26), and
these marks sit inside that line. The prevailing wind down the California coast
outside SoCal comes from the north, so the islands shadow the water behind them
and being south of San Clemente does not put a mark outside the shelter — the
425 is regularly shielded despite sitting well south of it (cameron). Check the
eddy state when the offshore forecast is bad rather than writing the inside off
(`S2L3KLSQ6Is`, cameron, [sea state](../conditions/sea-state.md),
[island structure](island-structure.md)).
Separately, a strong NW event crushes
the northern Channel Islands and the LA/Ventura shelf hardest while **south of
Newport** is less prone to wind-wave rollover, and the Coronados, Catalina and
San Clemente held their water through one such event (`HzE4FHHcvTk`,
[bight geography](bight-geography.md)). Both marks lie south of Newport and
inshore of every zone in that comparison, and **5.7 nm** off the Coronados that
held (cameron). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source names these two marks on either
map directly. ⚠ Fact-check (cite-unresolved, standing): the NW-event map is
doctrine on [bight geography](bight-geography.md), cited there to
`HzE4FHHcvTk`, and that transcript does not carry it — it is an offshore-only
report holding the paddy geography this page also uses and no NW event, no
Channel Islands and no Newport line, so a second bight report was probably
never recorded. **The map stands and will not be re-sourced to another
video**; Cameron's ruling (2026-08-26) is to back it with statistics instead —
a per-zone SST rollover analysis across recorded NW events. See the standing
flag on [bight geography](bight-geography.md).

**Regulatory — United States. Jurisdiction: California / CDFW. Checked as of
2026-08. Verify current rules, closures, MPA boundaries and take limits before
you fish** ([US waters](us-waters.md)). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source states a
closure, MPA boundary or restricted area at either position; the MPA-labelled
positions the library carries are all up the coast at La Jolla and in South
Orange County (cameron), not out here. Boundary compliance is positional rather
than depth-based, which matters twice in this zone — once for a closure line and
once for the international line ([US waters](us-waters.md)).

## Structure & bathymetry

**The two positions, and the way the line runs.** South 9 Mile Bank is at
**32°32.000'N 117°21.000'W** and the 439 at **32°29.500'N 117°24.000'W**
(cameron) — **3.6 nm** apart, the 439 offshore and south of its partner. The
line is the complex trending away from the beach as it runs south: South 9 Mile
Bank is **9.0 nm** from the Point Loma Pipe and the 439 **12.4 nm** from it
(cameron).

**One mark is a complex, the other is a number, and the corpus explains the
difference.** A bank is an isolated high spot rising out of otherwise deep water
and takes the **fathom depth of its shallowest point** as its name, six feet to a
fathom — 182 tops out at 182 fathoms, 209 at 209, 43 at 43 (`Rf1HKJG-SDg`,
[zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md)). Nine Mile Bank is the stated **exception**: many
closely spaced high spots of similar depth rather than one dominant peak, so no
single fathom figure describes it, and it takes its distance off the beach as a
name instead (`Rf1HKJG-SDg`). ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the source
does not present Nine Mile Bank as the *only* exception — the same transcript
distance-names the 14 the same way ("14 is 14 miles off the beach"), so
distance-naming is a small class in that source rather than a lone carve-out.
Read the two marks that way:

| spot | what the name states | in feet |
| --- | --- | --- |
| South 9 Mile Bank | nine miles off the coast, statute by the lexicon's reading — a distance, not a depth. The complex's high spots run up to roughly 600 ft in places, exact figure uncertain in the source audio (`Rf1HKJG-SDg`), and no source scopes that to the south end | about 600 ft if the complex figure applies here |
| 439 | read by the naming convention, 439 fathoms | about 2,634 ft |

**Read that way the 439 is the deep corner of the inner San Diego bank grid.**
**439 fathoms** is deeper than every charted neighbour's number read the same
way — the **302**, **226**, **178** and **182** — and it falls **below** the
**150–350 fathom** contour band the SoCal daytime swordfish fishery works
([swordfish](../species/swordfish.md),
[deep-drop swordfishing](../techniques/deep-drop-swordfishing.md)) and far below
the **300–900 ft** deep band the rockfish router assigns to offshore banks
([rockfish & lingcod](../species/rockfish-lingcod.md)). The complex's ~600 ft
crown, if it reaches this end, sits inside that rockfish band instead — so the
zone's two names claim water on opposite sides of two fisheries.
⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source confirms 439 as a charted sounding, or gives
relief, bottom composition or surrounding depth at either mark.

**"South" is a position on a bank, not a bank of its own.** The library carries
three Nine Mile waypoints — **North 9 Mile Bank**, **South 9 Mile Bank** and
**Lower 9 Mile Bank** — spanning only **7.3 nm**, inside the **12 nm**
clustering cap, yet the complex is split across **three** zones on this ladder:
the two marks here, [North 9 Mile Bank / 178](north-9-mile-bank-178.md), and
Lower 9 Mile Bank inside the [Coronado Islands](coronado-islands.md) zone
(cameron). What pushes the group past the cap is not the bank but its
neighbours — the **178** is **12.2 nm** from South 9 Mile Bank and **13.3 nm**
from the 439 (cameron). Read the complex as one piece of ground and the ladder
as an artefact of clustering, not as three separate banks.

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-internal): **South 9 Mile Bank and Lower 9 Mile Bank
are 0.65 nm apart** under two names in two different regions — South in
`socal-bight` on this page, Lower in `baja-pacific-north` inside the Coronado
Islands zone (cameron). Either the library carries one mark twice or the region
split runs between two positions closer together than the length of a drift.
Until it is resolved, do not read this page's `socal-bight` label as a statement
about which side of the international line either mark is on.

**Nothing here is attached to land, which sets how it would be worked.**
Standalone structure rising off the plain fishes in **any** direction, but each
bank still has its own **preferred directions** learned over repeat trips
([island structure](island-structure.md)). The one statement the corpus makes
about working this complex points at the **contour edges** rather than the high
spots: on the banks this fishery runs to — "the ridge or the 9 or the 178 or the
302 … or even the 43" — the preferred drift is **down and in along the contour
edges** where the fish dwell and feed (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the transcript's wording of that list is
"the ridge or the the 9 or the 178 or the you know three or two or whatever have
you um or even the 43" — **302** is a transcription inference rather than the
source's word, and the same quoted list is reused twice more on this page. The
"where we usually fish" framing and the down-and-in contour-edge drift are exact.
⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus
source: high-spot count, shape, preferred directions or current behaviour at
either mark.

**This zone is in the good half of the paddy split, and in the pressured part of
it.** The best paddy water in the Bight is **south of San Clemente Island /
Catalina** and the coastal strip from **Dana Point to Oceanside and southward**,
with paddies thinning north of Dana Point; within that, the closer to a dock and
the more boat traffic, the **less willing** the fish (`HzE4FHHcvTk`,
[bight geography](bight-geography.md),
[kelp paddies](../conditions/kelp-paddies.md)). This zone sits well south of
Oceanside and **10.5 nm** from the San Diego Bay entrance (cameron) — inside the
productive strip and inside the traffic. ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source
describes a paddy over either position.

**Presence here is a bait-and-structure question the corpus does not answer for
this water.** Bait stages on a high spot for shelter and the gamefish follow it,
which is the constant across every fishery region (`Rf1HKJG-SDg`,
[zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md)); and the fish "go where the food is — whether
that water moves in over the structure or it's outside of it, that's where
they're gonna be" (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): that second quote is spoken by Rush Maltz
of Key West, Florida — agreeing with the host — not by the San Diego captain
whose SoCal doctrine the rest of this paragraph's cite carries. It is a general
principle in the source, but as cited here it reads as SoCal doctrine from an
out-of-region speaker.
⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source describes
bait, squid, water colour or temperature over either mark, so the forage picture
has to be pulled per position
([water temperature](../conditions/water-temperature.md),
[water colour](../conditions/water-color.md)).

## How it fishes

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: the zone's program in full. Nothing states
which mark to start on, whether the two are run as one circuit, which end to work
first, or what tide, light, current or swell do here.

**The one program the corpus attaches to this bank is the deep-drop drift, and
it is attached to the complex rather than to this end.** On the banks "where we
usually fish … the ridge or the 9 or the 178 or the 302 … or even the 43," the
preferred set-up is a **down-and-in drift along the contour edges** where the
fish dwell and feed (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`). The operator's wind envelope on a
traditional straight NW wind is **10 to 12 or 13 kt** as the maximum, with
**10 lb** of weight and a drift of **one to two-and-a-half knots**
(`yMiBtZ7k8-w`); the species router states the same fishery as a fair-weather
one — **>15 kt = no-go**, comfortable **10–13 kt**, drift **≤ ~2–2.5 kt**
([swordfish](../species/swordfish.md),
[deep-drop swordfishing](../techniques/deep-drop-swordfishing.md)). Whether
either mark here holds the contour the fishery wants is unverified: the 439's
name read as fathoms lands **below** the band that fishery works, and no source
puts a sword drift on this end of the bank.
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the **one to two-and-a-half knot** drift
is stated in the transcript as the point where the presentation fails, not as a
working band — "when I'm drifting that fast I'm not getting the presentation I
like … I think that's about our limit right there" — so it is a ceiling, which is
how the router's `≤ ~2–2.5 kt` reads it. The **10 lb** is likewise his top-end
weight: "the least I fish is like seven … I think I have a ten and a half pound
one". The 10–12/13 kt NW wind maximum is exact.

**Life on arrival is the read this bank is named in.** Rolling out to the spot —
"whether that's the 9 mile bank or the 14 or the 43" — and finding whales,
dolphins and birds is a good sign even though all of it sits a thousand feet
above where the bait is presented, because the reason the mammals and birds are
there is food (`yMiBtZ7k8-w`). Method is in
[bird reading](../conditions/bird-reading.md) and
[search and glassing](../planning/search-and-glassing.md); grading what the
sounder shows over the edges is
[electronics and sounder](../planning/electronics-and-sounder.md).

**Bring a jig stick even when the day is a bottom trip.** The gear call for a run
"to the 9-mile bank fishing cods" is to carry a **jig-stick class** rod because
"you don't know what's going to jump out of the water" (`yn1suHH6b1Y`) — again
scoped to the complex, not to this end. Classes are in
[gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md); the rod itself is
[surface iron](../techniques/surface-iron.md), and the bottom program is
[rockfish deep-dropping](../techniques/rockfish-deep-dropping.md).

**The geometry reads as two compact spot-checks on one leg, and no source says
so.** The Bight's split is between a **~50–60 square-mile** complex you should
expect to spend a lot of the day driving around, not run a spot on, and the
compact piece of structure you idle up to, grade on the meter, and either commit
to or leave (`Blh2BA-7Ono`); the **20–30 minute** budget on that second read is
the [zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md)'s planning consequence rather than a figure
any source states. **3.6 nm** between the two marks is a short
enough hop to read both in a morning (cameron), which is the compact pattern —
except that the bank itself is explicitly the many-high-spots kind of ground
(`Rf1HKJG-SDg`), which is the search type. ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source
places either mark on that split.
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): `Blh2BA-7Ono` carries only half of that
split. It gives the square-mile figure and the driving-around half — "you get
probably 50 miles 60 square miles to look … you should expect to spend quite a
bit of time driving around, don't just expect to drive a spot and find those
fish" — scoped to the water below San Clemente Island (the 43, 181, 182, 289,
the Clemente Ridge up to the Mackerel Bank), not to the Bight generally. The
compact half is not in it: no "idle up to it, grade it on the meter, commit or
leave" contrast appears, only "it's not like running to the 302 or 371". The
figure itself is garbled in the captions ("50 miles 60 square miles", later "60
mile square area") and is never stated cleanly as 50–60 square miles.

**Conditions for the northeastern half come off a scored spot.** South 9 Mile
Bank is one of the BightSST eval targets — the original set listed the bank as
"9 Mile Bank (San Diego)" and the expansion splits it into **North + South**
(cameron, [eval targets](bightsst-eval-targets.md),
[upwelling and turnover](../conditions/upwelling-and-turnover.md)). Cameron's
turnover doctrine is that fishing is best **right before turnover** — the sharp
SST drop plus chlorophyll spike — so the output to read for this zone is the
approach, not the event. The **439** is not an eval target and is pulled per
position (cameron).

**Report coverage should be decent here, and the border cuts across it.** These
are inner banks inside **14 nm** of the bay entrance (cameron), so a quiet week
is more likely to be real than at an outer bank nobody ran to — but the water
**0.65 nm** northeast of South 9 Mile Bank is reported as Coronados water
([Coronado Islands](coronado-islands.md), cameron), so a report naming "the 9"
or "the Coronados" may be describing ground within sight of this zone under
another zone's name. Ageing and advection still apply to anything read off a
report ([report reading](../planning/report-reading-and-forecasting.md)).

## Spots

Two charted spots (cameron), listed northeast to southwest:

- **South 9 Mile Bank** — the inshore, northeastern mark and the south end of
  the distance-named Nine Mile complex rather than a bank of its own
  (`Rf1HKJG-SDg`). It is the **BightSST eval target** of the pair (cameron), the
  closest position in the zone to the bay entrance at **10.5 nm** from the Zuniga
  Jetty, and the closest position the library carries in `socal-bight` to both
  Mexican water — **0.65 nm** from Lower 9 Mile Bank — and the Coronados chain
  at **5.7 nm** from Pukey Point (cameron). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source
  names it, sounds it, or describes the bottom.
- **The 439** — the offshore, southwestern mark, **3.6 nm** at **225°** from its
  partner and the deepest number in the inner San Diego bank group read by the
  naming convention: **439 fathoms**, about 2,634 ft. It is the zone's corner
  furthest from every port position in the library — **14.0 nm** from the Zuniga
  Jetty, **12.4 nm** from the Point Loma Pipe (cameron) — and the mark whose
  side of the international line the KB cannot establish. ⚠ Flagged gap — no
  corpus source names it or confirms the depth its name claims.
