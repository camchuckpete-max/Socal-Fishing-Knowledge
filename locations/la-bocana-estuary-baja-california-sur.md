# La Bocana Estuary (the Estero), Baja California Sur

**[Baja only]** — The estuary behind the beachfront co-op camp at
[La Bocana](la-bocana.md), on the Pacific side of Baja California Sur, named on
camera only as **"the Estero"** (`Cobp85UvHmM`). It is the camp's inside-water
program, distinct from the two grounds the parent page documents — the inshore
rock a few miles off the beach and the tuna ground 25 miles out. The whole KB
record of it is a few minutes of one winter trip, in the upload dated
**2018-02-07** (`Cobp85UvHmM`), which is why nearly every section below is a
flagged gap rather than doctrine.

No coordinates are published here: the estuary carries no charted position in
[the spot library](../sources/spot-lists.md), whose sections stop at San
Quintín, so a day plan cannot pull per-spot conditions for it. Paperwork for
every Mexican trip is [Mexican waters](mexican-waters.md).

## Getting there

**The estuary is reached the way the camp is reached.** Access is the parent
page's fly-then-drive convoy through the CBX cross-border crossing to eight
cabins on the beach ([La Bocana](la-bocana.md)); the Estero sits behind that
camp (`Cobp85UvHmM`). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: how the estuary itself
is fished — panga, skiff, kayak or on foot — where its mouth is, whether the
mouth is passable, or how far the water runs back from the beach.

**Regulatory — Mexico. Jurisdiction: CONAPESCA for fishing, INM for entry;
local rules are set on top of those by the La Bocana co-op. Checked as of
2026-08. CDFW rules do not apply on this side of the line, and a Mexican limit
differing from a California one is two jurisdictions, not a conflict. Verify
current requirements before you fish ([Mexican waters](mexican-waters.md)).**
The co-op's own keep limit is what bites here first: the visiting group was
allowed **one grouper each** (`Cobp85UvHmM`), and grouper is the fish this
water actually produced. That is a co-op agreement recorded on or before
2018-02-07, not a federal or state rule — **confirm the current limits with the
co-op before you fish** ([regulatory claims](../sources/regulatory-claims.md)).

## How it fishes

**Nothing in the corpus says.** The one record gives no tide, no structure and
no technique for the Estero (`Cobp85UvHmM`) — not a bait, not a plastic, not a
retrieve, not a stage of the tide. ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: the read
that makes a day here good or bad, which is the one thing a day plan would most
want from this page.

**The SoCal tide-clock doctrine is a labeled contrast, not a transfer.** In the
[Bight](socal-bight.md) bays, spotted bay bass hold ambush stations keyed to
which way the water is moving, so the same edge fishes differently on the flood
and the ebb ([spotted bay bass](../species/spotted-bay-bass.md)). That is
`socal-bight` doctrine; no source applies it to this estuary, and the bay-shape
and tidal-range figures behind it are SoCal numbers. Treat it as the obvious
first thing to test here, not as something the corpus establishes.

**The camp's other two programs are the fallback.** A day at La Bocana that is
documented start to finish is an offshore run plus the inshore rock, with the
Estero as a side trip — see [La Bocana](la-bocana.md) for the surface-iron
program on the rock in 25–30 ft and the 25-mile troll (`Cobp85UvHmM`).
