# 380

**A zone is a run grouping.** This one is a single charted mark — the **380** at
**30°12.000'N 117°35.000'W** (cameron), the far southwest corner of the charted
offshore grid in [Baja Pacific — North](baja-pacific-north.md). The position in
the infobox is the charted mark itself, not an average of anything: a zone of one
has no centre to compute.

**No corpus source names it.** The 380 appears in no transcript, no evidence file
and no piece of fishing doctrine; the only places it occurs in the repo are its
coordinate line in [the spot library](../sources/spot-lists.md), the flagged-stub
row on [the region page](baja-pacific-north.md), and the neighbour pages that
reach this way and find nothing to hand off — [The Bumps](bumps.md), which
carries a stub for exactly this page, and [179 / 220](179-220.md). The zone
exists on coordinates (cameron), so this is a **position-and-ladder page**: where
the mark is, what water it sits in, what its number does and does not claim, and
what a source would have to supply. Nothing here lifts the region page's stub.
The flags below aggregate in [the gap report](../sources/gap-report.md).

**This is a zone of one because nothing else is within reach of it.** The census
clusters banks under a **12 nm** zone-diameter cap and then attaches a leftover
lone bank to the nearest zone inside **25 nm** (cameron). The nearest charted
position in the whole spot library is [The Bumps](bumps.md), **31.4 nm** away at
**037°** — past both thresholds — so this mark clustered with nothing and
attached to nothing, and minted a zone in its own name. **Nothing charted lies
within 30 nm of it in any direction**, exactly one of the library's 391 positions
lies inside **45 nm**, and only **five** lie inside **60 nm**.

**It is the second-most isolated position in the library, and its nearest
neighbour is the third.** Ranked by distance to the nearest other charted mark:
[12 Mile Reef](12-mile-reef.md) at **35.8 nm**, the 380 at **31.4 nm**,
[The Bumps](bumps.md) at **31.3 nm** and
[San Juan Seamount](san-juan-seamount.md) at **31.0 nm** (cameron) — and those
four positions are exactly the library's four single-mark zones. Two of the four
are in this region and they are each other's company: The Bumps is this mark's
nearest charted position in the entire library, and this mark is the Bumps'
second-nearest, **0.1 nm** behind Catchers Mitt. Two marks that are each other's
closest neighbour and still **6 nm** outside the attach radius is what a zone of
one looks like from both sides.

**Do not confuse it with the 378, the 381 or the 385.** The library charts all
four as separate marks in four separate zones — the **378** **102.9 nm** north in
[1010 Trench / 378 / 213](1010-trench-378-213.md), the **385** **86.4 nm** north
in [385 / 238 / 475](385-238-475.md), and the **381** **158.1 nm** northwest in
[81 / 381](81-381.md), which is in US waters on the other side of the border
(cameron). Numbered names on this grid are depth claims, not a sequence: adjacent
numbers are not adjacent places, and the four nearest numbers to this one are
spread across **158 nm** of coast.

**This is the furthest charted mark in the region from the region's working
port, and the stated Ensenada program stops nowhere near it.** The Ensenada
offshore day plan is a run of **20–30 mi due west** to a temperature break, then
north or west off the break by sea conditions, with a fallback search **20–30 mi**
north and the fleet spread out to **45–50 mi** (`0bcDBGzQnGw`); the furthest
offshore run the corpus puts a number on out of that port is the **42–43 mi** to
the "pretty big bank" fished across an August 2024 bluefin tournament
(`Ix0gG0-l3v0`, [Ensenada](ensenada.md)). This mark lies **102.7 nm** from Punta
Banda and **110.3 nm** from Punta Morro, and it bears roughly **204–205°** from
those positions — south-southwest, not west (cameron). Every Ensenada run the
corpus describes points a different way and stops less than half the distance
out.

**The travel band the corpus puts this latitude in is long-range, not a day
run.** [Colonet](colonet.md), **67.8 nm** northeast of here, is named as
long-range travel rather than overnight fishing (`PRNMGpLj7Pw`,
[179 / 220](179-220.md)), and the reason the water down here goes unfished is
stated as distance rather than fishing: as of **2021-05** the fleet was working
one area west of San Diego and two west of Ensenada, and no boat had been
"down outside of colonet and san [Quintín] in a while… because they haven't had
to go that far" (`fgTmUq78ofQ`). This mark sits **67.8 nm** outside Colonet and
**75.4 nm** outside the nearest [San Quintín](san-quintin.md) position, which
puts it well beyond even the water that statement is about. The **Point Loma
Pipe** off San Diego is **149.2 nm** away (cameron). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus
source states a run to this water from any port, on any boat, in any year.

**Weather is the thing no source scopes here, and there is no land in any
direction to hide behind.** The region's own doctrine is that offshore work off
this coast is chart-first — run to a break, then move by sea conditions
(`0bcDBGzQnGw`, [sea state](../conditions/sea-state.md),
[water temperature](../conditions/water-temperature.md)). ⚠ Flagged gap — no
corpus source: a wind or swell number that turns this zone off, a crossing rule
for this stretch of coast, or any sea-state envelope stated south of Ensenada.

**Regulatory — Mexico. Jurisdiction: CONAPESCA for fishing, INM for entry.

## Structure & bathymetry

**One position, and it is the corner of the library.** The mark is the
**second-southernmost** of the **391** charted positions in the spot library and
of the **125** in its offshore-banks section — only the **Tuna Hole (179)** at
**30°11.000'N 116°29.000'W**, **57.1 nm** due east at **091°**, lies further
south (cameron, [179 / 220](179-220.md)). It is also the westernmost charted
position south of **30°30'N** by more than a degree of longitude. Read the two
together and the shape is a corner: **every one of the other 390 positions in the
library bears between 300° and 100° from here** — northwest, through north, to
just south of east — and the entire arc from **100°** clockwise round through
south and west to **300°** holds **no charted position at any range at all**
(cameron). Read that as a fact about the library rather than about the ocean, but
read it: this mark has fallbacks in one half of the compass and nothing in the
other.

**The name is a depth claim, and it is a deep one.** A bank takes the **fathom
depth of its shallowest (highest) point** as its name, at six feet to a fathom
(`Rf1HKJG-SDg`, [zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md)) — so read literally the 380
tops out at **380 fathoms, about 2,280 ft**, and the number describes the peak,
not the surrounding bottom. That is deeper than any working depth the KB records
for a surface or yo-yo fishery, and **30 fathoms below the outer edge** of the
**150–350 fathom** contour band the KB gives for
[swordfish](../species/swordfish.md) — the deepest structure game it describes.
Its near-twin by name, the **378** in
[1010 Trench / 378 / 213](1010-trench-378-213.md), reads **12 ft** shallower and
sits **102.9 nm** away. ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source confirms the 380 as a
charted or sounded depth, states what the bottom does here, or resolves whether
the fathom convention extends to marks this deep.

**For scale, the depths the corpus does fish on this coast are an order of
magnitude shallower.** The charted [Colonet](colonet.md) high spots at **28 and
41 fathoms** are named as deep enough to call for the heaviest yo-yo iron — a
Salas 7X heavy rather than the 6X or 6X Jr fished at **60–150 ft**
(`hteLeDIy9Qs`, [yo-yo iron](../techniques/yo-yo-iron.md)) — and the name here
reads roughly **nine to thirteen times** those numbers. Nothing in the corpus
describes fishing a bank whose shallowest point is that deep.

**Nothing here is attached to land, which sets how it would be worked.**
Standalone structure rising off the plain has no lee to work against, and the
face to bias toward is the up-current one, where current forced against the high
spot drives the upwelling that stacks the food chain
([current structure](../conditions/current-structure.md),
[island structure](island-structure.md)) — though each individual mark still has
its own preferred directions learned over repeat trips. ⚠ Flagged gap — no
corpus source: bottom composition, relief, contour, surrounding depth, preferred
directions, drift or current behaviour at this mark.

**Everything charted is north and east of it.** Nearest positions in the spot
library, nearest first (cameron): **The Bumps** at **31.4 nm** northeast — which
records the same distance from its side — **Catchers Mitt** at **46.2 nm** and
the **950** at **48.0 nm**, both in [Lower Cross](lower-cross.md), **Lower
Cross** itself at **52.6 nm**, the **Tuna Hole (179)** at **57.1 nm** east, the
**330** at **61.6 nm** in [Colonet](colonet.md), and the **220** and **179** at
**64.7 nm** and **64.8 nm** in [179 / 220](179-220.md). Fifty-six of the
library's positions lie within **100 nm** and **347** of the other **390** sit in
the northern arc between **330°** and **030°**. The nearest coastal structure of
any kind is the [Colonet](colonet.md) pair at **67.8 nm** and **69.8 nm**, then
the [San Quintín](san-quintin.md) marks at **75.4–78.6 nm**.

## How it fishes

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: the zone's program in full. Nothing states an
approach, a starting depth, a drift, or what tide, light or current do here.

**A single mark reads as a compact spot-check rather than a search box, but no
source says so.** The two zone shapes the corpus draws are the complex — a
**~50–60 square-mile** box you plan hours of looking across — and the compact
spot you idle onto, grade on the meter, and either commit to or leave in
**20–30 minutes** (`Blh2BA-7Ono`, [zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md),
[search and glassing](../planning/search-and-glassing.md)). One charted position
is the shape of the second. The consequence of a blank here is harsher than
anywhere else in the library: the nearest fallback mark is **31.4 nm** off and is
itself corpus-silent, and the nearest one with any corpus material at all is
Colonet at **67.8 nm** — so a blank costs the better part of a day's running
before you are looking at anything a source has described.

**If the name is a depth, the surface program and the mark are two different
things.** A peak at **380 fathoms** is not water anyone in the corpus fishes to
the bottom of, so what such a mark can offer a day plan is the current and
upwelling that structure forces on the water above it
([current structure](../conditions/current-structure.md)) rather than a bottom
you work. ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source says how a bank this deep is fished,
or whether it is fished at all.

**The region's own offshore method is the break, not the bank.** The stated plan
is to run west to a temperature break and then work north or west off it by sea
conditions (`0bcDBGzQnGw`), which makes the mark a reference point in a
temperature search rather than a destination in itself
([water temperature](../conditions/water-temperature.md),
[water colour](../conditions/water-color.md),
[water regimes](../conditions/water-regimes.md)). Break position and break age
decide whether this zone is on a given day's plan
([report reading & forecasting](../planning/report-reading-and-forecasting.md)),
and the meter decides once you are there
([electronics and sounder](../planning/electronics-and-sounder.md)).

**This mark is not a BightSST eval target, and the nearest one is 127 nm away.**
Conditions here are pulled per charted position rather than read off a scored
spot ([eval targets](bightsst-eval-targets.md)); the closest scored spot is the
Rockpile at the [Coronado Islands](coronado-islands.md), **127.5 nm** to the
north (cameron) — the largest such separation of any zone in the region, and far
enough that it stands in for nothing out here. The per-position pull is the only
honest conditions read this zone has.
