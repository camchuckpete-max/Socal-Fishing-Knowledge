# La Bocana

**[Baja only]** — A beachfront fishing camp on the Pacific side of Baja
California Sur, described on camera as "just half way down the Baja Peninsula"
(`qri15R3caYE`), and the only documented piece of coast between Punta Eugenia
and Magdalena Bay in [Baja Pacific — South](baja-pacific-south.md). Two
grounds are documented and they are stacked on the same run: **inshore rock
structure** a few miles off the beach camp, and a **tuna ground 25 miles
offshore** that the boat reaches by running out past what the crew called the
"barracuda grounds" (`Cobp85UvHmM`). The town is part of a fishing co-op that
exists to protect the fishery and the local community, and that is the stated
reason a travelling angler finds "no crowds, no other boats" here
(`qri15R3caYE`). The whole KB record of this place is one winter trip — a
first day plus "the next two days" — documented in two uploads dated
**2018-02-01** and **2018-02-07** (`qri15R3caYE`, `Cobp85UvHmM`).

The camp carries **no charted position in
[the spot library](../sources/spot-lists.md)**, whose sections stop at San
Quintín, so no coordinates are published here and a day plan cannot pull
per-spot conditions for it. The estuary behind the camp — the **Estero** — is
fished as its own inshore program and is queued as its own page. Paperwork for
every Mexican trip is [Mexican waters](mexican-waters.md).

## Getting there

**You fish it off the camp, not off a boat run down from the north.** The
destination is a fishing village with a beachfront camp, and the town's co-op
exists to protect the fishery and the local community (`qri15R3caYE`).

**The documented access is fly-then-drive, on an outfitter's convoy.** The trip
on record flew out through the **CBX cross-border crossing** — presented on
camera as an easy option for getting into Mexico — and then drove straight to
the camp with Baja Fishing Convoys (`qri15R3caYE`, `Cobp85UvHmM`).
Accommodation is **eight cabins on the beach**, and the boats launch off that
same beach at sunrise (`qri15R3caYE`). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: the
road route and drive time from the airport, fuel, or any harbor or ramp
alternative to the beach launch.

**Fish travel home with you.** Catch is iced as it comes aboard, an ice truck
services the beach boats in the morning, and the co-op's processing plant
vacuum-seals and flash-freezes the fish for the trip home (`qri15R3caYE`,
`Cobp85UvHmM`).

**Once you are on the water the two grounds are minutes and hours apart.** The
rock is a few miles from the beach camp (`qri15R3caYE`); the tuna ground is a
**25-mile** run offshore, with the inshore "barracuda grounds" passed on the
way out (`Cobp85UvHmM`). A day here is documented as running offshore first
and finishing back on the inshore rock (`Cobp85UvHmM`).

**Regulatory — Mexico. Jurisdiction: CONAPESCA for fishing, INM for entry;
local rules are set on top of those by the co-op (`Cobp85UvHmM`). Checked as of
2026-08. CDFW rules do not apply on this side of the line, and a Mexican limit
differing from a California one is two jurisdictions, not a conflict. Verify
current requirements before you fish
([Mexican waters](mexican-waters.md)).**

**The co-op sets a keep limit on top of the federal rules.** The visiting group
was allowed to keep **one grouper each** (`Cobp85UvHmM`) — a co-op agreement,
not a federal or state regulation. Recorded on or before 2018-02-07; **confirm
the current limits with the co-op before you fish**
([regulatory claims](../sources/regulatory-claims.md)). ⚠ Flagged gap — no
corpus source: any other co-op requirement of a visiting angler, or any
closure or size rule specific to this camp.

## Structure & bathymetry

**The inshore ground is rock, and it is shallow.** The structure worked a few
miles off the beach camp is called out as **25–30 ft** deep (`Cobp85UvHmM`),
and it is the whole reason the place fishes: it holds a resident mixed bag
rather than a single target. Jigs dropped on the same structure were fished to
roughly **80 ft** (`qri15R3caYE`), so the band has depth on it as well as the
shallow crest.

**The inshore band has its own name.** Running out, the crew narrated leaving
"the Barracuda grounds" for "some tuna grounds" (`Cobp85UvHmM`) — the inshore
rock is understood locally as barracuda water, distinct from the offshore
water, which is a working boundary rather than a charted one.

**Offshore, the corpus names no structure at all.** The ground 25 miles out is
described only as "tuna grounds"; the one feature that produced there was
biological, not bathymetric — birds over a school of dorado sitting in water
only about **60 ft** deep (`Cobp85UvHmM`), which is shallow for an offshore
trolling ground. ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: bottom, banks, high spots,
canyon edges or soundings anywhere in the offshore 25 miles.

**Tuna, marlin and wahoo are the stated offshore targets, not documented
catches.** The crew named tuna and marlin as what the 25-mile run was for,
plus a third target heard on camera as "wasps" — asr-uncertain, read as wahoo
against the stated trip target (`Cobp85UvHmM`). One hookup was lost when a
tuna-rigged hook popped, and the
crew speculated on camera — without confirming — that it may have been a
marlin. Nothing offshore but the dorado was landed on camera. Execution for
the run itself is in [trolling](../techniques/trolling.md).

## How it fishes

**The documented day is a two-ground day, and the order matters.** Run offshore
in the morning for the pelagic chance, then finish inshore on the rock
(`Cobp85UvHmM`). The inshore ground is what actually produced across both parts
of the trip, so it is the fallback that always has something on it while the
25-mile run is the swing.

**The offshore find is made on birds, not on structure.** With no bank or high
spot named out there, the dorado school was found by breaking off the run to
check working birds — going to look "only takes 5 minutes", and the casts are
thrown as the boat comes up on them (`Cobp85UvHmM`); see
[bird reading](../conditions/bird-reading.md) and
[search and glassing](../planning/search-and-glassing.md). What told the crew
they had crossed onto the tuna grounds is not recoverable — the on-camera cue
is garbled in the captions ("a bunch of Sanders in the area", asr-uncertain)
and is not read as birds here (`Cobp85UvHmM`).

**Inshore, the iron is the first choice and the bottom is the option not
taken.** The structure also held grouper and sand bass as a bottom option with
sierra cruising alongside, and the crew stayed on the
[surface iron](../techniques/surface-iron.md) anyway (`Cobp85UvHmM`); the same
rock gave up barracuda on jigs dropped to about 80 ft on the earlier day
(`qri15R3caYE`). Plastics and surface iron are the two presentations the corpus
records here (`qri15R3caYE`).

**Boat-to-boat variance is real on the same day.** One boat's group went
without grouper while other boats in the same party reportedly connected
(`qri15R3caYE`) — a co-op fishery with few boats on it still spreads its
results across them.
