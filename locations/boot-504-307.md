# The Boot (504) / 307

**A zone is a run grouping.** This is two charted marks in open water north of
Catalina's West End — **The Boot (504)** and the **307** — **9.3 nm** apart on a
bearing of **067°**, the Boot to the southwest and the 307 to the northeast
(cameron). They are the northernmost offshore banks in the spot library on the
mainland side of the Bight: nothing in the library sits north of the 307 until
the Santa Barbara Channel and Point Arguello group, and the nearest of those is
the **12 Mile Reef**, **62.7 nm** away (cameron).

**The corpus names the Boot once, and it names it for marlin.** In the Bight
report of 2022-09-14, boats fishing the Masters marlin tournament over the
previous weekend "got a lot of marlin uh in by the boot," with a second
tournament running the weekend that followed (`5to3Q5P7w90`). That single line is
the whole documented fishery here — a fleet report, not a described program. The
**307** appears nowhere in the corpus but
[the spot library](../sources/spot-lists.md) (cameron).

**This is LA-side water.** The zone's nearest charted neighbour, **LA - 270**,
carries the landmass prefix for the LA mainland under the spot library's
collision rule ([zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md)) and sits **6.8 nm** from the 307
(cameron) — closer to both marks here than they are to each other.
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-internal): the rule the lexicon states is "when two
spots share a name, prefix with the **closest landmass**"
([zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md), [spot library](../sources/spot-lists.md)), and
LA - 270 at 33°41.000'N 118°34.500'W is **12.4 nm** from Johnson Rock on Catalina
against **36.5 nm** to Pelican Point, the nearest charted mainland position in
the library. Under the rule as written the closest landmass to that mark is
Catalina, not the LA mainland, so either the prefix or the lexicon's statement of
the rule is off. The **6.8 nm** and the "closer to both marks than they are to
each other" arithmetic both verify (6.83 nm to the 307, 7.11 nm to the Boot,
9.35 nm between the pair).

## Getting there

**Catalina is the nearest land, and it lies behind you.** The Boot sits
**16.3 nm** from Eagle Rock on [the back side](catalina-island-backside.md) and
**16.3 nm** from Johnson Rock on
[the front side](catalina-island-front-side.md), with the West End Humps at

Every bearing from these marks back to the island runs south — **159°** from the
Boot to Johnson Rock, **192°** from the 307 to the West End Humps — so the island
is not on the way out here, it is south of the zone.

([front side](catalina-island-front-side.md)).

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the **2.5 hours** in `L_FD-UzvEio` is not

## Structure & bathymetry

**The two positions.** The 307 sits at **33°47.500'N 118°32.000'W** and The Boot
(504) at **33°43.833'N 118°42.333'W** (cameron) — **3.7 minutes of latitude** and
**10.3 minutes of longitude** apart, which works out to **9.3 nm** and puts the
pair inside the census's **12 nm** zone-diameter cap, the clustering rule that
grouped them.

**The nearest mark to this zone is in the next zone.** LA - 270 measures
**6.8 nm** from the 307 and **7.1 nm** from the Boot (cameron) — closer to each
of them than they are to each other — and the 286 is **10.7 nm** from the Boot,
yet both are parented to the LA - 270 / 286 grouping rather than to this one. The
arithmetic is what splits them: a four-mark group would have spanned **13.4 nm**
from the 307 to the 286 and breached the 12 nm cap, so the census cut two pairs
out of four marks that all sit within a short run of one another. Carried here as
queued rather than re-parented, because the zone list is set by the census and
not by this page. The practical consequence for a day plan: this is one piece of
continuous bank ground published across two pages, and a boat working the Boot is
**7.1 nm** from a mark on the neighbouring page.

**The names are the only depth claims in the zone.** A bank is an isolated high
spot rising out of otherwise deep water and takes the **fathom depth of its
shallowest point** as its name, at six feet to a fathom
([zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md), `Rf1HKJG-SDg`). Read that way:

| spot | name read as fathoms | in feet |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 307 | 307 fathoms | about 1,842 ft |
| The Boot (504) | 504 fathoms, from its numeric alias | about 3,024 ft |

⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): `Rf1HKJG-SDg` states the convention as a
tendency and names its exception class in the same passage — "**most of them**
are the banks are called after the highest part of the bank on the fish finder
fedometer" (00:06:06), after "the nine mile bank that's not nine miles deep
that's nine miles off the coast … there's not really one high spot" and "14 is
14 miles off the beach" (00:05:26, 00:05:59). The lexicon carries that exception
under its own heading; this page does not, which leaves a distance reading of
"the 307" unconsidered beside the fathom reading the table builds on. "Six feet
is in a fathom" (00:06:37) and both conversions are exact. Sibling rows stand on
[Lower Cross](lower-cross.md), [179 / 220](179-220.md) and
[Upper 500 / Hidden Bank](upper-500-hidden-bank.md).

**The two readings land in different places.** The 307's figure falls in the deep
third of the **150–350 fathom** contour band the SoCal daytime swordfish fishery
works ([swordfish](../species/swordfish.md),
[deep-drop swordfishing](../techniques/deep-drop-swordfishing.md)). The Boot's
**3,024 ft** is the odd one: that is the same order as the **2,000–3,000 ft**
featureless plain the naming convention describes banks as rising *out of*,
which runs from Dana Point through Newport and Marina del Rey out to Catalina
(`Rf1HKJG-SDg`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the source's word for the bottom banks
rise out of is "**desert**", not featureless plain — "another mountain in the
middle of what you would call a desert there's just nothing around" (00:02:46).
The **2,000–3,000 ft** figure is the ambient depth it gives for the water from
about a mile off the beach out to Catalina — "from dana point newport marina del
rey you are in two to three thousand feet of water" (00:00:54) — and it carries
an exception the sentence drops: "maybe up in huntington a little farther than
anywhere else because we have the flats there" (00:00:32). The source does put
banks in deep water ("we have these giant pieces of water that are so deep and
then we have a little mountain in the middle", 00:02:00), but every depth it
gives *around* a bank is a separate, garbled line ("water can be 300 3000 feet
deep", 00:02:08; "then it pretty much levels off 200 2 000 feet all around it",
00:02:20). Pinning the specific coastal band to the ground the Boot rises out of
is this page's step, and it is what the "odd one" reading rests on.
The Boot is also the one mark in the zone the fishery calls by a
shape name rather than by its number (`5to3Q5P7w90`, cameron).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): `5to3Q5P7w90` uses the word "boot" once,
in "they got a lot of marlin uh in by the boot" (00:02:18), and says nothing
about the name, a shape, or a number; "504" appears nowhere in it. The cite
supports only that the corpus calls the mark "the boot" — the shape-versus-number
contrast is drawn here, and the 504 alias comes from the spot library alone.
⚠ Flagged gap — no
corpus source confirms either number as a charted depth, says what shape the Boot

**Nothing here is attached to land, which sets how it would be worked.**
Standalone structure rising off the plain fishes in **any** direction, but each
individual bank still has its own **preferred directions** learned over repeat
trips ([island structure](island-structure.md)).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-internal): [island structure](island-structure.md)

current may run
into one about approach direction generally, and lifts an island-adjacent read

The "preferred directions learned over repeat trips" half is accurate. Sibling

[Sniffer / West 400 / 300 (The Rampart)](sniffer-west-400-300.md).

two apart.** The **SBI – Nick – Clemente – Catalina square** is where the bigger
[bluefin](../species/bluefin-tuna.md) were in September 2022
(`5to3Q5P7w90`, [September](../seasonal/september.md),
[bight geography](bight-geography.md)).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the source never says bluefin. Its words
are "the volume of fish is still … from the santa barbara island to nick to
clementi to kathleen [Catalina] and that if you make draw a square in there
that's where the a lot of the bigger ones are" (00:02:06), inside a passage about
dorado *and* bluefin mixed. Narrowing "the bigger ones" to bluefin is this page's
inference; the square itself and "a lot of the bigger ones" verify verbatim.
Both marks here lie north of *every*
charted position on Catalina and Santa Barbara Island in the library — by at
least **14.7 nm** of latitude at the Boot and **18.4 nm** at the 307 (cameron) —
so the zone
is outside that box, and in the same report the marlin water is spoken of as its
own place: "if you're up that way it's probably the marlin guys" (`5to3Q5P7w90`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the transcript reads "if you're up that
way it's probably the marlin **guy**" (00:02:40), singular, and the line is a
boat-identification cue rather than a statement about a place — it follows "it's
a confusing when you see a bunch of boats all stacked on top", so "that way"
means wherever the reader sees the stack. The source never calls the marlin
ground its own piece of water and never ties this line to the Boot explicitly;
the tie is sentence order.

**What marlin doctrine says should be true here is unverified on these marks.**
SoCal striped marlin are a **fall** sight-and-troll fishery that lives in the
**clean blue water off a bait/colour edge** and bites the **slack tide**
([striped marlin](../species/striped-marlin.md), `HzE4FHHcvTk`, `6TBxHnkYXI0`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the doctrine is assembled from two
non-overlapping halves and neither cite carries the parts the sentence leans on.
**Fall** is in neither — `HzE4FHHcvTk` is an 08-17 report fishing marlin in
mid-August and makes no seasonal statement; `6TBxHnkYXI0` is a 02-20 inshore
video. **Sight** fishing appears in neither. `HzE4FHHcvTk` gives the troll and
the clear water ("we put the marlin jigs out on the edge of the uh where there
was still bait"; "as we trolled in the clear water where the marlin would likely
live the bait disappeared", 00:06:44) — but that observation runs *against* a
bait-plus-blue-water edge, because on that day the bait sat in the dirty water.
`6TBxHnkYXI0` supplies only the slack tide, as one item in a list — "fish will
bite on the slack tide, blue fin … Marlin bite on a slack tide … SE[abass] is the
same way yellows can be that way" (00:10:55) — with the mechanism attributed to
Sean Morgan and explicitly not endorsed ("**I don't know** he's got a lot of
marlin").
⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source describes water colour, temperature, a bait edge
or a tide window at the Boot or the 307, so the doctrine is what a plan brings to
this zone rather than something the zone is documented to show.

**This zone sits on the thin side of the paddy line.** The best paddy water in
the Bight is **south of San Clemente Island / Catalina** and the coastal strip
from **Dana Point to Oceanside and southward**; north of Dana Point paddies thin
out and hold fewer fish (`HzE4FHHcvTk`,
[bight geography](bight-geography.md)).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the recommended strip verifies verbatim —
"your best bet is i would say for that type of action is south of san clemente
island or south of catalina or along the beach below dana point um towards the
ocean side" (00:03:36) — but the thinning half does not. The source says "when
you get further up the **patties are fewer and further between and they're not
always holding**" (00:03:30), i.e. fewer paddies that fail to hold, not paddies
holding fewer fish, and it pegs the thinning to running "further up … to get on
the inside of cat" (00:03:26), not to a Dana Point line (Dana Point is only the
strip's southern anchor). The source's stated cause is dock pressure, not
latitude — "the closer the patties are to a dock the more likely they are to get
hit" (00:03:46). Load-bearing here: this is
the whole basis for placing both marks "in the scarce half of that split".
Both marks are north of Catalina and
north of Dana Point's latitude (cameron), which puts them in the scarce half of
that split. ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source describes a paddy over either
position either way; mechanics are in
[kelp paddies](../conditions/kelp-paddies.md).

## How it fishes

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: the zone's program in full. Nothing states
which mark to start on, whether the two are run as one circuit, which end to work
first, or what tide, light, current or swell do here.

**The one on-the-water rule the corpus attaches to this water is a boat-traffic
read, not a method.** Boats with **outriggers out, driving back over themselves
in slow circles, are a marlin or tournament fleet, not a tuna stop** — do not
crash it and start Mad-Mac-ing for tuna, because "if you're up that way it's
probably the marlin guys" (`5to3Q5P7w90`,
[striped marlin](../species/striped-marlin.md),
[October](../seasonal/october.md)). Read the fleet before you read the spot.
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the passage is "if you see a bunch of
boats that have outriggers out in their drive back in circles don't take control
and mad back [Mad Mac] for tuna they're they're actually looking for
[mar]lin … unless you want to try and catch [marlin] i'd probably go somewhere
else" (00:02:27). Outriggers, circles and the Mad Mac all verify. "**Slow**
circles" and "driving back **over themselves**" are not in it, and no form of
"crash" appears anywhere — the stated advice is don't start Mad-Mac-ing for tuna
and go somewhere else. "Marlin **or tournament** fleet" is also this page's
addition; the source identifies them only as boats looking for marlin.

**Mid-September is when to expect company.** Tournament marlin fleets are out by
mid-September, and in 2022 two tournament weekends ran back to back over this
water (`5to3Q5P7w90`, [September](../seasonal/september.md)). If marlin is not
the target, that is a reason to be somewhere else; if it is, the fleet is the
sign.
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the source ties only the FIRST tournament
to this water — "during the masters marlin tournament over the weekend they got a
lot of marlin uh in by the boot" — and gives the second no location at all:
"there's another pesky [presumably a caption garble] tournament this weekend"
(00:02:18). "Two tournament weekends ran back to back **over this water**"
supplies a place the report does not. "Tournament marlin fleets are out by
mid-September" is also a generalisation from this one 2022 report; nothing in the
corpus states a tournament calendar.

**On timing, the species doctrine points at the slack.** Marlin bite the slack
tide, and the instruction is to be on the highest-percentage spot **an hour
before the tide change** ([striped marlin](../species/striped-marlin.md),
`6TBxHnkYXI0`, [tide and slack](../conditions/tide-and-slack.md)).
⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source gives a tide or current window for these two
marks specifically.

**The geometry reads as spot-checks rather than a search box, and no source says
so.** The Bight's two zone types are the complex — a **~50–60 square-mile** box
you plan hours of looking across — and the compact spot you idle onto, grade on
the meter, and either commit to or leave in **20–30 minutes** (`Blh2BA-7Ono`,
[zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md)).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the transcript carries only the first half
of the contrast, and loosely — "you get probably 50 miles 60 square miles to
look" and "trying to find a you know 23 foot [boat] in a 60 mile square area"
(00:08:44, 00:09:04) — and it scopes that figure to one named ground, the
below-Clemente group (43, 181/182, 289, Clemente Ridge up to Mackerel Bank,
00:08:21), not to a general zone type. **No time figure of any kind appears in it**: "20–30
minutes" is nowhere in `Blh2BA-7Ono`, whose only "20 30" is shearwaters moving
"about 20 30 feet" (00:17:02). Idling onto a spot, grading the meter and
committing-or-leaving is likewise absent — the 302, the 371 and the west end of
Catalina are named only as the foil. That half traces to
[zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md)'s compact-spot bullet, which carries no cite of
its own. Sibling rows stand on [Lower Cross](lower-cross.md),
[179 / 220](179-220.md) and [Upper 500 / Hidden Bank](upper-500-hidden-bank.md).
Two isolated marks **9.3 nm** apart with open
water between them, plus a third in the next zone **6.8 nm** off the 307
(cameron), reads like a string of compact reads run on one line. ⚠ Flagged gap —
no corpus source places either mark on either side of that split.

**A NW blow is the weather question this zone cannot answer.** A big NW event
rolls the **LA / Ventura coastal shelf** hardest — **5–8 °F** off the surface
temperature — while Catalina and San Clemente held their water through the same
event ([bight geography](bight-geography.md),
[upwelling and turnover](../conditions/upwelling-and-turnover.md)). These marks
sit **16.3 nm** north of Catalina's West End and **36.2 nm** or more off the
charted coast (cameron), between the two halves of that map. ⚠ Flagged gap — no

## Spots

Two charted spots (cameron), listed north to south:

- **The 307** — the northern mark and the northernmost offshore bank in the
  library on the mainland side of the Bight, **6.8 nm** from LA - 270 in the
  adjacent zone and **35.7 nm** from the 14 Mile Bank. Its name read as fathoms
  is the only depth figure in this zone that lands inside a working band.
  Coordinates only; the corpus never names it.
- **The Boot (504)** — the southwestern mark, the closer of the two to Catalina
  at **16.3 nm** off the West End, and the only position in the zone the corpus
  carries: where the tournament fleet got its marlin over the Masters weekend in
  September 2022 (`5to3Q5P7w90`). It is also the one mark here the fishery calls
  by a shape name rather than by its number.

Charted positions for both are in
[the spot library](../sources/spot-lists.md). Neither spot carries corpus detail
beyond its position and, for the Boot, the marlin cite above, so each spot page
is coordinates plus its parent zone until sources arrive.
