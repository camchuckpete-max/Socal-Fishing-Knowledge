# San Miguel Island

**The northwest outer end of the [Channel Islands](channel-islands.md), and a
late-fall bottom-fishing destination that a Santa Ana opens.** The corpus names
this island almost exclusively as one half of "Rosa/Miguel" — the pair of outer
islands the fleet runs to for shallow rockfish and lingcod when the wind lies
down (`Z3rZqy2Pi8E`, `OYOda6T3f-8`, `5D1vx29LVpI`, `5p6gu14ZC4w`). The one
dated day placed on this island specifically is a private boat that fished San
Miguel on a Santa Ana weekend in November 2022 and took limits of reds and
lingcod (`Z3rZqy2Pi8E`,
[rockfish & lingcod](../species/rockfish-lingcod.md)). Everything else stated
about it is either weather — this is the end of the region a northwest blow
hits hardest — or group-level material carried on the
[island-group page](channel-islands.md) and labelled there as such.

**The zone rung above this page is missing, so it hangs off the island group.**
The northern Channel Islands carry no coordinates in
[the spot library](../sources/spot-lists.md) and therefore mint no zone, a gap
the [region page](socal-bight.md) already flags (cameron); `parent` points at
the group page the same way [Anacapa Island](anacapa-island.md) points at the
region. The nearest built ladder page to the northwest is
[Davis Knoll / San Miguel Gap / Rodriguez Seamount](davis-knoll-san-miguel-gap-rodriguez-seamount.md),
the region's NW corner zone, which takes half of its name from this island and
carries the charted [San Miguel Gap](san-miguel-gap.md) position — that
coordinate belongs to the gap, not to the island, so this page publishes none.

## Getting there

**Santa Barbara Landing is the stated jump-off, and the island is picked at the
dock.** The plan described on camera is to trailer up to Santa Barbara Landing
and then go to Santa Rosa or San Miguel "depending on sea conditions and
weather" — the choice between the two islands is made on the day, not before
(`Z3rZqy2Pi8E`). The same reporter frames a mid-week window as a drive up to
Santa Barbara and a run to Rosa, Miguel "or up towards Conception"
(`5p6gu14ZC4w`). Sport boats out of Santa Barbara Landing work the same water
(`Z3rZqy2Pi8E`). This is a different port framing from the Ventura/Oxnard
squid-and-variety day that covers the rest of the group
([Channel Islands](channel-islands.md), `6-8KfjEg0x8`).

**It is a long run, and the weather has to hold for days rather than hours.**
The December 2022 read calls light winds Saturday and Sunday — Saturday the
better of the two days, Friday a little offshore — "a great opportunity to make
a long run" out to Santa Rosa or San Miguel for shallow-water rockfish
(`5D1vx29LVpI`). The go/no-go rule is stricter than a single forecast
day: west and northwest wind blows around Point Conception at **night** and
quits in the morning, so a calm dawn reading is a trap and the crossing wants
**two or more consecutive days** of no wind or Santa Ana conditions before it is
committed to (`Z3rZqy2Pi8E`,
[rockfish & lingcod](../species/rockfish-lingcod.md)). That is why a
**12–24 h wind hindcast** for the route belongs in the conditions pull beside
the forward forecast
([sea state](../conditions/sea-state.md#wind-history-not-just-wind-forecast-1224-h-hindcast)).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the source says "a couple days of
continuous no wind or Santa anas" (@00:02:54), not two *or more*; the
night-wind / morning-calm half is verbatim (@00:02:36).

**A Santa Ana is the window, not merely a nice day.** Santa Ana events flatten
the northwest outer islands and are the stated trigger to run Rosa and Miguel
for shallow rockfish and lings in late fall (`OYOda6T3f-8`, `Z3rZqy2Pi8E`,
[November–December](../seasonal/november-december.md)). Presence is not access
here: the fish are reported as reliable and the crossing is what gates the trip
([sea state](../conditions/sea-state.md)).

**A northwest blow hits this island hardest of anywhere in the region.** After
the April 2022 NW event, San Miguel, Santa Rosa and the front side of Santa Cruz
were in the low 50s °F while backside Santa Cruz, San Clemente, Catalina and the
Coronados held their water, and a beach reading of **56 °F** was called seven or
eight degrees off pre-wind (`YZtX1MiT0y8`,
[bight geography](bight-geography.md),
[upwelling and turnover](../conditions/upwelling-and-turnover.md)). Within the
group the wind order is stated twice: Santa Cruz and
[Anacapa](anacapa-island.md) were called "plenty fishable" for the windy weekend
after 2022-07-21 while Santa Rosa "might be an issue for private boats"
(`Blh2BA-7Ono`), and the April 2022 event put the worst of it on Miguel, Rosa
and the front side of Santa Cruz (`YZtX1MiT0y8`). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus
source: this island's own wind threshold, or which islands stay fishable when it
is blown out. A NW forecast is the first gate on a run up here and the Santa Ana
is the second.
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the **56 °F** is an eyeballed read off
another angler's video, doubly hedged — "it looked like it was like 56 degree
water which is i think seven maybe eight degrees off" (@00:05:11) — and taken
along the beach **out of Long Beach harbour** (@00:04:46), not at these islands;
the low-50s line also drops the source's "in some of those areas" (@00:01:22).
The hardest-hit island list and the held-water list both verify (@00:01:14).

**Regulatory — United States. Jurisdiction: California / CDFW. Checked as of
2026-08. Verify current rules before you fish** ([US waters](us-waters.md)).
The season, depth and bag rules for the bottom targets that are the whole reason
to come here move every year and are registered in
[regulatory claims](../sources/regulatory-claims.md) and in
[rockfish & lingcod](../species/rockfish-lingcod.md) — the recreational rockfish
season closing at year-end is what makes the late-fall Santa Ana window the last
of the bottom season ([November–December](../seasonal/november-december.md)).
⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: closures, MPA boundaries, landing or anchoring
restrictions stated for San Miguel Island itself.

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: a distance, heading, run time, fuel figure or

a boat arrives on.

## Structure & bathymetry

**The only structure described here is shallow, hard and clear.** "A lot of
stuff up there you can fish in 20 or 30 feet of water" is how the inside water
is put, clear enough that a lingcod can be watched rolling on the bottom after
it eats (`Z3rZqy2Pi8E`). The stated day plan loads up on reds in unspecified
deeper water before the boat moves inside, so the working shape of a day here is
two bands rather than one mark (`Z3rZqy2Pi8E`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the "20 or 30 feet of water" quote is
verbatim and scoped to "up there" (@00:01:55), the Rosa/Miguel pair, but the
clear-water lingcod-watching is the reporter's own past catch and the source
places it at **Santa Rosa** — "I caught them at Ro[sa] before you fish slug on
the bottom and get bit" (@00:02:01, ASR "Row three") — not at this island. The
two-band day plan is verbatim (@00:02:12) and stated for Rosa or Miguel jointly.

⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: any charted position, sounding, kelp line,
boiler rock, pinnacle, bottom composition or shoreline orientation at San
Miguel. No island in this group carries a coordinate in
[the spot library](../sources/spot-lists.md) (cameron), which is why this page
publishes none — the charted [San Miguel Gap](san-miguel-gap.md) position
belongs to the seafloor gap northwest of here, not to the island.

**The general island read is what a plan has to work from.** Which face of an
island is worth running to is a wind-and-swell decision made per island and per
spot, and each mark has its own preferred conditions learned over repeat trips
([island structure](island-structure.md),
[current structure](../conditions/current-structure.md),
[zone lexicon](zone-lexicon.md)). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source names a
front side, a backside or a single spot on this island, or says which face to
start on for a given wind and swell.

**The shallow band the group is known for is not documented here.** The
100–250 ft island variety drift — reds, blue bass, bocaccio and lingcod off one
rock, with one stop given as roughly 175 ft — is scoped on camera to Santa Cruz,
Santa Rosa and [Santa Barbara Island](santa-barbara-island.md) (`6-8KfjEg0x8`,
[Channel Islands](channel-islands.md)). ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source says
whether that band fishes the same way at San Miguel.
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): `6-8KfjEg0x8` names no individual island
anywhere — only "the Channel Islands" and "just outside of Ventura" (@00:00:23)
— and gives no 100–250 ft band; its single depth figure is one stop, "a little
shallow spot. It's about 175 ft deep" (@00:03:01). The one-rock species list is
verbatim (@00:06:16). The scoping to three named islands, and with it the
negative inference that the band is "never" scoped to this island, is not in the
source.

## How it fishes

**The day is two halves: deep for reds, then inside for lings.** Load up on reds
in deeper water, then head inside and fish artificial lures in the 20–30 ft
clear water (`Z3rZqy2Pi8E`). The deep half is the standard gangion or heavy-jig
program in [rockfish deep-dropping](../techniques/rockfish-deep-dropping.md) and
[dropper loop](../techniques/dropper-loop.md); the inside half is what makes this
island worth the crossing.

**Inside, the eat is visible and the hookset waits a beat.** A slug on the
bottom is the only presentation the source has actually taken lings on in that
water — the fish rolls on the bottom as it eats, so wait about a second before
setting the hook (`Z3rZqy2Pi8E`,
[lead-head swimbait retrieve](../techniques/leadhead-swimbait-retrieve.md),
[soft-plastic swimbaits](../lures/soft-plastic-swimbaits.md)). An
[inshore crankbait](../techniques/inshore-crankbaits.md) and a
[spinnerbait](../techniques/spinnerbaits.md) are named as something to try on
the same trip, not as a proven eat (`Z3rZqy2Pi8E`).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the source claims no exclusivity — "the
only presentation" is this page's word, and the same passage then names a
crankbait and a spinnerbait — and the slug-on-the-bottom catch it describes is
the reporter's own, placed at **Santa Rosa** ("I caught them at Ro[sa] before
you fish slug on the bottom and get bit", @00:02:01). The hookset is verbatim in
substance ("get bit and when a second can you set the hook", @00:02:05, ASR for
"wait a second, then you set the hook") and the crankbait/spinnerbait intention
is verbatim (@00:02:17); the lead-head gloss is the page's, since the
transcript's only slug-and-head spec belongs to the San Clemente calico program
(@00:07:38).

**The gear class is named for this island by name.** Shallow-water rockfish "up
at San Miguel or Santa Rosa" is one of the stated jobs for a **300-size**
baitcaster — the faster gear ratio and heavier line against the 200-size
workhorse — loaded with **50 lb braid**, the same outfit used for a slug on a
lead head for suspended calicos (`ONH1K2MOp7Q`,
[rod and reel selection](../tackle/rod-and-reel-selection.md)). The heavier
bottom half of the day runs the heavy rockfish class in
[rockfish & lingcod](../species/rockfish-lingcod.md).

**The fall arc runs away from this island, not onto it.** As the coast cools and
the warm band fragments, the water tracks a repeatable path — backside Santa
Cruz and Rosa, then [Santa Barbara Island](santa-barbara-island.md), then
backside Catalina, then hooking to the 499 and Clemente (`XLVUhV8DW64`,
`OYOda6T3f-8`, [bight geography](bight-geography.md),
[October](../seasonal/october.md)). This island sits at the head of that arc and
every later station is further south, which is consistent with the corpus
treating it as a bottom-fishing destination in late fall rather than a pelagic
one ([water regimes](../conditions/water-regimes.md)).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): `XLVUhV8DW64` carries none of this chain
— no Santa Cruz, no Santa Rosa, no 499; its only movement track is bluefin
running Catalina West End → Clemente/the Nick/SBI → the east end and backside of
Clemente (@00:03:26). The whole path belongs to `OYOda6T3f-8` (@00:03:07,
@00:03:54), which names Clemente and the 499 as one terminal zone ("it hooks
around to Clementi and that's at 499 Zone"), adds a Dirt Clods leg the note
drops, and describes a current chart snapshot rather than a repeatable path.
Same finding already ledgered from
`locations/12-mile-reef.md`.

**Coverage up here is thin, and thin coverage is not a dead island.** October
2022 reports repeatedly note "really not a lot of coverage" across the outer
islands and the coast, and the August pattern is the same — with the fleet offshore, the islands stop being
reported (`5p6gu14ZC4w`,
[report reading & forecasting](../planning/report-reading-and-forecasting.md)).
Grade the water moving over the structure before grading the marks on the screen
([current structure](../conditions/current-structure.md),
[electronics and sounder](../planning/electronics-and-sounder.md)).
⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): "really not a lot of coverage" is
verbatim and appears twice in the cited October 2022 report (@00:02:14,
@00:04:03), and the fleet-offshore mechanism is stated there ("nobody's really
been at Clemente, the guys are… offshore", @00:02:54) — but **August** appears
nowhere in it; the nearest corpus analogue is July 2022, in a transcript this
sentence does not cite.

**Conditions come from a per-position pull, and there is no position.** San

no charted coordinate in this repo, so a day plan cannot pull SST or chlorophyll
for the island itself. ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: a public position for
