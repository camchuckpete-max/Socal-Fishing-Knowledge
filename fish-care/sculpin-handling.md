# Sculpin (Venomous Spine) Handling

Sculpin — the reddish bottomfish anglers pull up on rockfish/bottom gear,
commonly called "sculpin" on SoCal boats (California scorpionfish) — carry
venomous spines over most of the body. This note is a safety/handling
procedure only: it does not cover where/when to find them or a dedicated
technique to target them, just how to unhook and handle one without getting
stuck. Source is a short dedicated handling tutorial from Your Saltwater Guide
(`ty8FtA3Y2bA`, 2022-04-16).

## Safe unhooking

- **Thumb in the lower lip, and hold on tight.** Grab the sculpin by pinning
  your thumb into its lower lip — described as handling "a little
  rattlesnake." Once you have that grip, do not let go: if the fish wiggles
  free of the grip, a spine goes into you.
- **Never grab the hook with pliers to shake the fish loose.** The fish will
  spin in place on the hook, and one of the spines (back, face, pec fin, or
  anal fin) will drive into your hand. Keep the thumb-in-lip grip locked the
  entire time you back the hook out.

(Your Saltwater Guide, `ty8FtA3Y2bA`, 2022-04-16)

## Spine locations (where it can get you)

- Spines run the length of the back, all the way down.
- The head carries roughly 50 spines across the face, including around the
  eyes.
- More spines sit behind the head, on the sides.
- Large spines on the pectoral fins.
- Three large spines on the anal fin.

All of these spines carry bacteria. A puncture is not described as lethal, but
is described as intensely, "phenomenal[ly]" painful — enough that "you'll wish
you were dead." (`ty8FtA3Y2bA`)

## Keep size & table quality

- **Regulatory (California / CDFW, as of the 2022-04-16 source — verify
  current CDFW regs before keeping):** minimum size to keep was stated as
  **10 inches**.
- **Table quality:** sculpin is called out as very good eating — sweet, white
  meat — despite the intimidating spines.
