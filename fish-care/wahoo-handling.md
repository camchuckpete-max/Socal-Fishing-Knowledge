# Wahoo — Deck Safety (Razor Teeth)

Wahoo carry teeth sharp enough to cause a serious laceration — the same bite
pattern that marks up trolling plugs, casting bombs, and Raider jigs after a
hookup shows why: whatever's sharp enough to punch through a wahoo's bony jaw
is sharp enough to cut a person (`4uNPLknRAQg`). This note is deck-safety
procedure only; the fishing side lives in [wahoo](../species/wahoo.md),
[wahoo bomb & Raider jig casting](../techniques/wahoo-bomb-casting.md), and
[wahoo trolling — rigging](../techniques/wahoo-trolling.md#rigging).

## Before the lure clears the water — the "wahoo missile" risk

- **Let a wound-in lure settle and count four or five before lifting it out**
  of the water to recast or rack the rod, rather than winding straight
  through and pulling it clear. A following wahoo can hit the lure right at
  the boat and come out of the water with it — "it's happened many, many
  times" (`4uNPLknRAQg`).
- A questioner in the same session separately asked about waiting a
  **5-to-10 count** before pulling a jig or bomb out at the boat for this
  same reason — the same pause-before-lifting practice under a different
  number, not a second rule (`4uNPLknRAQg`).
- This boat-side habit layers on top of, not in place of, the pause-and-burn
  casting mechanics in
  [wahoo bomb & Raider jig casting](../techniques/wahoo-bomb-casting.md).

## Landing and neutralizing the fish

- **Clear a spot on deck and announce the fish is coming aboard before the
  gaff shot**, so nobody is standing where a thrashing, toothy fish is about
  to land (`4uNPLknRAQg`).
- **One or two crew gaff the fish and bring it aboard; a separate crew member
  then stuns it with a bat to the head.** The gaffer(s) focus on landing the
  fish while a different person neutralizes it, and it sometimes takes **two
  or three hits** before the fish stops flopping (`4uNPLknRAQg`). The stun
  step targets the teeth specifically: a flopping wahoo on deck is what
  causes a "pretty bad laceration" (`4uNPLknRAQg`).

## Hook removal and handling — crew only

- **The crew extracts the hook, never the angler.** Reaching in with pliers
  to pull your own hook while the fish can still flop is where injuries
  happen, especially since it can take multiple bat hits to fully neutralize
  a fish (`4uNPLknRAQg`).
- **Don't reach into the fish box for your own catch**, even for a
  personal-best or first-wahoo photo. A fish can sit in the box for a while
  before it reaches the RSW system if the crew is busy with other hookups,
  and stays dangerous at that point — wait for the crew to hand it out once
  it's safe (`4uNPLknRAQg`).

## Footwear on deck

- **Wear closed-toed shoes or sandals, not open-toe sandals (e.g. Tevas).**
  Multiple wahoo can end up lying on deck at once when the bite is hot and
  the crew is occupied with other hookups; stepping on or near a wahoo's
  mouth with an exposed toe is the stated risk (`4uNPLknRAQg`).
