# Wahoo — Deck Safety (Razor Teeth)

Wahoo carry razor-sharp teeth capable of a serious laceration — the same
bite pattern shows up as bite marks on trolling plugs, casting bombs, and
Raider jigs after a hookup (see [wahoo](../species/wahoo.md) and
[wahoo bomb & Raider jig casting](../techniques/wahoo-bomb-casting.md) for
the fishing side). This note is deck-safety procedure only, built around the
same reasoning wahoo bombs and trolling lures get sharpened for
(see [wahoo trolling — rigging](../techniques/wahoo-trolling.md#rigging)):
whatever's sharp enough to punch through a wahoo's bony jaw is sharp enough
to cut a person.

Source: Searcher Sportfishing, "Tackle Talk Live Recap (Wahoo 5.5.20),"

## Before the lure clears the water — the "wahoo missile" risk

- **Let a wound-in lure settle and count four or five before lifting it out**
  of the water to recast or rack the rod, rather than winding straight
  through and pulling it clear. A following wahoo can hit the lure right at
  the boat and come out of the water with it — "it's happened many, many
  times." A questioner in the same session separately asked about waiting a
  **5-to-10 count** before pulling a jig or bomb out at the boat for this
  same reason; both figures describe the same pause-before-lifting practice,
  not two different rules.
- This is a boat-side safety habit layered on top of, not a replacement for,
  the pause-and-burn casting mechanics in
  [wahoo bomb & Raider jig casting](../techniques/wahoo-bomb-casting.md).

## Landing and neutralizing the fish

- **Clear a spot on deck and announce the fish is coming aboard** before the
  gaff shot, so nobody is standing where a thrashing, toothy fish is about to
  land.
- **One or two crew gaff the fish and bring it on board; a separate crew
  member then stuns it with a bat to the head.** This is a deliberate
  division of labor — the gaffers focus on landing the fish, a different
  person neutralizes it — and it sometimes takes **two or three hits** before
  the fish stops flopping. The point of stunning it is specifically the
  teeth: a flopping wahoo on deck is what causes a "pretty bad laceration."

## Hook removal and handling — crew only

- **Let the crew extract the hook, never the angler.** Reaching in with
  pliers to pull your own hook while the fish can still flop is called out
  directly as when injuries happen, especially since it can take multiple bat
  hits to fully neutralize a fish.
- **Don't reach into the fish box for your own catch**, even for a
  personal-best or first-wahoo photo. Fish sometimes sit in the box for a
  while before making it to the RSW system if the crew is busy with other
  hookups, and a wahoo can still be dangerous at that point — let the crew
  hand it out once it's safe.

## Footwear on deck

- **Wear closed-toed shoes or sandals, not open-toe sandals (e.g. Tevas).**
  Multiple wahoo can end up laying on deck at once when the bite is hot and
  the crew is occupied with other hookups; stepping on or near a wahoo's
  mouth with an exposed toe is the stated risk.
