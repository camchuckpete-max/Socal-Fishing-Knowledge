# Evidence — Tuna Care

Per-source provenance backing [tuna care](../tuna-care.md). One line per
observation, grouped under the parent note's section headings, in order.

## Bleed and gill-and-gut on the water

- `H-vIGWPIPVc` (StokedOnFishing, presenter Shay, 2019-10-30, San Clemente
  Island; unregistered channel — medium confidence; species not clearly
  diarized in the transcript — the boat's catch that trip was a yellowfin
  tuna and bonito): described the mechanism the same way as the doctrine —
  cutting/pulling the gill opens the bloodline, which bleeds the fish out
  and also kills it faster — done immediately boat-side with the fish going
  on ice right after.
- `usHl-4SfqDA` (StokedOnFishing, 2024-11-13; unregistered channel — medium
  confidence): this charter's stated order is bleed, kill, then gut
  immediately on landing, followed by packing the cavity itself with ice and
  completely covering the fish — consistent with, not a new step beyond,
  the bleed/field-dress-then-chill chain; the cavity-packing detail is the
  addition.
- `Klfb433I3Uk` (StokedOnFishing, 2019-10-04, San Clemente Island, SoCal, "A
  Day of San Clemente Island Yellowtail Fishing"; unregistered channel —
  medium confidence; species: yellowtail, not tuna): as soon as the fish is
  on deck, cut the artery (exact cut location not specified beyond "down
  here" on camera) — framed on camera as "almost all the fish's blood goes
  through this artery within a matter of minutes" — then hold the fish
  upside down in a bucket. About 5 minutes was called out as enough for the
  blood to pump out; the fish then goes on ice, framed on camera as ready
  for sashimi/sushi ("sushimi") at that point.

## Chill times by fish size

- `Y1xeieQI3B4` (StokedOnFishing, 2025-05-10, Cortez Bank aboard the 85 ft
  *El Dorado*; unregistered channel — medium confidence): a multi-day
  charter alternative to the ice slurry — an onboard RSW (refrigerated
  seawater) fish hold, running at about 36°F, that fish go into and stay in
  until they're filleted, rather than being iced and pulled for processing.
  A different chilling mechanism (mechanical refrigeration vs. a salted
  ice/seawater mix); no chill-time or fillet-timing detail given on camera.

## Loin handling after filleting

- `usHl-4SfqDA` (StokedOnFishing, 2024-11-13; unregistered channel — medium
  confidence): a charter operation's dockside processing routine, once
  loins are cut off the carcass, filmed at Stoked On Fishing Charters'
  headquarters with deckhand Parker doing the filleting. Named failure
  mode: charters that just loin the fish, skin it, bag it in plastic, and
  hand it over with no salt-water step or attentive chilling are "a great
  way to waste a lot of the tuna" on a big-yield trip (100+ lb of loins
  cited).

## Estimating weight without a scale

- `mDmbGdQAy-4` (StokedOnFishing, 2025-10-06 upload, SoCal, NE of Santa
  Barbara Island, "Catching Monster Bluefin Using a Flying Bait | Catch &
  Cook"; unregistered channel — medium confidence): with no way to lift a
  big bluefin onto a scale on deck, the crew estimated weight with a tape
  measure and the length × girth² ÷ 800 formula, worked out loud on this
  fish as 46 in girth, 68.5 in length, for an estimated ≈181 lb.
