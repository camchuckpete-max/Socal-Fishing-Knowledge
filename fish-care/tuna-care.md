# Tuna Care

The tuna care chain runs from the gaff shot that boats the fish to the
slurry that holds its quality for the ride home. A tuna that is bled,
field-dressed, and chilled fast eats far better than one left warm in the
sun — every step below is a quality lever, not a formality. The chain
applies to [bluefin tuna](../species/bluefin-tuna.md) and other tuna; the
steps are parameters and judgment only — for the hands-on motions, watch the
linked videos rather than a step-by-step transcription here.

## 1. Boat the fish cleanly (the gaff shot)

Getting the fish aboard without tearing it up starts before the gaff shot
itself: how the boat is worked during the fight (gear-tap toward the boat,
down-swell walk, avoiding the death circle) sets up a controlled gaff
instead of a rushed one — see
[fighting a big bluefin](../techniques/fighting-big-bluefin.md). Walkthrough:
[how to gaff a bluefin from a boat](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w6DDCSLu8vM).

- Aim the gaff at the **head/shoulder** region, not the mid-body or tail — a
  head-forward shot controls the fish and keeps the gaff out of the meat
  (`w6DDCSLu8vM`).
- Gaff on a committed pass with the fish's head coming toward you, not on a
  wild swing, so the gaff sets where intended and the fish comes over the
  rail under control (`w6DDCSLu8vM`).
- Gaff sizing, the pec/flip-upside-down shot, hook orientation ("don't dig a
  hole"), and gaff safety are covered in full in [gaffing](gaffing.md)
  (`I84uoay_jwQ`).

## 2. Bleed and gill-and-gut on the water

Do this immediately, while the fish is still fresh, before it goes on ice.
Walkthrough:
[gill & gut a bluefin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JeexIvtUkZc).

- **Bleed** the fish, then pull the **gills and guts**. This removes the
  warm mass and the enzymes that spoil quality fastest, so the slurry cools
  the carcass instead of fighting a bellyful of heat (`JeexIvtUkZc`). The
  same bleed-then-gut sequence holds on a yellowfin/bonito trip, fish iced
  immediately after (`H-vIGWPIPVc`).
- One charter's stated order is **bleed, kill, then gut immediately on
  landing**, followed by **packing the cavity itself with ice and covering
  the fish completely** (`usHl-4SfqDA`).
- The yellowtail version of this step — artery cut, about 5 minutes upside
  down in a bucket, then ice (`Klfb433I3Uk`) — is in
  [yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md#landing--handling).

## 3. Wet-towel cooldown before icing

- Field-dress the fish, then give it a **wet-towel cooldown** before it goes
  into the slurry — the wet towel knocks the worst of the surface heat off
  the dressed carcass (`5to3Q5P7w90`). Dropping a hot fish straight into ice
  is less effective than letting it shed that heat first.

## 4. The below-freezing slurry

Rock salt mixed into ice and seawater drives the mix below the freezing
point of fresh water, which is what lets a big tuna chill through to the
core (`5to3Q5P7w90`).

- Mix **rock salt (roughly 2 cups per 7–10 lb bag of ice)** with the ice,
  plus a splash of seawater to make a wet slurry (`5to3Q5P7w90`).
- Keep the fish **fully in the slurry** and re-ice as the ice burns down — a
  fish only half in the cold chills unevenly.

## 5. Chill times by fish size

How long the fish needs in the slurry to chill through scales with its
mass. Hold it in the cold at least this long before calling it done
(`5to3Q5P7w90`):

| Fish size | Chill time |
|---|---|
| 30–40 lb | ≈ 2 h |
| ~100 lb | ≈ 3–5 h |
| >100 lb | overnight, re-iced |

- A **>100 lb** fish will not core-chill in an afternoon — hold it
  **overnight** and **re-ice** it as the slurry melts down (`5to3Q5P7w90`).
- On a multi-day charter, an onboard **RSW (refrigerated seawater) hold**
  running at about **36°F** replaces the ice slurry — fish go in and stay
  until they're filleted, rather than being iced and pulled for processing
  (`Y1xeieQI3B4`).

## 6. Loin handling after filleting — salt water, not fresh

One charter's dockside routine, once loins are cut off the carcass
(`usHl-4SfqDA`):

- **Rinse cut loins in a cold salt-water bath, never fresh water** — moisture
  from fresh water speeds up tuna meat decomposition. Mix **regular table
  salt (Morton's), about 3/4 cup per batch of ice**, into ice to chill the
  rinse water to **about 32°F, just under freezing**, and give loins a quick
  rinse right after they're cut (`usHl-4SfqDA`).
- High-end kitchens store tuna loins wrapped in paper that wicks moisture
  off without over-drying the fish — the same moisture-vs-decomposition
  logic behind skipping fresh water here.
- Loining, skinning, bagging in plastic, and handing the fish over with no
  salt-water step or attentive chilling wastes meat on a big-yield trip
  (100+ lb of loins) — loins go back on ice while still cold, straight from
  the rinse.

## Estimating weight without a scale

With no way to lift a big bluefin onto a scale on deck, weight can be
estimated with a tape measure using **length × girth² ÷ 800** (inches,
result in lb) (`mDmbGdQAy-4`).
