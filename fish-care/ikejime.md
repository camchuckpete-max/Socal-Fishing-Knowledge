# Ikejime (Iki-Jime)

Ikejime is driving a spike into the fish's brain for an instant kill, the top
tier of tuna care when quality is the goal. It stacks on top of — not in
place of — bleeding and chilling. The corpus source is a short walkthrough on
a bluefin tuna; watch it for the exact spike location and motion
(`WzT0RSHpaQc`).

## The spike and pithing

- **Brain spike.** Drive a spike into the fish's **brain** at a target point
  on the head to kill it instantly. The exact point is shown on camera on a
  bluefin, not described here — the video is the reference for placement
  (`WzT0RSHpaQc`).
- **Pithing.** After the spike, run a wire down the spinal cord to destroy the
  nerve line. Shutting the nervous system down cleanly stops the fish from
  thrashing, which is what protects the meat from being burned by a long
  fight-out (`WzT0RSHpaQc`).

## When it's worth it

- Reach for ikejime when the goal is top-quality/sashimi-grade flesh — an
  instant, clean kill leaves better meat than a fish that fights out in the
  hold (`WzT0RSHpaQc`).
- For a fish going to the smoker or the grill, bleeding and chilling alone
  carry it; ikejime is the extra step reserved for the best table fish
  (`WzT0RSHpaQc`).

## Stacks with bleeding and chilling

Ikejime does not replace [bleeding and the slurry chill](tuna-care.md). The
full quality chain is: kill clean (ikejime), bleed, field-dress, and chill in
a below-freezing slurry — each step protects the meat a different way
(`WzT0RSHpaQc`).

## Scope

The corpus source is one short video demonstrating the spike on a
[bluefin tuna](../species/bluefin-tuna.md) and little more. This note carries

depth come from the video and from hands-on practice, not from prose here
