# Evidence — Fishing Live Bait

Trip reports and per-source provenance for [Fishing Live Bait](../fishing-live-bait.md).

## Hook position by situation

- `Q-gQuOegAx4` (StokedOnFishing, trip fished summer 2017 per on-camera "this
  past summer," San Clemente Island, "Stoked on the El Dorado 2 Day," 2-day
  charter aboard the 85 ft El Dorado, Long Beach Sportfishing; unregistered
  channel): fishing a chum line for yellowtail on the inside of the island,
  the crew called switching bait hook position "the key" once seals — as
  stated on camera, "the seals won't come up and try to bu[y] them as much" —
  started following the boat; hooking so the bait swam down and away rather
  than staying up near the surface kept the seals from grabbing it as much.
  Captions render the position "button hook" (asr-uncertain on the exact
  term), but the described mechanism matches the butt-hook row in the main
  note exactly.

## Bait selection

- `D_Y2G0rBZCs` (StokedOnFishing, 2015-01-18, San Clemente Island, "San
  Clemente Island Yellowtail," aboard the Thunderbird; unregistered channel):
  the crew's between-bite advice to a newer angler was to wind in and cast
  back out rather than let the bait sit idle, since yellowtail moving through
  need a bait that stays presented, not parked.
- `XyKF34C5iB4` (Time On The Water panel, 2025-04-19; unregistered channel):
  worked example from the panel — a 7 lb fish (species not named on camera)
  came almost immediately on a small greenback mackerel fished on a 40 lb
  outfit at San Clemente Island, after the front side had gone completely
  dead on surface irons, coochies, hard baits, and swimbaits, confirming fish
  were still there before the crew went back to working their usual lures.

## Chumming with live bait

- `AIHvJj-paoo` (StokedOnFishing, 2016-11-29, offshore San Clemente Island
  near Desperation Point, sponsor-heavy Okuma reel-demo episode, trip-tips
  segment; unregistered channel): once the sport boats pulled off a spot, the
  on-camera advice for a cluster of private boats left on the school was to
  (1) join forces with the other private boats and chum off-and-on together —
  "acts as a party boat," keeping fish circling with the drifting boats
  instead of scattering once the bigger boat's steady chum line stopped — and
  (2) give neighboring boats some space, since these tuna take big runs and
  fishing too close risks sawing another boat's hooked fish off on your own
  line.
- `r4J5nP5Bkl4` (StokedOnFishing, trip framed on camera as "this past June,"
  resolved from the 2024-04-26 upload date to June 2023, backside San
  Clemente Island, "Summer Bluefin Bite on the ElDorado," 85 ft El Dorado;
  unregistered channel): at a kelp paddy holding dorado, live bait chummed
  from the tank was credited on camera with drawing fish from the bow up to
  the corner of the boat where anglers were positioned to fish — a visible
  bow-to-stern pull. The paddy fish were also working under birds sitting on
  it (see [bird reading](../../conditions/bird-reading.md)).
- `Klfb433I3Uk` (StokedOnFishing, 2019-10-04, San Clemente Island, "A Day of
  San Clemente Island Yellowtail Fishing"; unregistered channel): a
  seal-avoidance variant on chumming with dead (not live) bait. At the
  islands, throwing whole dead sardines overboard draws seals in immediately,
  so the crew's workaround was a dedicated "chum buddy" (chum bucket) device:
  dump the day's dead sardines into it and smash them up before any of it
  goes in the water, rather than pitching individual dead baits over the
  side. Broken into smaller pieces this way, the chum still "helps create
  smaller bait fish up and around the boat" without rallying seals to the
  boat the way whole dead bait does. Framed on camera as a good practice to
  start a stop with, rather than tossing occasional single dead sardines.

## Handling live squid

- `yuXr3IJ8ybg` (StokedOnFishing, 2017-06-09, San Clemente Island,
  sponsor-heavy Okuma gear-demo episode; unregistered channel): a second,
  thinner description of the same problem — gripping a live squid firmly
  enough that it can't wrap around the fingers and peck with its beak, to
  avoid getting inked while baiting up. Consistent with, not adding mechanism
  beyond, the grip technique in the main note.
