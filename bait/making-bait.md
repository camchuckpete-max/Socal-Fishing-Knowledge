# Making Bait

"Making bait" is **catching your own live bait** before you go fishing, as
opposed to buying it. In SoCal the two paths are the **bait barge** (buy
sardines) and **making your own** (catch mackerel on sabikis, or net anchovy /
sardine). Once you have it, keep it alive per [bait tanks](bait-tanks.md) and
fish it per [fishing live bait](fishing-live-bait.md).

## Buy vs. make

Cameron buys sardines from the bait barge when they're available; when he
makes bait himself it's always mackerel, caught on a sabiki. Bait shortages
in July 2026 pushed him onto sabikis more often — at both **La Jolla** and
the **Coronado Islands** — to make his own when the barge couldn't fill him
(cameron).

## Buying at the bait barge

Buying sardines at the barge is the buy side of the split above; how you
handle it affects both your wait and your bait quality:

- **Never board the barge itself.** If you can't get the boat alongside
  cleanly, ask the attendant for help rather than jumping onto the barge to
  fend the boat off — approach slow and mellow instead.
- **Follow the barge's side signal** — barges mark the side they want boats
  tied to with a cone or an arrow (at Dana Point, a cone on the ocean side).
- **Hail on VHF channel 11** if no attendant is visible dockside: give the
  barge's name twice plus your own boat's name, then wait for their response
  before approaching.
- **Tell the attendant you want it loaded light** and that you're not in a
  rush — it sets the pace and keeps the load conservative (see
  [bait tanks](bait-tanks.md) for judging how much to actually take). The
  attendant can make or break the day, and a tip at the end is the expected
  way to show it.
- **Avoid the 6:00 a.m. rush.** SoCal 3/4-day and half-day sportboats all
  load around 6:00 a.m., which is when the barge lines up. Arriving at
  **5:00 a.m.** or **7:00 a.m.** instead (or loading mid-day) avoids the line
  entirely (`7WapaxdtjQg`).

A repeated version of the same rule names the rush as charter, half-day,
**and** 3/4-day boats all converging by 6:00 a.m., with a line already
forming by a quarter to six, and states a tighter personal target of **at
least 5:00 a.m., "if not four thirty"** — a tighter version of the same
avoid-the-rush window above, not a conflicting number (`bM2vUS1B-yQ`).

## Making mackerel bait — sabiki

A sabiki is a multi-hook bait-catching rig fished vertically. Cameron's SoCal
mackerel program:

- **Where:** the **Coronado Islands** (also La Jolla during shortages).
- **How deep:** drops the sabiki to **~100 ft**.
- **Sinker:** **8 oz** to get the string down and hold it.
- **Result:** has **loaded 5 twelve-inch mackerel on one drop** — a full
  string of quality bait in a single pass (cameron).

Twelve-inch mackerel are big, hearty baits — heartier than sardine (cameron;
see [fishing live bait](fishing-live-bait.md) for how bait choice plays
against the gamefish).

**Baiting the hooks.** On a multi-hook rig, bait each hook rather than
fishing it bare, and bait with a piece of mackerel rather than sardine —
mackerel hangs on the hook, while a sardine strip gets knocked off almost
immediately (`A6DJoXbID4c`). This corroborates the mackerel-heartier-than-
sardine comparison above (full account in [evidence](evidence/making-bait.md)).

**Alternative — microfiber rag.** A microfiber rag fished/dragged in the
water works as a mackerel lure in its own right: mackerel bite the rag
itself, landing two-to-one against a mackerel-strip bait in one session
(`5FzBwvMtRP8`).

**Handling.** Never touch a mackerel destined for the bait tank with your
hands — hand contact strips off its protective slime coat. Flick the fish
off the hook instead, using a butter knife carried for that purpose, rather
than gripping the fish to unhook it (`5FzBwvMtRP8`).

## Long-range trip prep — carry your own bait-catching gear

Framed for **3-to-10-day trips**: even on a boat trip where bait is normally
supplied, you may need to supplement the boat's bait supply yourself at some
point, and being unprepared to help make bait is called out on camera as a
bad look ("you don't want to be that guy that doesn't help") (`cx7tKXHmiY4`).
Pack:

- A standard multi-hook rig — captions read **"cebiche rake,"** very likely an
  ASR mishearing of **sabiki** (flagged asr-uncertain) — rigged with **four
  or five flies**, **medium size**, for **larger mackerel**.
- A **smaller** rig of the same style for **other/smaller mackerel** species.
- A weighted **squid jig** — captions garbled the brand/model as **"way to
  close squid jig"** (not confirmed, flagged asr-uncertain) — in case the trip
  calls for making squid along the way.

No hook-line ratings, sinker weights, or exact rig names are given; this is a
packing tip, not a rig spec.

**Light vs. heavy sabiki.** Carry both a light sabiki and a heavier sabiki on
a long-range trip because the bait species get bigger as the trip runs
longer — specifically **jack mackerel** (captioned "gad mackerel," almost
certainly an ASR mishearing; flagged asr-uncertain) running **up to 2–3 lb
each**. A loaded string of **five** jack mackerel on one drop is enough to
**break the light sabiki off outright**, which is why the heavier sabiki
stays staged as backup once bait size ramps up on the longer trips
(`eLFVhVyyOTw`).

## Baja / Sea of Cortez sabiki — heavier-rated rig

A Sea of Cortez panga trip runs a noticeably heavier-rated sabiki than the
SoCal mackerel program above, because the bait itself runs bigger and
stronger — full-size mackerel down to smaller yellowtail, and a hooked
string of 2–4 yellowtail will snap a light sabiki instantly (`dEPuDrhoClM`):

- **Main line: 25–30 lb rated. Hooks/leaders: 20 lb rated.**
- **Sinker: 4–6 oz for mackerel/sardine-size bait, up to 8 oz when yellowtail
  are mixed in** — the heavier lead keeps the string straight instead of
  tangling when several fish hit the same drop at once. One angler ran **6 oz
  almost the entire trip**.
- Bait was **whatever stayed alive in the well** — mackerel, small
  yellowtail, and a mix of local small species (a spotted bay bass, a
  croaker-family fish locally called "perch," and a sarcastic-fringehead-type
  fish). Make bait **every night** whether it gets used or not — the
  leftover bait carries into the next day, including for bottom fishing when
  the target switches to cabrilla/grouper (`dEPuDrhoClM`).

Don't run cheap, inexpensive sabiki rigs on a Sea of Cortez trip — they don't
hold up. A string can go out with six hooks and come back with four, then
three, then one, and the window for making bait on a trip is short, so
losing hooks mid-session costs fishing time (`M8hOYQ_6rSg`). Run
heavier-line, more durable sabikis instead, trading hook count for
line/hook durability rather than running the lightest possible rig:

- **4-hook rig** for smaller bait.
- **2-hook rig** for bigger bait — fewer hooks but each one (and the line) is
  rated to handle a bigger fish on the string without snapping.

## La Paz, Baja California Sur — mackerel and sardine bait

A two-species bait program feeds the day's
[jurel/yellowtail shallow-water slow-troll](../techniques/trolling.md#la-paz-baja-california-sur-sea-of-cortez-side--shallow-water-live-bait-slow-troll-for-jurelyellowtail):
green mackerel jigged around floating structure described on camera as
"containers" (rig not specified, not confirmed as a sabiki) — the guide's
target was **20–40 mackerel per boat**, though one outing landed roughly a
dozen — plus **sardines bought from local vendors** ("sardinas from the
locals"), used both as **chum** and as **light bait** in their own right, a
buy-don't-make path run alongside the self-caught mackerel rather than
instead of it (`ORC1A68cEeM`). No sabiki hook count, line rating, or sinker
weight was given for the mackerel jigging (see
[evidence](evidence/making-bait.md) for trip detail and a follow-on
wind-condition note).

## Cameron's gear plans

- **Dedicated sabiki setup (building).** A hollow sabiki rod that stores the
  rigged sabiki inside the blank — the string tucks up into the rod so it
  travels rigged and tangle-free — paired with a levelwind line-counter
  reel, whose counter puts the string back at a known depth (e.g. ~100 ft)
  every drop. Intended as one always-rigged bait rod covering SoCal and BOLA
  (Bahía de los Ángeles) (cameron).
- **Cast net (considering).** A cast net for anchovy/sardine, to make bait in
  Mexican waters during shortages where the barge isn't an option (cameron).
