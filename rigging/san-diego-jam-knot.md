# San Diego Jam Knot — Doubled and Single-Pass Terminal Tie

The SoCal go-to terminal knot for tying line to a hook or lure, used from
[calico bass](../species/calico-bass.md) up to 200 lb-class
[bluefin](../species/bluefin-tuna.md). Kept as its own note rather than
folded into [Essential SoCal Knots](essential-knots.md), whose Parameters
section it outgrew — see that note for the Palomar, RP knot, uni-to-uni, and
other connection/terminal knots this one sits beside. See [San Diego Jam —
Single vs. Double](san-diego-jam-single-vs-double.md) for the selection rule
between the single-pass and doubled-through-the-eye versions, and [Improved
Clinch Knot](improved-clinch-knot.md) for a mechanically distinct
single-pass tie some presenters also call "San Diego jam" — a naming split
tracked in that note, not this one.

## When to use

- Ties a hook or lure eye directly to the line, holding across the SoCal
  grade range from calico bass to 200 lb-class bluefin.
- **Line-class breakpoint:** use the [improved
  clinch](improved-clinch-knot.md#parameters) at 40 lb test or less; step up
  to the San Diego jam above 40 lb (`tpmOYXYQwhU`).
- **Double (doubled-through-the-eye):** built for heavy line on artificial
  lures — roughly three times the bulk of the single-pass tie on the same
  hook. Avoid it for live bait; the extra bulk works against presentation
  (`5J7a6UwTA90`).
- **Single (single-pass):** the live-bait-hook default — avoids the bulk and
  drag of the doubled tie. Full selection rule in [San Diego Jam — Single
  vs. Double](san-diego-jam-single-vs-double.md).

## Parameters

- **Doubled-through-the-eye mechanic:** pass the line through the eye and
  back to form a doubled section (about a 15 in tag, wrapped around a finger
  to hold the loop), wrap the tag down the standing line, pass it through
  the lower loop and then the upper (finger) loop, lubricate, and slide the
  coils down snug to the eye before cinching (`1BH7nQdIg5Q`, `Qa6Q8mOAV6I`,
  `VyFpIk-Na9Q`, `5J7a6UwTA90`). One demo narrates sliding the coils "up with
  the main line" rather than down to the eye — the opposite direction word
  from every other source, recorded unresolved rather than silently
  corrected (asr-uncertain, `VyFpIk-Na9Q`).
- **Single-pass mechanic:** one pass through the hook or lure eye (not
  doubled), wrap the tag down the standing line, pass it through the lower
  loop nearest the eye and then the upper (finger) loop, wet, and cinch
  (`AGaVlYu61O4`, `lf3S28nh-kk`, `YeV--HarEYQ`, `6E39_PBt1P4`). A
  same-channel short repeats this tie without new detail (`rvmr9Jy9RjI`).
  Keep tension on the wraps throughout the tie rather than only at the end —
  it seats down easier (`YeV--HarEYQ`).
- **Wrap count — kept side by side; angler preference within a 3–7 range, not
  one correct number:**
  - **6 wraps** is the most repeated figure, for both the doubled tie
    (`1BH7nQdIg5Q`, `Qa6Q8mOAV6I`) and the single-pass tie (`6E39_PBt1P4`).
  - **5 wraps:** doubled, at 100 lb test, for bigger tuna (`VyFpIk-Na9Q`);
    single-pass (`VpW91AKOFVQ`, `AGaVlYu61O4`) — fewer wraps lower the odds
    of a wrap crossing over itself when tying quickly under time pressure
    (`VpW91AKOFVQ`).
  - **7 wraps:** single-pass, demonstrated on camera (`YeV--HarEYQ`); named
    without being demonstrated by the 5-wrap source above (`VpW91AKOFVQ`);
    6–7 wraps recommended for every lure/hook connection in one session —
    yo-yo iron, surface iron, Colt Sniper, flat-fall/knife jig
    (`lf3S28nh-kk`; full session context in [Searcher spring
    bluefin/yellowtail
    quiver](../tackle/searcher-spring-bluefin-yellowtail-quiver.md#knots-used-this-session)).
  - **3 wraps:** demonstrated on the doubled tie but called under-built for
    real use — with 80, 100, or 130 lb test, "you're probably going at least
    a half a dozen" (6) wraps (`5J7a6UwTA90`).
- **Tension and lubrication:** wet the knot before cinching — dry line
  abrades and burns under the friction of cinching (`VpW91AKOFVQ`). A
  friction demo without a knot makes the mechanism concrete: a paper towel
  drawn across dry 30 lb test line snaps it, and the same test breaks 100 lb
  test line too — the earliest-dated clip of its channel logged in this KB,
  predating `5g7pK63hYnc`, `3gykKyPdOvA`, `Aorcd0Om7eI`, and `7ivBSL-mhW8` in
  [Slim Beauty Knot](slim-beauty-knot.md) (`BcX-tp3I7LE`). Hold the hook or
  lure against a fixed point
  for tension while wrapping, or, tying quickly, let the weight of the
  swivel, clip, or hook itself provide the tension (`6E39_PBt1P4`). One
  clip's captions render the wetting rationale backwards — "so it won't
  slide easier," the opposite of the reasoning above — recorded unresolved
  rather than corrected (asr-uncertain, `VyFpIk-Na9Q`).
- **Overlap caution:** don't let the wraps cross or overlap around the eye,
  either laying them down the standing line or sliding the cinched coils
  home — keep the coils stacked cleanly (`Qa6Q8mOAV6I`). One clip frames a
  100 lb doubled tie as a breaking-strength test but cuts before a result
  shows, so no breaking-strength figure is recorded here (`Qa6Q8mOAV6I`).
- **Applications and line classes demonstrated:** doubled tie on 40 lb test
  straight to a surface iron with no separate leader (`49joKHD7Umc`); on 80
  lb mono for artificial lures generally (`5J7a6UwTA90`); on 100 lb test
  (`Qa6Q8mOAV6I`, `VyFpIk-Na9Q`). Ties to monofilament, fluorocarbon, and
  braid alike, and to a hook, swivel, clip, or lure (`6E39_PBt1P4`).
- **Naming:** the doubled-through-the-eye tie is also called the "double jam
  knot" (`5J7a6UwTA90`); the single-pass tie is independently named "San
  Diego jam knot" by two sources (`AGaVlYu61O4`, `YeV--HarEYQ`), alongside
  the usage tracked in [Improved Clinch
  Knot](improved-clinch-knot.md#when-to-use).
- **Weak-link variant (sinker connection):** tying a sinker onto a [rockfish
  dropper rig](../techniques/rockfish-deep-dropping.md), the same knot is
  tied deliberately weaker than the hook-knot version — 3 turns instead of
  5–6, and skip lubricating it. The connection is meant to be the
  sacrificial point: if the sinker hangs on bottom, it's cheaper to break
  off there than to lose the whole rig (`_ZThckj2TIM`).
- **Judgment:** wet connection knots before seating and cinch fully — a
  partly-set knot is where these fail (`VpW91AKOFVQ`, `Qa6Q8mOAV6I`).
