# Flying Fish Harness

The pre-rigged flying fish (dead natural, netted or sabiki-caught, or an
artificial) fished off the kite for large local Pacific bluefin. The rig
combines a body harness — skewers and zip ties that lock the wings open and
set a lifelike, tail-down profile — with a two-hook leader so the
presentation swims naturally across the surface and holds up through
repeated deployments.

## When to use

- [Kite fishing](../techniques/kite-fishing.md) offshore for
  [bluefin tuna](../species/bluefin-tuna.md), for the 100-300 lb grade of
  fish.
- The flying fish is one of the most preferred kite baits for that grade.
  Anglers can catch their own flyers on a sabiki rig, but it is difficult
  enough that most buy them pre-caught at a tackle shop instead.

## Parameters

- **Wing harness build:** set a skewer in behind the shoulder, just above
  the spine, then a second skewer through a hole between the ribs and the
  wing at the position you want the wing to sit. Hold the wings open with
  zip ties through additional holes on both sides, and cinch everything
  down tight — any slippage along the skewers during deployment kills the
  action (`GqcVaTIlyg0`).
- **Wing angle:** pin the wings at about 45° — set further out, a swell
  knocks the bait off its presentation (`YsiHziY_PWw`).
- **Body/profile skewer:** run a skewer down the middle of the limp fish,
  entered through the mouth, pushed about three-quarters of the way in.
  Lift the tail as you finish the push so the bait sits with a slight
  down-and-tail-in-the-water profile at the surface (`GqcVaTIlyg0`).
- **Mouth skewer:** pin the mouth closed with a final skewer — this keeps
  the bait presenting longer and stops water from being scooped through the
  lower lip and out the roof of the mouth (`GqcVaTIlyg0`).
- **Hook rig:** the standard San Diego bluefin flyer rig runs a large J hook
  with a large treble hook trailing off it as a stinger, on 200-400 lb mono
  leader (typically 400) (`GqcVaTIlyg0`). The main leader runs to the kite
  rod; set the treble stinger into the back of the fish, just behind the
  body skewer under the spine and out the other side.
- **Bite-leader length:** 3 ft, 3.5 ft at the max — any longer and the
  swivel reaches the rod tip before the fish is in gaff range. Tail treble:
  5/0 5X-strong (Mustad) (`YsiHziY_PWw`).
- **Failure modes:** loose skewers or zip ties let the harness slip and
  kill the action; too light a leader risks a bite-off on this grade of
  fish. Rig 10 to 12 flyers for a four-angler day and store them in the
  cooler ready to deploy (`GqcVaTIlyg0`).
- **Line-class corroboration (Hansen's kite program):** Capt. Dave Hansen's
  own kite program runs 200 lb braid main line to a 400 lb fluorocarbon
  leader — matching, at the top end, the 200-400 lb (typically 400) leader
  spec above — fished with 40 lb of drag on the reel (`JiJYamo0QHk`). Full
  fight-style context for the same rig is in
  [fighting a big bluefin](../techniques/fighting-big-bluefin.md).
- **Hook-count conflict:** hook count is a program choice here, not a
  conditions call. Most SoCal boats run the J-hook-plus-trailing-treble
  construction above, but Hansen's own boat runs a single hook only, no
  treble stinger: "we is single hook, no trebles. I know a lot of people
  have to use a treble and you got to keep using it, that's what you feel
  you need" (`JiJYamo0QHk`).

## Alternative wing-lock methods

- **Popsicle stick (field alternative):** staple a popsicle stick to the
  bait instead of building the skewer-and-zip-tie harness above — a faster
  field rig for the same wing-lock job. Pair it with a tandem rig: a big
  single hook up front and a treble hook trailing on the tail, the same
  J-hook-plus-trailing-treble construction as the standard rig above
  (`usHl-4SfqDA`).
- **Rubber band (field alternative):** secure the wing harness with a
  rubber band instead of a zip tie — another field simplification for the
  same wing-lock job (`Ix0gG0-l3v0`).
- **Braid instead of zip ties:** thread 30-50 lb braid through the wing
  hole and around the skewer instead of a zip tie (30 lb used on camera).
  Tie a simple overhand knot twice, then a third finishing/locking knot —
  without the third tie the wing knot can slip. For this build, the body
  skewer is a kitchen "spade kabob" skewer pushed through the bone just
  below the meat; rig the flyer partially thawed, not fully thawed, said to
  work better once deployed. Wing holes are punched at the second or third
  bone in from the edge, for more material for the tie to grab. Final
  leader/hooks: 400 lb leader, J hook through the body-skewer point, big
  treble hook through the back — the same construction as the standard rig
  above (`2TmLaSCmfC8`).
- **"Life jacket" float — no kite or balloon:** thaw the frozen flyer
  enough to work with, pin the wings open and rigid with toothpicks, then
  fit half of a pool noodle (cut lengthwise) over the fish's back like a
  life jacket for buoyancy. The bait floats on its own, so it can be fished
  on the slide or dropped off the bow and soaked out with no kite or
  balloon in the system at all; a Penn International 20-class outfit is a
  suitable pairing, sized to land a 300 lb-class bluefin. Especially suited
  to private boats (also seen fished off sportboats). Trade-off: more
  inconvenient than a standard kite/balloon rig — it has to be rigged ahead
  of time rather than deployed on the fly (`QHY5kmU7OTU`).

## Artificial and pre-rigged alternatives

- **Trolled rubber flyer ("Yummy Flyer"):** a pre-made rubber/artificial
  flying-fish lure skips the harness rig entirely and trolls at roughly 8.5
  kt under the kite. Reach for it when trolling under the kite to search,
  in wind of about 8 kt or more; the dead natural, harnessed flyer above is
  for the dead-boat, drift-fished presentation over fish already located.
  Full wind-based decision logic:
  [kite fishing](../techniques/kite-fishing.md#trolled-rubber-flyer-vs-dead-boat-dead-flyer-a-wind-driven-choice)
  (`2y0VznL2qk8`).
- **Commercial pre-rigged flyer:** a commercially made, pre-rigged
  flying-fish rig sold ready-to-fish, built with 300 lb leader material —
  called "wire" on camera, not confirmed against the 200-400 lb mono spec
  above (`Ix0gG0-l3v0`).
- **"California Flyer" — reusable slide-up lure:** a hard-bodied artificial
  fished dead-boat/drifted under the kite (distinct from the trolled rubber
  Yummy Flyer above). On a hookup it slides up the leader away from the
  fish's mouth, so it isn't damaged and can be reused rather than replaced
  outright; the wings are replaceable on their own (`vCskOx6N-XM`).
- **"California Delta Flyer 350" (product):** an artificial flying-fish
  imitation with a built-in floating harness — float/harness molded into
  the lure rather than rigged separately — and interchangeable wings.
  Stated presentation range: skipped, drifted, or slow-trolled, spanning
  the same skip/drift/slow-troll spectrum as the options above. No leader
  weight, hook spec, size, or price is given (`f22V2HCv8tI`).
- **"Yummy Flyer" and dead-flyer leader specs (Searcher):** two separate
  leaders for the kite spread, both terminating at a 500 lb ball-bearing
  swivel that connects to the kite rod's main line. "Yummy Flyer" leader:
  400 lb Izorline XXX (Triple X) mono to a 9/0-12/0 J hook (Owner) with a
  trailing 5X-strong treble (Owner) — the same J-hook-plus-trailing-treble
  construction as the standard rig above, with a named hook brand. Dead
  flying fish leader: a separate 3 ft length of 400 lb mono to a 9/0 Owner
  hook with a trailing 5X Owner treble — the 3 ft length and 400 lb mono
  corroborate the bite-leader spec above (`RM7cBgCDWFA`).
