# Crimping — Sleeve Crimps for Heavy Leaders

A crimp sleeve is a metal tube slid over doubled leader (or over leader plus a
hook/swivel eye) and mechanically compressed with a crimping tool, joining the
two strands without a knot. It's the standard connection for leader classes
heavy enough that a tied knot is impractical or unreliable, and it also
terminates single-strand wire (see [haywire twist](haywire-twist.md) for the
knotless twist alternative on wire specifically). This note is the general
mechanical how-to; [Bluefin Bite Leaders](bite-leaders.md#parameters--judgment)
carries the crimp/chafe-sleeve **sizing table** for a specific mono/fluoro
application (e.g. a 1.6 crimp on 130 lb fluoro) — use that note to pick sleeve
size, this one for the mechanic of seating it correctly.

## When to use it

- Any heavy mono, fluoro, or wire leader connection to a hook, swivel, or lure
  where a knot isn't the chosen method — demonstrated
  (Billy, Fisherman's Landing tackle shop, via SearcherSportfishing,
  `3zXcrGsIL-c`, 2020-09-10; unregistered channel, single mention — medium
  confidence) on **400 lb line (branded "P-Line" on camera; mono vs. fluoro
  not stated)**, a big-game/long-range leader class,
  with a High Seas-brand crimp (heard on the audio as "A2"; exact sizing
  designation unconfirmed — `asr-uncertain`) and a matching chafe/chafing
  sleeve sized for that test.

## Parameters & judgment (Billy, Fisherman's Landing / SearcherSportfishing)

- **Build order:** slide the crimp sleeve onto the leader first, then the
  chafe (chafing) sleeve, **then** attach the hook or swivel and pass the tag
  end of the leader back through the crimp sleeve to close the loop.
- **Ball the tag end before crimping:** burn the cut end of the leader to melt
  a small ball on the tip — stated as **just as important as the crimp
  itself**. Let the melted ball cool before pulling the leader tight; the
  chafe sleeve then seats up against that ball and stops there automatically,
  rather than sliding back off the end.
- **Leave the crimp ends flared:** don't compress the sleeve edge-to-edge —
  leave roughly a couple of millimeters flared/uncrimped at each end rather
  than crimping across the full width.
- **Crimp pressure — "firm handshake":** one firm squeeze per press, described
  as equivalent to a firm handshake — not a maximum-force squeeze (see the
  two-presses-per-sleeve note below).
- **Don't torque-test it against the boat:** after crimping, don't brace the
  leader against the rail (or similar) and twist/wrench on it to test it —
  that damages the line. A correctly seated firm-handshake crimp holds a
  hard, straight-line pull without needing that kind of check.
- **Two presses per crimp sleeve, not one:** after the first firm-handshake
  press, slide the crimping tool down to the sleeve's other position and
  repeat the same firm-handshake press there, leaving that end flared too —
  the same one crimp sleeve gets pressed near each end, matching the
  [bite leaders](bite-leaders.md#parameters--judgment) "two presses per crimp"
  rule.
