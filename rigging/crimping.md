# Crimping — Sleeve Crimps for Heavy Leaders

A crimp sleeve is a metal tube slid over doubled leader (or over leader plus a
hook/swivel eye) and mechanically compressed with a crimping tool, joining the
two strands without a knot. It's the standard connection for leader classes
heavy enough that a tied knot is impractical or unreliable, and it also
terminates single-strand wire (see [haywire twist](haywire-twist.md) for the
knotless twist alternative on wire specifically). This note is the general
mechanical how-to; [Bluefin Bite Leaders](bite-leaders.md#parameters) carries
the crimp/chafe-sleeve **sizing table** for a specific mono/fluoro application
(e.g. a 1.6 crimp on 130 lb fluoro) — use that note to pick sleeve size, this
one for the mechanic of seating it correctly.

## When to use

- Any heavy mono, fluoro, or wire leader connection to a hook, swivel, or lure
  where a knot isn't the chosen method — demonstrated on **400 lb** leader
  material with a High Seas-brand crimp (heard on the audio as "A2"; exact
  sizing designation unconfirmed — asr-uncertain) and a matching chafe/chafing
  sleeve sized for that test (`3zXcrGsIL-c`).
- Tying a knot in **200 lb** leader material "makes an awful big knot" that
  doesn't seat/cinch the way a knot does on lighter line — crimping works best
  at that class (`fgTmUq78ofQ`). The leader's crimp sleeve is matched to a
  swivel, and the lighter main line (example: **80 lb**) ties to that swivel
  with a conventional knot (a [San Diego jam knot](san-diego-jam-knot.md) in
  this build) — the knot lives on the lighter side of the connection, the
  crimp on the heavier side. Leader material sold by the roll (example: a
  25 ft roll of 200 lb) is typically labeled with the crimp size it calls
  for, and a tackle shop can point to the matching crimp for whatever leader
  is bought (`fgTmUq78ofQ`).
- For the top-shot-to-mainline connection specifically (see [hollow vs solid
  spectra](../tackle/spectra-hollow-vs-solid.md#a-second-competing-lean--solid-for-capacity-and-knot-speed-over-splice-time)),
  tying a knot is quicker than serving a top shot into hollow braid and is the
  default — but switching to crimping that connection is the call on a
  longer multi-day trip (example: a **10-day** trip) that calls for big gear.
  Trip length plus gear size is the stated trigger here, not a specific
  pound-test threshold (`jQzOdmP0zoQ`).

## Parameters

- **Build order:** slide the crimp sleeve onto the leader first, then the
  chafe (chafing) sleeve, **then** attach the hook or swivel and pass the tag
  end of the leader back through the crimp sleeve to close the loop
  (`3zXcrGsIL-c`).
- **Ball the tag end before crimping:** burn the cut end of the leader to melt
  a small ball on the tip — as important as the crimp itself. Let the melted
  ball cool before pulling the leader tight; the chafe sleeve then seats up
  against that ball and stops there automatically, rather than sliding back
  off the end (`3zXcrGsIL-c`).
- **Leave the crimp ends flared:** don't compress the sleeve edge-to-edge —
  leave roughly a couple of millimeters flared/uncrimped at each end rather
  than crimping across the full width (`3zXcrGsIL-c`).
- **Crimp pressure — "firm handshake":** one firm squeeze per press, not a
  maximum-force squeeze (`3zXcrGsIL-c`).
- **Two presses per crimp sleeve, not one:** after the first firm-handshake
  press, slide the crimping tool down to the sleeve's other position and
  repeat the same firm-handshake press there, leaving that end flared too —
  the same one crimp sleeve gets pressed near each end, matching the [bite
  leaders](bite-leaders.md#parameters) two-presses-per-crimp rule
  (`3zXcrGsIL-c`).
- **Don't torque-test it against the boat:** after crimping, don't brace the
  leader against the rail (or similar) and twist/wrench on it to test it —
  that damages the line. A correctly seated firm-handshake crimp holds a
  hard, straight-line pull without needing that kind of check (`3zXcrGsIL-c`).
- **Wire gets crimped differently than mono/fluoro — no flared "valley":** on
  wire leader specifically, crimp down over both ends of the sleeve,
  compressing each end fully tight — explicitly different from fluorocarbon,
  where the mono/fluoro build above leaves "a little valley in the crimp"
  rather than crimping it down hard. This sits beside, not contradicting, the
  flared-ends guidance above — read as material-specific parameters, wire vs.
  mono/fluoro. Demonstrated building a wahoo leader from **275 lb** steel wire
  with a crimp sleeve and swivel at each end; full build (length,
  swivel-to-jig connection) in [haywire twist — wahoo casting/trolling
  leader, crimped
  build](haywire-twist.md#wahoo-castingtrolling-leader--crimped-build-heavier-wire)
  (`o6Sawz5S7bk`).
- **Match sleeve number to the leader's stated pound test, on wire
  specifically:** wire leader is printed/stamped with a reference sleeve size
  for its pound test (example: **60 lb wire → size A2 sleeve**). Using an
  undersized sleeve for the wire test — the example given is an A2 sleeve on
  40 lb wire — crimps loose and lets the connection slip under a fish's
  fighting drag (`S80GRyuAbLY`, `_Z4yMtrYgeA`). ⚠ Fact-check
  (contradicted-internal): [Haywire Twist — wahoo live-bait
  leader](haywire-twist.md#wahoo-live-bait-leader) cites a different source
  (`4uNPLknRAQg`) showing on-camera 40 lb wire packaging calling for this
  same A2 sleeve, directly conflicting with the "undersized" framing here;
  that note resolves the conflict by matching the sleeve to the size printed
  on the wire's own spool rather than trusting either figure blind, a
  caveat this note doesn't carry. An earlier (2009), independent
  citation of the same match-crimp-to-wire-test rule, for wahoo
  casting/trolling-lure wire specifically, states it without a specific size

  casting/trolling-lure wire specifically" scope is this note's own
