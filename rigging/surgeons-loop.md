# Surgeon's Loop (Giant Yellowtail, Long-Range)

A surgeon's-knot loop tied into doubled heavy monofilament at the working end
of the main line, sized as a stable attachment point that different terminal
knots and hooks get tied onto as bait and conditions change through a
multi-day trip. A build the presenter calls "very popular" for **giant-grade
yellowtail on 6-to-8-day long-range trips well down the Baja Peninsula**
(`2O4Z0S78KLg`), where the fish run **30, 40, 50, 60 lb** — bigger than
anywhere near home (`CdjT_I_PBHQ`). Four SearcherSportfishing Tackle Tip
Thursday segments from 2018 to 2024 document the build; it is the same
doubled-line surgeon's-knot mechanic as the smaller single-hook rockfish
[dropper loop](essential-knots.md) already covered in Essential SoCal Knots,
scaled up here into a heavier, giant-yellowtail-specific rig.

## When to use

- Giant-grade yellowtail on long-range 6-to-8-day trips well down the Baja
  Peninsula, where standard lighter line is "just too light" — "these fish are
  too big and mean" (`2O4Z0S78KLg`).
- Fished day or night; big yellowtail are called out specifically as night
  feeders (`CdjT_I_PBHQ`).
- The finished loop is a general-purpose attachment point, not tied to one
  terminal rig — different terminal knots and hooks get tied onto it depending
  on the day's conditions and bait (`2O4Z0S78KLg`).

## Parameters

**Line and reel:** built into **80 lb, or better 100 lb, test** (`2O4Z0S78KLg`).
An earlier SearcherSportfishing segment states **60 or 80 lb** for the same
rig (`eLPTMO3-_1Q`, 2018) — a lower figure from six years earlier, not
reconciled to the 2024 number. Paired reel/line: a **Penn Fathom two-speed
model 80** conventional with an **80 or 100 lb** topshot — "nothing less than
80, 100 is better" (`CdjT_I_PBHQ`; full writeup in [rod & reel
selection](../tackle/rod-and-reel-selection.md#long-range-yellowtail-rodlineleader--tackle-tip-thursday-darren-searchersportfishing)).
The 2018 segment names only an ASR-garbled reel type, "juice beet reel,"
unresolved to a known model, stated as "kind of mandatory" (`eLPTMO3-_1Q`). ⚠
asr-uncertain: reel name unresolved.

**Building the loop (`2O4Z0S78KLg`):** double about **6 in** of line, tie a
simple overhand ("granny knot") in the doubled section, then pass the loop
through itself **3 to 4 times** — the on-camera pass count wavers between
three and four. Size the loop a little bigger than it looks like it needs to
be before cinching; it draws down smaller once seated. Before cinching, give
the doubled strand a light tug — a small **figure-eight** shape should appear
in the knot; if it doesn't show, retie rather than cinch a bad one. Cinch by
holding both legs of the doubled strand firmly — wrapped around the fingers —
and pulling hard, not a jerk. Heavy lubrication (saliva) is required to seat
the knot fully; if it won't tighten all the way, re-wet and continue rather
than force it dry. Finished size is a loop a little over **2 ft** — the size
these fish "like" on the Searcher — corroborated at **2 to 2½ ft** in a
same-series segment using the same "four-turn surgeon's knot" description
(`CdjT_I_PBHQ`), read as the same house figure rather than an independent
confirmation.

**2018 build, different length figures for the same mechanic (`eLPTMO3-_1Q`):**
described as "a method that was used on most long-range trips," with no
specific destination named. Start with about a **24 in** loop of doubled
line, tie a surgeon's knot with **3 passes** ("once, twice, three times"),
cinch tight, leaving an **18 in** intermediate dropper loop; after the hook is
tied on, about **12 in** of loop remains above the sinker. The rig fishes as a
straight bottom bait rig off this loop: the sinker sits at the very bottom,
tied on with a **clinch or improved clinch knot**, and the hook/bait rides on
the loop above it.

**Sinker:** **16 oz** minimum, stepped up to **24 oz or more** for bigger bait
or more wind/current — the crew's call on the day (`CdjT_I_PBHQ`). The same 16
oz minimum recurs in the 2018 build (`eLPTMO3-_1Q`) and in a Guadalupe Island
bottom application on an 80-100 lb-class outfit (`zuAuk-Kfa1Y`; full outfit in
[Searcher four-outfit Guadalupe
quiver](../tackle/searcher-four-outfit-guadalupe-quiver.md#outfit-4--talica-25-80100-lb-test--guadalupe-island-three-application-outfit)).
The same 16 oz/24 oz figures appear in [dropper loop — Baja, Sea of Cortez
reference weights](../techniques/dropper-loop.md#building-the-rig) for a
different fishery (Cortez cabrilla/grouper) — a cross-fishery match on the
numbers, not the same doctrine.

**Hook:** explicitly not a small live-bait hook — described on camera as **"a
60 J hook,"** stepped to an **"80 or a 90"** for bigger bait, read as **6/0, up
to 8/0 or 9/0** (`CdjT_I_PBHQ`; see [hooks — size, bait first, fish
second](../tackle/hooks.md#size--bait-first-fish-second)). ⚠ asr-uncertain:
the presenter's own on-camera hedge ("I believe is a...") leaves this figure
uncertain; recorded here as both the caption reading and the corrected size
notation. The 2018 build instead names a specific model, **Owner Gorilla
live-bait hooks, sized 5/0 to 7/0** (`eLPTMO3-_1Q`) — a narrower, smaller
range than the 2024 figure, a different-dated data point from the same
channel, not reconciled to one number. The Guadalupe Island citation gives
**6/0 or 7/0** on a nose-hooked sardine (`zuAuk-Kfa1Y`), inside both ranges
above; no loop-tying detail is given there, so it corroborates the sinker and
hook figures only.

**Attaching the hook to the loop — three ways (`CdjT_I_PBHQ`):** which one is
used is "really just what your favorite is," except the third is called out as
situational.

1. Thread the hook onto the doubled line before cinching the loop, so it rides
   captured inside the finished loop — "a lot of people like that" (`CdjT_I_PBHQ`).
2. Tie it on afterward with a **four-turn Uni knot** onto the closed loop, or
   any preferred knot.
3. **Paternoster (single-leg) variant, for a finickier bite:** clip one leg of
   the finished loop short, leaving the other long, and tie the hook to the
   end of that single leg. This converts the terminal presentation from
   doubled line to a single, longer line so the bait can swim around and be
   more active — "a little bit more finesse" while still fished on the 80-100
   lb main line. Reach for it specifically when a fish is a little finicky and
   might be easier to fool on the more natural presentation. ⚠ asr-uncertain:
   captions render the presenter's name for this rig as "pattern noer," read
   here as paternoster on phonetics and mechanism, not a name spelled out
   elsewhere in the corpus.

A concrete 2018 example of method 2 (`eLPTMO3-_1Q`): double the line, pass it
through the hook eye, then tie a **San Diego jam knot** — wrap the doubled
line down 3 to 4 times, pass the tag through the bottom loop and then the top
loop, cinch, and trim (mechanic in [San Diego Jam
Knot](san-diego-jam-knot.md)).

**Fishing it:** drop the bait to the bottom and leave the reel in gear — "not
a light tackle technique... not a finesse technique" outside the paternoster
variant's bait presentation (`CdjT_I_PBHQ`).
