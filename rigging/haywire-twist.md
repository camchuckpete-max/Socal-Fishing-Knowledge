# Haywire Twist

The connection that terminates single-strand wire leader — to a lure or a
hook — for toothy fish that shear even heavy mono. It twists the wire itself
(no crimp) and finishes with a barrel wrap and a clean break-off;
braided/cable-type wire is crimped instead (see [crimping](crimping.md)).

## When to use

- Single-strand wire terminals for toothy fish that shear mono no matter how
  heavy — wahoo, kingfish, and [barracuda](../species/barracuda.md). Wire is
  reached for only when heavy mono actually gets cut; mono stays preferred
  otherwise, for its lower visibility and easier handling (`lm7D9Tlc7Po`).
- Single-strand wire takes the haywire twist; braided/cable-type wire is
  crimped instead (`lm7D9Tlc7Po`).
- Trolling small hoochies for sierra off Cabo San Lucas, single-strand wire
  is the stated preference over braided wire, which is used only as a
  fallback when single-strand isn't available locally — consistent with the
  split above (`ll7r4A6atno`). See [Cabo surf-line sierra
  trolling](../techniques/trolling.md#cabo-san-lucas--surf-line-slow-trolling-for-sierra).

## Parameters

- **Tag length:** leave six to seven inches of working room, more if new to
  the twist (`lm7D9Tlc7Po`).
- **Setting the loop:** slide the wire through the eye; the first twist sets
  the loop size around the lure. Flat-jaw pliers hold the loop best for that
  first twist (`lm7D9Tlc7Po`).
- **Angle:** hold the two legs at roughly 45°, not 90° — both legs must
  twist around each other equally rather than one leg wrapping a straight
  other leg. Spread the legs to lock the angle, twist, back out, spread
  again, twist — repeat while drawing the twist out (`lm7D9Tlc7Po`).
- **Tension:** too tight (legs spread too far apart, too much pressure)
  overheats and weakens the wire; too loose (twists drawn out long and
  stretched) can slide and heat under load and break. A middle tension holds
  without either failure mode (`lm7D9Tlc7Po`).
- **Length, not count:** don't count twists — about one to one and a half
  inches of haywire twist is what carries the load (`lm7D9Tlc7Po`).
- **Barrel wrap finish:** bend the tag to 90° to the main wire and lay five
  or six barrel wraps side by side to finish the twist (`lm7D9Tlc7Po`).
- **Break-off:** don't cut the tag with pliers — the stub left behind will
  cut a hand while fishing. Bend the tag to 90° and crank it along the wire
  like a reel handle until it snaps off clean; get the broken-off tag into
  the garbage immediately, since loose on deck it can plug a bilge or stand
  up and go through a foot (`lm7D9Tlc7Po`).
- **A second, independent build** uses 69 lb wire — chosen for ease of
  twisting, not just to save weight — angles the legs before twisting six or
  seven times, then finishes by overlaying the tag back over the main line
  and wrapping it four or five times (an approximate count, not a fixed
  number) instead of a barrel wrap. The tag is broken off by pinching it
  between the fingers and working it up and down until it fatigues off
  clean, rather than cranking it against pliers (`0zgmoOH3Qag`).

## Wahoo casting leader — length and test

Casting a wahoo bomb or Raider jig (see [wahoo bomb & Raider jig
casting](../techniques/wahoo-bomb-casting.md)) attaches with seven-strand or
single-strand wire finished with a wire twist and a ring or swivel at the
top end. Leader length runs 12–15 in, wire test 50 lb (62 lb also usable) —
anything lighter risks a bite-off (`-62xDo4UQzo`). This build finishes both
the seven-strand and single-strand options with a wire twist, which does not
confirm or resolve the single-strand/haywire vs. braided/crimp split above;
no crimp sleeve or tool is shown on camera for the seven-strand option
(`-62xDo4UQzo`).

## Wahoo casting/trolling leader — crimped build, heavier wire

A wahoo cast/troll-to-jig leader also gets built from 275 lb steel wire,
crimped (not twisted) with a swivel crimped to each end — one end to the
main line, the other to the jig. Stated length is about 3 ft, though the
same clip separately states 39 in (asr-uncertain on which figure is
accurate — 39 in ≈ 3.25 ft, not an even 3 ft) (`o6Sawz5S7bk`). This is a far
heavier, crimped build than the 50–62 lb twisted leader above for what reads
as the same casting/trolling-to-jig application; "steel wire" here is not
stated as single- or multi-strand, so it doesn't resolve which stranding the
seven-strand build above used. See [crimping](crimping.md#parameters) for
this same video's crimp mechanic — wire crimped fully tight at both ends,
with no flared "valley," unlike the mono/fluoro build documented there.

A dedicated trolling-lure build (not casting) uses 200 lb wire (a second
demonstration in the same session shows 200–250 lb), uncoated, crimped with
the sleeve size printed on the wire's own packaging — sizing the sleeve up a
size risks losing the fish and the lure. Loop size holds at about 1 in
minimum; shorter costs the lure some of its swimming action, longer doesn't
meaningfully matter. Leader length runs 30–36 in — a 24 in leader shown on
camera is called too short. The swivel is heavy black, not chrome — a
chrome swivel gets bitten through often (`4uNPLknRAQg`). Full mechanics,
speed, and gear-class detail for this session are in [wahoo
trolling](../techniques/wahoo-trolling.md#rigging).

## Wahoo live-bait leader

A live-bait wahoo leader (a flylined sardine, no sinker) connects to the
hook with wire — single-strand or multi-strand — via small connector rings,
in 200 or 300 lb test (`9IhmYstB8sA`). See
[wahoo](../species/wahoo.md#situations--techniques) for the species-level
routing and the wind-through-the-bite hookset doctrine that goes with this
rig.

Wire-test figures for this same application vary widely across independent
tackle-tip builds and are not reconciled in the corpus — no source ties the
gap to bait size, leader length, or target grade: 44 lb wire to a 3/0 hook,
framed on camera as "all you need," with wire on the leader generally called
out as cutting bite-offs on flylined wahoo and fluorocarbon named as some
anglers' substitute (`G81HN0dIDg0`); 60 lb wire with a matching size-A2
crimp sleeve, 24 in of wire cut down to an 18–20 in finished leader with a
1-0 or 2-0 hook, built by sliding the sleeve on first, threading it through
the hook eye and back through the sleeve to close a roughly 1/2 in loop, tag
folded back before crimping down multiple times (`S80GRyuAbLY`); 40 lb
seven-strand wire to a hook heard on camera as "a number 100" (asr-uncertain
— no standard hook size runs that high; likely a mis-heard 1-0), 12–18 in
overall, with a small swivel or black ring to the 30–40 lb flyline main line
(`vNIazq1aVwc`); and 40 lb, seven-strand, uncoated Berkley wire to a Mustad
2/0 hook, tied to 30 or 40 lb mono main line, made up in batches of four or
five leaders per 30 ft spool of wire (`4uNPLknRAQg`). The 40 lb figure is
the only one independently corroborated twice (`vNIazq1aVwc`, `4uNPLknRAQg`).
On sleeve size specifically, the 60 lb and 40 lb citations directly
conflict on which pound test the A2 sleeve matches — one states 60 lb wire
takes the A2 and that an A2 on 40 lb wire crimps loose and fails
(`S80GRyuAbLY`), the other shows on-camera 40 lb wire packaging calling for
the same A2 sleeve (`4uNPLknRAQg`). Match the sleeve to the size printed on
the wire's own spool rather than trusting either figure blind.

The earliest build in this set pairs a single-strand leader — haywire twist
to a small ring, no stated wire test, flylined with no sinker on a 40 lb
outfit, called on camera "one of the best methods for catching wahoo" — with
a seven-strand alternative crimped to an 18 in minimum length; both
terminate in a 3/0 hook (`jqyu3wZdNF4`). This single-strand build is the one
that actually takes the haywire twist; the crimped multi-strand builds above
terminate with a crimp instead.

Gear: an 8 ft rod, a reel loaded with 40–50 lb main line, and "a short wire
leader" is rated for live-bait wahoo up to 60–70 lb, though no wire test or
leader length is given for that specific leader (`W6RuHvaqkHs`).

Bait hook position for this rig — nose, shoulder/collar, or belly — follows
the general [live-bait hook-position
table](../bait/fishing-live-bait.md#hook-position-by-situation)
(`vNIazq1aVwc`).
