# Tony Pena Knot — Mono-to-Spectra Splice

A heavy-wrap connection knot for splicing monofilament to spectra (braid) at
the tackle bench — built or repaired before a trip, not a mid-fight retie
(`7TPJsMc_clA`). Built from the same uni-knot family as the [uni-to-uni
connection in essential knots](essential-knots.md#parameters--judgment), but
tied with a much higher wrap count per pass and finished with a mechanical
line-gripping "knot puller" tool instead of hand-cinching. Three sources
describe the tie, and two mechanically distinct builds share the "Tony Pena
knot" name — see Parameters for both.

## When to use

- Splicing mono to spectra on 80 lb class line at the tackle bench, building
  or repairing long-range terminal tackle before a trip (`7TPJsMc_clA`). ⚠
  Fact-check (single-source): only `7TPJsMc_clA` gives a line-class figure
  for the 11-up/11-down tie; no other cited source in this note states 80 lb
  for it.
- Reach for the standard [uni-to-uni](essential-knots.md#parameters--judgment)
  (~6 wraps) for a quick repair splice; use the heavier 11-up/11-down,
  tool-cinched build below when there's bench time for the extra wrap count.
- Also usable as a general braid-to-mono/fluoro leader or top-shot
  connection across a wide line-class range, from very light line up to over
  100 lb test, with a working window of 60 to about 100-130 lb
  (`SK2M7L2vA1s`). ⚠ Fact-check (single-source): only `SK2M7L2vA1s` states
  this working window; `m6NxHaIifj8` is duplicated footage of the same
  demonstration (see Parameters below), not an independent confirming
  source.

## Parameters

**Two mechanically distinct ties share the "Tony Pena knot" name — check
which one before tying.**

- **11-up/11-down uni wrap, tool-cinched** (`7TPJsMc_clA`): each side of the
  splice is tied as a uni-style wrap taken 11 turns up, then 11 turns back
  down, at roughly a 45-degree angle to the standing line — three of these
  11-up/11-down passes per side. Cinch each pass close but not fully tight;
  snug enough to hold its shape, tightened fully only at the finishing step.
  Register the second side by marking the gap with a finger held where the
  first knot ends, wrapping the second line's tag the same 11-up/11-down way
  at that spot, then feeding the tag end into the finger-held gap to
  seat/lock it. Finish with a dedicated line-gripping "knot puller" tool
  rather than hand-cinching — wrap the tool around the line about three
  times for grip before pulling; the tool avoids hand/finger rope burn on a
  heavy-line splice that would otherwise have to be hand-cinched. Trim tag
  ends close, leaving about 1/8 in, and confirm which two tags are being cut
  (the tags, not the standing lines) before cutting. A specific knot-puller
  product is named for purchase at 30fathoms.com — a promotional mention,
  low confidence, kept separate from the knot mechanics above.
  - ⚠ asr-uncertain: the 165-second clip's auto-generated captions are thin
    on visual mechanics — exactly how the tag end tucks back through the
    finger-held gap, and whether the "three passes" are three independent
    uni knots stacked before cinching or three wraps within one knot, isn't
    fully resolvable from audio alone. Watch the source video (below) for
    the hand mechanics; the wrap counts and angle above are the reliable,
    preserved parameters.

- **Cross-and-loop wrap, diameter-scaled turn count** (`SK2M7L2vA1s`;
  re-cut as a standalone tutorial 13 days later in `m6NxHaIifj8`, not an
  independent confirmation): described as a knot for "attaching braided
  line to a piece of monofilament or fluorocarbon for leader or top shot"
  (`SK2M7L2vA1s`), usable "from as light of line as you can imagine all the
  way upwards of even heavier than 100 lb test," with a working window of 60
  to about 100-130 lb (`SK2M7L2vA1s`). Cross the mono/fluoro leader and the
  braid into an X, pulling 6-10 in of tag on each side. Form a loop in the
  monofilament end, wrap the tag around it 3 times, and cinch that small
  loop down. Pinch a finger in the gap between the leader and the braided
  line, then wrap the braid around the mono 7-14 times, working down toward
  the loop — a diameter-scaling rule: thicker line takes fewer wraps,
  thinner line takes more. A 50 lb braid to 40 lb leader example uses 8
  wraps down; a lighter pairing such as 10 lb braid to 4 lb leader takes
  more. Switch hands, pinch the same spot, and wrap back up the same count
  (8 in the 50/40 example). Pass the braid tag through the loop where the
  pinching finger was, then pull the main braid line to draw that small loop
  away and set the wraps. Lubricate with saliva, water, or chapstick —
  chapstick and saliva rate as noticeably better lubricants than water
  alone. Cinch by wrapping both lines around the hands or gloves and drawing
  down slowly until it all comes together.

- **Not resolved — same knot or a different one under a shared name:** the
  initial 3-wrap mono loop and the wrap-count-scales-with-diameter rule
  aren't described in the 11-up/11-down tie above, and the wrap counts
  differ outright (7-14 in a single pass, hand-cinched, vs. 11-up/11-down
  repeated three times, tool-cinched). Flagged as a same-name/
  possibly-different-mechanic pair rather than merged, per the pattern
  already established for the [Seaguar knot](seaguar-knot.md). The
  `m6NxHaIifj8` re-cut matches every parameter of the `SK2M7L2vA1s` tie
  exactly (tag pulls, loop wraps, diameter-scaling rule, the 50/40/8-wraps
  example, the lubrication ranking, hand-or-glove cinch) and is treated as
  duplicated footage of the same demonstration rather than a second
  confirming source — it does not raise confidence beyond the medium this
  unregistered channel already caps at.
