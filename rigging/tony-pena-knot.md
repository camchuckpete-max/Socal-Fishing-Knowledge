# Tony Pena Knot — Mono-to-Spectra Splice

A heavy-wrap connection knot for splicing monofilament to spectra (braid),
demonstrated by Capt. Shawn Allen of the long-range boat *Searcher* as
tackle-bench prep rather than an on-the-water retie. It is built from the same
uni-knot family as the [uni-to-uni connection in essential
knots](essential-knots.md#parameters--judgment), but uses a much higher wrap
count per pass and is finished with a mechanical line-gripping "knot puller"
tool instead of cinching by hand — see that note for the standard-wrap-count
uni-to-uni this sits beside.

## When to use it

- Splicing **mono to spectra** on 80 lb class line at the tackle bench —
  building or repairing long-range terminal tackle before a trip, not a
  mid-fight retie.
- Choose the standard [uni-to-uni](essential-knots.md#parameters--judgment)
  (6-ish wraps) for a quick repair splice; reach for this heavier, more
  deliberate version when you have bench time and want the extra wrap count
  this note preserves.

## Parameters & judgment

- **Line class demonstrated:** 80 lb.
- **Wrap count:** each side of the splice is tied as a uni-style wrap taken
  **11 turns up, then 11 turns back down**, at roughly a **45-degree angle**
  to the standing line. Capt. Allen ties **three** of these 11-up/11-down
  passes per the demonstrated side. Cinch each pass down close but **not**
  fully tight yet — snug enough to hold its shape, tightened fully only at
  the finishing step below.
- **Registering the second side:** he marks the gap with a finger held right
  where the first knot ends, wraps the second line's tag the same
  11-up/11-down way at that spot, and only after the wraps are laid in does
  he feed the tag end into the same finger-held gap to seat/lock it.
- **Finishing with a knot puller:** rather than cinching the splice tight by
  hand, he seats it with a dedicated line-gripping "knot puller" tool,
  wrapped around the line about **three times** for grip before pulling —
  the tool's purpose is avoiding hand/finger rope-burn on a heavy-line splice
  that would otherwise have to be hand-cinched. He points viewers to a
  specific knot-puller product sold at 30fathoms.com — a product mention,
  **low confidence** (promotional), kept separate from the knot mechanics
  above.
- **Trim:** cut the tag ends close, leaving about **1/8 in**, and double-check
  which two tags you're cutting (i.e. not the standing lines) before cutting.
- **What the transcript doesn't resolve:** the auto-generated captions on this
  165-second clip are thin on the visual mechanics — exactly how the tag end
  tucks back through the finger-held gap, and whether the "three passes" are
  three independent uni knots stacked before cinching or three wraps within
  one knot, isn't fully resolvable from audio alone. Watch the source video
  (linked below) for the hand mechanics; treat the wrap counts and angle above
  as the reliable, preserved parameters. Flagged `asr-uncertain`.
