# Trap Rig

A two-hook live-bait rig that puts a second hook behind the lead hook so a
fish grabbing the back half of the bait still finds steel. Two applications
share the mechanism under the same name: a J-hook-plus-treble build for
[California halibut](../species/california-halibut.md) off Catalina, and a
two-single-hook build for Sea of Cortez grouper/cabrilla fished on longer live
baits.

## When to use

- Drifting live bait for California halibut in shallow water off Catalina
  (`qIKGJSEE2aY`) — a refinement of the basic
  [sliding sinker](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md) live-bait drift, adding a
  stinger to convert more short bites into hookups.
- Sea of Cortez grouper/cabrilla bottom fishing over reef structure, when the
  live bait is long rather than large — small yellowtail or barracuda
  (`M8hOYQ_6rSg`).

## Parameters

- **Halibut build — components:** a lead J hook with an additional stinger
  hook, a treble, set behind it (`qIKGJSEE2aY`).
- **Halibut build — bait rigging (sardine):** nose-hook the bait with the J
  hook right in front of the eyes, then set the treble stinger into the back
  half of the fish, leaving a little slack in the connection between the two
  hooks (`qIKGJSEE2aY`).
- **Halibut build — why the stinger:** halibut commonly grab and hold the
  tail end of a bait; the trailing treble on the back half hooks the bites a
  single nose hook would miss (`qIKGJSEE2aY`).
- **Halibut build — judgment:** keep slack to the stinger so the bait swims
  naturally instead of being bound tight between the two hooks.

## Baja, Sea of Cortez grouper variant

A same-family "trap hook" rig for grouper/cabrilla bottom fishing over reef
structure — the same idea as the halibut build above (a second hook covers
the part of the bait a fish grabs first), applied to a different target and a
longer live bait.

- **Components:** one hook through the **nose** of the bait, a second hook
  placed **toward the middle of the body or toward the tail** (`M8hOYQ_6rSg`).
- **When to fish it:** live baits that run **longer**, not necessarily
  larger — small yellowtail or barracuda used as bait. A grouper **under
  roughly 100 lb** often can't swallow a whole big bait, but will grab the
  **tail** of a long bait like a barracuda or small yellowtail; the trap hook
  covers that tail-grab bite a single nose hook would miss (`M8hOYQ_6rSg`).
- **Judgment:** choose the trap hook over a straight single grouper hook (see
  [grouper leader](../techniques/sliding-sinker.md#baja-sea-of-cortez-grouper-leader))
  by bait length and expected fish size, not as a fixed rule (`M8hOYQ_6rSg`).
