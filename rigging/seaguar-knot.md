# Seaguar Knot — Mono-to-Fluoro Bite-Guard Splice

A connection knot purpose-built to join **monofilament to fluorocarbon** — not
braid to fluoro, which is the [RP knot's](essential-knots.md#parameters--judgment)
job. The go-to for building a mono-main-line-to-fluoro-bite-guard
[flyline](../techniques/flyline.md) rig; see that note for the leader-sizing
logic it serves. Six sources across three channels demonstrate a "Seaguar
knot" for this connection, but they describe **two mechanically distinct
ties** under the same name (see Parameters) — kept as its own note, split out
of [Essential SoCal Knots](essential-knots.md) once that note passed ~400
lines, so the naming split stays visible instead of getting buried in a
larger knots catalog. ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-internal): [Essential SoCal
Knots](essential-knots.md) twice describes this same split as "five sourced
variants," against the six sources named here and in this note's own front
matter. ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): this lead also says "three
channels," but the six cited transcripts resolve to four — Dirty Hookers
(`48ZFXnCTTQE`), StokedOnFishing (`06lxuie5cZQ`, `tzeXXPAjqUY`, `i3qIAHW-SJc`),
SearcherSportfishing (`M-W5mEjh1MY`), and Tackle Express (`SK2M7L2vA1s`).

## When to use

- Tying a **fluorocarbon leader/bite guard onto a monofilament main line** —
  the [flyline](../techniques/flyline.md) application named across every
  source in this note.
- Not documented here as a general leader-to-main-line knot for braid
  systems — use the [RP knot](essential-knots.md#parameters--judgment) for a
  braid main line instead.
- One source frames it more broadly, for mono-to-mono as well as
  mono-to-fluoro connections generally (`SK2M7L2vA1s`).

## Parameters

**Two mechanically distinct ties share the "Seaguar knot" name — check which
one before tying.**

- **Opposite-direction lay, 2½ turns** (`48ZFXnCTTQE`): lay the mono tag and a
  ~3 ft fluoro tag running in opposite directions from each other, hold the
  two lines together, and lubricate the section about to be worked (chapstick;
  keep the gripped section dry so it doesn't slip in the hand). Cross the
  working end away from the angler, pinch the crossover, and feed the tag back
  through the loop just formed — going the same direction the loop was made —
  for **2½ turns**; that half-turn count is what separates this tie from a
  uni or a surgeon's knot rather than a plain overhand or wrap-and-jam. Bring
  the tag through, seat it, then pull the two main lines and the two tags
  down together to cinch. Rated very strong under a hard hand-pull test on
  camera, and preferred by the presenter over both the uni-to-uni (his prior
  go-to for ~4 years) and the surgeon's knot (his more recent ~1.5-year
  go-to) as the fastest and strongest mono-to-fluoro connection he's tied.
- **Loop/wrap-back/figure-eight family, five citations** (`06lxuie5cZQ`,
  `tzeXXPAjqUY`, `i3qIAHW-SJc`, `M-W5mEjh1MY`, `SK2M7L2vA1s`) — a different
  mechanic than the opposite-direction tie above, despite three of the five
  (`tzeXXPAjqUY`, `M-W5mEjh1MY`, `SK2M7L2vA1s`) also naming it "Seaguar knot"
  on camera: form a loop where the two overlapped/crossed
  lines meet, twist or wrap the loop back on itself several times, then work
  a tag (or the whole leader) back through the loop and draw it out before
  cinching. Correctly tied, the drawn-through bundle shows a **"figure
  eight"** shape just before cinching — the shared tell named in four of the
  five clips. The same-name split is the inverse of the San Diego jam /
  fisherman's knot split logged in [Improved Clinch
  Knot](improved-clinch-knot.md) — there, one mechanic carries two names;
  here, one name covers two mechanics. Turn counts: unambiguous **3 turns**
  (`M-W5mEjh1MY`,
  `SK2M7L2vA1s`); ASR-uncertain "three or four" (`tzeXXPAjqUY`);
  ASR-uncertain low counts in `06lxuie5cZQ` and `i3qIAHW-SJc` — read as
  approximately 3 turns rather than a confirmed different number.

Per-clip specifics for the loop/wrap-back/figure-eight family:

- `06lxuie5cZQ`: the on-camera audio does not confirm a name for this tie, so
  it is not assumed to be the Seaguar knot — treated as a distinct build
  pending corroboration. No line class, species, or region stated.
- `tzeXXPAjqUY`: states "this is called the Seaguar knot" on camera
  [00:00:22]. Lay the mono main line and fluoro leader in parallel, grab the pair
  at the midpoint, form the loop, wrap it back on itself, reach through, grab
  the fluoro leader and its own tag together, and draw them through.
  Possibly the same build as `06lxuie5cZQ` (same channel) — not merged, since
  neither clip's wrap count is confirmed precisely enough to compare.
- `i3qIAHW-SJc`: frames the leader length as a cost-saving choice — leave
  about **a foot** of monofilament above the connection point before
  pinching in the fluorocarbon, since leader material is the expensive side
  of the connection. A second technique from this clip, hanging a sinker off
  this knot's tag end, is logged in [rubber-band deep
  rig](rubber-band-deep-rig.md#tag-end-sinker-attachment-off-the-mono-to-fluoro-knot-stokedonfishing).
- `M-W5mEjh1MY`: names it "the Seaguar knot" on camera [00:00:15]. Cut a
  **3–4 ft** piece of fluorocarbon for the leader/bite
  guard and oppose it to the mono main line off the reel (opposite-direction
  lay, matching the `48ZFXnCTTQE` lay above, though the fold/wrap that
  follows it matches this loop family instead). Grab all **four strands**
  (two main lines, two tags) and pull together slowly; wet the knot just
  before it fully seats, drop the two tag ends, pull the two main lines
  together the rest of the way, then trim. The only other entry in this note
  with a stated leader length, in the same range as the ~3 ft fluoro tag
  above.
- `SK2M7L2vA1s`: names it "the seagar [Seaguar] knot" on camera [00:14:16],
  and scopes it wider than the flyline bite-guard use named
  elsewhere in this note — "attaching mono to mono or mono to fluorocarbon."
  Cross the leader and main line into an X, pulling **6–8 in** of tag on
  each side (a tag length not given elsewhere in this note); twist the loop
  3 times, lubricate, then grab all four strands and cinch slowly, finishing
  by pulling the main line down first, then both tag lines, to work
  remaining slack out. Trim both tags to a couple centimeters.

**Trimming the tag (general to any knot):** clip it close with a clamp or
nail-clipper-type tool rather than biting it off — biting can nick and
weaken the line. As an alternative to a flush trim, leave a small loop in
the tag end instead of cutting it fully off; looped over a line-keeper/
tackle-organizer clip, it has no effect on the connection's strength
(`06lxuie5cZQ`).
