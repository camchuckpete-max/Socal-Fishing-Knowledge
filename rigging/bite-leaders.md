# Bluefin Bite Leaders

A bite leader is a short, heavy fluorocarbon section crimped between the main
line and the jig — the most critical part of a
[bluefin](../species/bluefin-tuna.md) knife-jig setup (`kO_BqzUYayc`). A hooked
tuna often inhales the jig deep, and braid or lighter mono sawing against its
teeth and gill rakers there can cut the leader; the heavy fluoro takes that
abuse instead. The gear is set for the biggest fish that could eat the drop —
anything from a 20 lb schoolie to a 300 lb trophy on the same jig — so the bite
leader is sized for the worst case (`kO_BqzUYayc`). It sits in the
[line and leader](../tackle/line-and-leader.md) system between the braid main
line and the lure.

## When to use

- Standard on [knife jigging](../techniques/knife-jigging.md) for bluefin,
  where a 200–300 g jig drops straight up and down and any grade of fish may
  hit — especially the night bite, when the bigger fish tend to come and
  anglers run the heaviest gear (`kO_BqzUYayc`).
- Complements the trolling and casting approaches in
  [bluefin tuna](../species/bluefin-tuna.md) and
  [bluefin trolling](../species/bluefin-tuna-trolling.md) — the bite leader is
  the jig-fishing terminal, not a troll rig.
- On [casting small heavy metal at a bluefin
  foamer](../techniques/foamer-casting.md), a short **~2 ft, 150–200 lb** bite
  leader is an optional confidence add over a bare top-shot-to-lure
  connection, not a requirement — length in range with the ≤2–2.5 ft rule
  below, weight heavier than the 130 lb knife-jig build (`42A8Owhc8fw`).

## Parameters

- **Length:** no more than about **2 to 2.5 ft**. The large swivel crimped to
  the top end will not wind through the guides, so a longer leader strands the
  angler at the top-shot connection with the fish still at deep color and no
  line left to wind (`kO_BqzUYayc`).
- **Fluoro class:** heavy, abrasion-resistant fluoro — **130 lb** in the base
  build, over **80–100 lb braid** main line, sometimes with a short shock
  leader above (`kO_BqzUYayc`).
- **Crimp matched to leader:** on 130 lb fluoro, a **size 1.6 crimp** with a
  matching **1.6 chafe sleeve** — the sleeve protects the loop of mono riding
  around the lure's ring. Crimpers are numbered to sleeve size; seat the crimp
  in the matching slot (e.g. the 1.5–2 slot) (`kO_BqzUYayc`).
- **Crimp pressure:** snug, not maximal — two presses per crimp (one top, one
  bottom). Over-crimping bites into and weakens the fluoro; leave a tag
  through the second side of the crimp so the loop can't pull back
  (`kO_BqzUYayc`). See [crimping](crimping.md) for the general build order and
  seating mechanic (ball the tag end, chafe sleeve, flared ends,
  firm-handshake pressure) behind any crimped connection, including the
  sizing above.
- **Main-line connection:** crimp a large swivel to the top end — too large to
  pass the guides, which is why leader length is capped above (`kO_BqzUYayc`).
- **Jig connection:** for a dedicated single-jig leader, crimp directly
  through the lure's solid wire ring rather than a split ring — fewer twists,
  the strongest join. Use a split ring only for one bite leader swapped across
  several jigs (`kO_BqzUYayc`).
  - A [flat-fall-class jig](../lures/knife-jigs.md) build instead crimps a
    swivel to the top end and a heavy-duty split ring to the bottom — the
    split-ring option above, tied to a named flat-fall build. Same crimp
    mechanic each end: crimp sleeve then chafe sleeve slid on before
    attaching the swivel/split ring, tag end burned and balled, crimp seated
    in the tool hole matching sleeve size, one firm-handshake press per side
    — matching the general build order in [crimping](crimping.md). No
    pound-test or crimp-sleeve size given (`6wbO7qaU3sI`).
  - An earlier build on the same flat-fall class states the numbers that one
    omits: **24 in** of **200 lb fluorocarbon** (a brand name is spoken on
    camera but renders unrecognizably in captions — asr-uncertain), reasoned
    because a flat-fall tends to get eaten on the sink — the lure and leader
    sit well down in the water, so chafe resistance matters more than on a
    lure fished shallow. Build order: **size 1 crimp sleeve** slid on, a short
    piece of chafe gear added, tag end passed through the flat-fall's ring
    then back through the crimp sleeve to close the loop, tag end burned to a
    mushroomed ball, crimped leaving roughly **1/8 in** of sleeve
    flared/uncrimped at each end — the same ball-the-tag-end and flared-ends
    mechanics [crimping](crimping.md) describes. This clip shows only the
    jig-end loop, not the main-line-side connection, so it isn't reconciled
    with the swivel-top/split-ring-bottom build above (`KGrussv1s3U`).
- **Failure modes:** leader too long to wind (swivel jams at the tip); crimp
  mismatched to leader diameter; over-crimped fluoro; too little tag through
  the crimp; connecting through a weak split ring on a big-fish jig
  (`kO_BqzUYayc`).
- **Direct-tie alternative:** some crews skip the crimped bite leader
  entirely — 80–100 lb braid, FG knot to a **15–20 ft mono/fluoro top shot
  tied straight to the jig, no crimp** (West Coast Jiggerz, `j37zxs33gws`).
  Both schools are fished in the SoCal bluefin fleet; the choice turns on
  hardware vs. length — the crimped leader adds a swivel-and-crimp connection
  sized to survive the biggest fish that could eat the drop, held to a short
  (≤2.5 ft) section, while the direct-tied top shot skips that extra hardware
  but puts 15–20 ft of mono/fluoro between braid and jig, with no separate
  heavier section dedicated to absorbing a big fish's teeth. See
  [knife/speed jigging](../techniques/knife-jigging.md) for the other
  leader-connection builds fished alongside these two.
