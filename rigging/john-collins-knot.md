# John Collins Knot — Braid to Mono/Fluoro Splice

A named connection knot joining **braid to monofilament or fluorocarbon**.
Demonstrated across three SearcherSportfishing Tackle Tip Thursday episodes —
Captain Rob Taylor (Vol. 214), Pete Gray of the Let's Talk Hook-Up radio show
(Vol. 150), and an unidentified presenter (Vol. 96). It is a high-wrap,
symmetrical splice in the same family as the
[RP knot and uni-to-uni](essential-knots.md#parameters--judgment) and the
[Tony Pena knot](tony-pena-knot.md), but with its own name and wrap count —
kept as its own note rather than folded into [Essential SoCal
Knots](essential-knots.md), which is already long, per the same reasoning
already applied to [Springer Knot](springer-knot.md) and
[Tony Pena Knot](tony-pena-knot.md).

## When to use it

- A **braid-to-mono or braid-to-fluoro** connection knot — the presenter
  names both leader materials on camera ("John Collins not braid mono or
  Floral [fluoro]"). No line class, species, or specific rig application is
  stated in this clip.
- Stated result: with big braid to big mono, it finishes as "a nice clean
  barrel" — read as a heavier-line splice in the same territory as the
  [RP knot](essential-knots.md#parameters--judgment) and [Tony Pena
  Knot](tony-pena-knot.md), though no pound-test figure is given here to
  compare directly.
- **Line-class threshold, with figures (Pete Gray, Let's Talk Hook-Up /
  SearcherSportfishing, Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 150, `b-oixz7pgAo`,
  2021-06-10):** the presenter's own stated boundary — use the John
  Collins/RP knot on connections **up to about 80 lb test**; above 80 lb
  test, switch to a loop-to-loop connection with a **Sato crimp**, built
  onto a pre-made or self-tied [wind-on leader](wind-on-leader.md), instead.
  He states using the John Collins knot itself at **15 lb, 20 lb, and 60 lb
  test**. (The Sato crimp is named only — no build detail is given in this
  clip, so it isn't broken out as its own note here; treat it as a named
  alternative to look up separately for >80 lb connections.)

## Parameters & judgment

- **Setup:** feed the working line up through the bottom, leaving plenty of
  tag line to work with, then pinch the end.
- **Wrap count — symmetrical, up and back down:** wrap **9 times** one
  direction, then let go with the wrapping hand, hold with the other hand,
  and wrap **9 times** back down — a matched 9-up/9-down count, mechanically
  similar in shape to the Tony Pena knot's 11-up/11-down passes but with a
  different count and, unlike that knot, tied by hand rather than finished
  with a knot-puller tool.
- **The trick — exit through the loop, not down through it:** on the return
  pass, do not pass back down through the loop; come back **up through the
  loop** instead. The presenter calls this out explicitly as the step that
  makes the knot work.
- **Cinching:** very carefully take the slack out so the wraps come down on
  top of themselves and seat clean, forming the "nice clean barrel" described
  above.
- **Trim:** cut the braid tag end off nice and tight, and separately cut the
  mono/fluoro tag end off nice and tight, "so that it goes through your
  guides" — both tags trimmed close for a low-profile, guide-passing finish.

    around **5 times**, pinch at the bottom of the wraps, then wrap **back 5

    not because more wraps fail.

    point before the final cinch — stated to seat the knot a little better.
    Pulling against something rigid (he suggests doweling) seats it a
    little better than pulling it tight by hand/wrapped around your
    sleeves, which is the fallback he demonstrates.

    cinching, for a thin knot that passes the guides cleanly.
