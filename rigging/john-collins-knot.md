# John Collins Knot — Braid to Mono/Fluoro Splice

A named connection knot joining **braid to monofilament or fluorocarbon**.
Demonstrated across three SearcherSportfishing Tackle Tip Thursday episodes —
Captain Rob Taylor (Vol. 214), Pete Gray of the Let's Talk Hook-Up radio show
(Vol. 150), and an unidentified presenter (Vol. 96). It is a high-wrap,
symmetrical splice in the same family as the
[RP knot and uni-to-uni](essential-knots.md#parameters--judgment) and the
[Tony Pena knot](tony-pena-knot.md), but with its own name and wrap count —
kept as its own note rather than folded into [Essential SoCal
Knots](essential-knots.md), which is already long, per the same reasoning
already applied to [Springer Knot](springer-knot.md) and
[Tony Pena Knot](tony-pena-knot.md).

## When to use it

- A **braid-to-mono or braid-to-fluoro** connection knot — the presenter
  names both leader materials on camera ("John Collins not braid mono or
  Floral [fluoro]"). No line class, species, or specific rig application is
  stated in this clip.
- Stated result: with big braid to big mono, it finishes as "a nice clean
  barrel" — read as a heavier-line splice in the same territory as the
  [RP knot](essential-knots.md#parameters--judgment) and [Tony Pena
  Knot](tony-pena-knot.md), though no pound-test figure is given here to
  compare directly.
- **Line-class threshold, with figures (Pete Gray, Let's Talk Hook-Up /
  SearcherSportfishing, Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 150, `b-oixz7pgAo`,
  2021-06-10):** the presenter's own stated boundary — use the John
  Collins/RP knot on connections **up to about 80 lb test**; above 80 lb
  test, switch to a loop-to-loop connection with a **Sato crimp**, built
  onto a pre-made or self-tied [wind-on leader](wind-on-leader.md), instead.
  He states using the John Collins knot itself at **15 lb, 20 lb, and 60 lb
  test**. (The Sato crimp is named only — no build detail is given in this
  clip, so it isn't broken out as its own note here; treat it as a named
  alternative to look up separately for >80 lb connections.)

## Parameters & judgment

- **Setup:** feed the working line up through the bottom, leaving plenty of
  tag line to work with, then pinch the end.
- **Wrap count — symmetrical, up and back down:** wrap **9 times** one
  direction, then let go with the wrapping hand, hold with the other hand,
  and wrap **9 times** back down — a matched 9-up/9-down count, mechanically
  similar in shape to the Tony Pena knot's 11-up/11-down passes but with a
  different count and, unlike that knot, tied by hand rather than finished
  with a knot-puller tool.
- **The trick — exit through the loop, not down through it:** on the return
  pass, do not pass back down through the loop; come back **up through the
  loop** instead. The presenter calls this out explicitly as the step that
  makes the knot work.
- **Cinching:** very carefully take the slack out so the wraps come down on
  top of themselves and seat clean, forming the "nice clean barrel" described
  above.
- **Trim:** cut the braid tag end off nice and tight, and separately cut the
  mono/fluoro tag end off nice and tight, "so that it goes through your
  guides" — both tags trimmed close for a low-profile, guide-passing finish.
- **What the transcript doesn't resolve:** this is a 129-second clip with
  thin auto-generated captions and no narrated line class, species, or
  application; the exact hand mechanics of the "up through the loop, not
  down through it" step are visual and not fully resolvable from audio
  alone. Flagged `asr-uncertain` for the fine mechanics; watch the source

- **Third demonstration, wrap-count corroboration and origin story (Pete
  Gray, Let's Talk Hook-Up radio show, via SearcherSportfishing, Tackle Tip
  Thursday Vol. 150, `b-oixz7pgAo`, 2021-06-10 — dated between the Vol. 96
  and Vol. 214 clips above; registered channel, and this is now a third
  independent demonstration of the same knot, which is why confidence is
  raised to high above):** origin credited on camera to **John Collins**, "a
  very well-known knot expert and fisherman," who "invented this knot in the
  wheelhouse of the *Royal Polaris*" — the presenter also calls it the **RP
  knot** by that origin, and uses the two names interchangeably for what he
  presents as one knot. **Naming conflict with [Essential SoCal
  Knots](essential-knots.md#parameters--judgment), not reconciled:** that
  note's "RP knot" entry (sourced from other clips) is a **different**
  construction — a doubled fluoro line with the braid passed up through it,
  6-up/6-down wrap — kept there as mechanically distinct from the John
  Collins knot's exit-through-the-loop, 9-up (Vol. 214) or 5-up (Vol. 96,
  this clip) construction. The two entries share a ~80 lb "by the book"
  threshold and both independently mention a wood dowel for seating, which
  is suggestive, but the wrap counts as sourced still disagree, so this is
  logged as a conflict rather than merged into one knot. Demoed joining
  PowerPro (hollow-ace variant here, but stated to work the same with
  regular PowerPro) to Seaguar Fluoro Premier fluorocarbon (described as
  thinner-diameter and softer than standard fluoro, and easy to tie).
  - **Wrap count — 5 up, 5 down, matching Vol. 96's figure, not Vol. 214's:**
    loop the working line, pass the braid through, pinch, wrap the braid
    around **5 times**, pinch at the bottom of the wraps, then wrap **back 5
    times** through the loop already made — "it doesn't matter which side it
    comes in or out of." This corroborates the earlier Vol. 96 5-up/5-down
    count rather than the later Vol. 214 9-up/9-down count; kept side by
    side per the standing wrap-count conflict above, not reconciled.
  - **Explicit rationale for staying at 5 (decision reasoning, not just a
    number):** anticipating the question, the presenter states you can tie
    it with 7 or 8 loops instead, "but it doesn't matter" — his
    recommendation is to stick with 5 because it makes "a nice nice thin
    easy through your guides knot," i.e. a low-profile, "stealthy" result,
    not because more wraps fail.
  - **Seating tip:** after bringing the knot down, jerk it once at that
    point before the final cinch — stated to seat the knot a little better.
    Pulling against something rigid (he suggests doweling) seats it a
    little better than pulling it tight by hand/wrapped around your
    sleeves, which is the fallback he demonstrates.
  - **Trim:** cut both tag ends (braid and mono/fluoro) close after
    cinching, for a thin knot that passes the guides cleanly.
  - **What this transcript doesn't resolve:** a 227-second clip with thin
    auto-generated captions; the fine hand mechanics of forming the initial
    loop and feeding the braid through are visual and not fully resolvable
    from audio alone. Flagged `asr-uncertain` for those mechanics.

## A fifth wrap-count data point, scaled by line weight in one demo (Logan, SearcherSportfishing, via Turner's Outdoorsman, Norwalk, `eehDVb6_GoI`, "Tackle Talk Live at Turner's Outdoorsman in Norwalk," 2021-06-30; `searcher-sportfishing` registered channel — medium confidence, single mention)

Demonstrated live on a heavier-diameter cord (for visibility, not the actual
line class being tied) as both the **"RP knot"** and (per Art, coaching from
beside him) the **John Collins knot** — the same dual naming already logged
above. A fifth distinct wrap-count figure for this knot family in this KB,
alongside the 9-up/9-down, 5-up/5-down, 6-up/6-down, and 7-turns/8-for-light
counts already logged:

- **Wrap count, scaled by line weight, his own stated practice:** for
  **lighter line**, **7 or 8 turns**; for **heavier line** (his example:
  **60–80 lb**), **3 or 4 turns** — explicitly framed as personal preference
  ("it's all personal preference... I mix it up trying to see what stitches
  down better"), not a fixed rule. The fewer-wraps-for-heavier-line
  relationship matches the general pattern already logged elsewhere in this
  note (the Vol. 96 clip's 3-wraps-for-thicker-demo-material step-down).
- **Symmetry emphasized as the thing that matters, not the exact count:**
  demonstrated tying the mono/fluoro side first (matching the wrap count to
  whatever was used on the braid side), then coming back through the loop
  "the same way you went in" — his stated caution: going through the loop
  the *opposite* way from how you entered causes the knot to fail when
  cinched down. This is a construction detail not narrated in the other
  citations above.
- **Line-class-linked construction note, from the same session (Art,
  coaching alongside):** on this build, the mono/fluoro top shot should run
  **at least 50 ft**, and its length should be **shorter than the rod** — a
  **7–8 ft rod wants roughly a 4 ft leader**, not an 8 ft one, because a
  leader as long as the rod puts the connection knot through the guides and
  hampers casting. A further, tuna/mono-top-shot-specific citation of the
  leader-length-to-rod-length rule already logged for kelp/rock calico bass
  in [line & leader](../tackle/line-and-leader.md#connections-parameters--steps-are-in-the-rigging-notes).

## A named "single San Diego" / RP variant — 7 turns (8 for light line), plus a double-pass finishing trick (Steve Carson, Penn, SearcherSportfishing, `QHY5kmU7OTU`, uploaded 2021-01-29, filmed 2021-01-28; `searcher-sportfishing` registered channel — medium confidence, single mention of this specific wrap count)

For the mono-to-braid connection specifically (distinct from the hook-end
single San Diego jam knot he ties on the same rig — see [San Diego jam
knot](san-diego-jam-knot.md)), Carson names his go-to tie the **"RP knot" or
"John Collins knot"** — the same dual naming already logged above. (The
"learned it in 1973 aboard the *Searcher*, taught by Kenny" story he tells on
camera in this same segment is about the hook-end single San Diego jam knot,
not this knot — not carried over here.) A further wrap-count data point, kept
side by side with, not reconciled to, the 9-up/9-down and 5-up/5-down counts
already logged above:

- **Wrap count — 7 turns, standard; 8 turns on lighter line** (his own
  example: 20 lb test). A fourth distinct wrap-count figure for this knot
  family in this KB, alongside the 9-up/9-down, 5-up/5-down, and 6-up/6-down
  ([RP knot](essential-knots.md#parameters--judgment)) counts already logged.
- **A finishing trick not named in the other citations above:** on the final
  pass, as the tag end comes through to finish the knot, wrap it around and
  pass it through **a second time** before cinching — his own stated
  "little extra tip," different from anything else he does compared to how
  "everybody else" ties it.
- **Lubrication and re-tie discipline, general to all knots on this rig, not
  specific to this one:** wet every knot heavily with spit before seating so
  it cinches down tight; if a knot doesn't seat down tight, cut it and re-tie
  rather than fish it as-is — the same standing risk of a bad knot costing
  "the biggest fish of the trip" is given as the reason never to let a
  marginal knot go out.
