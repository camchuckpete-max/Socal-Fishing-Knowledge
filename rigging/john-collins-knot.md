# John Collins Knot — Braid to Mono/Fluoro Splice

A named connection knot joining **braid to monofilament or fluorocarbon**,
demonstrated across five tutorial clips (`Bz0WZNAofks`, `FN9-rgyC9ic`,
`b-oixz7pgAo`, `QHY5kmU7OTU`, `eehDVb6_GoI`). It is a high-wrap, symmetrical
splice in the same family as [uni-to-uni](essential-knots.md#parameters) and
the [Tony Pena knot](tony-pena-knot.md) — and, per one source, the same tie
as the [RP knot](rp-knot.md#parameters--judgment) under a different name,
though the wrap counts sourced for each disagree. Kept as its own note
rather than folded into [Essential SoCal Knots](essential-knots.md), which is
already long, per the same reasoning already applied to [Springer
Knot](springer-knot.md) and [Tony Pena Knot](tony-pena-knot.md).

## When to use

- A braid-to-mono or braid-to-fluoro connection knot — the on-camera audio
  captions to "John Collins not braid mono or Floral [fluoro]," read here as
  naming braid, mono, and fluoro; no line class, species, or rig application
  is stated in this clip (`Bz0WZNAofks`).
- **Line-class threshold, with figures:** use the John Collins/RP knot on
  connections up to about **80 lb test** "by the book"; above 80 lb test,
  switch to a loop-to-loop connection with a **Sato crimp**, built onto a
  pre-made or self-tied [wind-on leader](wind-on-leader.md), instead. The
  same source ties the John Collins knot itself at **15 lb, 20 lb, and 60 lb
  test** (`b-oixz7pgAo`). The Sato crimp is named only — no build detail is
  given in this clip — so it isn't broken out as its own note; treat it as a
  named alternative to look up separately for connections above 80 lb.
- With big braid to big mono, the finished knot is a "nice clean barrel"
  (`Bz0WZNAofks`) — the same heavy-line territory as
  [uni-to-uni](essential-knots.md#parameters) and the [Tony Pena
  knot](tony-pena-knot.md), though no pound-test figure is given in this clip
  to compare directly.
- **Origin and a naming conflict, kept side by side, not reconciled:** one
  source credits the knot's origin to "John Collins," on camera described as
  "a very well-known knot expert and fisherman" who "invented this knot in
  the wheelhouse of the *Royal Polaris*," and uses "John Collins knot" and
  "RP knot" interchangeably for what it presents as one tie (`b-oixz7pgAo`) —
  the same interchangeable naming recurs in two further clips
  (`QHY5kmU7OTU`, `eehDVb6_GoI`). [RP Knot](rp-knot.md#parameters--judgment)
  sources a **different** construction under that name — a doubled fluoro
  loop with the braid passed up through it, a 6-up/6-down wrap — so the two
  notes stay mechanically distinct despite the shared origin story, a shared
  ~80 lb "by the book" threshold, and both independently citing a wood dowel
  for seating.
- Demoed joining PowerPro (a hollow-ace variant here, stated to work the same
  with regular PowerPro) to Seaguar Fluoro Premier fluorocarbon — described
  as thinner-diameter and softer than standard fluoro, and easy to tie
  (`b-oixz7pgAo`).

## Parameters

- **Setup — two sourced starting points:** feed the working line up through
  the bottom, leaving plenty of tag line, then pinch the end (`Bz0WZNAofks`);
  or form the loop in the monofilament first, leaving plenty of tag line,
  then wrap up and back down (`FN9-rgyC9ic`).
- **Wrap count — five sourced figures, kept side by side; the choice turns on
  line diameter and how low-profile the finished knot needs to be, not one
  correct count:**
  - **9 up, 9 down** — the most recently dated of the clips (2023-02-16);
    exit back **up through the loop** on the return pass rather than down
    through it, called out explicitly as the step that makes the knot work
    (`Bz0WZNAofks`). Per "prefer the latest," treat this as the current
    figure — mechanically similar in shape to the [Tony Pena
    knot](tony-pena-knot.md)'s 11-up/11-down passes, but with a different
    count and, unlike that knot, tied by hand rather than finished with a
    knot-puller tool.
  - **7 turns standard, 8 on lighter line** (example: 20 lb test); a
    finishing trick on the final pass — wrap the tag around and pass it
    through the loop a second time before cinching, a "little extra tip"
    different from how everybody else ties it (`QHY5kmU7OTU`).
  - **7-8 turns on light line, 3-4 turns on heavy line** (example: 60-80 lb)
    — stated as personal preference, not a fixed rule (`eehDVb6_GoI`).
  - **5 up, 5 down** — corroborated in two independent clips (`FN9-rgyC9ic`;
    `b-oixz7pgAo`, "it doesn't matter which side it comes in or out of"). The
    second explicitly recommends staying at 5 rather than 7-8 (which also
    work): 5 makes "a nice, nice thin, easy through your guides" knot, a
    low-profile choice, not a strength ceiling — this figure predates the
    9-up/9-down clip above and is preserved as a superseded data point rather
    than dropped.
  - **3 up, 3 down** — the same source as the first 5-up/5-down figure above,
    scaled down for a thicker demo cord "because of the size" of the
    material — the same fewer-wraps-for-heavier/larger-diameter-line
    relationship logged for other connection knots (e.g. the surgeon's-knot
    turn counts in [Essential SoCal Knots](essential-knots.md#parameters))
    (`FN9-rgyC9ic`).
- **Exit/direction mechanics — two details from different clips, not stated
  as contradicting each other:**
  - Exit back up through the loop, not down through it (`Bz0WZNAofks`), vs.
    wrap direction on the return pass making no difference — same or
    opposite rotational direction from the first wrap (`FN9-rgyC9ic`).
  - Tie the mono/fluoro side first, matching the wrap count used on the
    braid side, then come back through the loop the same way you went in —
    going through the opposite way is stated to cause the knot to fail when
    cinched (`eehDVb6_GoI`).
- **Cinching:**
  - Take the slack out carefully so the wraps come down on top of themselves
    and seat clean, forming the "nice clean barrel" result above
    (`Bz0WZNAofks`).
  - Cinch by pulling the spectra tag line; tied correctly, all the
    wraps/loops line up evenly before the final cinch (`FN9-rgyC9ic`).
  - Jerk the knot once after bringing it down, before the final cinch, to
    seat it a little better; pulling against something rigid (a wood dowel)
    seats it better than pulling by hand or wrapped around your sleeves
    (`b-oixz7pgAo`).
- **Trim:** cut the braid tag end and the mono/fluoro tag end close and
  separately, "so that it goes through your guides" (`Bz0WZNAofks`;
  corroborated `FN9-rgyC9ic`, `b-oixz7pgAo`, `QHY5kmU7OTU`).
- **Result stated:** fast, strong, and reliable (`FN9-rgyC9ic`).
- **Leader length scales with rod length (`eehDVb6_GoI`):** the mono/fluoro
  top shot should run **at least 50 ft** ⚠ Fact-check (single-source): this
  figure sits immediately beside the "shorter than the rod" guidance below
  and is not reconciled with it in the transcript — a 7-8 ft rod cannot take
  both a ≥50 ft leader and a ~4 ft one; only this clip states the 50 ft
  figure. It should also run shorter than the rod — a 7-8 ft
  rod wants roughly a 4 ft leader, not an 8 ft one, because a leader as long
  as the rod pulls the connection knot through the guides and hampers
  casting. This clip ties the demonstration on a heavier-diameter cord for
  visibility, not the actual line class being tied. The same
  leader-length-to-rod-length rule is logged for kelp/rock calico bass in
  [line & leader](../tackle/line-and-leader.md#connections-parameters--steps-are-in-the-rigging-notes)
  ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-internal): that note's kelp/boiling-rock rule
  is "roughly the length of the rod, so the leader knot stays outside the
  tip guide," the opposite of "shorter than the rod" stated here.
- **Judgment, general to all knots tied on this rig, not specific to this
  one:** wet every knot heavily before seating so it cinches down tight; if a
  knot doesn't seat down tight, cut it and re-tie rather than fish it as-is —
  a marginal knot risks the biggest fish of the trip (`QHY5kmU7OTU`, tied
  there specifically for the mono-to-braid connection, distinct from the
  hook-end [San Diego jam knot](san-diego-jam-knot.md) tied on the same rig).
- **What the transcripts don't resolve (asr-uncertain, all five sources):**
  the three dedicated tackle-tip clips (`Bz0WZNAofks`, `FN9-rgyC9ic`,
  `b-oixz7pgAo`) run under four minutes each with thin auto-generated
  captions and no narrated species or rig application; the two seminar
  clips (`QHY5kmU7OTU`, `eehDVb6_GoI`) run over an hour each and carry this
  knot as one segment within a longer auto-generated transcript. Across all
  five, the fine hand mechanics of forming the initial loop, feeding the
  braid through, and exiting through vs. around the loop are visual and not
  fully resolvable from audio alone. Watch the
  source videos below for the hand movements rather than relying on this
  paraphrase alone to re-tie it.
