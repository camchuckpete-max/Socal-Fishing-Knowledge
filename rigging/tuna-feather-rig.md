# Tuna Feather Rig

Rigging a tuna feather, a West Coast [trolling](../techniques/trolling.md)
staple. A skirt slides over a chrome or lead head; a barrel swivel joins the
leader to the hook and lets the hook swing and reset after a strike. Feathers
come in a range of sizes, shapes, and colors: a heavier jet head — drilled
with holes so it smokes and swims across varied sea conditions — down to
smaller, mini-marlin-lure-style heads about half the size, used when the fish
are on small bait (`CWVPLM6NheY`). It is a general-purpose lure for most
everything that swims in SoCal offshore waters (`CWVPLM6NheY`).

## When to use

- A general-purpose bait in the [trolling](../techniques/trolling.md) spread.
  See [tuna feathers and skirts](../lures/tuna-feathers-and-skirts.md) for the
  lure itself and where each size/color fits the spread.

## Parameters

- **Thread the skirt on first.** Flip the lure onto the leader before tying
  any knots — rigging the leader without doing this first forces a re-tie
  (`CWVPLM6NheY`).
- **Leader:** about 6 ft of leader material, 60-80 lb test. 40 lb was the
  older standard but chafes unnoticed under load and snaps off; 60-80 lb
  holds unless the fish are unusually leader-shy (`CWVPLM6NheY`). ⚠
  Fact-check (single-source): verified verbatim in the cited transcript but
  this note cites no other source anywhere, and the parallel restatement in
  [Tuna Feathers and Skirts](../lures/tuna-feathers-and-skirts.md) cites the
  same transcript rather than an independent one.
- **Connection knots:** a barrel swivel is tied to each end of the leader
  with whatever knot the angler is confident in — improved clinch, San Diego,
  or uni all hold (`CWVPLM6NheY`).
- **Hook:** about a 9/0 tuna hook. Match the hook to the swivel before tying
  so the hook point can pass through the swivel's round end, then feed the
  hook through the swivel eye and secure it to the swivel with a zip tie —
  a smaller tie is better so it doesn't affect the lure's action — and trim
  it (`CWVPLM6NheY`). ⚠ Fact-check (single-source): the ~9/0 hook size
  matches the transcript's own hedge ("I think this is about a size 9-0")
  but is uncorroborated — no other source in the note, or in [Tuna Feathers
  and Skirts](../lures/tuna-feathers-and-skirts.md), gives a hook size for
  this rig.
- **Swivel does double duty:** it lets the hook pivot freely on every axis,
  so a knocked or fouled hook swings itself back into position, and it
  spaces the hook off the head (`CWVPLM6NheY`).
- **Hook position:** slide the lure down the swivel until the hook's bend
  lines up with the skirt's end; riding up to an inch inside the skirt is
  fine, but keep it toward the back. Too far forward and the skirt fouls the
  hook points; kept back in the lighter skirt material, the point comes
  through cleanly and finds the jaw (`CWVPLM6NheY`).
