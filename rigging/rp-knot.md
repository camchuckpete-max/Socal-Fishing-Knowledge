# RP Knot — Braid-to-Fluoro/Mono Connection (Royal Polaris)

A doubled-loop braid-to-leader connection knot — also named the **Royal
Polaris knot**, the **"R-P" knot**, and the **"Roy Rose" knot** across sources
in this KB — spun out of [Essential SoCal Knots](essential-knots.md) (batch-3,
2026-08-21) once its own sourced material (three corroborating clips plus a
dedicated step-by-step tying demonstration) outgrew the shared note's
parameters section.

## When to use it

- The preferred braid-to-fluoro/mono leader join in this KB's SoCal source
  corpus — small enough profile to pass the guides. Use it to build a
  leader onto the braid main line.
- **Not the same knot as the [PR Knot](pr-knot.md)** despite the
  near-identical name: PR is tied with a weighted bobbin tool rather than by
  hand tension. Kept as a separate note so the names aren't conflated.
- **Not the same knot as the [Slim Beauty Knot](slim-beauty-knot.md)**, which
  covers the same low-profile guide-passing territory by a different
  construction (a uni knot on the topshot side plus a multi-wrap pass from
  the spectra tag).
- **Naming conflict, not reconciled, with the [John Collins
  Knot](john-collins-knot.md):** the sources behind this note and the ones
  behind John Collins are kept as separate ties (different wrap counts —
  6-up/6-down here vs. 9-up/9-down there), but a SearcherSportfishing
  presenter (Pete Gray, Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 150, `b-oixz7pgAo`,
  2021-06-10) states on camera that "RP knot" and "John Collins knot" are the
  same tie, both named for its wheelhouse-of-the-*Royal-Polaris* origin —
  consistent with this note's own ~80 lb "by the book" figure matching Vol.
  150's stated 80 lb threshold, and with both entries independently citing a
  wood dowel for seating/cinching. Kept side by side rather than merged,
  since the wrap counts as sourced still disagree.

## Parameters & judgment

- **Base mechanic:** make a doubled loop in the fluoro/mono leader, pass the
  braid up through it, wrap the braid **6 times up and 6 times back down**,
  and exit the loop the same way you entered. Lubricate, draw slow, then a
  quick snap to seat. Commonly tied to ~80 lb by the book, but it holds well
  beyond — tied clean to 100 lb and even ~130 lb. Wrap count is personal
  preference — some tiers go 8 or 10 wraps instead of 6 (YGLFn8lPMu0).
- **Wrap count scales with the line-diameter ratio, not a fixed number
  (Chad, Tackle Express, `p1KyyR0i7Kc`, 2018-09-08; unregistered channel —
  medium confidence).** Framed as an update to an earlier Tackle Express
  video on the same knot ("we learned a couple things since then"). Stated
  rule of thumb: **the lighter the line, the more wraps; the heavier the
  line, the fewer wraps** — five sourced data points:
  - 65 lb braid (4-carrier) to 25 lb monofilament topshot → **7 wraps** each
    direction (demonstrated on camera).
  - 100 lb braid to 80 lb topshot → **5 wraps** — stated as the floor
    ("the least amount you're gonna go is five wraps").
  - 100 lb braid to 50 lb topshot → **6 wraps**. (The same passage also
    gestures at a 100 lb-to-65 lb data point, but the wrap count for it is
    lost to a garbled transcript segment — not carried here rather than
    guessed.)
  - 20 lb braid to 6 lb topshot (his example: bass or trout fishing) →
    **9 wraps**.
  - Same 20 lb-to-6 lb ratio, but on a **slicker line** (an 8-carrier braid
    vs. the 4-carrier used in the demo) → add one wrap over the normal count,
    i.e. **10, "maybe even 11"** — his read is 10 is usually enough.
  - **Tell:** if the knot slips on you while tightening, you didn't do enough
    wraps — add one more next time.
  - Wrap direction: go around the mono/fluoro clockwise (or counterclockwise
    — "whatever way you like"), pinch the last wrap, then reposition and wrap
    back the *same* rotational direction to return. **Tell that both wraps
    went the same way:** push the two strands together — if tied correctly
    they slide up and down through the hole together; if one was wrapped the
    wrong way, one strand moves one direction and the other moves the
    opposite way when pushed.
  - **Critical pinch step:** pinch hard on both the braid (tag + main
    together) and both mono/leader strands at the exit point — this is what
    "sucks" the loop in tight against the rest of the wraps before the final
    cinch; you can watch the loop draw in as you pinch.
- For extra security before cinching, pass the braid tag back through the
  exit loop one more time first.
- **Lubrication (Chad, `p1KyyR0i7Kc`):** chapstick over spit —
  makes a smaller, tighter-seating knot. Rub *with* the grain of the wraps,
  not across them; moving the loops back and forth against the grain creates
  friction that can warp the mono or fluorocarbon. Work the lubricant in
  between each individual strand of the braid with a rolling finger motion so
  there's no dry contact left to cause friction on the pull.
  Wet the knot (plain water is also fine per the base-mechanic citation
  above), then massage/work the coils down snug before the final hard cinch
  — watch for the braid to darken and the fluoro to go translucent as the
  tell that it's seated.
- **Cinching technique (Chad, `p1KyyR0i7Kc`):** hold the two mono/leader
  strands together in one hand and only the single braided line in the
  other; draw the loop in a little first, then pull **slow, not fast** —
  pulling fast risks warping/burning the line. Pull lighter on lighter line.
  Gloves with a sticky/tacky palm grip help maintain hold without the line
  slipping through your fingers, especially on heavier tests where you're
  pulling harder. As you pull, the knot will visibly move/slide a little —
  that's expected; once it stops moving on its own, add a bit more pressure
  to fully set it, finishing with one long, hard, steady pull to make sure
  it's cinched tight. (On his 65 lb-to-25 lb demo pair he doesn't need to
  pull especially hard to seat it.) A line-gripping puller (wood dowel +
  tape) rather than bare hands on heavier tests, to avoid line cuts, is
  noted for this knot family generally per the base-mechanic citation above
  — not stated by Chad in this clip, which only demonstrates sticky-palm
  gloves.
- **Presenter's own track record (Chad, `p1KyyR0i7Kc`):** states he has
  tested this knot "dozens and dozens of times" and it has never broken at
  the knot itself — failures happen on the braid instead, which he attributes
  to braid having no stretch combined with the short length of line between
  the knot and the rest of the rig.
- **Trimming (Chad, `p1KyyR0i7Kc`):** on the braid side, a small leftover tag
  doesn't matter and cuts easily with sharp cutters. On the mono/fluoro
  (leader) side, pull the leader's main line back against the knot to expose
  the tag fully, then cut it flush/angled right against the knot so
  virtually nothing is left — his stated reason: a leftover tag as small as
  1/16 in can cut/take a chunk out of your thumb on a hard cast, and if it's
  outside the reel it can catch the guides and reduce casting distance.
- Optionally finish by tying the fluoro tag off around the main braid with a
  3-wrap uni knot, which cones the connection for a smoother pass through the
  guides — skippable when re-tying in a hot bite (YGLFn8lPMu0).
- **Observed** (Dirty Hookers, date unstated — recalled from an earlier,
  unlinked video): presenter recounts landing an estimated triple-digit
  (100 lb+) bluefin on 65 lb braid to 80 lb fluoro tied with this knot,
  locked drag, no knot failure (YGLFn8lPMu0).
- **Full-name + tag-length corroboration (StokedOnFishing, `AT6zmDYxjW4`,
  2020-05-06; unregistered channel — medium confidence):** a separate, short
  StokedOnFishing clip (presenter John, at a tackle shop) names this knot in
  full as the **"Royal Polaris knot"** — also called the "R-P knot" and the
  **"Roy Rose knot."** Mechanic matches the entry above: form a loop in the
  fluoro/mono, bring the braid up through it with about **12 in** of tag,
  wrap the braid **6 times** down the standing fluoro, then wrap back **6
  times** toward the loop and exit the same direction it entered — a second,
  independent corroboration of the 6-and-6 wrap count and same-direction
  exit already logged above. Also corroborates the extra-security pass: at
  the critical exit point he passes the tag through **twice** ("I go once
  and then I go another time to give it a lock") before cinching. To seat:
  pull the tag end and the fluoro/mono main line together tight first, then
  release the tag and pull the two main lines to finish the cinch. No
  SoCal-specific parameters (line class, species) given in this clip.
