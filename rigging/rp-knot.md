# RP Knot — Braid-to-Fluoro/Mono Connection (Royal Polaris)

A doubled-loop braid-to-leader connection knot — also named the **Royal
Polaris knot**, the **R-P knot**, and the **Roy Rose knot** across sources
in this KB — spun out of [Essential SoCal Knots](essential-knots.md) once its
own sourced material (three corroborating clips plus a dedicated step-by-step
tying demonstration) outgrew the shared note's parameters section.

## When to use

- The preferred braid-to-fluoro/mono leader join in this KB's SoCal source
  corpus — small enough profile to pass the guides. Use it to build a
  leader onto the braid main line.
- **Not the same knot as the [PR Knot](pr-knot.md)** despite the
  near-identical name: PR is tied with a weighted bobbin tool rather than by
  hand tension. Kept as a separate note so the names aren't conflated.
- **Not the same knot as the [Slim Beauty Knot](slim-beauty-knot.md)**, which
  covers the same low-profile guide-passing territory by a different
  construction (a uni knot on the topshot side plus a multi-wrap pass from
  the spectra tag).
- **Naming conflict, not reconciled, with the [John Collins
  Knot](john-collins-knot.md):** the sources behind this note and the ones
  behind John Collins are kept as separate ties — different wrap counts,
  6-up/6-down here vs. 9-up/9-down there — but one source states on camera
  that "RP knot" and "John Collins knot" are the same tie, both named for the
  wheelhouse of the *Royal Polaris* (`b-oixz7pgAo`). That source's stated
  80 lb threshold matches this note's own ~80 lb "by the book" figure, and
  both entries independently cite a wood dowel for seating/cinching. Kept
  side by side rather than merged, since the wrap counts as sourced still
  disagree — **tie by wrap count, not by name**: follow this note's
  6-up/6-down or John Collins' 9-up/9-down, either holds to the shared
  ~80 lb ceiling, so the naming question doesn't change which count to use.

## Parameters & judgment

- **Base mechanic:** make a doubled loop in the fluoro/mono leader, pass the
  braid up through it, wrap the braid **6 times up and 6 times back down**,
  and exit the loop the same way you entered. Lubricate, draw slow, then a
  quick snap to seat. Commonly tied to ~80 lb by the book, but it holds well
  beyond — tied clean to 100 lb and even ~130 lb. ⚠ Fact-check
  (contradicted-by-source): `YGLFn8lPMu0` demonstrates only an 80 lb-to-80 lb
  tie and separately references a 65 lb braid-to-80 lb fluoro connection that
  landed an estimated 100+ lb bluefin; the transcript never states the knot
  itself ties clean to 100 lb or ~130 lb line. Wrap count is personal
  preference — some tie 8 or 10 wraps instead of 6 (`YGLFn8lPMu0`).
- **Wrap count scales with the line-diameter ratio, not a fixed number: the
  lighter the line, the more wraps; the heavier the line, the fewer wraps.**
  This rule updates earlier fixed-wrap-count guidance from the same source;
  treat it as the current figure (`p1KyyR0i7Kc`). ⚠ Fact-check
  (single-source): the ratio rule and all five data points below rest on this
  one video; no other cited source in this note gives a wrap-count-by-line-
  diameter table (the flat 6-up/6-down base count is independently
  corroborated elsewhere in this note, but the scaling table itself is not).
  Five sourced data points:
  - 65 lb braid (4-carrier) to 25 lb monofilament topshot → **7 wraps** each
    direction (demonstrated on camera).
  - 100 lb braid to 80 lb topshot → **5 wraps** — stated as the floor
    ("the least amount you're gonna go is five wraps").
  - 100 lb braid to 50 lb topshot → **6 wraps**. (The same passage also
    gestures at a 100 lb-to-65 lb data point, but the wrap count for it is
    lost to a garbled transcript segment — not carried here rather than
    guessed.)
  - 20 lb braid to 6 lb topshot (bass or trout fishing) → **9 wraps**.
  - Same 20 lb-to-6 lb ratio, but on a **slicker line** (an 8-carrier braid
    vs. the 4-carrier used in the demo) → add one wrap over the normal count,
    i.e. **10, "maybe even 11"** — 10 is usually enough.
  - **Tell:** if the knot slips while tightening, that's too few wraps —
    add one more next time.
  - Wrap direction: go around the mono/fluoro clockwise (or counterclockwise
    — either works), pinch the last wrap, then reposition and wrap back the
    *same* rotational direction to return. **Tell that both wraps went the
    same way:** push the two strands together — if tied correctly they slide
    up and down through the hole together; if one was wrapped the wrong way,
    one strand moves one direction and the other moves the opposite way when
    pushed.
  - **Critical pinch step:** pinch hard on both the braid (tag + main
    together) and both mono/leader strands at the exit point — this is what
    "sucks" the loop in tight against the rest of the wraps before the final
    cinch; the loop visibly draws in as you pinch.
- For extra security before cinching, pass the braid tag back through the
  exit loop one more time first.
- **Lubrication (`p1KyyR0i7Kc`):** chapstick over spit —
  makes a smaller, tighter-seating knot. Rub *with* the grain of the wraps,
  not across them; moving the loops back and forth against the grain creates
  friction that can warp the mono or fluorocarbon. Work the lubricant in
  between each individual strand of the braid with a rolling finger motion so
  there's no dry contact left to cause friction on the pull.
  Wet the knot — plain water also works (`YGLFn8lPMu0`) — then massage/work
  the coils down snug before the final hard cinch — watch for the braid to
  darken and the fluoro to go translucent as the tell that it's seated.
- **Cinching technique (`p1KyyR0i7Kc`):** hold the two mono/leader
  strands together in one hand and only the single braided line in the
  other; draw the loop in a little first, then pull **slow, not fast** —
  pulling fast risks warping/burning the line. Pull lighter on lighter line.
  Gloves with a sticky/tacky palm grip help maintain hold without the line
  slipping through your fingers, especially on heavier tests where you're
  pulling harder. As you pull, the knot will visibly move/slide a little —
  that's expected; once it stops moving on its own, add a bit more pressure
  to fully set it, finishing with one long, hard, steady pull to make sure
  it's cinched tight. On a 65 lb-to-25 lb demo pair, it doesn't need to be
  pulled especially hard to seat. A line-gripping puller (wood dowel + tape)
  rather than bare hands on heavier tests, to avoid line cuts, is noted for
  this knot family generally — not shown in this clip, which demonstrates
  sticky-palm gloves only.
- **Track record (`p1KyyR0i7Kc`):** tested "dozens and dozens of times" with
  no knot failure at the tie itself — failures happen on the braid instead,
  attributed to braid's lack of stretch combined with the short length of
  line between the knot and the rest of the rig. A hookup on 65 lb braid to
  80 lb fluoro tied with this knot landed an estimated 100+ lb bluefin under
  locked drag with no knot failure, corroborating the strength claim
  (`YGLFn8lPMu0`).
- **Trimming (`p1KyyR0i7Kc`):** on the braid side, a small leftover tag
  doesn't matter and cuts easily with sharp cutters. On the mono/fluoro
  (leader) side, pull the leader's main line back against the knot to expose
  the tag fully, then cut it flush/angled right against the knot so
  virtually nothing is left — a leftover tag as small as 1/16 in can
  cut/take a chunk out of your thumb on a hard cast, and if it's outside the
  reel it can catch the guides and reduce casting distance.
- Optionally finish by tying the fluoro tag off around the main braid with a
  3-wrap uni knot, which cones the connection for a smoother pass through the
  guides — skippable when re-tying in a hot bite (`YGLFn8lPMu0`).
- **Full-name and tag-length corroboration:** a separate clip names this knot
  in full as the **"Royal Polaris knot"** — also called the "R-P knot" and
  the **"Roy Rose knot."** The mechanic matches the entry above: form a loop
  in the fluoro/mono, bring the braid up through it with about **12 in** of
  tag, wrap the braid **6 times** down the standing fluoro, then wrap back
  **6 times** toward the loop and exit the same direction it entered — a
  second, independent corroboration of the 6-and-6 wrap count and
  same-direction exit already logged above. It also corroborates the
  extra-security pass: at the critical exit point, pass the tag through
  **twice** ("I go once and then I go another time to give it a lock")
  before cinching. To seat, pull the tag end and the fluoro/mono main line
  together tight first, then release the tag and pull the two main lines to
  finish the cinch (`AT6zmDYxjW4`). No SoCal-specific parameters (line
  class, species) are given in this clip.
