# Perfection Loop Knot — Sliding-Loop Hook Tie

An "old school" terminal knot that ties a **free-sliding loop** rather than a
fixed eye — the hook rides on the loop and can pivot/slide back and forth on
it, "kind of like a ring." Demonstrated by Captain Art Taylor of the
*Searcher* for a Tackle Tip Thursday (transcript renders the name "captain
arc taylor" — read as **Captain Art Taylor** per the same ASR-to-name
resolution already logged for this presenter in [Improved Clinch
Knot](improved-clinch-knot.md#parameters--judgment), where "captainard" /
"Captain Art" self-introduction phrasing was cross-checked against other
SearcherSportfishing clips; `asr-uncertain` on the exact surname spelling).
SearcherSportfishing is a registered channel/series source
(`sources/source-registry.md`), but this is the only corpus mention of this
specific knot, so it rates `medium` rather than `high`. Kept as its own note
rather than folded into the loop-knot entry in [Essential SoCal
Knots](essential-knots.md) — that note is already at the ~400-line split
threshold, and this is a mechanically distinct loop-forming knot with its own
name.

## When to use it

- Framed as a knot **"brought back"** from the 1980s, when it was used
  specifically for **tying on trolling hooks and trolling lures**. The
  presenter states it "can be used for any application" and demonstrates it
  here for **live bait**.
- **Live-bait rationale, stated on camera:** because the finished loop lets
  the hook slide/pivot rather than sitting fixed, pinning a live sardine on
  it "maybe... swims a little bit better" than a fixed-eye tie — a stated
  reason, not just an observation.
- Demonstrated on **200 lb spectra** (chosen "for demonstration purposes," to
  be visible on camera) with a **4/0 J hook**; the presenter states it can
  also be tied on **20 lb** line.
- Stated properties: "a very strong knot."
- **Adjustable loop size:** making the first loop smaller shrinks the
  finished ring (and vice versa), so the loop can be sized to the hook eye
  and how freely you want it to swing.
- **Same knot, a different application already in this KB:** the perfection
  loop is also used as the loop-to-loop connection joining a hoop net's main
  rope to its spare "rope shot" extension section — see [hoop
  netting](../techniques/hoop-netting.md#rope-length-match-it-dont-over-carry-it-qs9oesh3b_w).
  That use is rope-to-rope, not a hook tie; kept separate rather than merged,
  since the material and job differ, but the same named knot underlies both.

## Parameters & judgment

- **Paraphrased mechanic (thin auto-generated captions on a 168-second clip —
  `asr-uncertain` on the precise loop count and hand mechanics; watch the
  source video for the visual steps rather than relying on this paraphrase
  alone to re-tie it):** start with about **8–10 in** of tag line. Form a
  first overhand-style loop. Pass the tag line through the hook eye, then
  bring the hook down close to the tying hand. Form a second loop and grasp
  both loops between the fingers. Bring the hook over the top of a third loop
  and back through the first loop. Cinch down — done correctly, the two lines
  running through the loop sit **parallel** — and the knot draws down into a
  clean finished loop/ring in the line.
- **Sizing judgment:** the first loop sets the finished ring size — make it
  smaller for a tighter, more fixed-feeling loop, larger for more hook play.
- No species, region, or specific rig/application beyond live bait and
  trolling is stated in this clip.
