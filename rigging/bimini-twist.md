# Bimini Twist — 100% Double-Line Loop

A **double-line/loop** knot, not a hook tie or a line-to-line connection like
the rest of the [essential SoCal knots](essential-knots.md) family — it ties a
doubled section of line into a stable twisted loop. Used to build the reel-end
loop for a [wind-on leader](wind-on-leader.md) and as the standing double line
ahead of a topshot on rail-rod big-tuna setups (line-class ladder in
[rod & reel selection](../tackle/rod-and-reel-selection.md)). Its selling
point, stated on camera by Billy at Fisherman's Landing Tackle Shop, is that it
holds close to **100% of the line's breaking strength "because technically
it's not a knot"** (`nM7B5NQLy44`, 2020-12-24) — the load is carried on two
parallel strands rather than through a knot's internal friction/cinch losses.
Once tied, a monofilament topshot connects to the finished loop with a [worm
knot](essential-knots.md#parameters--judgment) for fast field replacement
after a break-off.

## When to use it

- Building the **reel-end loop** for a [wind-on leader](wind-on-leader.md) —
  see that note's "braid loop" parameter, sized to pass a wrist through for
  tying a bimini/cat's-paw to the running braid.
- As the **standing double line** ahead of a topshot on rail-rod big-tuna
  setups, where a near-full-strength doubled section takes the brunt of a
  long fight before the lighter topshot/leader.
- Two independently sourced tackle-shop demonstrations are logged here — a
  fast ~20–25-wrap quick-tie and a fuller ~30–50-wrap traditional tie with an
  added locking-wrap finish — kept side by side as two different turn-count
  philosophies for the same knot, not reconciled to one number.

## Parameters & judgment

- **Quick-tie approach (StokedOnFishing, `jahddqzKhLY`, 2012-03-28;
  unregistered channel — `medium` confidence):** presented as a fast,
  no-long-loop way to twist up a double line in about 30 seconds once
  practiced. Cinch a short starting loop (no need for the traditional big
  loop), then spin roughly **20–25 wraps** down the twisted section — the
  presenter's own on-camera count is uneven and restarts partway through, so
  read this as an approximate range rather than an exact figure. Set tension
  by hooking the loop over the reel handle and turning it, then run the
  wraps up to the size you want the finished loop to be and hold the tag
  end. Lock it off with a half hitch around the first turn (cinched), then a
  half hitch around the second turn (cinched), then a final locking wrap
  ("go one, two, three" per the transcript). **Flag:** the exact wrap count
  on that final locking pass is ASR-uncertain — the two-half-hitch-then-lock
  mechanic is clear, the count on the last pass is not. No SoCal-specific
  parameters (line class, species, or region) are given in this clip.
- **Traditional/full approach (SearcherSportfishing, Billy at Fisherman's
  Landing Tackle Shop, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 131," `nM7B5NQLy44`,
  2020-12-24; `searcher-sportfishing` registered channel, single mention —
  `medium` confidence):** framed as good "for connecting wind-ons with solid
  spectra and a bunch of other stuff."
  - **Starting loop:** take roughly an **arm's span** (he estimates "two and
    a half feet or whatever") of line and double it up — a starting point
    only, adjustable to however big a loop is wanted.
  - **Turn count:** put in **30 to 50 turns, "depending on who you ask"** —
    he ties 30 on camera to keep the demo short, framing 30–50 as a range of
    personal/tackle-shop preference rather than a fixed rule. This
    30–50-turn range sits well above the ~20–25-wrap quick-tie figure logged
    above from the same knot family — kept side by side as two different
    turn-count philosophies, not reconciled to one number.
  - **Setting the twist:** hook the loop end onto a fixed anchor point (he
    says it doesn't matter what). Take the tag end, hold it under tension
    (mouth or hand), then slide a finger up the two twisted strands so it
    unravels the twist above the finger while the twist re-forms below it,
    walking the twisted barrel section down toward the loop until it's fully
    set.
  - **Locking the twist — double half hitch:** half hitch the tag around one
    leg of the twist and cinch it down; the twist is now sealed on its own
    at this point ("your bimini is sealed right now"). Repeat the same half
    hitch on the other leg.
  - **Extra security wraps (his own preference, stated as going beyond what
    "some guys" do — "some guys I know will just fish it like that"):** wrap
    the tag around **both legs together, three times** ("one, two, three")
    for a more sealed finish than the double half hitch alone provides.
  - **Permanent finish — uni/nail-knot-style tie:** trim the tag shorter,
    then tie what he calls "basically like a uni knot or a nail knot" with
    **5 turns**. To make it lay clean: lay the tag across the bimini and the
    standing line, then wrap it back the **opposite direction** from how it
    was unwrapped for another **5 turns**, then pull the whole thing tight
    slowly ("nice and slow to keep that loop from going over itself"). Trim
    the tag close.
  - **Before use:** unravel/spin the very top of the finished loop a couple
    of times so the twist lays even and even before tying a loop-to-loop
    connection or moving on to whatever connection the loop feeds.
  - **Stated property:** "it's a hundred percent of the line's breaking
    strength because technically it's not a knot... you can [put] as hard a
    [pull] as you can with it" — an on-camera claim of the knot's general
    reputation, not a lab-tested figure. No line class, species, or region
    is stated in this clip.
