# Bimini Twist — 100% Double-Line Loop

A **double-line/loop** knot, not a hook tie or a line-to-line connection like
the rest of the [essential SoCal knots](essential-knots.md) family — it ties a
doubled section of line into a stable twisted loop. It builds the reel-end
loop for a [wind-on leader](wind-on-leader.md) and serves as the standing
double line ahead of a topshot on rail-rod big-tuna setups (line-class ladder
in [rod & reel selection](../tackle/rod-and-reel-selection.md)). The knot
holds close to **100% of the line's breaking strength "because technically
it's not a knot"** (`nM7B5NQLy44`) — the load runs on two parallel strands
instead of through a knot's internal friction/cinch losses. Once tied, a
monofilament topshot connects to the finished loop with a [worm
knot](essential-knots.md#parameters--judgment) for fast field replacement
after a break-off.

**Spun out of** [Essential SoCal Knots](essential-knots.md) (batch-3 split, no
content dropped): that note was already at the ~400+ line size signal, and a
second, independently sourced tying method was a better fit for its own note
than a small addendum.

## When to use

- Building the **reel-end loop** for a [wind-on leader](wind-on-leader.md) —
  see that note's "braid loop" parameter, sized to pass a wrist through for
  tying a bimini/cat's-paw to the running braid.
- As the **standing double line** ahead of a topshot on rail-rod big-tuna
  setups, where a near-full-strength doubled section takes the brunt of a
  long fight before the lighter topshot/leader.

## Parameters

Two demonstrations, independently sourced, differ on turn count: a faster
~20–25-wrap quick-tie and a fuller ~30–50-wrap traditional tie with an added
locking-wrap finish. Neither source states a SoCal-specific line class,
species, or region (`jahddqzKhLY`, `nM7B5NQLy44`).

- **Quick-tie** (`jahddqzKhLY`): cinch a short starting loop — no need for
  the traditional big loop — then spin roughly **20–25 wraps** down the
  twisted section (asr-uncertain — the on-camera count is uneven and
  restarts partway through, so read this as an approximate range rather
  than an exact figure). Set tension by hooking the loop over the reel
  handle and turning it, then run the wraps up to the size wanted for the
  finished loop and hold the tag end. Lock it off with a half hitch around
  the first turn (cinched), then a half hitch around the second turn
  (cinched), then a final locking wrap (asr-uncertain(final wrap count) —
  the two-half-hitch-then-lock mechanic is clear, the count on the last
  pass is not).
- **Traditional/full tie** (`nM7B5NQLy44`): built for "connecting wind-ons
  with solid spectra and a bunch of other stuff" (`nM7B5NQLy44`).
  - **Starting loop:** double up roughly an **arm's span** (~2.5 ft) of
    line — a starting point only, adjustable to whatever size loop is
    wanted.
  - **Turn count:** put in **30 to 50 turns**, "depending on who you ask"
    (`nM7B5NQLy44`) — 30 turns tied on camera to keep the demo short, with
    30–50 framed as a range of personal/tackle-shop preference rather than
    a fixed rule. This sits well above the ~20–25-wrap quick-tie figure
    above — two different turn-count conventions for the same knot, not
    one reconciled number.
  - **Setting the twist:** hook the loop end onto a fixed anchor point (any
    fixed point works). Hold the tag end under tension (mouth or hand),
    then slide a finger up the two twisted strands so it unravels the
    twist above the finger while the twist re-forms below it, walking the
    twisted barrel section down toward the loop until it's fully set.
  - **Locking the twist — double half hitch:** half hitch the tag around
    one leg of the twist and cinch it down — the twist is sealed on its
    own at this point. Repeat the same half hitch on the other leg.
  - **Extra security wraps** (a step beyond the double half hitch, which
    some anglers fish as-is): wrap the tag around **both legs together,
    three times** for a more sealed finish.
  - **Permanent finish — uni/nail-knot-style tie:** trim the tag shorter,
    then tie a uni/nail-knot-style finish with **5 turns**. To lay it
    clean, lay the tag across the bimini and the standing line, wrap it
    back the **opposite direction** from how it was unwrapped for another
    **5 turns**, then pull the whole thing tight slowly to keep the loop
    from going over itself. Trim the tag close.
  - **Before use:** unravel/spin the very top of the finished loop a
    couple of times so the twist lays even before tying a loop-to-loop
    connection or moving on to the next connection the loop feeds.
  - **Stated property:** "it's a hundred percent of the line's breaking
    strength because technically it's not a knot... you can [put] as hard
    a [pull] as you can with it" (`nM7B5NQLy44`) — an on-camera claim of
    the knot's general reputation, not a lab-tested figure.
