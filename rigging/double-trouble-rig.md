# Double Trouble Rig

A live-bait alternative to flying a kite bait: two live baits on separate
crimped hook droppers, sharing one swivel, ride together at the surface under
the kite instead of one flyer (`UrEymGvZx00`). It targets
[bluefin tuna](../species/bluefin-tuna.md) primarily, but a Cortez Bank
session also took [yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md) on it
(`nQvJnfb5jQ4`). The build's central requirement is keeping the two droppers
parallel and even so neither bait rides higher than the other.

## When to use

- A live-bait alternative to flying a kite bait for
  [bluefin tuna](../species/bluefin-tuna.md), run off two rods under
  conditions that support kite fishing (`UrEymGvZx00`).
- Also takes yellowtail: a Cortez Bank session ran the double trouble under
  the kite with sardines hooked in the back and landed a yellowtail on it
  rather than the bluefin the rig is usually aimed at; the crew reported
  yellowtail "buy double trouble all the time" (`nQvJnfb5jQ4`). See
  [yellowtail](../species/yellowtail.md) for the trip context.

## Parameters

- **Line class by grade of fish:** 100 lb line for fish in the 20-40 lb
  range; 150 lb or 200 lb line for bigger fish. Built on 150 lb test (Seaguar
  Big Game fluorocarbon) (`UrEymGvZx00`). A Cortez Bank session where 100 lb
  line was run alongside 30 lb called the 100 lb easier for hooking the
  bigger fish being seen — consistent with, not new beyond, this grade-based
  table (`nQvJnfb5jQ4`).
- **Hook size by line class:** 100-150 lb class line pairs with size 4/0
  hooks (varies by brand); 200 lb test line steps up to a bigger hook. Built
  with 4/0 circle hooks (`UrEymGvZx00`).
- **Build:** a little more than an arm's length of leader, crimped
  throughout, crimp sizes matched to line diameter. One crimped loop holds a
  heavy-duty swivel rated on the order of 200/300 lb, with a small tag-end
  loop left so the swivel keeps movement. The two hook droppers are crimped
  on, each with a burned tag end flattened to act as a stopper against its
  crimp (`UrEymGvZx00`).
- **Critical adjustment:** bring the two hooks as parallel and even as
  possible so the baits are not offset. If one dropper hangs a few inches
  higher, one bait fishes at the surface and the other does not; the goal is
  both baits together right at the surface (`UrEymGvZx00`).
- **Hooking the baits:** match bait size to the grade of fish. Collar-hook or
  shoulder-hook the baits rather than nose-hooking as on a flyline bait, so
  they plane out flat going out instead of tilting nose-up (`UrEymGvZx00`).

## Named variant — "double treble" (Guadalupe Island yellowfin)

A SearcherSportfishing tackle segment names a build called "double treble"
rather than "double trouble" — plausibly the same rig under a different
name, not confirmed as identical hardware to the build above
(`fgTmUq78ofQ`). As described: 200 lb mono, two droppers each ending in a
3/0 or 4/0 circle hook — in range with the 4/0-hook, 100-200 lb line-class
table above — bait shoulder-hooked on each hook (matching the
collar/shoulder-hook-not-nose-hook doctrine above), so the two baits "dance
around the surface." Named on camera as one of the crew's favorite ways to
catch yellowfin at [Guadalupe Island](../locations/guadalupe.md), Baja
(`fgTmUq78ofQ`) — a species/location pairing not otherwise documented in
this note, which elsewhere covers this rig against bluefin (its usual
target) and one observed yellowtail catch at Cortez Bank.
