# Springer Knot — Heavy-Line Hook Tie

A named terminal knot for tying a hook onto **heavier line**, demonstrated by
Billy at Fisherman's Landing Tackle Shop for the *Searcher*'s Tackle Tip
Thursday series. Kept as its own note rather than folded into [Essential SoCal
Knots](essential-knots.md) — that note is already long, and this is a single
thin clip with a mechanic distinct enough from the Palomar/San Diego jam

## When to use it

- Billy frames it specifically as **"good for tying in heavier line"** — no
  line-class number, species, or rig application is stated in this clip.
- Stated properties: a "really, really strong knot" that's "really easy and
  fast to tie."

## Parameters & judgment

- **Bench setup:** anchor the hook to a fixed point ("hook it onto something,
  whatever that may be") before starting — a tackle-bench tying aid to hold
  tension with both hands free, not part of the knot's own mechanic.
- **Paraphrased mechanic, as far as the audio alone resolves:** take the
  running line and make two wrapping passes down toward the hook, taking care
  the two passes don't cross each other. On what would be a third such pass,
  instead wrap the tag around the **top two strands two times** ("one, two").
  Then cinch it down — the transcript is explicit that, unlike the two
  wrapping passes above, you are **not** trying to keep the line from
  crossing itself at this step ("without trying to prevent the line from
  crossing itself, you're just going to cinch it down") — then pull the
  connection fully tight and trim the tag end.
- **What the transcript doesn't resolve:** this is a 119-second clip with thin
  auto-generated captions and no narrated detail on whether the line passes
  through the hook eye, how many total wraps precede the "top two strands"
  step, or the exact geometry of the wraps — the demo is visual and the words
  alone don't pin it down. Flagged `asr-uncertain`; watch the source video
  (linked below) for the hand mechanics rather than relying on this
  paraphrase to re-tie it correctly.
