# Springer Knot — Heavy-Line Hook Tie

A named terminal knot for tying a hook onto **heavier line**, demonstrated in
two clips of the *Searcher*'s Tackle Tip Thursday series (`Bo5Fj-XuWHo`,
`VW2t_G8eorI`) — the same knot recurring across two numbered episodes of the
same series is repeated doctrine. ⚠ Fact-check (contradicted-internal):
[Essential SoCal Knots](essential-knots.md) describes this note's source
material as "a single thin tackle-shop clip," not two. Kept as its own note rather than folded into
[Essential SoCal Knots](essential-knots.md) — that note is already long, and
this is a mechanic distinct enough from the Palomar/San Diego jam family there
that it shouldn't be merged into either.

## When to use

- Ties a hook onto heavier line; no line-class number, species, or rig
  application is stated in this clip (`Bo5Fj-XuWHo`).
- Ties for "virtually any pound test," named down to 20 lb and 30 lb, with a
  stated preference for 60 lb and up (`VW2t_G8eorI`). ⚠ Fact-check
  (single-source): only `VW2t_G8eorI` gives a pound-test number; `Bo5Fj-XuWHo`
  says only "heavier line," with no figures.
- Strong and fast: "really, really strong knot," "really easy and fast to
  tie" (`Bo5Fj-XuWHo`).
- **Low-profile advantage:** "even with bigger pound test you're going to
  have a very very small knot" — the stated biggest advantage — which keeps a
  stealthy presentation even when fishing sardines for bait (`VW2t_G8eorI`).
- **Failure mode:** crossing the wraps while tying makes "a very poor knot"
  (`VW2t_G8eorI`) — the wraps must stay parallel, corroborated independently
  (`Bo5Fj-XuWHo`).

## Parameters

- **Bench setup:** both demos tie against a fixed anchor point rather than in
  hand, to hold tension with both hands free — a tying aid, not part of the
  knot's own mechanic (`Bo5Fj-XuWHo`, `VW2t_G8eorI`). ⚠ asr-uncertain: neither
  clip resolves whether the tie anchors to a hook eye specifically or shows
  the mechanic in isolation.
- **Mechanic (`Bo5Fj-XuWHo`):** make two wrapping passes down toward the hook
  without letting the passes cross. On what would be a third pass, wrap the
  tag around the top two strands twice instead. Cinch down — unlike the two
  wrapping passes, this cinch step does not try to keep the line from
  crossing itself — then pull the connection fully tight and trim the tag.
- **Mechanic (`VW2t_G8eorI`):** wrap the line twice around, holding the wraps
  with a thumb; feed the tag end twice through that loop without letting the
  lines cross; bring the tag down, then pass it twice through the bottom
  loop; bring the tag back over — called "a critical part of the knot."
  Remove the thumb and pull down: the two lines should run parallel at this
  point, stated as "the key to this knot." Pull the connection tight and
  trim.
- **Cross-check between the two demos:** both independently describe a
  multi-pass wrap that must not cross itself, ending in a pull-tight-and-trim.
  `VW2t_G8eorI` resolves more wrap-count detail (twice around, twice through,
  twice through the bottom loop, then back over); `Bo5Fj-XuWHo` resolves a
  top-two-strands-twice finishing move the other doesn't name. The two
  accounts don't share exact order or vocabulary, reading as two anglers
  describing the same named knot from memory rather than one precise
  transcript. ⚠ asr-uncertain: `Bo5Fj-XuWHo` is a 119-second
  auto-generated-caption clip and `VW2t_G8eorI` a 136-second
  auto-generated-caption clip; neither narrates a hook-eye pass or exact wrap
  geometry precisely enough to re-tie from words alone — watch the source
  videos below for the hand mechanics.
