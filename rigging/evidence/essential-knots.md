# Evidence — Essential SoCal Knots

Per-source provenance backing [Essential SoCal Knots](../essential-knots.md).

## Parameters

- `LPhnsEamRwI` (Mason, Dirty Hookers, 2021-06-09; unregistered channel —
  medium confidence): bluefin-specific version of the "step up past 100 lb"
  Palomar guidance — ties the hook on his [rubber-band deep
  rig](../rubber-band-deep-rig.md) with a San Diego jam rather than a Palomar
  for the same bite-through-the-knot reason.
- `G6YRT4HNxr8` (Roman Castro, 2018-04-17; `roman-castro` registered, single
  mention — medium confidence): ties a Palomar over an entire multi-arm
  harness (a [swimbait umbrella/A-rig](../../techniques/swimbaits.md#umbrella-rig-setup-bay-bass-roman-castro)),
  not just a single hook/lure. He and his fishing partner considered a uni
  knot for the job but suspected it might slip under a hard pull, so tied a
  Palomar instead, confident it wouldn't. Pulling the main line (not the tag)
  to take up the big loop costs very little line — "you don't lose that
  much" — leaving only a short tag to trim.
- `SK2M7L2vA1s` (Tackle Express, 2024-12-06; unregistered channel — medium
  confidence): recommends monofilament or fluorocarbon over braid for the
  Palomar — braid "has a more likely reason to slip on you" — though the
  demo itself uses braid for visibility. States, in his own words, that "if
  you're going to be going anything larger, whether it be a long lure or
  anything larger, you will have to compensate and make that loop longer" —
  the same loop-to-what's-passing-through-it principle as Roman Castro's
  whole-rig Palomar above, though Castro's citation doesn't state a
  "longer loop" rule in those words. Also the source for the San Diego jam's
  6-wrap figure logged in the main note (this video demonstrates Palomar,
  San Diego jam, and RP knot together).
- `b-oixz7pgAo` (Pete Gray, SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday
  Vol. 150," 2021-06-10; `searcher-sportfishing` registered channel —
  medium confidence): states on camera that "RP knot" and "John Collins
  knot" are the same tie, both named for its wheelhouse-of-the-*Royal
  Polaris* origin — consistent with the RP knot note's own ~80 lb
  "by the book" figure matching Vol. 150's stated 80 lb threshold, and with
  both notes independently citing a wood dowel for seating/cinching. The two
  notes still record different wrap counts (6-up/6-down vs. 9-up/9-down),
  so the identity claim and the wrap-count evidence disagree.
- `NkjjDf6XPcE` (StokedOnFishing, 2020-02-20; unregistered channel — medium
  confidence): a second, separate StokedOnFishing clip ties uni-to-uni
  specifically for fluorocarbon leader to monofilament main line (not
  braid). Paraphrased mechanic: lay the two lines parallel with about
  12–15 in of overlap and find the midpoint; on one line, form a loop with
  the tag end, pinch it, wrap the tag around both lines 3 or 4 times, pull
  down and draw the coils together tight; repeat on the other line in the
  opposite direction (loop, wrap the tag around 3 times, pull down and
  tighten); pull the two main lines together to cinch the two knots against
  each other and trim the tag ends.
- `qtCZAB4EBs4` ("Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 93 (Double Uni Knot),"
  SearcherSportfishing, 2020-04-02; `searcher-sportfishing` registered
  channel, single mention on this specific knot — medium confidence;
  presenter not identified on camera): leave about 12 in of tag on each side
  of the fluorocarbon and monofilament; grab the two lines together at the
  middle, form a loop back toward the thumb, pinch it, wrap the tag around
  both lines together. States the rule as 5 wraps per side, though the demo
  line itself was too short to show all 5 on camera ("it's five times, I
  don't have a long enough line to do that... so we got three times") — the
  stated rule is 5, not the 3 actually shown. Cinch the first side by
  pulling the tag line, turn the leader around and repeat the same
  loop-and-wrap on the other side (again stated as 5 wraps), then pull both
  standing lines to draw the two coils together and seat the knot. No line
  class, species, or application beyond "fluorocarbon to monofilament" is
  given in this 2:08 clip.
- `zKovnvOwlFc` ("Searcher Sportfishing Fluoro to Mono Knots,"
  SearcherSportfishing, 2017-05-04; `searcher-sportfishing` registered
  channel, presenter not clearly identified on camera — medium confidence):
  same channel's companion demo to the surgeon's-knot citation logged in
  [Surgeon's Knot — Mono-to-Fluorocarbon Leader
  Connection](../surgeons-knot-mono-to-fluoro.md#parameters--judgment).
  Ties the uni-to-uni for fluoro-to-mono, mono-to-mono of different sizes,
  and braid-to-braid alike. Paraphrased mechanic: lay the two lines opposite
  directions with plenty of tag, pinch the midpoint, form a loop, wrap the
  tag around both lines together 4 times before pulling it snug; repeat on
  the other line, also 4 times; wet the two finished uni knots and pull the
  main lines together until they jam. The same presenter, having tied both
  knots on camera, states a personal preference for the surgeon's knot on
  ordinary re-rigs because it's faster to tie (under 30 seconds for him),
  while granting uni-to-uni "looks a little bit better" — but states both
  knots hold the same strength and are equally effective at landing fish;
  full context in [Surgeon's Knot — Mono-to-Fluorocarbon Leader
  Connection](../surgeons-knot-mono-to-fluoro.md#parameters--judgment).
- `Ga8Z1PyBqdE` ("Master the UNI UNI Knot," Tackle Express, 2024-12-02;
  unregistered channel, single mention — medium confidence): demoed
  braid-to-braid, naming mono-to-mono, mono-to-fluoro, and
  braid-to-any-mono/fluoro-leader as other valid uses. Paraphrased mechanic:
  cross the two lines into an X, leaving 6–10 in of tag on each side; form a
  loop in one line and wrap the tag through it 6 times, pull that side snug
  (deliberately not fully cinched yet); slide down to the other line and
  repeat the same loop-and-6-wrap tie; pull all four strands together to
  bring the two coiled knots close, lubricate, draw both ends tight until
  the two knots meet, cinch them down against each other, and trim both
  tags.
- `48ZFXnCTTQE` (Cesar, Eric's Tackle, via Dirty Hookers, 2023-11-01;
  registered voice, single mention — medium confidence): loop knot for a
  non-ringed hook — full mechanic in the main note. Judgment: lubricate
  before seating — fluorocarbon is hard enough that spit alone
  under-lubricates and lets the coils burn/abrade each other on the pull;
  chapstick lets the coils slide down snug and compact instead of seating
  rough. Preferred by the presenter over a ringed hook for two reasons even
  when a ring is available: it adds no extra hardware weight to a bait
  that's already not fully healthy, and it's silent — no ring-on-hook clank
  to spook a finicky, picky bite.
- `eEcRPEoG4DQ` (StokedOnFishing, 2018-08-28; unregistered channel — medium
  confidence): surgeon's knot paraphrased mechanic — double the line into a
  loop, tie it like a square knot, pass the loop through three times
  (rather than once) before tightening. Presented as "the simplest knot you
  can tie" for a single-hook rockfish dropper loop, rated by the presenter
  as more than strong enough to hold.
- `3gykKyPdOvA` (Wendy Toshihara, SearcherSportfishing, 2020-12-10;
  unregistered channel — medium confidence): second, independent
  corroboration of the same three-turn surgeon's-knot mechanic, framed on
  camera as a quick way to tie a dropper loop "by not using a regular
  dropper loop knot" — the surgeon's knot substitutes for the dedicated
  dropper-loop knot/spider hitch. One explicit addition not stated in the
  `eEcRPEoG4DQ` clip: wet the knot before cinching it down. Also
  demonstrates repeating the same knot lower down the leader to build a
  swivel-free double dropper.
- `mpcSgkQvIzg` (StokedOnFishing, 2012-03-29; unregistered channel — medium
  confidence): worm knot mechanic — pass a finger through the Bimini
  twist's loop to hold it open, wrap the mono tag around the whole doubled
  loop section roughly 10 times (the presenter's on-camera count is uneven —
  "two three four... maybe 10 times... 6 7 8 9 10" — read as approximate
  rather than exact); pull the bitter end back through the same loop the
  finger was holding, pull against the mono main line first, then pull
  tight to cinch into a small knot and clip the tag close. Field-repair
  judgment: because the Bimini's twisted section is left intact when this
  knot is cut away, it's the fast fix for a mid-trip break-off — clip the
  worm knot near the bitter end (a slightly tighter clip than flush, per
  the presenter), work the remaining knot fragment off the Bimini loop with
  pliers, twist on a fresh length of topshot with a new worm knot, tie the
  hook back on, and you're back fishing in seconds without re-tying the
  Bimini itself. No SoCal-specific parameters (line class, species) are
  given in this clip.
- `ftEvyfwjZFU` (Dave Hansen): a same-family single-pass tie, spun 7 times,
  tied straight to lure/hook eyes rather than to a Bimini loop — the
  identical mechanic used for the improved clinch/"San Diego jam" naming
  split, applied to a glide swimbait, a stickbait, and a flylined sardine
  hook — see [Improved Clinch Knot](../improved-clinch-knot.md) for that
  naming detail and the straight-tie, no-swivel-no-bead rigging judgment
  (also covered independently in [flyline](../../techniques/flyline.md)
  and [tuna poppers and
  stickbaits](../../lures/tuna-poppers-and-stickbaits.md)).
