# Evidence — Haywire Twist

Per-source provenance backing [Haywire Twist](../haywire-twist.md).

## When to use

- `lm7D9Tlc7Po` (Capt. Scott Goodwin, BDOutdoors, "How to Rig Wire Leader
  (Perfect Haywire Twist)," 2020-02-03; unregistered channel — medium
  confidence): the note's primary how-to source; the split above (haywire
  twist for single-strand, crimp for braided/cable-type) and every base
  parameter below come from this clip.
- `ll7r4A6atno` (Your Saltwater Guide / Capt. Dave Hansen, "How to Catch
  Dorado | Living in Cabo San Lucas," 2021-01-19, Cabo San Lucas, Baja;
  unregistered channel, single mention — medium confidence): rigging small
  trolled hoochies for sierra ("razor blade teeth," "like little baby
  wahoos"), single-strand wire is the stated preference, with braided wire
  used as the fallback only when single-strand wasn't available locally.
  See [Cabo surf-line sierra
  trolling](../../techniques/trolling.md#cabo-san-lucas--surf-line-slow-trolling-for-sierra).

## Parameters

- `lm7D9Tlc7Po` — same source as above: tag length, loop-setting mechanic,
  the 45°-not-90° angle rule, tight-vs-loose tension, the length-not-count
  rule, the barrel-wrap finish, and the bend-and-crank break-off.
- `0zgmoOH3Qag` (Searcher Sportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 122
  (Haywire Twist)," 2020-10-22; `searcher-sportfishing` registered
  channel/series, single mention on these specific parameters — medium
  confidence): a second, independent walk-through of the same mechanics,
  demonstrated on 69 lb "toothy wire" — chosen for ease of twisting, "it's
  perfect, it's easier to tie your knots with." Forms the loop by putting the
  two legs at an angle, then twists six or seven times. Finishes by
  overlaying the tag back over the main line, described as ending up looking
  "almost like a San Diego knot," wrapped four or five times — the exact
  count doesn't matter, "it just looks really good." Break-off: pinch the
  tag between the fingers and move it up and down until it fatigues off
  clean, rather than cutting it with a tool, which "will cut your fingers
  every time you grab your lure."

## Wahoo casting leader — length and test

- `-62xDo4UQzo` (Searcher Sportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 85 (Wahoo
  Lures)," 2020-01-30; `searcher-sportfishing` registered channel/series,
  single mention — medium confidence): rigging a wahoo bomb or Raider jig
  for cast-and-retrieve fishing — seven-strand or single-strand wire, a wire
  twist finish, ring or swivel at the top, 12–15 in, 50 lb (62 lb also
  usable), lighter called "a little dicey." Finishes both stranding options
  with "a wire twist" on camera — no crimp sleeve or tool is shown for the
  seven-strand option, so this clip alone doesn't confirm which method the
  seven-strand build actually used.

## Wahoo casting/trolling leader — crimped build, heavier wire

- `o6Sawz5S7bk` (Searcher Sportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 73 (Wahoo
  leaders)," 2019-11-07; `searcher-sportfishing` registered channel/series,
  single mention — medium confidence): 275 lb steel wire, crimp sleeves at
  both ends, a swivel crimped to each end (main line side and jig side).
  Length stated on camera as both "about three feet" and, separately, 39 in
  — the two figures don't reconcile (39 in ≈ 3.25 ft). Brand names for the
  crimp sleeve and swivel are spoken on camera but render unrecognizably in
  captions — asr-uncertain, omitted from the main note rather than guessed.
  Predates the `-62xDo4UQzo` casting-leader entry above by about two and a
  half months and gives a starkly different spec for what reads as the same
  cast/troll-to-jig application. Crimp-mechanic detail (wire crimped fully
  tight at both ends, no flared "valley," different from the mono/fluoro
  build) is in [Crimping — Sleeve Crimps for Heavy
  Leaders](../../rigging/crimping.md#parameters).
- `4uNPLknRAQg` (Searcher Sportfishing, "Tackle Talk Live Recap (Wahoo
  5.5.20)," 2020-05-06; `searcher-sportfishing` registered channel/series,
  single mention on these parameters — medium confidence): a trolling-lure
  build (a Yo-Zuri Marauder shown on camera) — 200 lb wire (a second
  demonstration in the same session shows 200–250 lb), uncoated, crimped
  with the sleeve size printed on the wire's own packaging; loop ~1 in
  minimum; leader 30–36 in (a 24 in leader on camera called too short);
  heavy black swivel, not chrome (chrome "gets bitten through a lot"). Full
  session context in [wahoo trolling](../../techniques/wahoo-trolling.md#rigging).

## Wahoo live-bait leader

- `9IhmYstB8sA` (Searcher Sportfishing / Fisherman's Landing, "Tackle Tip
  Thursday Vol. 74 (Wahoo Fishing)," 2019-11-14; unregistered channel,
  single mention — medium confidence): small connector rings ("little black
  rings") plus wire, either single-strand or multi-strand, 200 or 300 lb
  test, hook tied on and fished like any live-bait sardine presentation. No
  specific ring product, wire brand, or leader length given on camera.
- `G81HN0dIDg0` (Taylor, Searcher Sportfishing / Fisherman's Landing Tackle
  Shop, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol.193 (Wahoo Lures 101)," 2022-08-11;
  unregistered channel — medium confidence): for flylining wahoo, wire on
  the leader is "very important," with "some guys" using fluorocarbon
  instead; stated benefit ("a lot less chance of getting bit off," wahoo
  "have big mean teeth") is given for having wire on there at all, not
  specifically attributed to choosing fluoro over wire — wire is the stated
  preference. Build: 44 lb wire to a 3/0 hook, "all you need." Roughly a
  fourth to a seventh of the 200–300 lb figure from `9IhmYstB8sA` above, from
  the same shop/channel, same "wahoo live bait" application; no stated
  reason for the gap (bait size, leader length, wire type/brand not given in
  either video).
- `S80GRyuAbLY` (Searcher Sportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 160 (Wahoo
  Wire Leaders)," 2021-11-04; `searcher-sportfishing` registered
  channel/series, single mention on this build — medium confidence):
  crimp-sleeve build (not haywire twist), stranding not stated on camera.
  60 lb wire, matching size-A2 sleeve — "it gives you the reference, so 60
  pound you use an A2 standard sleeve"; an A2 sleeve on 40 lb wire "would
  crimp loose and fail." Cut length 24 in, finished leader 18–20 in. Hook:
  1-0 or 2-0 live-bait hook, ring not required. Hook-end build order: sleeve
  onto wire, wire through the hook eye, back through the same sleeve to
  close a ~1/2 in loop (helps the bait swim), tag folded back flat before
  crimping with a proper tool, crimped over a couple of times. Top-end
  build: same sleeve-first order to a small black ring or swivel instead of
  a hook, same loop and multi-pass crimp. Stated failure mode: an undersized
  sleeve for the wire test crimps loose under a fighting fish.
- `_Z4yMtrYgeA` ("Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 123 (Wahoo Leader)," 2020-10-29):
  duplicate-of `S80GRyuAbLY` — the same demonstration re-cut under an
  earlier volume number and title (same 24 in/18–20 in figures, same hook,
  same loop and crimp steps, same A2/60 lb and 40 lb-crimps-loose lines),
  not a second independent source; pushes the doctrine's earliest recorded
  date back to 2020-10-29.
- `jqyu3wZdNF4` (Searcher Sportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 21,"
  2018-11-08; `searcher-sportfishing` registered channel/series, single
  mention on these parameters — medium confidence): the earliest-dated build
  in this set. Two leaders shown together, both to a 3/0 hook: a
  seven-strand leader with proper crimps/sleeves, a small swivel, 18 in
  minimum; and a single-strand leader with a haywire twist and a small ring,
  tied to a 40 lb outfit, flylined with no sinker, "one of the best methods
  for catching wahoo." No wire pound test given for either build. Predates
  every other Searcher wahoo live-bait leader tackle tip in this note; its
  single-strand-gets-a-haywire-twist / seven-strand-gets-crimped split is
  consistent with the note's stated twist-vs-crimp rule, unlike
  `-62xDo4UQzo` above, which shows both stranding options finished with "a
  wire twist."
- `vNIazq1aVwc` (Searcher Sportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 126 (Live
  Bait Wahoo Fishing)," 2020-11-19; `searcher-sportfishing` registered
  channel/series, single mention on these parameters — medium confidence):
  seven-strand leader, 40 lb (60 lb also offered as usable), "the correct
  crimps," to a hook heard on camera as "a number 100" — no standard hook
  size runs that high, carried as asr-uncertain, likely a mis-heard "1-0."
  Hook named as a cheap, inexpensive Mustad live-bait hook. Small swivel
  (also described as "a black ring") ties to a 30 or 40 lb flyline outfit.
  Overall length 12–18 in. Bait: live sardine, hooked sideways through the
  nose ("like a swim bait," the speaker's preference), shoulder-hooked in
  front of the dorsal, or belly-hooked toward the tail — the same three
  positions in the general [nose / collar-shoulder-dorsal / belly
  hook-position
  table](../../bait/fishing-live-bait.md#hook-position-by-situation), here
  independently named for wahoo. Live-bait hookset doctrine from this same
  video (free-spool, count to ~15) is logged in [wahoo — doctrine &
  conflicts](../../species/wahoo.md#doctrine--conflicts).
- `4uNPLknRAQg` (Searcher Sportfishing, "Tackle Talk Live Recap (Wahoo
  5.5.20)," 2020-05-06; `searcher-sportfishing` registered channel/series,
  single mention on these parameters — medium confidence; predates the
  `vNIazq1aVwc` 40 lb citation above by about six months, the first-dated
  source for this figure): 40 lb, seven-strand, uncoated Berkley wire, a
  small-gauge swivel or small black ring, Mustad 2/0 hook — specifically the
  cheap 7–8 cent model, not a premium ~$4 Owner hook — tied to 30 or 40 lb
  mono main line. Made up in batches of four or five leaders per Ziploc bag
  from a 30 ft length of wire. On-screen packaging for this 40 lb wire
  states the A2 standard sleeve — directly conflicting with `S80GRyuAbLY`'s
  A2-matches-60-lb figure; both citations show on-camera wire packaging as
  their source and neither reconciles the other.
  - **Titanium wire — a named but unadopted alternative (same session):**
    asked for an opinion on titanium leader wire, the speaker relays a
    secondhand opinion from Mike Lackey, given at the Long Beach show, that
    titanium holds up longer than standard wire, but has no personal
    experience with it. The speaker's own stated practice remains uncoated
    seven-strand, "forever." Carried as an attributed, unconfirmed opinion,
    not adopted as this note's doctrine.
- `W6RuHvaqkHs` (Logan, Searcher Sportfishing / Fisherman's Landing, "Tackle
  Tip Thursday Vol. 195 (Rigs for Wahoo)," 2022-09-01;
  `searcher-sportfishing` registered channel/series, single mention on these
  parameters — medium confidence): an 8 ft rod, "a good sized reel," 40–50
  lb main line, "a short wire leader like the one I have tied on here" — no
  wire test or leader length given for this specific leader. Stated
  capability: "perfect for live bait fishing wahoo up to 60 to 70 pounds."
