## Stock flat-fall assist hooks wear out — upgrade, and size to the grade of fish

- `fgTmUq78ofQ` (Art Taylor, owner/operator, SearcherSportfishing, and Capt.
  Mike, live seminar at Turner's Outdoorsman, San Marcos, hosted by Lori
  Sackow, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 147 — Live at Turner's Outdoorsman,"
  2021-05-14; `searcher-sportfishing` registered channel, mechanism/
  parameters rate normally but this is a single mention of this specific
  hook product — medium confidence): stock flat-fall assist hook failure
  mode (loses fish after an estimated three or four hookups), the crew's
  practice of swapping stock assist hooks (and Colt Sniper-style trebles/
  split rings) on essentially every jig before fishing it, the fix (bigger
  hook, bigger split ring, no added hardware), a 2021 Tady wire-cord assist
  hook (49-strand wire, 65 lb test, Kevlar-coated) named on camera as a
  product — low confidence, sponsored-claim caveat — and the size-to-grade
  reasoning (a ~20 lb bluefin's teeth are small enough that stock is
  probably fine; 25–40 lb "probably okay"; the real risk is a bigger fish on
  80 lb line), stated against a mixed-grade 30–150 lb bite that week, and
  that boat passengers can have the crew swap hooks/rings for them.

## Hook shape — shank, bend, and point (selection, not tying)

- `-nIhadZwxAA` (Benny Ortiz, CustomRodandReel seminar, 2025-05-20;
  unregistered channel, East Coast/Gulf-based slow-pitch instruction —
  general hook mechanics, not species-specific — medium confidence): shank
  length trade-off (short shank e.g. Gamakatsu 510-class vs. long shank e.g.
  Gamakatsu 620-class), deep-belly bend for hookup ratio, straight vs.
  "tuna-blade" point orientation (quoted "like a hypodermic needle rather
  than a 2×4"), the full preference stack (long shank, deep belly, curved
  point, narrow taper, thicker gauge, spade eye — mechanism for the spade-eye
  claim not given), single-top (speed jigging) vs. two-single-hooks
  top-and-bottom (slow-pitch) hook count and placement, the single-vs-double
  hookset-quality difference (buries past the jaw vs. catches "in and
  around" the mouth), and the no-wire-anywhere principle (accepting an
  occasional bite-off over tackle scaled for toothy fish).
