# Evidence — Rubber-Band Deep Rig

Trip reports and per-source provenance for
[rubber-band-deep-rig.md](../rubber-band-deep-rig.md).

## Primary demo video

- `sYrsPGXiYhI`: captions are badly garbled throughout; only the general
  concept and method (breakaway rubber band, torpedo sinker) could be
  captured from this transcript. Specific sinker weights, band sizes, and
  leader lengths were not reliably transcribed and are omitted from the main
  note — confirm those details against the video before relying on them.

## When to use it

- `5CvFDpvlfSE` (Steve Carson, SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday
  Vol. 225 (Sinker Rigs for Bluefin)," 2023-08-03, aboard the *Searcher*;
  unregistered channel, medium confidence): demonstrates the two rubber-band
  builds plus the drop-shot/Bralla build side by side as "old school" vs.
  "new school" options.
- `42A8Owhc8fw` (Ray Sharifi, Dirty Hookers, 2023-08-02; `ray-sharifi`
  registered channel, SoCal-scoped claim so this entry caps at medium
  confidence): ties on an 80 lb fluorocarbon leader and builds this rig
  specifically once the boat comes onto a metered-but-not-foaming school, in
  place of casting metal at a visible foamer.
- `G1YIlakiRqg` (Capt. Matt, SD Fish and Sips, uploaded 2024-03-13;
  unregistered channel/speaker, medium confidence): states the rig gets bit
  best on a mixed-grade, depth-stacked school in flat calm, and is notorious
  for tangling if seeked/free-spooled too fast.
- `qLKLlZdiflA` (Tackle Express, "Essentials You Need For Tuna Fishing in
  Southern California," 2023-04-20; unregistered channel, medium
  confidence): the sinker-on-top spin/half-dead-bait failure mode and the
  8–12 oz standard weight for that build.

## Concept and attachment

- `5CvFDpvlfSE`: demonstrates the pull-through and wrapped builds side by
  side aboard the *Searcher*, framed as "old school" options.
- `KYE14piJAzI` (SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 55 (Sinker
  rig fishing)," 2019-07-03; unregistered channel, medium confidence): four
  years before Vol. 225, walks the same two mechanics under the names
  "double it over... put the sinker on" (pull-through) and "bridling it on"
  (wrapped), with the same neighboring-angler-tangle reasoning for the
  wrapped build.
- `nWq2DVzBNeI` (SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 104 (Sinker
  rig fishing)," 2020-06-18, presenter Armando; `searcher-sportfishing`
  registered, high confidence for the mechanic itself): target stated as
  tuna and, in a partly garbled phrase, possibly yellowtail —
  `asr-uncertain`, not confidently resolved.
- `ewfWc7MTBPk` (SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 110 (In-line
  sinker rig fishing)," 2020-07-30, presenter Billy at Fisherman's Landing
  Tackle Shop — the same presenter as [Crimping](../crimping.md) and
  [Springer Knot](../springer-knot.md); registered channel, high confidence
  for the mechanic): calls this "pretty much the most effective way" to
  catch bluefin the week before the 2020-07-30 upload — a dated claim, not
  generalized beyond that window. Does not mention a breakaway or state what
  happens to the sinker on a hookup, so that detail is not asserted for this
  citation either way.
- `pB10vaDaETM` (SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 84 (Sinker
  rig fishing)," 2020-01-23, presenter not named on camera — captions too
  sparse to identify a speaker; registered channel, high confidence for the
  mechanic): a short (2:21) segment; the placement wording ("below your
  connection") is terse and no visual demo was available to confirm which
  side of the knot is meant.
- `qBP3qRnK4H4` (SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday Vol. 71 (Sinker
  rig fishing)," 2019-10-24, presenter not named, captions sparse on this
  69-second clip; registered channel, high confidence for the mechanic):
  closest to the simple pull-through build, though the captions are too
  sparse to confirm the build identification beyond doubt —
  `asr-uncertain`. Bait: nose-hooked live sardine.

## Leader, knot, and tie-on sequence

- `LPhnsEamRwI` (Mason, Dirty Hookers, aboard the *Pride*, 2021-06-09;
  unregistered channel, medium confidence): ties the rig start to finish;
  credits a captain he calls "Ray" (not further identified) with the same
  tie-on preference — plausibly Ray Sharifi, though the transcript gives no
  surname to confirm it.
- `nWq2DVzBNeI`: leader tied to the mono main line with a surgeon's knot
  passed three times ("one two three times"), cinched tight, tags trimmed.
  Hook knot named as a "quick Palomar" for speed, with a mention that the
  presenter personally uses a San Diego knot — phrasing is garbled on which
  knot is actually tied in this demo, `asr-uncertain`.

## Sinker and hook sizing

- `RfiC8sfIWTk` (Ray Sharifi, 2023-05-04; `ray-sharifi` registered
  2026-08-17 but Baja-scoped, so this SoCal claim caps at medium
  confidence): size 4 round sinker, 4–6 oz.
- `149px8WQ2Ng` (Ray Sharifi, Dirty Hookers, Highliner deckhand POV,
  2023-04-24, ten days before `RfiC8sfIWTk`, same channel; `ray-sharifi`
  registered, SoCal-scoped, medium confidence): corroborates the 4–6 oz / 5
  oz-sweet-spot range; only 2 of the day's bites came on the sinker rig that
  trip vs. ~90% on flyline — a minority presentation, not the primary one
  that day.
- `jQW2HLkMsmY` (Ray Sharifi, Dirty Hookers, 2022-05-02; `ray-sharifi`
  registered 2026-08-17 but Baja-scoped, medium confidence; predates the two
  citations above): names the technique the "rubber band rig."
- `fg2v1kxoTMA` (Carl, Fisherman's Landing/SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Tip
  Thursday Vol. 190," 2022-07-21; registered channel, medium confidence,
  single mention): the bigger-bait/3–4/0 hook data point.
- `eehDVb6_GoI` (named on camera only as "Art," plausibly the same Capt. Art
  Taylor named by full name in `fgTmUq78ofQ` below, not confirmed by surname
  in this transcript — "Tackle Talk Live at Turner's Outdoorsman in
  Norwalk," 2021-06-30; registered channel, medium confidence — corroborating
  `fgTmUq78ofQ` for the sinker range, new for the hook range and the
  mid-fight sinker detail): about six weeks after `fgTmUq78ofQ`, plausibly
  the same presenter at a different Turner's Outdoorsman event.
- `fgTmUq78ofQ` (Art Taylor/Mike, SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday
  Vol. 147 — Live at Turner's Outdoorsman," 2021-05-14; registered channel,
  medium confidence, single mention of this specific build).
- `qBP3qRnK4H4`: sinker size called out as what "you need... in your tackle
  box these days" — a data point at the heavier end of the 4–8 oz range

- `tU4jhAkdzNw` (StokedOnFishing, 2017-05-11 upload, "Top Gun 80 Epic 5 Day,
  part 3," SoCal islands, exact zone not named on camera, 5-day charter
  aboard the *Top Gun 80*; unregistered channel, medium confidence): a
  trip-tips segment framed around fishing big, picky bluefin, giving a much
  lighter build (40 lb fluorocarbon top shot, half-ounce sliding sinker
  riding above the leader connection, No. 2/No. 3 circle hooks,
  corner-of-the-mouth hookset) than the torpedo-sinker rigs logged
  elsewhere. The transcript renders a stray "9" immediately before "small
  hooks work best" — almost certainly an ASR mis-transcription of a filler
  word, not a stated hook count; not carried as a parameter.

## Rod, reel, and line pairing

- `jQW2HLkMsmY`: rod/reel pairing — Daiwa Saltist 50 lever-drag reel
  (captions render "Soltus," corrected here) on a Phoenix Axis rod. Reasoning
  given for reaching for this rig: it gets a live bait into a deep zone when
  fish are marking deep with little surface boiling but the captain is still
  calling fish on the sounder.
- `RfiC8sfIWTk`: rod/reel step from 50 lb to 80 lb outfit as fish grade
  bigger; field-expedient re-rig of a knife-jig outfit's leader.

## Depth, mid-fight behavior, and a daytime alternative

- `qBP3qRnK4H4`: crew-called depth off the sounder, consistent with the
  crew-directed-depth pattern logged throughout this note.
- `eehDVb6_GoI`: standard crew practice on this boat when the sinker rides
  up mid-fight — angler backs off the rail, crew pulls the sinker and
  pockets it. Daytime flyline alternative (25 lb test, 2/0 circle hook)
  called the best approach on a trip where fish were showing on the surface
  daily.
- `_jDXMtCrUZg` (Steve Carson, SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday
  Vol. 59 (Fly line outfit)," 2019-10-16; unregistered-at-the-time channel,
  now `searcher-sportfishing` registered, medium confidence, single
  mention): a data point between Vol. 55 (2019-07-03) and Vol. 225
  (2023-08-03) in upload order, presented as part of a 40/50 lb-class
  flyline outfit rather than as its own dedicated sinker-rig segment — full
  rod/reel/line spec for that outfit is in
  [Searcher big-tuna rig ladder](../../tackle/searcher-big-tuna-rig-ladder.md#workhorse-flylining-outfit-4050-lb-class--guadalupe-island-steve-carson-pennsearchersportfishing-2019-10-16).
  Called out on camera for what Carson names "sinker baits," on the same
  [Guadalupe Island](../../locations/guadalupe.md) tuna trips as the flyline
  outfit.

## Ring-and-tag-line rig ("Bralla")

- `G1YIlakiRqg` (Capt. Matt, SD Fish and Sips, uploaded 2024-03-13;
  unregistered channel/speaker, medium confidence): built this rig a few
  years before the seminar (year not stated on camera) after watching boats
  fishing standard rubber-band rigs get spooled out on a deep-marked school.
  The first time he fished the build, he hooked up on his second drop and
  landed a 170 lb fish.
- `UP_3fBu7VPA` (Steve Carson panel, "Gear Needed for Half to Full Day
  [Yellowtail] Fishing," 2024 Pacific Coast Sportfishing Show, Fisher Newb
  upload, 2024-03-20; unregistered channel/speakers, medium confidence):
  frames this build as the "new and improved" successor to the original
  rubber-band-topped sinker rig, worth reading alongside but not confirmed
  identical in hardware to the SD Fish and Sips or Carson full builds.
- `5CvFDpvlfSE`: in the same breath as the two rubber-band builds, calls
  this one "the drop shot rig or the [Bralla] rig" — the first source in
  this note to use both names for what sounds like one build (hardware not
  confirmed identical to the other two builds). Carson runs a 2.5 ft
  dropper; notes Captain Mike prefers 3 ft — kept side by side as two crew
  members' own preferences, not reconciled. Calls it the most productive of
  the three rigs shown that (2023) season, though which rig is getting bit
  "changes just about every single day."
- `83HROAgGW6Q` (Steve Carson, SearcherSportfishing, "Tackle Tip Thursday
  Vol. 233 (Drop-shot Rig)," 2024-05-16; unregistered channel, medium
  confidence): a dedicated follow-up to the Vol. 225 build, same presenter,
  adding rod/reel/line detail the earlier video didn't give. Hook captioned
  "ringed M hook," almost certainly a ringed Mutu-pattern hook (Owner's
  ringed Mutu circle hooks are a common SoCal bluefin choice) — not
  confidently resolved to an exact model, `asr-uncertain`.
- `gimFbgN5Jjk` (Brian Wen, 2025 PCS Show "Slow Pitch to Speed Jigging"
  seminar, uploaded 2025-03-14; unregistered channel/panel, medium
  confidence): described in a Q&A answer only, no visual demo, thinner
  detail than the builds above. Reports success fishing it from a rod
  holder while jigging it.
