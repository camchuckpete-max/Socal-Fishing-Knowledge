# Wind-On Leader

A wind-on leader is a fluorocarbon (or mono) top shot joined to hollow-core
braid without a knot: the leader threads *inside* the hollow braid so the
braid's weave clamps down on it (the Chinese-finger-trap principle), and the
braid finishes in its own doubled loop on the reel end. The connection runs
close to 100% of line strength with almost no diameter bump — smooth enough
to wind through the rod guides and back onto the reel (`zkSKgP2bq10`). ⚠
Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): the transcript confirms "almost 100%
strength" but never states a diameter-bump or guide-passage claim — that
detail isn't in the cited transcript. It
swaps in and out at the loop, so a fresh top shot goes on in seconds without
re-spooling. See [line and leader](../tackle/line-and-leader.md) for the
braid and fluoro classes this pairs with.

## When to use

- Any application where the leader must pass through the guides during the
  fight and a knot catching on the way out is a problem: tuna
  [fly-lining](../techniques/flyline.md), [knife jigging](../techniques/knife-jigging.md),
  and [kite fishing](../techniques/kite-fishing.md) (`zkSKgP2bq10`).
- Fast top-shot changes on the water: carry pre-made wind-ons of different
  lengths/classes and connect them loop-to-loop instead of retying
  (`_KE9InIHx8M`).
- Requires **hollow-core** braid (e.g., 16-strand thread-lock construction) on
  the reel; it does not work with solid braid (`zkSKgP2bq10`).
- Replaces a swivel-and-crimp fight leader on heavy-class outfits where knot
  bulk and tangle risk matter — see Wind-on vs. crimped fight leader below.

## Parameters

- **Match the classes.** Keep braid and leader within roughly one pound-test
  step of each other — 100 lb braid to 100 lb fluoro works, and 130 lb braid
  to 100 lb leader is workable, but a large mismatch such as 130 lb braid to
  40 lb leader will not hold (`zkSKgP2bq10`). Pick the leader diameter closest
  to the braid's rating.
- **Bury length is the whole game.** The longer the fluoro sits inside the
  braid, the harder the finger-trap grips; do not be stingy with it. Treat
  **2 ft as the minimum** bury and cut a long working section (about 6 ft) so
  there is slack to feed in (`zkSKgP2bq10`). Older doctrine ran the bury
  around **5-6 ft** with a served finish (`_KE9InIHx8M`); both hold — the
  shorter version leans harder on a locked terminal wrap.
- **The braid loop** on the reel end is a doubled, tucked loop sized to pass a
  wrist through, for tying a [bimini twist](bimini-twist.md)/cat's-paw to the
  running braid (`zkSKgP2bq10`). Adjust it before cutting anything off the
  spool.
- **Lock the tail.** The bare finger-trap slides out under slack, so finish
  the leader end one of two ways: a served wrap of dental floss or 30-65 lb
  braid, closed with a three-turn uni knot (`_KE9InIHx8M`); or a series of
  about **15-20 same-direction half hitches** finished with a full hitch that
  tucks the tag two strands back (`zkSKgP2bq10`). Set either finish with a
  drop of super glue (`_KE9InIHx8M`, `zkSKgP2bq10`).
- **Leader length** is builder's choice — a common finished top shot runs
  about **25 ft** (`_KE9InIHx8M`). Prep several at once (e.g., 100 lb, various
  lengths) and store them coiled.
- **Failure modes:** peeling braid off the spool over the top adds a
  half-twist that jams the needle — strip it straight instead (`_KE9InIHx8M`);
  pushing the needle through the braid wall at too steep an angle instead of
  laying flat (`zkSKgP2bq10`); too short a bury; skipping the terminal lock so
  the leader creeps out under slack (`zkSKgP2bq10`).

## Wind-on vs. crimped fight leader

A heavy wind-on (**130-200 lb, roughly 25 ft**) replaces the common
tackle-shop alternative on a night-bluefin heavy outfit — a short (3-4 ft)
heavy mono/fluoro "fight leader" joined to the main line with a swivel and
crimp, then the lure tied or crimped to that (`G1YIlakiRqg`; full build in
[bluefin rig ladder by grade](../tackle/bluefin-rig-ladder-by-grade.md#nighttime-rig-ladder-by-aggressiveness-and-grade-gavin-pacific-queen)).
Three reasons favor the wind-on over that setup:

- **Knot difficulty at heavy line classes.** Tying 100 lb main line to a
  200 lb leader by knot is difficult to do well, and even a well-tied heavy
  knot is difficult to pass smoothly back through the rod guides; a wind-on's
  finger-trap splice stays smooth and low-diameter enough to run through
  guides cleanly (see Parameters above) (`G1YIlakiRqg`).
- **One fewer failure point.** A swivel-and-crimp fight leader is "just an
  extra knot and an extra piece of terminal tackle that has a possibility of
  failure" — running the wind-on straight to the lure removes it entirely
  (`G1YIlakiRqg`).
- **Tangle reduction on a crowded sportboat — the largest factor.** On a boat
  carrying 30-plus anglers, Spectra tangles routinely wrap around
  swivel-and-crimp connections and take "hours" to clear; a wind-on-to-lure
  setup without that hardware spends less time tangled and more time fishing,
  since tangled time costs hookups (`G1YIlakiRqg`). ⚠ Fact-check
  (contradicted-by-source): the transcript's actual line is "I have spent
  hours of my life unwrapping Spectra off of one of those swivels" — a
  cumulative career statement, not a claim that a single tangle takes hours
  to clear. Tie or crimp the lure
  directly to the wind-on leader's terminal end.
