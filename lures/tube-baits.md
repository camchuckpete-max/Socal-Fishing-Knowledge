# Tube Baits (HookUp Baits)

A tube bait is a soft plastic with the lead head molded inside the body — head
inside the plastic, not a bare jig head threaded with a plastic trailer
(cameron). Tube baits are a SoCal staple, and HookUp Baits is the brand that
made the modern bullet-shaped version ubiquitous here; a Tackle Express source
calls the generic 1.5–2 oz version "the stupid tube," a standing calico-bass
go-to (`9a-Zy_D6c3w`). Tube baits are a distinct soft-plastic class from
[glide baits](../techniques/glide-baits.md), a jointed hard-bait class this
note's content previously sat under in error (cameron). This note covers
construction, scent, and color selection; rigging, rods, and per-species/
application execution live in
[tube-bait fishing](../techniques/tube-bait-fishing.md).

⚠ adjudicated (Cameron, 2026-08-23). A 2018 seminar described the bait as
"hard-shelled, articulated" (`0HwYl0HO2tc`); a 2020 seminar describes it
correctly, matching the construction row above (`5Oh3IPIC5g4`). The 2020
description is the accepted one — this is a soft-plastic tube bait, not a
jointed hard-bait glide bait — and the 2018 "hard-shelled, articulated" line
is a misextraction, not a second construction variant (cameron).

Cutting the tail adds kick at the cost of some of the body's planing/floating
action on the fall (`5Oh3IPIC5g4`). Size-selection doctrine — distance vs.
time-in-zone, the big-bait size filter, downsizing when fish follow but won't
commit — lives in [tube-bait fishing](../techniques/tube-bait-fishing.md).

## Scent

HookUp Baits' proprietary scent is reapplied roughly every third cast on
tournament days, more often on a slow bite. It's formulated deliberately thin
on purpose — a scent that's easy to reapply gets reapplied, one that's a
hassle to use doesn't (`0HwYl0HO2tc`). Two named lines: a shrimp/crab scent
that also tints the bait's belly reddish and leaves a holographic flake/scale
trail, and a sardine/anchovy scent for the same scale-trail effect
(`0HwYl0HO2tc`). These are product-specific claims and stay low confidence;
the underlying idea — scent plus a visual belly/scale cue, reapplied on a
short cycle — is the part worth carrying forward.

A second scent source ties each scent to a specific forage cue rather than
treating color and scent as separate systems, and corroborates the
scale-trail idea above with a stated feeding-reaction mechanism
(`5Oh3IPIC5g4`):

- **Yellow ("Fin Bait," sardine oil):** matched to the bright yellow gill
  plate and yellow lateral line common to jack mackerel, sardines, and
  (freshwater) shad.
- **Red (shrimp/crab oil):** matched to the orange/red belly seen on
  swimbaits generally, paired with the company's red-crab-profile baits.
- **White, scented with garlic — a cover scent, not the primary attractant:**
  the baits already carry a faint garlic smell out of the package as a
  manufacturing byproduct; the added garlic scent is framed as masking that
  plastic/manufacturing smell, with a second, stronger garlic-only product
  for anglers who want more of it.
- **Glitter/flake in every scent — the "scale trail" mechanism:** feeding
  fish key on loose scales drifting in the water from bait getting torn up
  in a bite (illustrated with a fishkeeping analogy: goldfish keying up when
  scales come loose in a tank). The glitter leaves that scale trail behind
  the bait, a third attraction channel alongside color and smell —
  corroborating, with an explicit mechanism, the holographic flake/scale
  trail already logged above for the shrimp/crab and sardine/anchovy scents.

## Color selection

- **Yellowtail:** mint is named the top overall color, sardine second — both
  reliable all-around choices; green is also called a strong all-around
  confidence color ("kind of owns everything"); red crab produces well on
  tougher/harder bite days (`5Oh3IPIC5g4`).
- **Dorado:** a pink ("Dorado") color in 1 oz or 5/8 oz, particularly fished
  at kelp paddies, is named a favorite dorado color/pattern (`5Oh3IPIC5g4`).
- **Bait weight — a casting-distance-vs.-time-in-the-zone trade-off, not
  species-specific:** a 1 oz bait casts less far but can be worked slower and
  stays in the strike zone longer once it lands; a 1.5 oz bait casts farther
  but has to be worked faster to keep it up out of the zone it's meant to
  pass through. Pick the heavier size specifically when fish are holding
  boat-shy and a long cast matters more than a slow presentation
  (`5Oh3IPIC5g4`).
- **Red crab at red-crab-holding structure** (Catalina Island, San Clemente
  Island, and specifically fish sitting tight to the Coronado Islands, Cedros
  Island, and Ensenada/Baja): red crab is named consistently deadly on
  resident bass and [sheephead](../species/sheephead.md) holding tight to
  structure known to carry red crab (`5Oh3IPIC5g4`).
- **[California halibut](../species/california-halibut.md), sized by
  water:** sized up for halibut outside (surf/coastal water), and a smaller
  3/8 or 5/8 oz bait inside a bay. Mint and glow green both work well on
  halibut; a white "shad"-pattern bait is called out specifically for
  morning and evening (`5Oh3IPIC5g4`).
- **The "Oreo cookie effect" — a forage-satiation nuance:** at a spot already
  packed with live red crab, red crab-flavored/colored bait becomes the
  hardest bait to get bit — the stated theory is satiation, "how many Oreo
  cookies can you eat" — and a sardine or mint bait outfishes red crab there
  instead, reportedly even winning tournaments on that read. At structure
  that holds red crab without being saturated with it, the red crab bait is
  what fires the fish instead — the two situations look similar (red crab
  present) but call for opposite bait choices depending on how much red crab
  is actually on the spot, not just whether it's there at all (`5Oh3IPIC5g4`).

## When to choose it

- Kelp/reef edges, bay structure, drifting rock-fishing, and prospecting
  paddies or a skipjack layer for tuna: routed from
  [tube-bait fishing](../techniques/tube-bait-fishing.md) and the species
  notes it links (calico bass, sand bass, yellowtail, rockfish/lingcod,
  halibut, sheephead, dorado, skipjack/yellowfin tuna).
- Red-crab-holding structure for sheephead and resident bass, unless the spot
  is already saturated with red crab (the Oreo cookie effect, above) — then
  switch to sardine or mint.
- Sizing and color for [California halibut](../species/california-halibut.md):
  inside a bay vs. outside on the coast, above.
- Gear class: bass-casting/swimbait, stepping up to 40 lb-class for 3–4 oz
  baits — see [gear classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md#lure-classes-cross-reference)
  and [tube-bait fishing](../techniques/tube-bait-fishing.md#rod-parabolic-bend-for-big-baits).
