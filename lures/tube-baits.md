# Tube Baits (HookUp Baits)

**The class (Cameron, 2026-08-23 — high confidence):** a tube bait is a
**soft plastic with the lead head molded inside the body** — head inside the
plastic, not a bare jig head with a plastic threaded on. Tube baits are a
**SoCal staple**, and **HookUp Baits** is the brand that made the modern
bullet-shaped version ubiquitous here. The corpus corroborates the vernacular:
a Tackle Express panga source calls the generic 1.5–2 oz version *"the stupid
tube"* as a standing go-to.

**They are not glide baits.** The KB previously filed this entire class under
[glide baits](../techniques/glide-baits.md) — a jointed hard-bait class —
from a misread of the 2018 designer seminar (`0HwYl0HO2tc`) that described
the bait as "hard-shelled, articulated." The 2020 co-owner seminar
(`5Oh3IPIC5g4`) describes the product correctly — **a solid one-piece
soft-plastic body molded over a lead head, with a hollow scent cavity and a
trimmable tail** — and Cameron's review (2026-08-23) resolves the flagged
discrepancy in the 2020 source's favor: that is a tube bait. The 2018
"hard/articulated" reading is superseded as an extraction misinterpretation.

**Confidence:** both seminar sources are the maker's own principals at trade
shows, so product/performance claims stay **low** per the sponsored-claim
rule. The class definition itself is `cameron` — high.

## Construction and sizes

- One-piece soft-plastic body over an internal lead head; **hollow cavity**
  holds scent; the **tail is trimmable** — cutting it adds kick at the cost
  of some of the body's planing/floating action on the fall (`5Oh3IPIC5g4`).
- Weights fished across this corpus: **3/8, 5/8, 1, 1.5, 3, and 3–6 oz**,
  in "Bullet" and "Big Game" profiles. Size selection doctrine (distance vs
  time-in-zone, big-bait size filter, follows-but-won't-commit downsizing)
  is in [tube-bait fishing](../techniques/tube-bait-fishing.md).
- Stock hook is not weedless; the Owner "sled" weedless conversion is in
  [tube-bait fishing](../techniques/tube-bait-fishing.md#weedless-rig-for-grass-and-heavy-kelp-owner-sled-system).

## Scent

A proprietary scent, reapplied roughly **every third cast** on tournament
days (more often on a slow bite), formulated deliberately thin so anglers
actually keep reapplying it. Two named lines: a **shrimp/crab** scent that
also tints the bait's belly reddish and leaves a holographic
flake/scale trail, and a **sardine/anchovy** scent for the same scale-trail
effect. Product-specific — carried at **low confidence** for the underlying
idea (scent plus a visual belly/scale cue, reapplied on a short cycle)
rather than for the named products.

**A second, more detailed HookUp Baits scent/color source (Chad, co-owner,
`5Oh3IPIC5g4`, 2020)** ties each scent to a specific forage cue rather than
treating color and scent as separate systems, and corroborates the
scale-trail idea above with a stated feeding-reaction mechanism:
- **Yellow ("Fin Bait," sardine oil):** matched to the bright yellow gill
  plate and yellow lateral line common to jack mackerel, sardines, and
  (freshwater) shad.
- **Red (shrimp/crab oil):** matched to the orange/red belly seen on
  swimbaits generally, paired with the company's red-crab-profile baits.
- **White, scented with garlic — described as a cover scent, not the
  primary attractant:** the baits already carry a faint garlic smell
  straight out of the package as a byproduct of manufacturing; the added
  garlic scent is framed as masking that unnatural plastic/manufacturing
  smell, with a second, stronger garlic-only product for anglers who want
  more of it.
- **Glitter/flake in every scent, explained as a "scale trail":** stated
  mechanism — feeding fish key on loose scales drifting in the water from
  bait getting torn up in a bite (illustrated with a fishkeeping analogy:
  goldfish keying up when scales come loose in a tank). The glitter leaves
  that scale trail behind the bait, a third attraction channel alongside
  color and smell — corroborates, with an explicit mechanism, the
  holographic flake/scale trail already logged above for the shrimp/crab
  and sardine/anchovy scents.


## Color selection and the "Oreo cookie effect" (Chad, HookUp Baits co-owner, `5Oh3IPIC5g4`, 2020)

- **Yellowtail:** **mint** named as the top overall color, **sardine**
  second — both described as reliable all-around choices. **Green** is
  also called a strong all-around confidence color ("kind of owns
  everything"). **Red crab** produces well on tougher/harder bite days.
- **Dorado:** a **pink ("Dorado")** color in **1 oz or 5/8 oz**, particularly
  fished at kelp paddies, is named as a favorite dorado color/pattern.
- **Bait weight — a general casting-distance-vs.-time-in-the-zone
  trade-off, not species-specific:** a **1 oz** bait casts less far but can
  be worked slower and stays in the strike zone longer once it's landed
  (worked more slowly on the surface); a **1.5 oz** bait casts farther but
  has to be worked faster to keep it up out of the zone it's meant to pass
  through. Pick the heavier size specifically when fish are holding boat-shy
  and a long cast matters more than a slow presentation.
- **Red crab at red-crab-holding structure** (Catalina, San Clemente
  Island, and specifically fish sitting **tight to the Coronado Islands**,
  Cedros Island, and Ensenada/Baja): red crab is named as consistently
  deadly on resident bass and sheephead holding tight to structure known to
  carry red crab.
- **[California halibut](../species/california-halibut.md), sized by
  water:** he sizes up for halibut **outside** (surf/coastal water) and
  runs a smaller **3/8 or 5/8 oz** bait **inside a bay**. **Mint** and
  **glow green** are both named as working well on halibut; a **white
  "shad"**-pattern bait is called out specifically for **morning and
  evening**.
- **The "Oreo cookie effect" — a forage-satiation nuance, decision and
  reason stated on camera:** at a spot **already packed with live red
  crab**, red crab-flavored/colored bait becomes the **hardest** bait to
  get bit — the stated theory is satiation, "how many Oreo cookies can you
  eat" — and a **sardine or mint** bait outfishes red crab there instead,
  even winning tournaments on that read. But at structure that holds red
  crab **without being saturated with it**, the red crab bait is what
  fires the fish — the two situations look similar (red crab present) but
  call for opposite bait choices depending on **how much** red crab is
  actually on the spot, not just whether it's there at all.
