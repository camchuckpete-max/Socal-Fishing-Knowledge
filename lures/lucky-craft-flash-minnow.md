# Lucky Craft Flash Minnow

A shallow-diving hard bait treated as the generic term for a surf minnow lure
in SoCal beach halibut fishing (Tackle Express, `E4CDqBFOaP8`, 2025-12-23):
"if you call it a Lucky Craft to a surf angler, they'll know exactly what
you're talking about," used to describe any minnow-type lure in this
category. Single-source, unregistered-channel doctrine — stays **medium**
confidence.

## Specs

- **Length: 110 mm.**
- **Colors:** available in multiple color patterns; the exact count is not
  confidently transcribed from the auto-captions (rendered as a garbled
  "7,400,38" — **asr-uncertain**, not carried as a figure).
- **Running depth:** a shallow runner by design — does not dive deep.

## Why it's built shallow

Stated design reason: "I don't want a jerkbait that gets down to the fish, I
want a jerkbait or minnow lure that gets the fish up to the minnow lure" — a
deeper diver digs into the sand in skinny water and doesn't get bit. Fishable
in **3, 4, or 5 ft of water** without hitting bottom.

**When** this lure applies versus a deeper hardbait or a leadhead-and-swimbait
switch past ~10 ft is decided in
[California halibut](../species/california-halibut.md#situations--techniques),
alongside the other attributed surf/hardbait doctrine for this species.
