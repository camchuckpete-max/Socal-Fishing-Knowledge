# Lucky Craft Flash Minnow

A 110 mm shallow-diving hardbait, and the generic term SoCal surf anglers use
for any minnow-type lure in this class: "if you call it a Lucky Craft to a
surf angler, they'll know exactly what you're talking about" (`E4CDqBFOaP8`).
Built to fish skinny water without digging into the sand, it is the default
hardbait for beach halibut in the 1–5 ft band. Choosing it over a deeper
hardbait or a leadhead-and-swimbait switch is a
[California halibut](../species/california-halibut.md#situations--techniques)
call.

## Specs

- **Length: 110 mm** (`eOLoHxoqk6E`).
- **Colors:** ships in multiple color patterns; the exact count is not
  reliably transcribed from the auto-captions (rendered as a garbled
  "7,400,38," asr-uncertain) and is not carried here as a figure
  (`E4CDqBFOaP8`).
- **Running depth:** a shallow runner by design — fishable in 3, 4, or 5 ft
  of water without hitting bottom (`E4CDqBFOaP8`).

## Why it's built shallow

A lure that dives too deep in skinny surf digs into the sand instead of
drawing a strike: "I don't want a jerkbait that gets down to the fish, I
want a jerkbait or minnow lure that gets the fish up to the minnow lure"
(`E4CDqBFOaP8`).

## Color selection

- **Default starting color: white** — the color to tie on first when
  narrowing down a choice (`Qqdt6f8Mjd4`).
- **White with a pink belly** is rated a strong choice — this specific
  two-tone pattern, not plain white or plain pink alone (`Qqdt6f8Mjd4`).
- **Pink is a classic halibut color, and more so for surf halibut
  specifically** than for bay/boat presentations (`Qqdt6f8Mjd4`).
- Pink is also said to be more popular in Japan; it isn't fully clear on
  camera whether that popularity is about the color specifically or about
  hardbait flounder fishing generally, so it's carried here as stated
  (`Qqdt6f8Mjd4`).
- Many SoCal/US halibut hardbaits, including this one, trace their design
  lineage to Japanese flounder-fishing tackle — even though California, not
  Japan, holds the bigger halibut of the two (`Qqdt6f8Mjd4`).

### Why white works

A halibut lying on the bottom sees a baitfish's white belly from below;
from that upward angle the halibut is camouflaged, fast, and positioned to
rush the bait — the stated reason solid white rates as a strong
reaction-bait color for halibut specifically (`XfpUV_z5gFI`). Offered as an
unconfirmed theory, not an established mechanism (`XfpUV_z5gFI`).

## Hooks, hardware, and connection

- **Stock trebles swapped for Owner ST46TN** — a wide-gap, very sharp treble
  with a needle point, replacing the stock hooks (`eOLoHxoqk6E`).
- **Front split ring downsized to a number 2** — on a stock Lucky Craft 110
  the front two hooks sit close enough to hook onto each other; a smaller
  split ring on the front hook cuts down how often that happens, without
  eliminating it. Called the bait's only flaw (`eOLoHxoqk6E`).
- **Terminal connection: Fish Kicker 25 lb quick clips** — a snap-style clip
  (one arm hooks the bait's line-tie eye; pushing the back of the clip loads
  it on) fished instead of tying direct. Loops on quickly and, unlike a
  cross-lock snap, can't come unlocked (`eOLoHxoqk6E`).

## How to fish it

Cast and retrieve, keeping the bait working the 1–4 ft band rather than
letting it dig into the bottom (`E4CDqBFOaP8`). Retrieve cadence and
hook/split-ring upgrade mechanics generalize to
[inshore crankbaits](../techniques/inshore-crankbaits.md), which also scopes
California halibut as an application.

## When to choose it

- Skinny surf water (roughly 1–5 ft (`E4CDqBFOaP8`)), where a deeper hardbait
  digs into the sand instead of drawing a strike: this class is the default
  pick — full situational comparison in
  [California halibut](../species/california-halibut.md#situations--techniques).
- Past roughly 10 ft, the same table routes to a leadhead-and-swimbait
  switch instead.
