# Bay Bass Plastics

The small **finesse soft-plastics** class for the back bays: little scented
sticks, flukes, worms, and grubs fished slow and low for [spotted bay bass
(spotties)](../species/spotted-bay-bass.md) and the mixed bag that shares
their bottom. The bay fish are pressured and not giants, so the whole class
runs **small, light, and bottom-oriented** — a subtle profile fish will
commit to when they refuse a full-size swimbait. This note owns the baits,
heads, and sizing; how they're fished is [ned rig](../techniques/ned-rig.md)
and [drop shot](../techniques/drop-shot.md).

**Sizes:** small — 4-inch flukes/grubs and short TRD-size sticks; the fish
aren't big and the finesse profile is the point.

**Colors:** natural bay-forage patterns (green pumpkin, morning dawn, motor
oil, smelt/sardine, root beer) — match the light and the water and keep it
subtle, on top of the named per-bait colours in the table above.

## Finesse rigging detail

### Ned rig — ZMan Finesse TRD

A mushroom-style Ned head paired with a short soft stickbait — the ZMan
Finesse TRD is the benchmark because the elastic plastic survives **50 fish
on one bait** (`um5MAeCjNDg`). The TRD is tailless, so it's a bottom-hop
bait, not a straight swim. It has a top and a bottom — slightly thinner
toward the top, tapering to a thicker bottom that sinks better, usually
marked with a small line — seat it off the thicker bottom with the hook
exiting the marked top (`um5MAeCjNDg`). Fished [ned rig](../techniques/ned-rig.md).

### Zoom Fluke — nose-entry depth sets the underwater walk

Worked around dock pilings and structure where spotties hold — a subtle
darting profile on a light head or weightless/weighted to fall along a
piling. On a round tungsten jig head (brand/model garbled in the
auto-captions, `asr-uncertain`), thread the fluke in through the nose and
bring the hook point back out **short** — too far in and the bait sits
wrong, fixable by tearing the plastic back a bit to shorten it. The goal is
to see the back of the fluke droop down as it comes off the head rather than
sit straight out; leaving a little of the hook shank exposed (not buried
flush) is what drops the tail and produces a walk-the-dog action fished
**underwater** rather than on top. A drop of super glue at the nose entry can
keep the bait from sliding (`KFdvKXTHSeU`).

### C3 Baits Shimmy — match the hook's own bend, not a fixed measurement

A straight-worm-style finesse stick rigged Texas-style on a worm hook (size
"number two"; brand/model beyond that not confirmed on camera,
`asr-uncertain`). Rig off the bait's one flat side (the belly): enter through
the tip of the bait, center-punched, then immediately bend the hook point
back out — a short skin-hook at the nose, not a deep entry. Slide the bait up
the hook shank past the worm keeper, covering the keeper's tip, then rotate
the hook back so its point faces the bait's belly for the final exit. The
key to keeping the worm straight instead of curled: lay the hook against the
bait first and thumb-mark exactly where the hook's own bend touches the
plastic — that mark, not a guessed distance, is where the point re-enters;
guessing the exit point is the named cause of a bait that curls like a "C"
(`NBSJsN7uTKQ`). The **ice pick**, **Joe Boo**, and **teaser** are named
alongside the Shimmy as also drop-shot-friendly, on the same [VMC Spin Shot
#2 hook](../techniques/drop-shot.md#hook-options) — no separate rigging
detail is given for these three (`_rcxIWhNMSE`).

The ice pick also crosses over to the surf, fished on darter heads for
barred surfperch and corbina, per the same presenter's secondhand account of
other anglers' use — no rigging detail beyond the head type is given
(`aKQqNbgISHY`). The source video is otherwise freshwater
reservoir/lake drift-fishing content — Carolina-rigging the Shimmy for lake
bass, not a SoCal saltwater species — and is not carried here beyond this one
crossover mention.

### Gulp grubs

Scented curly-tail grubs; the scent is the activator for bottom-feeding bay
fish. A grub swims on a constant retrieve (rod tip down at ~45°, steady
wind) on a drop-shot or a light jighead, unlike the tailless TRD.

### Drop-shot plastics

A 4-inch scented finesse fluke (ZMan) is the standard drop-shot bait; swap it
for a grub or a small swimbait. Rig on a long-shank J-hook tied so the hook
points up, suspended 6 to 10 inches above the weight (`k4mD2d6C81k`). Fished
[drop shot](../techniques/drop-shot.md).

## Model builds (single-source, kept side by side)

### Big Hammer 3 in swim bait, orange jighead

A San Diego Bay/Mission Bay spotted bay bass bait, a step up in profile from
the Ned/fluke/grub finesse class above — a small swim (tail-kick) bait rather
than a bottom-hop stick. A 3 in Big Hammer swim bait, fished on an orange jig
head paired with an Invader body — the stated substitute for a discontinued
in-house bait ("margarita") with a similar orange-belly-spot profile.
Also produces incidental calico bass and California halibut on the same bait
(`46ha7J1Fc6A`). The 3 in size is a personal pick for spotted bay bass
specifically; the same swimbait body is also fished in freshwater
largemouth/shallow-river applications, out of scope here. Router: [spotted
bay bass (spotties)](../species/spotted-bay-bass.md); a related seasonal
retrieve-speed pattern (slow winter swimbait, fast summer crankbait) is
logged in [spotted bay bass — feeding
triggers](../species/spotted-bay-bass.md#feeding-triggers).

### Basstrix Flash Trick — halibut drop-shot lure

Named as the best-found lure for a light-finesse drop-shot approach to
[California halibut](../species/california-halibut.md), fished around
structure the presenter calls "green bass" (his own term, not translated to
a species on camera, and not the same as calico bass) — see [drop
shot](../techniques/drop-shot.md) for the rig it's tied into (3/16 oz ball /
1/4 oz drop-shot sinkers, 10–12 lb line, small red octopus or black/red
mosquito hooks). Fished in **3-in and 4-in** sizes. Four named best-producing
colours: **live bait**, **electric smelt**, **baby anchovy**, and **pearl
white** — Bass Trix only started producing pearl white for 2025, and it is
named as a consistently strong halibut colour (`BPxE3xn3gAc`). A shorter
re-cut clip repeats the same bait/colour segment word-for-word — not an
independent sighting, logged for provenance only (`sPs3Civek1w`). Router:
[California halibut](../species/california-halibut.md).

### Berkeley Gulp 3 in shrimp — natural and New Penny

A San Diego Bay half-day/family-charter bait, fished in two colours only —
**natural** and **"New Penny"** (orange and white); a camo pattern and plain
white also work but are not what's actually run. It's a scented soft plastic
that needs no worked retrieve — put it on the bottom and pop it — an easy,
low-skill presentation for half-day charters with kids and families. The
bite still turns on tide, not bait: without the right tide "you can go
through a thousand different baits and the bite's still not going to be
good," but with a good tide the fish "buy a lot of different stuff," so the
Gulp shrimp is the default because it's the easiest to fish well, not
because it out-produces every alternative regardless of conditions
(`AH4Eiya1Hn0`). See the [tide-swing
threshold](../species/spotted-bay-bass.md#where--when) on the species note.
Router: [spotted bay bass (spotties)](../species/spotted-bay-bass.md).

### Freshwater-bass leadheads crossed over — Big Spin & Big Buddy

Freshwater bass tackle works on SoCal calico/spotted bay bass with two
adjustments: step up to thicker-wire hooks and keepers, because SoCal
saltwater bass fight harder than freshwater largemouth/spotted bass, and
expect some colours to change for water clarity/current differences —
otherwise the same baits and approach carry over directly. The **Big
Spin** is a leadhead with a small tail blade for extra flash, vibration, and
profile, built **1/4 oz up to 2 oz**, fished dragged on the bottom or
hopped/bounced through both rock and kelp structure, targeting spotted bay
bass (smaller sizes) and calico bass. The **Big Buddy** leadhead runs
**3/8 oz and 1/2 oz** most, down to **1/4 oz**; weight and colour are both
personal-preference calls — individual anglers lock onto one confidence
colour (example: "red crab") and two anglers on the same water (San Diego
Bay) can each outfish on their own different pick. **Dark green pumpkin** is
named as a freshwater best-seller that has crossed over successfully to
calico bass (`NXvqLUZ6qp8`; name and lure company, Phoenix Baits, as given in
auto-generated narration, `asr-uncertain`; this is a seminar promoting the
presenter's own product line). Router: [spotted bay bass
(spotties)](../species/spotted-bay-bass.md), [calico
bass](../species/calico-bass.md).

### Keitech Shad Impact — pin-tail build

Part of the broader [soft-plastic swimbaits](soft-plastic-swimbaits.md)
class, named there as one of the model benchmarks. The pin-tail build shown
here is visibly different from Keitech's more
common fork-tail version — described as extremely erratic, on a super soft,
pliable plastic that is **not as durable** as some alternatives (contrast
with the TRD's "50 fish on one bait" durability claim above). The plastic
comes pre-scented, so no additional scent is needed. Two head styles for the
coming season: the **Owner Dart(er) head** (captioned "owner daughter heads"
in the auto-generated transcript, read as a likely "darter head,"
`asr-uncertain`) — the general-purpose pick, used across many baits and
styles — and the **Core Tackle jig head**, called out as popular for
hover-strolling with forward-facing sonar. The presenter doesn't personally
own a forward-facing sonar unit but plans to fish the Core Tackle heads
anyway; the stated reason is the head's rigging, not the sonar pairing: it
can be rigged fully enclosed inside the bait, leaving only the hook tie and
the hook itself exposed (`ulWK3kaVQ5k`). This is the same hover-strolling
application separately noted from a different bait/head pairing in [leadhead
mods](../rigging/leadhead-mods.md) (a Bassrix Hover Tricks bait on a
Scrounger/ChatterBait head, for halibut) and in [inshore
crankbaits](../techniques/inshore-crankbaits.md) — three independent
mentions of this freshwater-bass-derived technique crossing into SoCal use,
none yet describing the technique's own mechanics (boat positioning, sonar
reading, retrieve) in enough depth for a dedicated technique note.

## Rigging tips

- **Glue the head to the plastic.** A drop of super glue between a jighead
  and a soft-plastic body (Ned/fluke/paddletail) stops the bait sliding down
  the hook shank when a fish grabs it off-center — a loose bait rides low on
  the hook and misses the hookset (`EGKesj7V64M`).
- **Rotate the hook position to stretch one bait further.** On a thin-wire
  Ned hook, repeated hooksets stretch and eventually tear the plastic right
  at the hook bend; once torn there, the stretch point moves past the hook
  and a fish gets more slack material to mouth before it reaches metal — a
  hookup killer. Rather than replace the bait at the first tear, re-seat the
  hook through a fresh, untorn spot — rotate around the body (top, belly, one
  side, the other side, roughly a quarter turn each time) until the first end
  is used up, then flip the bait and repeat on the other end. Roughly
  **10–20 fish per hook position** is reported, so a single durable-plastic
  bait (TRD or similarly built) can land on the order of **100 fish** across
  all the rotations before it's spent — one session went through 100 spotties
  on one bait, having lost two other rigged baits to a bad hookset and a
  snag. A thin-wire hook is why the tearing happens at all; Z-Man's
  PowerFinesse Ned combo uses a slightly thicker, taller-gap hook reported to
  tear the bait less (`EGKesj7V64M`, `6L8nIFeqvkw`).

## How to fish it

- **Ned rig** — [ned rig](../techniques/ned-rig.md): let the mushroom-head
  TRD sink to the bottom watching the line, bounce-and-drop, and read the
  bite in the line before you feel it. Light spinning: 10 lb braid, 8 lb
  leader, a 4-to-10 lb rod.
- **Drop shot** — [drop shot](../techniques/drop-shot.md): weight on the
  bottom, bait suspended just above, shake it in place; twitch-reel-twitch a
  fluke, steady wind a grub. Light spinning, 10–12 lb leader, light drag.
- **Drag-and-pause retrieve** — an alternative cadence to the Ned
  bounce-and-drop or the drop-shot twitch: with the bait already down, lift
  the rod tip (no reeling) to drag the bait a short distance across the
  bottom, then lower the rod while reeling up the resulting slack so the bait
  sits still through the reset; repeat drag-pause-drag. From the fish's side
  it reads as a drag then a dead stop, not a swim (`EGKesj7V64M`).
- **Wacky rig** — [wacky rig](../techniques/wacky-rig.md): fish a stick worm
  hooked wacky-style with slack in the line, not tight, so both ends keep
  their free wiggle on the fall; read the bite off the line on the surface (a
  segmented "sun line" makes the sink and bottom-stop easy to see) rather
  than waiting to feel it in the rod (`xFW2002SaQk`). No species or location
  named on camera.

## When to choose it

- Tide running, fish locked to ambush stations — bomb a small plastic at the
  structure from up-current: [spotted bay bass — situations →
  techniques](../species/spotted-bay-bass.md#situations--techniques).
- Docks, pilings, riprap — pitch finesse plastics into cover rather than
  crank past it: [spotted bay bass — situations →
  techniques](../species/spotted-bay-bass.md#situations--techniques).
- Back-bay, smaller and more aggressive fish — the Ned rig is the default
  finesse pick: [spotted bay bass — situations →
  techniques](../species/spotted-bay-bass.md#situations--techniques).
- Light-finesse drop-shot on structure — the Basstrix Flash Trick is the
  named pick for [California halibut](../species/california-halibut.md).
- Freshwater-bass leadheads (Big Spin/Big Buddy) — the crossover pick for
  [calico bass](../species/calico-bass.md) rock/kelp structure as well as
  bay docks.
