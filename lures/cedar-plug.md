# Cedar Plug

The cedar plug is a plain weighted-nose wood cylinder — no bib, no rattle — that
has caught tuna in SoCal/Baja for generations. It runs on the surface, either
right in the prop wash or a long way back, on a species-matched **40–60
lb-and-up class** outfit depending on rig. This note covers the standard 6"
cedar plug.

## Specs

| Parameter | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Class | 40–60 lb-and-up trolling class (species-matched) |
| Size | 6 in standard (also seen 8 in, painted, as a contrast option) |
| Troll speed | 5–9 kt; bluefin 5–5.5 kt, yellowfin 6–6.5 kt |
| Running depth | surface / wash |
| Setback | 15–40 ft in the prop wash OR 50–100+ yd back — nothing in between |
| Leader / top-shot | rig-dependent; factory pre-rigged versions ship tied to a leader (`8UAtGqEjDtU`) |

Two positions are where a cedar plug swims right: tight in the wash (15–40
ft) or way back (50–100+ yd) — the water between them does not produce a
working swim. ⚠ Flagged gap — no corpus source: mechanism (why the
in-between distance fails to swim isn't stated). One daisy-chained rig ran at
**~7 mph, set back ~30 yd** (`AH4Eiya1Hn0`) — inside this speed and setback
band, not a separate parameter.

## How to run it

Match the troll speed to the species — 5–5.5 kt for bluefin, 6–6.5 kt for
yellowfin — and place the plug in one of its two working positions, tight in
the wash or way back, never the middle. Run it as the calm, mixed-grade,
over-bait change-up when a skirt or a swimmer isn't drawing bites.

[skipjack tuna](../species/skipjack-tuna.md#gear-summary-class-terms) trolls

both lure notes state" — but the species-keyed speeds here top out at 6–6.5 kt
(yellowfin) and 5–5.5 kt (bluefin), so 6–8 kt sits inside this note's

## Color and finish

An unpainted, natural finish outperforms painted finishes — yellow, blue,
purple, black, orange, green, and brown are all paint jobs on the same wood
body, and the unpainted plug is the one to reach for (`AH4Eiya1Hn0`). ⚠
Fact-check (contradicted-by-source): in `AH4Eiya1Hn0` this cite covers only
the color-list/terminology correction (the colors are all the same wood body,
"wood" meaning "natural") — natural is named as one of three liked patterns
alongside amp chobby and sardine, not as an outperformer; the source never
states natural beats painted finishes or is "the one to reach for."
"Cedar color" is on-the-water vernacular for that natural, unpainted finish
(cameron). A pre-rigged plug's stated favorite pattern is natural
(`8UAtGqEjDtU`), and given a choice on the shelf between an unpainted 6 in
plug and a painted 8 in plug, the unpainted 6 in plug is the pick
(`FXWOIB0TPfE`) — three independent sources on the same natural-over-painted
side. Two other patterns are named as producers: an anchovy pattern
(transcribed "amp chobby," asr-uncertain) and a sardine pattern
(`AH4Eiya1Hn0`).

A cedar plug is a standard confidence lure to keep rigged at all times —
credited with "hundreds of fish" trolled on a daisy chain (`AH4Eiya1Hn0`) and
framed as a decades-proven pick aboard Searcher, still working after two
decades in rotation (`8UAtGqEjDtU`, `FXWOIB0TPfE`).

## When to choose it

- Calm seas, mixed-grade fish, or a bite already keyed on natural bait — pull
  a cedar plug as the change-up over a skirt, a swimmer, or a speed-trolled
  [Mad Mac](mad-mac.md); full when-to-pull logic is in [bluefin tuna —
  trolling](../species/bluefin-tuna-trolling.md).
- General towing mechanics (spread setup, speed control) are in
  [trolling](../techniques/trolling.md).
