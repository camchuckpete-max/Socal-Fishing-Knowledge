# Crocodile-Style Metal Spoons — Major Craft JP Standard & JP Micro

The **crocodile** is a traditional cast-and-retrieve metal spoon shape, long
fished by SoCal anglers generally (`M4G8IKsZEFU`) — the same broad family as
the [Krocodile](iron-jigs.md) already named alongside the SoCal yo-yo irons.
Two named models carry distinct swims: the **Major Craft JP Standard** and
the **Major Craft JP Micro**. In the SoCal surf it is cast for halibut as a
long-cast option at low tide (`eOLoHxoqk6E`); in the Sea of Cortez it is
named as bait in a general casting/yo-yo tackle kit (`M8hOYQ_6rSg`).

| Retrieve behavior | rises through the water column on the wind rather than diving for bottom |

The JP Micro flip-flops side to side on the retrieve — the classic spoon
kick. The JP Standard does not flip-flop on a slow wind; instead it swims
with a body wobble more like a hard bait and stays upright through the
retrieve (`M4G8IKsZEFU`). Because the JP Standard stays upright, the rod tip
can be used to modulate its running depth through the retrieve — no
equivalent rod-tip-depth claim is made for the JP Micro in this source
(`M4G8IKsZEFU`). Both models rise through the water column as they are wound
in rather than hunting bottom, which is the stated reason to reach for either
one in shallow water rather than over deep structure (`M4G8IKsZEFU`).

## When to choose it

- Shallow water where a metal lure that rises as it is wound in — rather
  than digging for bottom — is wanted: pick the flip-flop JP Micro for the
  traditional spoon kick, or the JP Standard when depth control off the rod
  tip matters (`M4G8IKsZEFU`).
- SoCal surf, casting for [California halibut](../species/california-halibut.md):
  a long-cast option at low tide when a diving hard bait would dig into the
  shallow sand. The same underlying footage also appears inside a longer
  halibut surf-fishing video — not independent corroboration, but it supplies
  this species/setting context that the standalone clip lacks
  (`eOLoHxoqk6E`).
- Sea of Cortez casting/yo-yo kit, alongside surface irons and 3–6 oz yo-yo
  jigs — named as bait without further detail on species or technique
  (`M8hOYQ_6rSg`).
- See [iron jigs](iron-jigs.md) for the broader SoCal cast-and-retrieve metal
  class this sits beside, and [surface
  iron](../techniques/surface-iron.md#why-the-kick-works--the-mechanism) for
  the mechanism behind why a kicking/flip-flopping metal draws a bite.
