# Crocodile-Style Metal Spoons — Major Craft JP Standard & JP Micro

The **crocodile** is a traditional cast-and-retrieve metal spoon shape —
the same broad family as the [Krocodile](iron-jigs.md) already named
alongside the SoCal yo-yo irons — long fished by SoCal anglers generally
("we all used to fish crocodiles," Tackle Express, `M4G8IKsZEFU`,
2026-05-11; unregistered channel — medium confidence). Two named models
from this source: the **Major Craft JP Standard** and the **Major Craft JP
Micro** (shown in a **15 g** and a **10 g** size). This note owns the swim
distinction between them; it does not name a species or a specific fishing
location, since neither is stated in the source.

## Two different swims in the same shape

- **JP Micro — flip-flop, traditional-spoon action.** On the retrieve it
  flip-flops side to side, the classic spoon kick.
- **Rises on the wind rather than hunting bottom.** As you wind a crocodile
  of this type in, it comes up through the water column — stated as the
  reason it suits **shallow water**: it doesn't tend to want to dive down
  and hunt the bottom the way a bottom-oriented metal would.
- **JP Standard — a body-wobble action, staying upright.** It does *not* do
  the flip-flop on a slow wind. Instead it swims with a body wobble more
  like a hard bait, and stays upright through the retrieve rather than
  rolling/flopping side to side.
- **Depth control off the rod tip.** Because the JP Standard stays upright
  and swims with that hard-bait-like wobble, the rod tip can be used to
  modulate running depth through the retrieve (no equivalent rod-tip-depth
  claim is made for the JP Micro in this source).

## Reach for this when

Shallow water where a metal lure that rises as it's wound in — rather than
digging for bottom — is wanted; pick the flip-flop JP Micro for the
traditional spoon kick, or the JP Standard when depth control off the rod
tip matters. See [iron jigs](iron-jigs.md) for the broader SoCal
cast-and-retrieve metal class this sits beside, and
[surface iron](../techniques/surface-iron.md#why-the-kick-works--the-mechanism)
for the mechanism behind why a kicking/flip-flopping metal draws a bite.
