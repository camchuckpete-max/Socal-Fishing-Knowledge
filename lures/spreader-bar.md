# Spreader Bar

A spreader bar is a rigid horizontal bar carrying a teaser school of hookless
squids or birds with one stinger lure trailing on the center line — it
presents as a small bait ball rather than a single lure, which is why it
produces on school-grade bluefin. It runs in the **80–100 lb top-shot class**
and lives in the **elevated (shotgun) holder** because the bar itself rides
on the surface (cameron). This note covers full-size **Half Fish Bait Co.**
bluefin bars and a smaller **micro** class fished for dorado in the Sea of
Cortez.

## Specs

**Half Fish bluefin bars (full-size)**
- **Troll speed:** 4–14 kt — bars are the speed-agnostic member of the
  spread, so they match whatever the rest of the spread needs (cameron).
- **Running depth:** rides on the surface, out of the water — a teaser, not
  a diver (cameron).
- **Holder:** the [elevated shotgun
  holder](../techniques/trolling.md#spread-size-and-setback), tip up — the
  highest holder in the spread, so the bar planes on top (cameron).
- **Top-shot / leader:** 80–100 lb top-shot max, drag set well under the
  100 lb internal line so a hooked fish never loads the bar's internal
  rigging to failure (cameron).
- **Internal line (newer Half Fish models):** 100 lb stinger line down the
  middle, 80 lb sides, all Izorline XXX copolymer mono, not fluoro (cameron).
- **Setback:** no fixed distance is established for Half Fish gear
  specifically; a 3-bar spread of unconfirmed brand ran two corner bars
  ~75 yd back and one center bar ~250 yd back, well outside this note's
  80–100 lb top-shot range (`U1AgwmlY5bI`; single-trip data point — see
  [evidence](evidence/spreader-bar.md)).

**Micro spreader bars (dorado, Sea of Cortez)**
- **Class:** a distinct, smaller bar than the Half Fish bluefin bars above,
  scaled to dorado-sized bait (`M8hOYQ_6rSg`).
- **Why it works:** more baits in the water at once draws dorado in far
  better than a single lure — the same small-bait-ball mechanism that makes
  the full-size bluefin bars effective, scaled down (`M8hOYQ_6rSg`).
- **Rigging:** a single rod out back running one micro bar pulls dorado well
  on its own — it doesn't need to anchor a full spread the way a
  bluefin-scale bar does (`M8hOYQ_6rSg`).
- **Context:** feathers are not commonly trolled for dorado in the Sea of
  Cortez; when they are, they run small. The micro spreader bar is the more
  commonly reached-for option there (`M8hOYQ_6rSg`). See the [Baja trolling
  lure kit](../techniques/trolling.md#baja-sea-of-cortez-trolling-lure-kit)
  for the rest of the Sea of Cortez spread.

## When to choose it

- Light-to-moderate, calm-ish conditions and a school-grade bluefin bite,
  wanting a small-bait-ball presentation over a kite, a speed-trolled [Mad
  Mac](../lures/mad-mac.md), or a diver: see [bluefin
  trolling](../species/bluefin-tuna-trolling.md).
- Dorado in the Sea of Cortez: a single micro spreader bar on one rod is a
  strong, low-effort producer on its own — see [Baja trolling lure
  kit](../techniques/trolling.md#baja-sea-of-cortez-trolling-lure-kit).
- General spread geometry, holder elevation, and rod/line-class pairing: see
  [trolling](../techniques/trolling.md#spread-size-and-setback) and [gear
  classes](../tackle/gear-classes.md).
